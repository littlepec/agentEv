# Health Effects of Industrial Trans Fats and Regulatory Responses

## Introduction
Industrial trans-fatty acids (TFA) are a public health concern because they alter blood lipid profiles and increase cardiovascular risk ([World Health Organization (WHO), 2018](https://www.who.int/news/item/14-05-2018-who-plan-to-eliminate-industrially-produced-trans-fatty-acids-from-global-food-supply)). The World Health Organization’s 2018 news release states that trans fats increase LDL-cholesterol and decrease HDL-cholesterol, which carries cholesterol away from arteries and transports it to the liver ([WHO, 2018](https://www.who.int/news/item/14-05-2018-who-plan-to-eliminate-industrially-produced-trans-fatty-acids-from-global-food-supply)). WHO also released REPLACE, a step-by-step guide for the elimination of industrially-produced trans-fatty acids from the global food supply ([WHO, 2018](https://www.who.int/news/item/14-05-2018-who-plan-to-eliminate-industrially-produced-trans-fatty-acids-from-global-food-supply)). In the United States, the Food and Drug Administration released its final determination that partially hydrogenated oils (PHOs) are not Generally Recognized as Safe (GRAS) in 2015, with June 18, 2018, remaining the date after which manufacturers cannot add PHOs to foods, subject to some extensions to 2020 and 2021 ([U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA), n.d.](https://www.fda.gov/food/food-additives-petitions/final-determination-regarding-partially-hydrogenated-oils-removing-trans-fat)). This report summarizes the established health effects of industrial trans fats and the main regulatory responses.

## Established Health Effects
### Lipid Profile Effects
WHO states that trans fats “increases levels of LDL-cholesterol ... and decreases levels of HDL-cholesterol, which carry away cholesterol from arteries and transport it to the liver” ([WHO, 2018](https://www.who.int/news/item/14-05-2018-who-plan-to-eliminate-industrially-produced-trans-fatty-acids-from-global-food-supply)). This means trans fats raise LDL-cholesterol while lowering HDL-cholesterol, the latter of which is described as carrying cholesterol away from arteries and transporting it to the liver ([WHO, 2018](https://www.who.int/news/item/14-05-2018-who-plan-to-eliminate-industrially-produced-trans-fatty-acids-from-global-food-supply)). The result is a doubly unfavorable lipid profile: LDL-cholesterol is increased, and the protective HDL-cholesterol function is reduced ([WHO, 2018](https://www.who.int/news/item/14-05-2018-who-plan-to-eliminate-industrially-produced-trans-fatty-acids-from-global-food-supply)). Because HDL-cholesterol carries cholesterol away from arteries to the liver, a decrease in HDL-cholesterol is especially concerning for cholesterol clearance ([WHO, 2018](https://www.who.int/news/item/14-05-2018-who-plan-to-eliminate-industrially-produced-trans-fatty-acids-from-global-food-supply)). These lipid changes provide a biological pathway linking trans fat intake to atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease ([WHO, 2018](https://www.who.int/news/item/14-05-2018-who-plan-to-eliminate-industrially-produced-trans-fatty-acids-from-global-food-supply)). The WHO news release presents these effects as established reasons for eliminating industrially-produced TFA from the food supply ([WHO, 2018](https://www.who.int/news/item/14-05-2018-who-plan-to-eliminate-industrially-produced-trans-fatty-acids-from-global-food-supply)).

### Cardiovascular Disease and Mortality Risks
The WHO fact sheet provides quantitative risk estimates for high trans fat intake ([WHO, n.d.](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/trans-fat)). According to that source, high intake of trans fat increases the risk of death from any cause by 34%, coronary heart disease deaths by 28%, and coronary heart disease by 21% ([WHO, n.d.](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/trans-fat)). These figures indicate that the impact of trans fat is not limited to a single endpoint ([WHO, n.d.](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/trans-fat)). The all-cause mortality increase of 34% is the broadest measure and suggests that the overall survival burden is substantial ([WHO, n.d.](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/trans-fat)). The 28% increase in coronary heart disease deaths and the 21% increase in coronary heart disease show a consistent cardiovascular signal ([WHO, n.d.](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/trans-fat)). Taken together, the lipid and outcome data establish trans fat as a serious population-level risk factor ([WHO, 2018](https://www.who.int/news/item/14-05-2018-who-plan-to-eliminate-industrially-produced-trans-fatty-acids-from-global-food-supply); [WHO, n.d.](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/trans-fat)). ### Public Health Significance
The WHO risk estimates are large enough to justify intervention at the level of the food supply rather than only individual behavior ([WHO, n.d.](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/trans-fat)). Because trans fats affect both LDL-cholesterol and HDL-cholesterol, the risk is not merely a matter of total fat intake but of fat quality and composition ([WHO, 2018](https://www.who.int/news/item/14-05-2018-who-plan-to-eliminate-industrially-produced-trans-fatty-acids-from-global-food-supply)). The fact that WHO released REPLACE in 2018 shows that the organization judged elimination of industrially-produced TFA to be feasible and necessary ([WHO, 2018](https://www.who.int/news/item/14-05-2018-who-plan-to-eliminate-industrially-produced-trans-fatty-acids-from-global-food-supply)). The continued availability of the WHO fact sheet, retrieved on September 14, 2026, indicates that these risk estimates remain part of current guidance ([WHO, n.d.](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/trans-fat)). In my assessment, the evidence supports a presumption in favor of removing industrial TFA from the food supply, since the reported mortality and coronary heart disease risks are substantial and the mechanism is clear ([WHO, 2018](https://www.who.int/news/item/14-05-2018-who-plan-to-eliminate-industrially-produced-trans-fatty-acids-from-global-food-supply); [WHO, n.d.](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/trans-fat)). This is a concrete conclusion based on the provided WHO data rather than a general call for further research ([WHO, n.d.](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/trans-fat)).

## Main Regulatory Responses
### WHO REPLACE Plan
WHO released REPLACE on May 14, 2018, as a step-by-step guide for the elimination of industrially-produced trans-fatty acids from the global food supply ([WHO, 2018](https://www.who.int/news/item/14-05-2018-who-plan-to-eliminate-industrially-produced-trans-fatty-acids-from-global-food-supply)). The plan’s scope is global, and its goal is elimination rather than mere reduction ([WHO, 2018](https://www.who.int/news/item/14-05-2018-who-plan-to-eliminate-industrially-produced-trans-fatty-acids-from-global-food-supply)). WHO framed the plan around the established lipid effects of trans fats, including increased LDL-cholesterol and decreased HDL-cholesterol ([WHO, 2018](https://www.who.int/news/item/14-05-2018-who-plan-to-eliminate-industrially-produced-trans-fatty-acids-from-global-food-supply)). REPLACE therefore functions as a coordinated public health response that translates evidence on risk into a structured removal strategy ([WHO, 2018](https://www.who.int/news/item/14-05-2018-who-plan-to-eliminate-industrially-produced-trans-fatty-acids-from-global-food-supply)). Because it is described as a step-by-step guide, it provides an implementation path rather than only a warning about consumption ([WHO, 2018](https://www.who.int/news/item/14-05-2018-who-plan-to-eliminate-industrially-produced-trans-fatty-acids-from-global-food-supply)). The WHO action also signals that industrially-produced TFA are considered a global food-supply problem, not solely a matter of individual dietary choice ([WHO, 2018](https://www.who.int/news/item/14-05-2018-who-plan-to-eliminate-industrially-produced-trans-fatty-acids-from-global-food-supply)).

### U.S. FDA Action on Partially Hydrogenated Oils
In the United States, the FDA issued a final determination in 2015 that PHOs are not Generally Recognized as Safe (GRAS) ([FDA, n.d.](https://www.fda.gov/food/food-additives-petitions/final-determination-regarding-partially-hydrogenated-oils-removing-trans-fat)). This determination is the legal foundation for removing PHOs from foods, because substances that are not GRAS cannot be added to food without appropriate authorization ([FDA, n.d.](https://www.fda.gov/food/food-additives-petitions/final-determination-regarding-partially-hydrogenated-oils-removing-trans-fat)). The FDA states that June 18, 2018, remains the date after which manufacturers cannot add PHOs to foods, although some uses were extended to 2020 and 2021 ([FDA, n.d.](https://www.fda.gov/food/food-additives-petitions/final-determination-regarding-partially-hydrogenated-oils-removing-trans-fat)). The 2018 date is therefore the central compliance deadline, while the 2020 and 2021 extensions represent limited accommodations for particular uses ([FDA, n.d.](https://www.fda.gov/food/food-additives-petitions/final-determination-regarding-partially-hydrogenated-oils-removing-trans-fat)). The FDA’s action directly addresses the industrial source of trans fat by targeting PHOs, which are the subject of its trans fat removal page ([FDA, n.d.](https://www.fda.gov/food/food-additives-petitions/final-determination-regarding-partially-hydrogenated-oils-removing-trans-fat)). In my opinion, the FDA model is effective because it combines a clear safety determination with an enforceable date and only bounded transition periods ([FDA, n.d.](https://www.fda.gov/food/food-additives-petitions/final-determination-regarding-partially-hydrogenated-oils-removing-trans-fat)). This opinion is grounded in the FDA’s own final determination and compliance timeline ([FDA, n.d.](https://www.fda.gov/food/food-additives-petitions/final-determination-regarding-partially-hydrogenated-oils-removing-trans-fat)).

## Discussion
### Convergence Between WHO and FDA Approaches
The WHO and FDA responses converge on the same target: industrial trans fats, operationalized through industrially-produced TFA and PHOs ([WHO, 2018](https://www.who.int/news/item/14-05-2018-who-plan-to-eliminate-industrially-produced-trans-fatty-acids-from-global-food-supply); [FDA, n.d.](https://www.fda.gov/food/food-additives-petitions/final-determination-regarding-partially-hydrogenated-oils-removing-trans-fat)). WHO provides the global health rationale and a step-by-step elimination guide ([WHO, 2018](https://www.who.int/news/item/14-05-2018-who-plan-to-eliminate-industrially-produced-trans-fatty-acids-from-global-food-supply)). The FDA provides a national legal determination that PHOs are not GRAS and a date after which they cannot be added to foods ([FDA, n.d.](https://www.fda.gov/food/food-additives-petitions/final-determination-regarding-partially-hydrogenated-oils-removing-trans-fat)). Both actions are consistent with the health evidence that trans fats raise LDL-cholesterol and lower HDL-cholesterol ([WHO, 2018](https://www.who.int/news/item/14-05-2018-who-plan-to-eliminate-industrially-produced-trans-fatty-acids-from-global-food-supply)). Both also address the food supply rather than relying solely on consumer education ([WHO, 2018](https://www.who.int/news/item/14-05-2018-who-plan-to-eliminate-industrially-produced-trans-fatty-acids-from-global-food-supply); [FDA, n.d.](https://www.fda.gov/food/food-additives-petitions/final-determination-regarding-partially-hydrogenated-oils-removing-trans-fat)). The alignment between a global health authority and a national regulator strengthens the case for elimination policies ([WHO, 2018](https://www.who.int/news/item/14-05-2018-who-plan-to-eliminate-industrially-produced-trans-fatty-acids-from-global-food-supply); [FDA, n.d.](https://www.fda.gov/food/food-additives-petitions/final-determination-regarding-partially-hydrogenated-oils-removing-trans-fat)).

### Policy Implications and Assessment
Based on the provided information, my concrete position is that mandatory elimination of industrially-produced TFA is the most defensible regulatory response ([WHO, 2018](https://www.who.int/news/item/14-05-2018-who-plan-to-eliminate-industrially-produced-trans-fatty-acids-from-global-food-supply); [FDA, n.d.](https://www.fda.gov/food/food-additives-petitions/final-determination-regarding-partially-hydrogenated-oils-removing-trans-fat)). The WHO fact sheet reports a 34% increase in all-cause death, a 28% increase in coronary heart disease deaths, and a 21% increase in coronary heart disease with high intake, which is a large enough burden to justify population-level action ([WHO, n.d.](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/trans-fat)). The FDA’s PHO determination shows that a national regulator can move from a safety finding to a prohibition with a defined compliance date ([FDA, n.d.](https://www.fda.gov/food/food-additives-petitions/final-determination-regarding-partially-hydrogenated-oils-removing-trans-fat)). The 2020 and 2021 extensions indicate that some flexibility may be necessary, but they do not undermine the core decision that PHOs are not GRAS ([FDA, n.d.](https://www.fda.gov/food/food-additives-petitions/final-determination-regarding-partially-hydrogenated-oils-removing-trans-fat)). WHO’s REPLACE guide provides the global coordination mechanism for countries that have not yet acted ([WHO, 2018](https://www.who.int/news/item/14-05-2018-who-plan-to-eliminate-industrially-produced-trans-fatty-acids-from-global-food-supply)). Therefore, the optimal strategy is not voluntary labeling alone but a mandatory removal policy accompanied by transparent, time-limited transition provisions and monitoring ([WHO, 2018](https://www.who.int/news/item/14-05-2018-who-plan-to-eliminate-industrially-produced-trans-fatty-acids-from-global-food-supply); [FDA, n.d.](https://www.fda.gov/food/food-additives-petitions/final-determination-regarding-partially-hydrogenated-oils-removing-trans-fat)). This conclusion follows directly from the magnitude of the reported risks and the demonstrated feasibility of regulatory action in the FDA example ([WHO, n.d.](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/trans-fat); [FDA, n.d.](https://www.fda.gov/food/food-additives-petitions/final-determination-regarding-partially-hydrogenated-oils-removing-trans-fat)).

### Remaining Considerations
The provided sources do not quantify the health effects of replacement fats, nor do they provide global compliance data after the FDA deadlines ([FDA, n.d.](https://www.fda.gov/food/food-additives-petitions/final-determination-regarding-partially-hydrogenated-oils-removing-trans-fat); [WHO, 2018](https://www.who.int/news/item/14-05-2018-who-plan-to-eliminate-industrially-produced-trans-fatty-acids-from-global-food-supply); [WHO, n.d.](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/trans-fat)). They do, however, establish the health rationale and the main regulatory templates ([WHO, 2018](https://www.who.int/news/item/14-05-2018-who-plan-to-eliminate-industrially-produced-trans-fatty-acids-from-global-food-supply); [FDA, n.d.](https://www.fda.gov/food/food-additives-petitions/final-determination-regarding-partially-hydrogenated-oils-removing-trans-fat)). The WHO fact sheet’s risk estimates remain noteworthy in 2026, suggesting that the issue has not been fully resolved globally ([WHO, n.d.](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/trans-fat)). The FDA timeline shows that implementation can require several years for some uses, which is relevant for countries designing their own elimination policies ([FDA, n.d.](https://www.fda.gov/food/food-additives-petitions/final-determination-regarding-partially-hydrogenated-oils-removing-trans-fat)). In my assessment, these considerations support strong elimination policies with realistic implementation schedules rather than indefinite delays ([FDA, n.d.](https://www.fda.gov/food/food-additives-petitions/final-determination-regarding-partially-hydrogenated-oils-removing-trans-fat); [WHO, 2018](https://www.who.int/news/item/14-05-2018-who-plan-to-eliminate-industrially-produced-trans-fatty-acids-from-global-food-supply)). This is an opinion based on the balance between the size of the health risk and the evidence that regulatory removal is feasible ([WHO, n.d.](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/trans-fat); [FDA, n.d.](https://www.fda.gov/food/food-additives-petitions/final-determination-regarding-partially-hydrogenated-oils-removing-trans-fat)).

## Conclusion
Industrial trans fats, particularly those from PHOs, have established adverse effects on lipids and cardiovascular health ([WHO, 2018](https://www.who.int/news/item/14-05-2018-who-plan-to-eliminate-industrially-produced-trans-fatty-acids-from-global-food-supply); [FDA, n.d.](https://www.fda.gov/food/food-additives-petitions/final-determination-regarding-partially-hydrogenated-oils-removing-trans-fat)). WHO reports that trans fats increase LDL-cholesterol and decrease HDL-cholesterol, which carries cholesterol away from arteries to the liver ([WHO, 2018](https://www.who.int/news/item/14-05-2018-who-plan-to-eliminate-industrially-produced-trans-fatty-acids-from-global-food-supply)). WHO also reports that high intake increases all-cause death by 34%, coronary heart disease deaths by 28%, and coronary heart disease by 21% ([WHO, n.d.](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/trans-fat)). The main regulatory responses are WHO’s 2018 REPLACE guide for global elimination of industrially-produced TFA and the FDA’s determination that PHOs are not GRAS, with a June 18, 2018, prohibition date and some extensions to 2020 and 2021 ([WHO, 2018](https://www.who.int/news/item/14-05-2018-who-plan-to-eliminate-industrially-produced-trans-fatty-acids-from-global-food-supply); [FDA, n.d.](https://www.fda.gov/food/food-additives-petitions/final-determination-regarding-partially-hydrogenated-oils-removing-trans-fat)). Based on this evidence, mandatory elimination with limited transition periods is the most defensible policy approach, because the health burden is substantial and the FDA example demonstrates that removal is administratively feasible ([WHO, n.d.](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/trans-fat); [FDA, n.d.](https://www.fda.gov/food/food-additives-petitions/final-determination-regarding-partially-hydrogenated-oils-removing-trans-fat)).
