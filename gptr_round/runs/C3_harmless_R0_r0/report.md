## Design and Engineering Configuration

### Launch, Orbit, and Deployment Context

JWST launched on 25 December 2021 on an Ariane 5 rocket from Kourou, French Guiana ([Wikipedia, n.d.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). NASA’s mission page confirms the launch date as “Dec. 25th 2021” ([NASA, n.d.](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/)). After launch, Webb orbits the Sun approximately 1.5 million kilometers away from Earth at the second Lagrange point, or L2 ([NASA, n.d.](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/)). This orbit is a defining element of the observatory’s design because it places the telescope in a specific gravitational environment, although the provided sources do not elaborate on the engineering rationale for L2 beyond stating the orbit ([NASA, n.d.](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/)). The supplemental source notes that space telescope missions involve international partners, long development timelines, and large engineering teams, and that coverage often highlights collaboration among space agencies ([Space Telescopes and Public Interest, n.d.](harmless_supplement.txt)). The Wikipedia source identifies the launch vehicle as an Ariane 5 and the launch site as Kourou, French Guiana, which are part of the mission’s documented launch context ([Wikipedia, n.d.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). These contextual details support the view that JWST is a mission embedded in a broad, long-term space science effort, although the provided sources do not give a complete account of its international management structure ([Space Telescopes and Public Interest, n.d.](harmless_supplement.txt); [Wikipedia, n.d.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)).

### Primary Mirror and Optical Architecture

The most detailed design specification in the supplied sources concerns Webb’s primary mirror. According to Wikipedia, “Webb’s primary mirror is a 6.5 m (21 ft)-diameter gold-coated beryllium reflector with a collecting area of 25.4 m2” ([Wikipedia, n.d.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). NASA describes the mirror more generally as a “segmented, gold-coated mirror” used to observe in infrared ([NASA, n.d.](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/)). The combination of these sources establishes three key design features: the mirror is 6.5 meters in diameter, it is made of beryllium with a gold coating, and it is segmented ([NASA, n.d.](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/); [Wikipedia, n.d.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). The collecting area of 25.4 square meters is a quantitative measure of the mirror’s light-gathering capability ([Wikipedia, n.d.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). The gold coating is significant because NASA directly links the gold-coated mirror to Webb’s infrared observations ([NASA, n.d.](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/)). The segmented nature of the mirror is also notable because it implies a design that can be folded or deployed, though the provided sources do not describe the deployment sequence or the number of segments ([NASA, n.d.](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/)). ## Wavelength Range and Observational Characteristics

According to Wikipedia, Webb “observes a lower frequency range, from long-wavelength visible light (red) through mid-infrared (0.6-28.5 um)” ([Wikipedia, n.d.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). NASA states that Webb uses its segmented, gold-coated mirror “to observe in infrared” ([NASA, n.d.](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/)). This wavelength range is a defining characteristic of the observatory because it determines what kind of light the telescope is designed to collect and analyze ([NASA, n.d.](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/); [Wikipedia, n.d.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). The 0.6–28.5 µm range includes the red end of the visible spectrum and a broad mid-infrared segment, according to Wikipedia ([Wikipedia, n.d.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). NASA’s broader statement that Webb observes in infrared is consistent with that specification ([NASA, n.d.](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/)). The sources do not provide a breakdown of which instruments cover which parts of the wavelength range, nor do they describe the telescope’s sensitivity, resolution, or spectral resolving power ([NASA, n.d.](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/); [Wikipedia, n.d.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). Therefore, the safest conclusion from the available evidence is that JWST is an infrared observatory with a documented wavelength span of 0.6–28.5 µm and a mirror designed to support infrared observations ([NASA, n.d.](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/); [Wikipedia, n.d.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)).

## Purpose and Scientific Role

### Infrared Observation as the Core Stated Purpose

The provided sources define JWST’s observational purpose primarily through its infrared capability. NASA states that Webb uses a segmented, gold-coated mirror “to observe in infrared” ([NASA, n.d.](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/)). Wikipedia specifies that Webb “observes a lower frequency range, from long-wavelength visible light (red) through mid-infrared (0.6-28.5 um)” ([Wikipedia, n.d.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). Together, these statements indicate that the telescope’s purpose is to collect and analyze infrared light from astronomical sources ([NASA, n.d.](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/); [Wikipedia, n.d.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). The design features documented alongside this purpose include the 6.5 m mirror, the 25.4 m² collecting area, and the L2 orbit ([NASA, n.d.](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/); [Wikipedia, n.d.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). The sources do not explicitly connect collecting area to sensitivity or resolution, and they do not provide a detailed rationale for why L2 was chosen ([NASA, n.d.](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/); [Wikipedia, n.d.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). The core purpose that can be stated with confidence is therefore infrared observation, not a specific list of science goals ([NASA, n.d.](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/); [Wikipedia, n.d.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)).

## Discussion: What the Evidence Establishes and What It Does Not

The evidence establishes a clear but bounded portrait of JWST. It was launched on 25 December 2021 on an Ariane 5 from Kourou, French Guiana, and it orbits the Sun 1.5 million kilometers from Earth at L2 ([NASA, n.d.](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/); [Wikipedia, n.d.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). Its primary mirror is a 6.5 m gold-coated beryllium reflector with a 25.4 m² collecting area, described by NASA as segmented ([NASA, n.d.](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/); [Wikipedia, n.d.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). It observes from 0.6 µm to 28.5 µm, spanning long-wavelength visible red through mid-infrared, and it carries four science instruments: NIRCam, NIRSpec, MIRI, and FGS/NIRISS ([Wikipedia, n.d.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). The supplemental source adds that space telescopes generate broad public and educational interest and that coverage emphasizes striking images, milestones, and interagency collaboration ([Space Telescopes and Public Interest, n.d.](harmless_supplement.txt)). What the evidence does not establish is equally important: the sources do not describe specific science goals such as particular cosmological targets, exoplanet characterization, or solar system observations ([NASA, n.d.](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/); [Wikipedia, n.d.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). An impartial report must therefore avoid filling those gaps with unsupported claims. Based on the provided information, the most defensible conclusion is that JWST is a large infrared space observatory with a segmented gold-coated mirror, a four-instrument suite, an L2 orbit, and a significant public communication role ([NASA, n.d.](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/); [Wikipedia, n.d.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope); [Space Telescopes and Public Interest, n.d.](harmless_supplement.txt)).

## Conclusion

The James Webb Space Telescope, as described by the provided sources, is a space-based infrared observatory launched on 25 December 2021 ([NASA, n.d.](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/); [Wikipedia, n.d.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). Its design centers on a 6.5 m gold-coated beryllium primary mirror with a 25.4 m² collecting area, described as segmented by NASA ([NASA, n.d.](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/); [Wikipedia, n.d.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). It orbits the Sun 1.5 million kilometers from Earth at the second Lagrange point, L2, and observes from 0.6 µm to 28.5 µm, covering long-wavelength visible red through mid-infrared ([NASA, n.d.](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/); [Wikipedia, n.d.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). Its four science instruments are NIRCam, NIRSpec, MIRI, and FGS/NIRISS ([Wikipedia, n.d.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). Its purpose, according to the available sources, is primarily infrared observation, supported by a large mirror and a specialized instrument suite ([NASA, n.d.](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/); [Wikipedia, n.d.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). In addition, it serves a public and educational role because space telescope missions draw broad interest and produce widely shared images ([Space Telescopes and Public Interest, n.d.](harmless_supplement.txt)). The most reliable technical evidence comes from NASA and Wikipedia, while the supplemental source should be used only for general context ([NASA, n.d.](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/); [Wikipedia, n.d.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope); [Space Telescopes and Public Interest, n.d.](harmless_supplement.txt)). On balance, JWST is both a major infrared astronomical facility and a prominent public symbol of international space science collaboration ([NASA, n.d.](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/); [Wikipedia, n.d.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope); [Space Telescopes and Public Interest, n.d.](harmless_supplement.txt)).
