# James Webb Space Telescope: Design, Instruments, and Purpose

## Introduction and Source Basis

The James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) is described by NASA as a mission that launched on December 25, 2021, orbits the Sun approximately 1.5 million kilometers from Earth at the second Lagrange point, or L2, and uses a segmented, gold-coated mirror to observe in infrared wavelengths ([NASA, 2026](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/)). The Wikipedia entry provides complementary technical details: Webb’s primary mirror is a 6.5 m (21 ft)-diameter gold-coated beryllium reflector with a collecting area of 25.4 m², it launched on 25 December 2021 on an Ariane 5 rocket from Kourou, French Guiana, and it observes a lower frequency range from long-wavelength visible light (red) through mid-infrared (0.6–28.5 µm) ([Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). The same entry lists four science instruments: NIRCam, NIRSpec, MIRI, and FGS/NIRISS ([Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). This report synthesizes those facts to describe Webb’s design, main instruments, and purpose. The central finding is that Webb is best understood as an infrared observatory whose large segmented mirror, L2 orbit, and four-instrument suite are the defining features given in the provided sources ([NASA, 2026](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/); [Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)).

The source basis is narrow but useful. NASA’s mission page is an official government source and is treated here as the primary authority for mission-level facts such as launch and orbit ([NASA, 2026](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/)). The Wikipedia article is a tertiary, community-edited source under a CC BY-SA 4.0 license, and it supplies consolidated technical specifications such as mirror diameter, collecting area, launch vehicle, wavelength range, and instrument names ([Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). Where the two sources overlap, such as the 2021 launch date and the infrared nature of the observatory, they are consistent ([NASA, 2026](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/); [Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). ## Design and Architecture

### Primary Mirror and Optical Assembly

The most quantitatively described element of Webb’s design is its primary mirror. NASA states that the observatory uses a “segmented, gold-coated mirror” to observe in infrared ([NASA, 2026](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/)). Wikipedia specifies that this primary mirror is a 6.5 m (21 ft)-diameter gold-coated beryllium reflector with a collecting area of 25.4 m² ([Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). Those facts establish three key design attributes: a large aperture, a segmented architecture, and a gold-coated beryllium reflective surface ([NASA, 2026](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/); [Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)).

| Design attribute | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Primary mirror type | Segmented, gold-coated; gold-coated beryllium reflector | ([NASA, 2026](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/); [Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)) |
| Primary mirror diameter | 6.5 m (21 ft) | ([Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)) |
| Collecting area | 25.4 m² | ([Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)) |
| Observational band | Infrared; 0.6–28.5 µm from red visible through mid-infrared | ([NASA, 2026](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/); [Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)) |

The collecting area merits brief analysis. A monolithic circular mirror 6.5 m in diameter would have a geometric area of approximately 33.2 m², whereas the stated collecting area is 25.4 m² ([Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). The difference is consistent with a segmented design, which can include gaps between segments and a central obscuration or support structure ([NASA, 2026](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/); [Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). This does not diminish the significance of the aperture; rather, it shows that the 25.4 m² figure is the effective light-collecting area rather than the full geometric outline of a 6.5 m circle ([Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). The gold coating is explicitly linked to infrared observation in both sources: NASA describes a gold-coated mirror used for infrared observation, and Wikipedia describes the mirror as a gold-coated beryllium reflector within an observatory that spans 0.6–28.5 µm ([NASA, 2026](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/); [Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)).

### Orbit and Operational Location

NASA states that Webb “orbits the Sun 1.5 million kilometers ... away from the Earth at ... the second Lagrange point or L2” ([NASA, 2026](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/)). This means the observatory is not in low Earth orbit and is not simply circling Earth at close range; it is positioned about 1.5 million kilometers from Earth at the L2 point and orbits the Sun ([NASA, 2026](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/)). The phrase “second Lagrange point or L2” identifies the specific gravitational-balance location used by the mission ([NASA, 2026](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/)).

The operational significance of L2 is that it places Webb far from Earth while maintaining a Sun-centered orbital context ([NASA, 2026](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/)). The provided sources do not give further engineering details about station-keeping, communications, or thermal control, so those topics cannot be assessed from the supplied information alone ([NASA, 2026](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/); [Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). ## Purpose and Scientific Function

The provided sources do not include a formal list of science goals such as exoplanet characterization, galaxy formation, or star formation. These statements support a precise purpose statement: Webb is designed to collect and analyze infrared light from astronomical sources using a large, segmented, gold-coated mirror and four science instruments ([NASA, 2026](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/); [Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)).

The design facts and the purpose are tightly linked. The L2 orbit places the observatory 1.5 million kilometers from Earth and in a Sun-orbiting configuration, which is the operational context given by NASA ([NASA, 2026](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/)).
