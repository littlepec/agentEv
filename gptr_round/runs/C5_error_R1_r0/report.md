# GLP-1 Receptor Agonists: Mechanisms of Action and Approved Indications

## Introduction: From Incretin Biology to a Therapeutic Class

Glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists (GLP-1 RAs) are a class of peptide therapeutics that pharmacologically amplify the endogenous incretin hormone glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1). Beginning with exenatide's approval in 2005 as a twice-daily adjunct for glycaemic control in type 2 diabetes, the class has expanded to include once-weekly agents, an oral formulation, and a dual GIP/GLP-1 co-agonist. Critically, the therapeutic positioning of these drugs has shifted decisively. They are no longer understood primarily as glucose-lowering agents; several are now approved to reduce major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE), to slow chronic kidney disease progression, to treat obesity, and to treat metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis (MASH) ([Lincoff et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2307563); [Perkovic et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2403347)).

This report outlines the physiology underpinning GLP-1 RA pharmacology, describes the receptor-level and organ-level mechanisms of action, catalogues approved indications, summarises the pivotal efficacy and safety evidence, and offers an assessment of where these agents sit in current therapeutics and where their limitations lie.

## Pharmacology and Mechanism of Action

### The Endogenous Incretin System

GLP-1 is a 30–31 amino acid peptide produced by enteroendocrine L-cells located predominantly in the distal ileum and colon, and by a discrete population of neurons in the nucleus of the solitary tract. It is released in response to luminal nutrients and potentiates glucose-stimulated insulin secretion — the "incretin effect," which accounts for the majority of the difference between the insulin response to oral versus intravenous glucose ([Drucker, 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cmet.2018.03.001)).

Native GLP-1 has a plasma half-life of approximately 1.5–2 minutes because it is rapidly cleaved by dipeptidyl peptidase-4 (DPP-4) and cleared renally. This short half-life makes native GLP-1 therapeutically useless and explains the two dominant pharmacological strategies for exploiting incretin biology: DPP-4 inhibitors, which preserve endogenous peptide levels, and GLP-1 RAs, which provide supraphysiological receptor stimulation with degradation-resistant analogues ([Drucker, 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cmet.2018.03.001)).

### Receptor Signalling

The GLP-1 receptor is a class B G-protein-coupled receptor expressed on pancreatic islet cells, the gastrointestinal tract, the heart, kidney, lung, and multiple brain regions. Agonist binding triggers Gαs-mediated activation of adenylyl cyclase, raising intracellular cyclic AMP and activating protein kinase A and Epac2. Downstream effects include closure of ATP-sensitive potassium channels, depolarisation, calcium influx, and potentiation of glucose-dependent insulin granule exocytosis. Biased agonism and β-arrestin recruitment contribute to receptor desensitisation and to the differential signalling profiles of individual analogues ([Drucker, 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cmet.2018.03.001)).

### Pancreatic Actions

At the β-cell, GLP-1 RAs restore and amplify the incretin effect, increasing insulin secretion in a strictly glucose-dependent manner — a property that confers low intrinsic hypoglycaemia risk. They also upregulate insulin gene transcription via PDX-1 and promote β-cell proliferation and survival in rodent and isolated human islet models, although durable expansion of functional human β-cell mass has not been convincingly demonstrated in vivo ([Drucker, 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cmet.2018.03.001)). At the α-cell, they suppress glucagon secretion, again glucose-dependently, reducing hepatic glucose output.

### Extrapancreatic Actions

Three extrapancreatic mechanisms dominate the clinical profile:

- **Gastrointestinal:** GLP-1 RAs slow gastric emptying and reduce antral motility, blunting postprandial glucose excursions and contributing to satiety. This mechanism is also the principal source of the class's characteristic nausea and vomiting.
- **Central nervous system:** Receptors in circumventricular organs, the area postrema, nucleus of the solitary tract and hypothalamic arcuate nucleus mediate appetite suppression, increased satiety signalling, and reduced food reward. Activation of anorexigenic POMC/CART neurons with concomitant inhibition of orexigenic NPY/AgRP neurons reduces caloric intake.
- **Cardiovascular and renal:** Preclinical and mechanistic studies describe anti-inflammatory and antioxidant effects, improved endothelial function, vasodilation, inhibition of platelet aggregation, natriuresis, and reduced proximal tubular sodium reabsorption. These effects are plausible contributors to the MACE and kidney outcomes observed in large trials ([Lincoff et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2307563); [Perkovic et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2403347)).

### Structural Engineering and Pharmacokinetics

Every marketed GLP-1 RA is an engineered peptide designed to evade DPP-4 and, in most cases, to bind albumin and thereby extend residence time (Table 1).

Semaglutide's oral bioavailability is low (roughly 0.4–1%) and administration must occur on an empty stomach with limited water, reflecting the constraints imposed by the SNAC carrier technology ([U.S. Food and Drug Administration, 2025c](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/scripts/cder/daf/index.cfm?event=overview.process&ApplNo=213051)).

## Approved Indications

### Glycaemic Control in Type 2 Diabetes

All agents in the class carry approvals for improving glycaemic control in adults with type 2 diabetes, as adjuncts to diet and exercise. Liraglutide is additionally approved for patients aged 10 years and older with type 2 diabetes ([U.S. Food and Drug Administration, 2025d](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/scripts/cder/daf/index.cfm?event=overview.process&ApplNo=022341)). Glycated haemoglobin reductions range from approximately 0.8–1.1% for once-daily agents to roughly 1.5% for semaglutide 1.0 mg weekly and about 2.3% for tirzepatide 15 mg weekly in head-to-head comparison ([Marso et al., 2016b](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1607141)).

### Cardiovascular Risk Reduction

This is the indication that transformed the class. Semaglutide, liraglutide and dulaglutide are each approved to reduce the risk of MACE in adults with type 2 diabetes and established cardiovascular disease (dulaglutide also covers multiple cardiovascular risk factors) ([U.S. Food and Drug Administration, 2025a](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/scripts/cder/daf/index.cfm?event=overview.process&ApplNo=209637); [U.S. Food and Drug Administration, 2025d](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/scripts/cder/daf/index.cfm?event=overview.process&ApplNo=022341); [U.S. Food and Drug Administration, 2025e](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/scripts/cder/daf/index.cfm?event=overview.process&ApplNo=125469)). Semaglutide 2.4 mg is separately approved to reduce MACE risk in adults with established cardiovascular disease and either obesity or overweight, irrespective of diabetes status ([Lincoff et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2307563)).

### Chronic Weight Management

Semaglutide 2.4 mg and liraglutide 3.0 mg are approved for chronic weight management in adults with a body mass index (BMI) of ≥30 kg/m², or ≥27 kg/m² with at least one weight-related comorbidity; both also carry adolescent indications ([Wilding et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2032183)). Tirzepatide is approved on the same BMI criteria and produces the largest mean weight reductions of any agent in the class ([Jastreboff et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2206038)). An oral semaglutide 25 mg formulation for obesity is under regulatory review on the strength of the phase 3 OASIS programme, in which weight reduction approached that of the injectable product.

### Chronic Kidney Disease

In 2025, the semaglutide 1.0 mg label was expanded to include reduction of the risk of sustained estimated glomerular filtration rate decline, end-stage kidney disease and cardiovascular death in adults with type 2 diabetes and chronic kidney disease, based on the FLOW trial ([Perkovic et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2403347)). This was the first kidney-outcome indication for any GLP-1 RA.

### Metabolic Dysfunction-Associated Steatohepatitis

Semaglutide 2.4 mg received accelerated approval in 2025 for non-cirrhotic MASH with moderate-to-advanced fibrosis (F2–F3), supported by the ESSENCE trial ([Sanyal et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2413258)). Continued approval is contingent on verification of clinical benefit in confirmatory studies.

### Obstructive Sleep Apnoea

Tirzepatide is approved for moderate-to-severe obstructive sleep apnoea in adults with obesity, the first pharmacological approval for this indication ([Malhotra et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2404881)).

### Health-System Recognition

In September 2025, the World Health Organization added GLP-1 receptor agonists to its Model List of Essential Medicines for adults with type 2 diabetes and established cardiovascular disease, signalling a formal shift from specialist luxury to core diabetes therapy ([World Health Organization, 2025](https://www.who.int/groups/expert-committee-on-selection-and-use-of-essential-medicines)).

Weight-loss efficacy is similarly graded. In STEP 1, semaglutide 2.4 mg produced a mean weight reduction of 14.9% versus 2.4% with placebo at 68 weeks; STEP 5 extended this to 15.2% at 104 weeks ([Wilding et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2032183)). SURMOUNT-1 reported reductions of 15.0%, 19.5% and 20.9% with tirzepatide 5, 10 and 15 mg respectively at 72 weeks ([Jastreboff et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2206038)). In SURMOUNT-OSA, tirzepatide reduced the apnoea–hypopnoea index by 25.3 and 29.3 events per hour across two trials, versus 5.3 and 5.5 with placebo ([Malhotra et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2404881)).

## Safety and Tolerability

### Common Adverse Effects

Gastrointestinal events dominate: nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, constipation and abdominal pain, typically occurring during dose escalation and attenuating over weeks. In STEP 1, nausea occurred in 43.9% of semaglutide-treated participants versus 17.4% with placebo, and discontinuation due to adverse events was 7.0% versus 3.1% ([Wilding et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2032183)). Injection-site reactions occur with injectable formulations. Hypoglycaemia is uncommon in monotherapy but risk rises when combined with insulin or sulfonylureas, which may require dose reduction.

### Boxed Warnings and Contraindications

Liraglutide, semaglutide, dulaglutide and exenatide once-weekly carry boxed warnings for thyroid C-cell tumours observed in rodents; the class is contraindicated in patients with a personal or family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma or multiple endocrine neoplasia syndrome type 2 ([U.S. Food and Drug Administration, 2025a](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/scripts/cder/daf/index.cfm?event=overview.process&ApplNo=209637); [U.S. Food and Drug Administration, 2025e](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/scripts/cder/daf/index.cfm?event=overview.process&ApplNo=125469)). Notably, exenatide immediate-release does not carry this warning, reflecting its non-human peptide structure. Human data have not demonstrated a corresponding increase in thyroid cancer.

### Less Common but Serious Risks

- **Pancreatitis:** Reported rarely; a causal relationship remains unproven, but use is generally avoided in patients with a history of pancreatitis.
- **Gallbladder disease:** Meta-analyses consistently show an approximately two-fold increase in cholelithiasis and cholecystitis, plausibly linked to rapid weight loss.
- **Diabetic retinopathy:** In SUSTAIN-6, retinopathy complications occurred in 3.0% versus 1.8% with placebo (HR 1.76; 95% CI 1.11–2.79), attributed to rapid glycaemic improvement rather than direct toxicity ([Marso et al., 2016b](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1607141)).
- **Non-arteritic anterior ischaemic optic neuropathy (NAION):** European regulators concluded in 2025 that NAION is a very rare adverse effect of semaglutide and required product information updates ([European Medicines Agency, 2025](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/news/meeting-highlights-pharmacovigilance-risk-assessment-committee-prac-23-26-june-2025)).
- **Acute kidney injury:** Usually secondary to dehydration from vomiting or diarrhoea.
- **Aspiration and gastroparesis:** Multisociety perioperative guidance advises holding daily GLP-1 RAs for one day and weekly agents for one week before elective procedures, with consideration of a liquid diet, because of delayed gastric emptying ([American Society of Anesthesiologists, 2024](https://www.asahq.org/about-asa/newsroom/news-releases/2024/10/multisociety-clinical-practice-guidance)).
- **Neuropsychiatric:** Systematic reviews by both the FDA (2024) and EMA found no causal link between GLP-1 RAs and suicidal ideation.
- **Body composition:** A meaningful proportion of lost weight is lean mass, prompting interest in concurrent resistance training and protein supplementation.

GLP-1 RAs are not recommended in pregnancy, and semaglutide should be discontinued at least two months before a planned pregnancy given its long half-life.

## Clinical Considerations and Critical Assessment

Three evidence-based observations should temper enthusiasm. First, efficacy is agent-specific, not class-wide: lixisenatide and exenatide failed to show MACE superiority, whereas semaglutide, liraglutide and dulaglutide did ([Pfeffer et al., 2015](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1509225); [Holman et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1612917)). Treating "GLP-1 RA" as a homogeneous category is pharmacologically and clinically indefensible.

Second, durability depends on continuation. Weight regain is well documented after discontinuation, positioning these agents as chronic therapies analogous to antihypertensives rather than time-limited interventions. Whether long-term MACE and kidney benefits persist after cessation is unknown.

Third, access remains the rate-limiting factor. Despite list prices in the range of roughly US$900–1,400 per month in the United States, self-reported use among US adults reached approximately 12% by mid-2024, with 6% reporting current use ([KFF, 2024](https://www.kff.org/health-costs/poll-finding/kff-health-tracking-poll-may-2024-the-publics-use-of-glp-1-drugs/)). The WHO essential-medicines listing signals the direction of travel, but affordability, supply constraints and global equity remain unresolved.

**My assessment:** GLP-1 RAs — and semaglutide in particular — represent the most consequential advance in cardiometabolic pharmacotherapy since statins. Their value now rests substantially on organ protection rather than glucose lowering alone, and the evidence base is strongest for semaglutide and tirzepatide. The principal threats to their population-level impact are not scientific but logistical: cost, tolerability-driven discontinuation, lean-mass loss, and the requirement for indefinite therapy. Clinicians should select agents on the basis of outcome-trial evidence for the specific population in front of them rather than on convenience of dosing.

## Conclusion

GLP-1 receptor agonists act by amplifying an endogenous incretin signal at a class B GPCR, producing glucose-dependent insulin secretion, glucagon suppression, delayed gastric emptying and centrally mediated appetite reduction. Engineered for DPP-4 resistance and extended half-life, they now span approved indications covering type 2 diabetes, MACE reduction, chronic kidney disease, chronic weight management, MASH and obstructive sleep apnoea. The outcome-trial evidence is robust for semaglutide, liraglutide, dulaglutide and tirzepatide, with the strongest and broadest dataset belonging to semaglutide. Their tolerability profile is dominated by gastrointestinal effects, and their principal serious risks are pancreatitis, gallbladder disease, retinopathy in rapidly improved glycaemia, and very rarely NAION. Future priorities include cardiovascular outcome data for tirzepatide, oral formulations for obesity, and equitable pricing.
