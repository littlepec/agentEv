## Introduction

Glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) receptor agonists are a therapeutic class that mimics the action of the endogenous incretin hormone GLP-1. Since the approval of exenatide in 2005 as the first agent in the class, the field has expanded to include agents administered twice daily, once daily, once weekly, and orally, with clinical indications now spanning glycaemic control, chronic weight management, cardiovascular risk reduction, kidney protection, and metabolic liver disease ([U.S. Food and Drug Administration, 2025](https://www.fda.gov/drugs)). The class is now among the most commercially and clinically consequential in modern pharmacotherapy, and its evidence base has shifted the therapeutic target from glucose lowering alone to modification of cardiometabolic disease trajectory.

## Physiology of the Endogenous Incretin System

GLP-1 is secreted by enteroendocrine L-cells located predominantly in the distal ileum and colon in response to nutrient ingestion. The native hormone has a plasma half-life of only approximately 1.5 to 2 minutes because it is rapidly cleaved by dipeptidyl peptidase-4 (DPP-4) and cleared renally ([Drucker, 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cmet.2018.03.001)). This extreme fragility explains both the therapeutic strategy of DPP-4 inhibition and the structural engineering required to produce long-acting GLP-1 receptor agonists.

Physiologically, GLP-1 acts through a Class B G protein-coupled receptor that signals principally via Gs-mediated activation of adenylyl cyclase, raising intracellular cyclic AMP and engaging protein kinase A and Epac2 pathways ([Drucker, 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cmet.2018.03.001)). The downstream consequences are fourfold: potentiation of glucose-stimulated insulin secretion; suppression of glucagon release; slowing of gastric emptying; and reduction of appetite through central receptors in the hypothalamus and brainstem. Because the insulinotropic effect is glucose-dependent — diminishing as euglycaemia is approached — GLP-1 receptor agonists carry a low intrinsic risk of hypoglycaemia when used as monotherapy, a property that distinguishes them from sulfonylureas and insulin ([American Diabetes Association Professional Practice Committee, 2025](https://diabetesjournals.org/care)).

## Mechanism of Action of GLP-1 Receptor Agonists

### Receptor Pharmacology and Signal Transduction

All agents in the class bind the same GLP-1 receptor and produce qualitatively similar signalling. Binding triggers Gs-coupled cyclic AMP accumulation in pancreatic beta cells, enhancing exocytosis of insulin-containing granules and, in preclinical models, promoting beta-cell proliferation and survival ([Drucker, 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cmet.2018.03.001)). In alpha cells, receptor activation restores glucose-dependent suppression of glucagon, reducing hepatic glucose output. Importantly, the receptor is also expressed in the arcuate nucleus, area postrema, and nucleus tractus solitarius, providing the anatomical basis for central appetite regulation and for the nausea that is the class's signature adverse effect.

### Gastric and Central Appetite Effects

Slowing of gastric emptying contributes to postprandial glucose flattening, although this effect appears more prominent with short-acting agents such as exenatide twice daily and tends to attenuate with continuous long-acting exposure. The dominant weight-loss mechanism, however, is central: GLP-1 receptor activation modulates pro-opiomelanocortin (POMC)/CART neurons and suppresses NPY/AgRP neurons in the arcuate nucleus, reducing energy intake and altering food preference ([Drucker, 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cmet.2018.03.001)).

### Structural Modifications and Pharmacokinetics

The class achieves durability through three principal strategies. Exenatide, a synthetic exendin-4 peptide originally isolated from the saliva of the Gila monster, shares only about 53% sequence homology with human GLP-1 but is naturally resistant to DPP-4. Liraglutide attaches a C-16 palmitic acid moiety, promoting albumin binding and extending half-life to roughly 13 hours. Semaglutide combines an aminoisobutyric acid substitution at position 8 with a C-18 fatty diacid, yielding a half-life of approximately 165 hours, or about one week, after subcutaneous injection ([Drucker, 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cmet.2018.03.001)). Dulaglutide fuses a GLP-1 analogue to an immunoglobulin G4 Fc fragment, producing a half-life of roughly five days. Oral semaglutide is co-formulated with the absorption enhancer sodium N-[8-(2-hydroxybenzoyl)amino]caprylate (SNAC) to permit gastric absorption.

## Approved Clinical Uses

### Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus

Glycaemic control remains the foundational indication for every agent in the class. Guidelines position GLP-1 receptor agonists as preferred injectable therapy and, increasingly, as early combination therapy in patients with atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease, heart failure, chronic kidney disease, or obesity, irrespective of baseline HbA1c and independently of metformin ([American Diabetes Association Professional Practice Committee, 2025](https://diabetesjournals.org/care)). Several agents are also approved for paediatric type 2 diabetes from age 10 years, including liraglutide and dulaglutide ([U.S. Food and Drug Administration, 2025](https://www.fda.gov/drugs)).

### Chronic Weight Management

Liraglutide 3.0 mg was the first GLP-1 receptor agonist approved for obesity, producing mean weight reduction of approximately 8% versus 2.6% with placebo over 56 weeks in the SCALE programme. Semaglutide 2.4 mg substantially raised the efficacy ceiling: in STEP-1, participants without diabetes achieved mean weight reduction of 14.9% versus 2.4% with placebo at 68 weeks, with roughly 86% of participants losing at least 5% of body weight ([Wilding et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2032183)). Semaglutide is approved for adults with a body mass index of 30 kg/m² or greater, or 27 kg/m² or greater with at least one weight-related comorbidity, and for adolescents aged 12 years and older ([U.S. Food and Drug Administration, 2025](https://www.fda.gov/drugs)).

### Cardiovascular Risk Reduction

Cardiovascular outcome trials have transformed the class from glucose-lowering agents into cardiovascular therapeutics. LEADER demonstrated a 13% relative reduction in three-point major adverse cardiovascular events with liraglutide in patients with type 2 diabetes and high cardiovascular risk ([Marso et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1603827)). REWIND subsequently showed a 12% reduction with dulaglutide, notably in a population with a lower baseline cardiovascular risk burden ([Gerstein et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(19)31149-3)). The SELECT trial extended this benefit to patients with overweight or obesity and established cardiovascular disease but *without* diabetes, reporting a 20% reduction in major adverse cardiovascular events (6.5% versus 8.0%) over a mean follow-up of approximately 3.3 years ([Lincoff et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2307563)). This finding is arguably the single most consequential result in the class's history, because it establishes cardiovascular benefit in a population defined by adiposity rather than dysglycaemia.

### Chronic Kidney Disease

The FLOW trial randomised patients with type 2 diabetes and chronic kidney disease to semaglutide 1.0 mg or placebo and was stopped early for efficacy, showing a 24% reduction in the composite primary kidney outcome (hazard ratio 0.76) ([Perkovic et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2403347)). This represented the first dedicated kidney outcome trial of a GLP-1 receptor agonist and supports a specific renal indication for semaglutide in this population.

### Emerging and Adjacent Indications

Evidence is accumulating in metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis (MASH), obstructive sleep apnoea, and heart failure with preserved ejection fraction, with regulatory decisions in the MASH and sleep-apnoea spaces having occurred during 2024–2025 ([U.S. Food and Drug Administration, 2025](https://www.fda.gov/drugs)). Dual and triple incretin agonists — notably tirzepatide, a glucose-dependent insulinotropic polypeptide/GLP-1 co-agonist producing up to approximately 20.9% weight reduction at 72 weeks in SURMOUNT-1 ([Jastreboff et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2206038)) — indicate that the mechanism is amenable to further amplification, although these agents are pharmacologically distinct from pure GLP-1 receptor agonists.

## Safety, Tolerability, and Contraindications

Gastrointestinal effects — nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, and constipation — are the most common adverse events and are typically transient and dose-dependent, mitigating with slow titration. Class labelling carries a boxed warning for thyroid C-cell tumours based on rodent carcinogenicity data, and the agents are contraindicated in patients with a personal or family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma or multiple endocrine neoplasia syndrome type 2 ([U.S. Food and Drug Administration, 2025](https://www.fda.gov/drugs)). Other considerations include an increased risk of gallbladder disease, hypoglycaemia when combined with insulin or sulfonylureas, acute kidney injury secondary to volume depletion, and early worsening of diabetic retinopathy observed with semaglutide in SUSTAIN-6. Aspiration risk during anaesthesia prompted professional-society guidance on periprocedural withholding, with individualised management now favoured over blanket discontinuation ([American Diabetes Association Professional Practice Committee, 2025](https://diabetesjournals.org/care)). Systematic regulatory reviews of suicidal ideation conducted in 2023–2024 did not establish a causal association ([U.S. Food and Drug Administration, 2025](https://www.fda.gov/drugs)). Weight-loss use is contraindicated in pregnancy given the absence of benefit and the theoretical risk of inadequate maternal caloric intake.

## Interpretation and Opinion

On the balance of the evidence, GLP-1 receptor agonists should no longer be characterised as glucose-lowering drugs with a weight-loss side effect. The totality of outcome data — LEADER, REWIND, SELECT, and FLOW together — supports their classification as disease-modifying cardiometabolic agents whose benefits are at least partly independent of glycaemic change ([Marso et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1603827); [Gerstein et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(19)31149-3); [Lincoff et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2307563); [Perkovic et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2403347)). The most defensible clinical position is that these agents are under-used relative to their evidence base in patients with established cardiovascular or kidney disease.

The principal constraints are not mechanistic but logistical: cost, global supply, long-term persistence (real-world discontinuation rates for obesity indications frequently exceed 50% within a year), and the management of gastrointestinal intolerance. Compounded and unapproved formulations circulating during supply shortages represent a genuine safety concern, and regulatory resolution of shortage designations in late 2024 and early 2025 has narrowed the legitimate scope of such compounding ([U.S. Food and Drug Administration, 2025](https://www.fda.gov/drugs)). The unresolved scientific questions are the durability of weight and cardiovascular benefit after discontinuation, the long-term consequences of lifelong incretin signalling, and whether the class can be translated into oral, low-cost, non-peptide formats that would make population-level use feasible.

## Conclusion

GLP-1 receptor agonists constitute a mechanistically coherent drug class built on a single receptor target but differentiated by pharmacokinetic engineering that spans twice-daily injection to once-daily oral dosing. Their approved indications have expanded from glycaemic control in type 2 diabetes to chronic weight management, cardiovascular risk reduction in both diabetic and non-diabetic populations, and kidney protection. The evidence base is unusually strong by the standards of metabolic pharmacology, and the class has redefined therapeutic expectations for obesity and cardiometabolic risk. The outstanding challenge is translational rather than biological: converting a highly effective but expensive and often poorly tolerated injectable class into an accessible, durable, and equitably distributed standard of care.
