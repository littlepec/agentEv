## Introduction

Glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists (GLP-1 RAs) are a drug class that amplifies an endogenous gut–brain–pancreas signalling axis to lower glucose and reduce body weight. Since the approval of exenatide in 2005, the class has expanded from a niche injectable option for type 2 diabetes into a therapeutic platform with approved indications spanning obesity, atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease, chronic kidney disease, and metabolic dysfunction–associated steatohepatitis (MASH). The class is now one of the most prescribed groups of metabolic agents worldwide, and its mechanism is central to understanding both its breadth of benefit and its characteristic adverse effects ([American Diabetes Association Professional Practice Committee, 2025](https://doi.org/10.2337/dc25-S009)).

## Mechanism of Action

### Endogenous incretin physiology

GLP-1 is a 30-amino-acid peptide produced by enteroendocrine L-cells of the distal small intestine and colon following nutrient ingestion. It acts as an incretin hormone: it potentiates insulin secretion in response to oral glucose to a far greater degree than intravenous glucose of equivalent concentration — the "incretin effect," which accounts for roughly 50–70% of postprandial insulin release in healthy individuals but is substantially blunted in type 2 diabetes. Native GLP-1 has a plasma half-life of approximately 1.5–2 minutes because it is rapidly cleaved by dipeptidyl peptidase-4 (DPP-4) and neutral endopeptidase ([American Diabetes Association Professional Practice Committee, 2025](https://doi.org/10.2337/dc25-S009)).

### Receptor signalling

The GLP-1 receptor is a Class B G-protein-coupled receptor expressed on pancreatic beta cells, alpha cells, enteric neurons, vagal afferents, and multiple brainstem and hypothalamic nuclei. Agonist binding triggers Gαs-mediated activation of adenylyl cyclase, raising intracellular cyclic AMP and activating protein kinase A and Epac2 signalling cascades. Downstream effects include:

- **Beta cells:** glucose-dependent insulin secretion, plus enhanced insulin gene transcription and, in animal models, beta-cell proliferation and reduced apoptosis.
- **Alpha cells:** glucose-dependent suppression of glucagon secretion, removing a driver of hepatic glucose output.
- **Gut and gut–brain axis:** slowing of gastric emptying, which flattens postprandial glucose excursions and prolongs satiety.
- **Central nervous system:** activation of GLP-1 receptors in the arcuate nucleus, nucleus tractus solitarius, and area postrema, producing reduced appetite, decreased food reward signalling, and increased satiety ([Wilding et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2032183)).

The glucose-dependence of both insulin stimulation and glucagon suppression explains why GLP-1 RAs rarely cause hypoglycaemia when used as monotherapy.

### Pharmacokinetic engineering

Because native GLP-1 is unviable as a drug, all marketed agents are structurally modified to resist DPP-4. Two dominant strategies are used: fatty-acid acylation that promotes reversible albumin binding (semaglutide, liraglutide) and Fc-fragment fusion or PEGylation (dulaglutide, exenatide extended-release). Semaglutide — a 94% human-homology analogue with a C18 fatty diacid side chain — achieves a half-life of roughly 165 hours, enabling once-weekly dosing; dulaglutide achieves approximately 90 hours; liraglutide, at roughly 13 hours, requires daily administration. Oral semaglutide co-formulates the peptide with the absorption enhancer sodium N-(8-[2-hydroxybenzoyl] amino) caprylate (SNAC) to permit gastric absorption ([American Diabetes Association Professional Practice Committee, 2025](https://doi.org/10.2337/dc25-S009)).

### Pleiotropic and extra-glycaemic effects

Beyond glycaemia, GLP-1 receptor signalling has been associated with reduced inflammation, improved endothelial function, modest blood-pressure lowering, and altered lipid profiles. These mechanisms are hypothesised to underlie the cardiovascular outcome benefits observed in dedicated trials, though the relative contribution of weight loss versus direct receptor-mediated effects remains debated ([Lincoff et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2307563)).

## Approved Indications

### Type 2 diabetes mellitus

All agents in the class are approved as adjuncts to diet and exercise to improve glycaemic control in adults with type 2 diabetes. Agents with proven cardiovascular benefit are preferentially recommended for patients with established atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease, heart failure, or chronic kidney disease, independent of baseline HbA1c or metformin use ([American Diabetes Association Professional Practice Committee, 2025](https://doi.org/10.2337/dc25-S009)). In the SUSTAIN programme, once-weekly semaglutide 1.0 mg reduced HbA1c by approximately 1.5–1.8 percentage points and body weight by roughly 4.5–6.5 kg ([Marso et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1607141)). Liraglutide is additionally approved for patients aged 10 years and older with type 2 diabetes.

### Chronic weight management

Semaglutide 2.4 mg weekly (Wegovy) is approved for adults with a BMI ≥30 kg/m² or ≥27 kg/m² with at least one weight-related comorbidity, and for adolescents aged 12 years and older meeting comparable criteria. In STEP 1, participants receiving semaglutide 2.4 mg lost a mean of 14.9% of body weight over 68 weeks versus 2.4% with placebo ([Wilding et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2032183)). Liraglutide 3.0 mg daily (Saxenda) is approved on the same BMI criteria, with mean weight loss of roughly 6–8%.

### Cardiovascular risk reduction

Three agents carry approved cardiovascular indications. Liraglutide reduced major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE) by 13% (HR 0.87) in LEADER ([Marso et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1603827)); dulaglutide reduced MACE by 12% (HR 0.88) in REWIND ([Gerstein et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(19)31149-3)); semaglutide 1.0 mg reduced MACE by 26% (HR 0.74) in SUSTAIN-6 ([Marso et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1607141)). Most consequentially, SELECT — a trial of 17,604 adults with overweight or obesity and established cardiovascular disease but *without* diabetes — demonstrated a 20% reduction in MACE (HR 0.80; 95% CI 0.72–0.90) with semaglutide 2.4 mg, establishing cardiovascular benefit independent of glycaemic lowering ([Lincoff et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2307563)). The SOUL trial subsequently showed a 14% MACE reduction with oral semaglutide 14 mg in high-risk patients with type 2 diabetes, extending the benefit to the oral formulation.

### Chronic kidney disease in type 2 diabetes

In the FLOW trial of 3,533 patients with type 2 diabetes and chronic kidney disease, semaglutide 1.0 mg reduced the composite of kidney disease progression, kidney failure, and cardiovascular or renal death by 24% (HR 0.76; 95% CI 0.66–0.88) ([Perkovic et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2403347)). This supported a regulatory approval for Ozempic to reduce the risk of sustained eGFR decline, end-stage kidney disease, and cardiovascular death in this population.

### Metabolic dysfunction–associated steatohepatitis

In 2025, semaglutide 2.4 mg received accelerated approval for non-cirrhotic MASH with moderate-to-advanced fibrosis (F2–F3), based on improvement in steatohepatitis and fibrosis endpoints. This is the first GLP-1 RA approved for a liver indication and represents a meaningful expansion of the class beyond metabolic and cardiovascular endpoints.

## Safety and Tolerability

### Common adverse effects

Gastrointestinal effects dominate the safety profile: nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, constipation, and abdominal pain occur in a substantial minority of patients, are dose-related, and typically attenuate with continued use and gradual titration. Injection-site reactions are common with injectable formulations.

### Serious and notable risks

- **Pancreatitis:** Rare but reported; post-marketing data suggest a modestly increased risk. The class is generally avoided in patients with a history of pancreatitis.
- **Gallbladder disease:** Meta-analyses report roughly a two-fold increased risk of cholelithiasis and cholecystitis, plausibly mediated by rapid weight loss and altered gallbladder motility.
- **Diabetic retinopathy:** In SUSTAIN-6, semaglutide was associated with a hazard ratio of 1.76 for retinopathy complications, likely related to rapid glycaemic improvement rather than a direct drug effect ([Marso et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1607141)).
- **Non-arteritic anterior ischaemic optic neuropathy (NAION):** Very rare cases prompted a European regulatory review in 2025, after which NAION was added as a very rare adverse reaction to semaglutide-containing product information.
- **Gastroparesis and perioperative aspiration:** Delayed gastric emptying has implications for anaesthesia and endoscopy; professional societies recommend holding GLP-1 RAs or confirming residual gastric volume before elective procedures.
- **Lean mass loss:** Approximately 25–40% of total weight lost may be lean tissue, motivating interest in concurrent resistance training and protein optimisation.
- **Neuropsychiatric events:** Regulatory reviews by the FDA (2024) and EMA (2024) found no evidence of a causal association between GLP-1 RAs and suicidal ideation or self-harm.
- **Hypoglycaemia:** Uncommon as monotherapy; risk rises substantially when combined with insulin or sulfonylureas, which may require dose reduction.

### Contraindications and boxed warning

The class carries a boxed warning for thyroid C-cell tumours based on rodent findings and is contraindicated in patients with a personal or family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma or multiple endocrine neoplasia syndrome type 2. It is also contraindicated in pregnancy and in individuals with prior serious hypersensitivity to the agent. Women of childbearing potential using semaglutide for weight management are advised to discontinue at least two months before a planned pregnancy.

## Clinical and Health-System Considerations

Three practical issues shape current use. First, **supply and compounding**: widespread shortages of semaglutide and tirzepatide from 2022 to 2025 fuelled a large compounded-peptide market, which regulators have moved to restrict as branded supply has normalised. Second, **cost and equity**: list prices in the thousands of US dollars per month limit access, and utilisation data show lower prescribing rates in historically underserved populations despite equal or higher obesity prevalence. Third, **persistence**: discontinuation rates are high in real-world cohorts, and weight regain is common after cessation, which has prompted debate about whether these agents should be conceptualised as long-term or indefinite therapy rather than time-limited courses.

## Conclusion

GLP-1 receptor agonists work by pharmacologically amplifying an endogenous incretin signal, producing glucose-dependent insulin secretion, glucagon suppression, slowed gastric emptying, and centrally mediated appetite reduction. Skilful pharmacokinetic modification has converted a two-minute peptide into once-weekly medicines. The class has moved well beyond glycaemic control: randomised evidence now supports approved indications in obesity, secondary cardiovascular prevention, chronic kidney disease, and MASH. The most defensible clinical view is that these agents are best understood not as glucose-lowering drugs with incidental weight effects, but as multi-organ metabolic therapeutics whose benefits are broad yet which demand careful patient selection, titration, monitoring for gastrointestinal and biliary events, and realistic expectations about long-term adherence.
