# The James Webb Space Telescope: Design, Instruments, and Purpose

## Introduction

The James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) is one of the most significant astronomical observatories described in the provided source set. However, the source set contains a serious conflict. One document presents JWST as a 2.4-meter ultraviolet-and-optical telescope launched in 2019 into low Earth orbit, with a primary instrument called the Wide Ultraviolet Spectrograph (WUVS) ([The James Webb Space Telescope at a Glance, n.d.](document_1.txt)). In contrast, NASA and Wikipedia describe JWST as a large infrared observatory launched on December 25, 2021, operating 1.5 million kilometers from Earth at the second Lagrange point, or L2, with a 6.5-meter segmented, gold-coated mirror and four science instruments ([NASA, n.d.](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/); [Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). The reliable sources therefore support a very different account from the one in the first document.

This report describes JWST’s design, main instruments, and purpose using the most reliable available information. It prioritizes NASA’s official mission page and the Wikipedia entry because they are dated, linked, and mutually corroborating. The anonymous “At a Glance” document is treated as unreliable because it contains multiple factual claims that directly contradict the official and encyclopedic sources. The central conclusion is that JWST is a 6.5-meter infrared space observatory at L2, launched in 2021, with four named science instruments, and that the ultraviolet/low-Earth-orbit description is not credible.

## Source Reliability and Conflicting Accounts

The most important step in describing JWST accurately is to determine which provided sources should be trusted. NASA’s page states that Webb launched on December 25, 2021, orbits the Sun 1.5 million kilometers away from Earth at L2, and uses a segmented, gold-coated mirror to observe in infrared ([NASA, n.d.](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/)). This is an official, public-domain mission source and therefore carries high authority for basic mission facts. Wikipedia adds specific technical details: the primary mirror is a 6.5-meter gold-coated beryllium reflector with a collecting area of 25.4 square meters; the launch occurred on December 25, 2021, on an Ariane 5 rocket from Kourou, French Guiana; the telescope observes from long-wavelength visible red light through mid-infrared, approximately 0.6 to 28.5 micrometers; and its four science instruments are NIRCam, NIRSpec, MIRI, and FGS/NIRISS ([Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)).

The “At a Glance” document makes a series of incompatible claims. It states that JWST is a 2.4-meter ultraviolet-and-optical observatory launched in 2019 into low Earth orbit, where it is periodically serviced; that its primary science instrument is the Wide Ultraviolet Spectrograph, or WUVS; and that it reuses the Hubble Space Telescope’s mirror design and orbital approach ([The James Webb Space Telescope at a Glance, n.d.](document_1.txt)). These claims are directly contradicted by NASA and Wikipedia. The launch year is off by two years, the mirror size is off by more than four meters, the orbit is described as low Earth orbit instead of L2, the wavelength range is described as ultraviolet and optical instead of red visible through mid-infrared, and the instrument list omits the four instruments named by Wikipedia ([NASA, n.d.](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/); [Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope); [The James Webb Space Telescope at a Glance, n.d.](document_1.txt)).

Because the “At a Glance” document has no author, no URL, no retrieval date, and no corroborating evidence, it cannot be treated as a reliable source for JWST. The NASA and Wikipedia accounts are not identical in scope, but they agree on every major fact that the unreliable document contradicts. The most defensible position is therefore to reject the ultraviolet/low-Earth-orbit description and to base the remainder of this report on the NASA and Wikipedia data.

## Design and Configuration

### Primary Mirror and Optical Assembly

The defining physical feature of JWST is its large primary mirror. According to Wikipedia, “Webb’s primary mirror is a 6.5 m (21 ft)-diameter gold-coated beryllium reflector with a collecting area of 25.4 m²” ([Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). NASA describes the mirror as “segmented, gold-coated” and states that it is used to observe in infrared ([NASA, n.d.](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/)). These details are mutually consistent: the mirror is large, segmented, gold-coated, and made of beryllium, with a total collecting area of 25.4 square meters ([NASA, n.d.](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/); [Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)).

This design is fundamentally different from the one described in the unreliable document. A 2.4-meter ultraviolet-and-optical mirror would be similar in size to Hubble’s primary mirror, but JWST’s actual mirror is 6.5 meters in diameter, with a collecting area of 25.4 square meters ([Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). The gold coating and beryllium construction are also specific design choices that belong to the JWST described by NASA and Wikipedia, not to the “At a Glance” description ([NASA, n.d.](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/); [Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). The reliable sources therefore establish that JWST’s optical design is optimized around a large, segmented, gold-coated infrared mirror.

### Orbit and Launch

JWST was launched on December 25, 2021, according to NASA ([NASA, n.d.](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/)). Wikipedia specifies that the launch used an Ariane 5 rocket from Kourou, French Guiana ([Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). This is a concrete, dated, and location-specific fact that directly refutes the claim that JWST launched in 2019 ([The James Webb Space Telescope at a Glance, n.d.](document_1.txt)).

After launch, JWST orbits the Sun at a distance of about 1.5 million kilometers from Earth, at the second Lagrange point, or L2 ([NASA, n.d.](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/)). This is not low Earth orbit. The L2 location places the telescope far beyond Earth’s immediate vicinity, which is consistent with the reliable sources’ description of a deep-space observatory rather than a periodically serviced low-orbit platform ([NASA, n.d.](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/); [The James Webb Space Telescope at a Glance, n.d.](document_1.txt)). The reliable sources do not describe JWST as being periodically serviced; the “At a Glance” document’s servicing claim is therefore unsupported and contradicted by the L2 orbital description ([NASA, n.d.](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/); [The James Webb Space Telescope at a Glance, n.d.](document_1.txt)).

### Wavelength Coverage

The wavelength range is one of the clearest points of disagreement. NASA states that JWST uses its gold-coated mirror “to observe in infrared” ([NASA, n.d.](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/)). Wikipedia expands this by stating that JWST “observes a lower frequency range, from long-wavelength visible light (red) through mid-infrared (0.6-28.5 um)” ([Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). This places the telescope’s primary sensitivity in the red visible to mid-infrared part of the spectrum, not in the ultraviolet and optical range claimed by the unreliable document ([The James Webb Space Telescope at a Glance, n.d.](document_1.txt)).

This wavelength coverage is a fundamental design characteristic. A telescope built to observe from 0.6 to 28.5 micrometers must have different optics, detectors, and thermal requirements than a telescope built for ultraviolet and visible light. The provided reliable sources do not give full engineering detail, but they do establish that JWST is an infrared-focused observatory with a mirror and orbit suited to that purpose ([NASA, n.d.](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/); [Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). The “At a Glance” description of a 2.4-meter ultraviolet-and-optical observatory is therefore not merely a minor error; it misidentifies the telescope’s entire observational regime ([The James Webb Space Telescope at a Glance, n.d.](document_1.txt)).

## Main Instruments

Wikipedia names four science instruments for JWST: NIRCam, NIRSpec, MIRI, and FGS/NIRISS ([Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). This is a precise and verifiable list. The “At a Glance” document instead says the primary science instrument is the Wide Ultraviolet Spectrograph, or WUVS ([The James Webb Space Telescope at a Glance, n.d.](document_1.txt)). WUVS does not appear in the Wikipedia list of JWST’s four instruments, and it is not mentioned by NASA’s provided mission summary ([NASA, n.d.](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/); [Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). The reliable source therefore identifies four instruments, while the unreliable source identifies only one, and that one is not corroborated elsewhere.

The provided sources do not give detailed descriptions of what each of the four instruments does. They name the instruments but do not explain their individual capabilities, wavelength sub-ranges, or observing modes ([Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). For that reason, a responsible report should not invent instrument-by-instrument functions that are absent from the provided information. What can be stated with confidence is that JWST’s science payload consists of NIRCam, NIRSpec, MIRI, and FGS/NIRISS, and that these four instruments are associated with a telescope designed to observe from red visible light through mid-infrared wavelengths ([NASA, n.d.](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/); [Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)).

The names themselves provide limited additional context. NIRCam and NIRSpec contain “NIR,” which conventionally points toward near-infrared, while MIRI contains “MIR,” which conventionally points toward mid-infrared. FGS/NIRISS combines a fine guidance sensor function with a near-infrared instrument designation. However, these expansions are not explicitly supplied in the provided source set, so they should be treated as cautious inferences from the acronyms rather than as fully sourced descriptions ([Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). The essential fact remains that the four-instrument list is the reliable one, and WUVS is not part of it.

## Purpose

The purpose of JWST, as described in the reliable sources, is to observe the universe in infrared light. NASA states that the telescope uses a segmented, gold-coated mirror “to observe in infrared” ([NASA, n.d.](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/)). Wikipedia states that JWST observes a lower frequency range extending from long-wavelength visible red light through mid-infrared, covering 0.6 to 28.5 micrometers ([Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). Taken together, these statements define JWST’s purpose as infrared astronomical observation across a broad wavelength range that includes the red end of the visible spectrum and a substantial portion of the mid-infrared.

The design features support this purpose. A 6.5-meter gold-coated beryllium mirror with a 25.4-square-meter collecting area provides a large light-gathering surface ([Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). The segmented and gold-coated construction is explicitly tied in the NASA summary to infrared observation ([NASA, n.d.](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/)). The L2 orbit, 1.5 million kilometers from Earth, places the observatory in deep space rather than in low Earth orbit ([NASA, n.d.](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/)). These facts together indicate a purpose-built infrared observatory, not an ultraviolet/optical telescope of the kind described in the unreliable document ([The James Webb Space Telescope at a Glance, n.d.](document_1.txt)).

The provided sources do not list specific science goals such as exoplanet atmospheric characterization, early-galaxy observations, or star formation studies. They also do not provide a formal mission-objectives statement beyond the wavelength and instrument information. Therefore, the most accurate statement supported by the given information is that JWST’s purpose is to perform astronomical observations in the infrared, from red visible light through mid-infrared, using its four science instruments ([NASA, n.d.](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/); [Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). Any broader purpose claims would require additional sources not present in the provided set.

## Integrated Assessment

The evidence strongly supports a single coherent description of JWST. It is a 6.5-meter infrared observatory with a gold-coated beryllium primary mirror, a collecting area of 25.4 square meters, and a segmented mirror design ([NASA, n.d.](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/); [Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). It launched on December 25, 2021, on an Ariane 5 rocket from Kourou, French Guiana ([NASA, n.d.](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/); [Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). It orbits the Sun at L2, approximately 1.5 million kilometers from Earth ([NASA, n.d.](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/)). It observes from roughly 0.6 to 28.5 micrometers, spanning long-wavelength visible red light through mid-infrared ([Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). Its four science instruments are NIRCam, NIRSpec, MIRI, and FGS/NIRISS ([Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)).

The alternative description in the “At a Glance” document is not supported by any reliable source in the provided set. It gives the wrong launch year, wrong mirror size, wrong wavelength range, wrong orbit, and wrong instrument name ([The James Webb Space Telescope at a Glance, n.d.](document_1.txt)). It also claims that JWST reuses Hubble’s mirror design and orbital approach, which conflicts with the 6.5-meter L2 observatory described by NASA and Wikipedia ([NASA, n.d.](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/); [Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope); [The James Webb Space Telescope at a Glance, n.d.](document_1.txt)). The only reasonable conclusion is that the “At a Glance” document is erroneous and should not be used for factual claims about JWST.

A further limitation is that the reliable sources provided here do not give a full instrument-by-instrument breakdown or an explicit list of science goals. They establish the telescope’s size, materials, orbit, launch, wavelength range, and instrument names, but they do not explain how each instrument contributes to specific scientific investigations ([NASA, n.d.](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/); [Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). This means the most defensible report must remain focused on the facts that are actually provided. It should not invent detailed purpose statements or instrument roles that are absent from the source set.

## Conclusion

Based on the reliable provided sources, the James Webb Space Telescope is a large infrared space observatory, not a 2.4-meter ultraviolet/low-Earth-orbit telescope. Its primary mirror is a 6.5-meter gold-coated beryllium reflector with a collecting area of 25.4 square meters, and NASA describes the mirror as segmented and gold-coated ([NASA, n.d.](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/); [Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). It launched on December 25, 2021, aboard an Ariane 5 from Kourou, French Guiana, and it orbits the Sun 1.5 million kilometers from Earth at L2 ([NASA, n.d.](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/); [Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). It observes from long-wavelength visible red light through mid-infrared, approximately 0.6 to 28.5 micrometers, and it carries four science instruments: NIRCam, NIRSpec, MIRI, and FGS/NIRISS ([Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). Its purpose, as documented in the reliable sources, is infrared astronomical observation across that wavelength range ([NASA, n.d.](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/); [Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). The “At a Glance” document’s opposing claims are contradicted on every major point and should be excluded from any accurate account of the mission ([The James Webb Space Telescope at a Glance, n.d.](document_1.txt)).
