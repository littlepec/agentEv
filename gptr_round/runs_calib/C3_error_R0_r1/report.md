## Launch and Orbital Design

JWST launched on December 25, 2021, aboard an Ariane 5 rocket from Kourou, French Guiana ([Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). NASA confirms the launch date of December 25, 2021 ([NASA, 2026](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/)). The observatory orbits the Sun approximately 1.5 million kilometers away from Earth at the second Lagrange point, or L2 ([NASA, 2026](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/)). This orbital location is a defining element of its design. It is not in low Earth orbit, and the provided reliable sources do not state that it is periodically serviced. The L2 orbit places it far from Earth, which is consistent with an infrared observatory needing a stable, cold environment, although the provided sources do not explicitly state this rationale.

The launch and orbit data are specific and consistent across the trusted sources. NASA states that Webb “orbits the Sun 1.5 million kilometers … away from the Earth at … the second Lagrange point or L2” ([NASA, 2026](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/)). Wikipedia states that “The Webb was launched on 25 December 2021 on an Ariane 5 rocket from Kourou, French Guiana” ([Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). These facts establish the basic mission profile: a large space observatory placed far from Earth, not in a low Earth orbit.

## Primary Mirror and Optical Design

JWST’s primary mirror is a 6.5 m (21 ft)-diameter gold-coated beryllium reflector with a collecting area of 25.4 m² ([Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). NASA describes the mirror as “segmented” and “gold-coated” ([NASA, 2026](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/)). Thus, the design combines a large overall aperture with a segmented architecture. The use of beryllium and gold coating is specified by Wikipedia. The segmented nature is specified by NASA. The exact number of segments is not provided in the supplied materials, so it cannot be stated from these sources. The collecting area of 25.4 m² is smaller than the area of a completely filled 6.5 m circle, which is approximately 33.2 m². This indicates that the segmented design does not form a fully filled circular aperture; there are gaps or losses inherent in the architecture. This geometric observation follows from the cited diameter and collecting area, but the sources do not explain the exact segment layout ([NASA, 2026](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/); [Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)).

The mirror is designed for infrared observation. JWST observes a lower frequency range, from long-wavelength visible light (red) through mid-infrared (0.6–28.5 µm) ([Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). NASA states that it uses a segmented, gold-coated mirror to observe in infrared ([NASA, 2026](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/)). The wavelength range therefore includes the red end of the visible spectrum and extends deep into the mid-infrared. This is a critical design feature: the observatory is not primarily an ultraviolet or optical telescope. Its sensitivity is directed toward longer wavelengths than human vision and longer than the ultraviolet band.

## Science Instruments

JWST has four science instruments: NIRCam, NIRSpec, MIRI, and FGS/NIRISS ([Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). These are the main instruments listed by the reliable secondary source. The provided sources do not give detailed descriptions of each instrument’s individual capabilities, so this report does not speculate beyond their names and status as science instruments. What is clear is that the instrument suite is plural and includes multiple specialized components rather than a single primary spectrograph. This contrasts with document_2, which claims that JWST’s primary science instrument is the Wide Ultraviolet Spectrograph (WUVS). That claim is unsupported by NASA and Wikipedia and is inconsistent with JWST’s infrared design ([Document 2, n.d.](document_2.txt); [Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)).

## Purpose and Scientific Orientation

The stated purpose of JWST in the provided reliable sources is to observe in infrared. NASA says it uses a segmented, gold-coated mirror to observe in infrared ([NASA, 2026](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/)). Wikipedia specifies the wavelength range as long-wavelength visible light (red) through mid-infrared (0.6–28.5 µm) ([Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). This means its scientific purpose is tied to collecting and analyzing infrared radiation from astronomical sources. The provided sources present the large segmented mirror, gold coating, L2 orbit, and infrared wavelength range as design features associated with this infrared observation. The sources do not enumerate specific science goals such as exoplanet characterization, early galaxy formation, or star formation. Therefore, the purpose can be stated broadly: JWST is an infrared space observatory designed to observe the universe at wavelengths from the red end of the visible spectrum through the mid-infrared ([NASA, 2026](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/); [Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)).

## Synthesis and Concrete Assessment

Based on the reliable provided sources, JWST is a large infrared space observatory. It launched on December 25, 2021, on an Ariane 5 from Kourou, French Guiana ([Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). It orbits the Sun about 1.5 million kilometers from Earth at L2 ([NASA, 2026](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/)). Its primary mirror is a 6.5 m gold-coated beryllium reflector with a collecting area of 25.4 m² and a segmented, gold-coated architecture ([NASA, 2026](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/); [Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). It observes from 0.6 to 28.5 µm, covering red visible light through mid-infrared ([Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). Its four science instruments are NIRCam, NIRSpec, MIRI, and FGS/NIRISS ([Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). Its purpose, as described in the sources, is infrared observation ([NASA, 2026](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/)).

The most significant design feature is the combination of a large segmented mirror with an infrared-optimized wavelength range and a distant L2 orbit. This combination distinguishes JWST from a low-Earth-orbit ultraviolet-optical telescope. The most significant source issue is the presence of Document 2, whose claims are not corroborated and are directly contradicted. A careful report must therefore reject Document 2’s factual assertions and rely on NASA and Wikipedia ([NASA, 2026](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/); [Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope); [Document 2, n.d.](document_2.txt)).

The provided sources leave some gaps: they do not specify the number of mirror segments, the detailed function of each instrument, the exact science objectives, or the operational status after launch. These gaps should not be filled with speculation. Within the available evidence, the description of JWST is clear and consistent across the trusted sources.
