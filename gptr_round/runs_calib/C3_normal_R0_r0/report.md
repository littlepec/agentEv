## Introduction

Wikipedia corroborates the launch date and adds that "The Webb was launched on 25 December 2021 on an Ariane 5 rocket from Kourou, French Guiana" ([Wikipedia contributors, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). ## Design and Optical Architecture
### Primary Mirror
The primary mirror is a central element of Webb's design. Wikipedia states that "Webb's primary mirror is a 6.5 m (21 ft)-diameter gold-coated beryllium reflector with a collecting area of 25.4 m2" ([Wikipedia contributors, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). This specification indicates that the mirror is 6.5 meters in diameter, equivalent to 21 feet, and has a collecting area of 25.4 square meters. The mirror is described as a reflector, meaning it collects and reflects light. It is made of beryllium and coated with gold. NASA independently describes Webb as using a "segmented, gold-coated mirror" to observe in infrared ([NASA, n.d.](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/)). Taken together, the sources indicate that the primary mirror is both segmented and gold-coated, and that it is a beryllium reflector with a 6.5-meter diameter and a 25.4-square-meter collecting area.

### Segmented and Gold-Coated Construction
NASA's description emphasizes that the mirror is "segmented" and "gold-coated" ([NASA, n.d.](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/)). The gold coating is also highlighted by both NASA and Wikipedia ([NASA, n.d.](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/); [Wikipedia contributors, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). Wikipedia specifies that the gold coating is applied to a beryllium reflector ([Wikipedia contributors, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)).
