# Comprehensive Report on the James Webb Space Telescope: Design, Instruments, and Purpose

## Introduction
The James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) is a space-based astronomical observatory designed to observe the universe in infrared light. According to NASA, "Webb launched on Dec. 25th 2021" and "orbits the Sun 1.5 million kilometers ... away from the Earth at ... the second Lagrange point or L2," where it uses a "segmented, gold-coated mirror" to observe in infrared ([NASA, 2026](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/)). Wikipedia adds that "The Webb was launched on 25 December 2021 on an Ariane 5 rocket from Kourou, French Guiana" and that the observatory's primary mirror is a "6.5 m (21 ft)-diameter gold-coated beryllium reflector with a collecting area of 25.4 m2" ([Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). The same source states that "Webb observes a lower frequency range, from long-wavelength visible light (red) through mid-infrared (0.6-28.5 um)" and that its "four science instruments are NIRCam, NIRSpec, MIRI, and FGS/NIRISS" ([Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). This report describes the design, main instruments, and purpose of JWST using these two sources.

## Mission Profile and Launch
JWST's launch profile is a key part of its design and operational identity. NASA records the launch date as "Dec. 25th 2021" ([NASA, 2026](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/)). Wikipedia records the same event as "25 December 2021" and specifies the launch vehicle and site: "The Webb was launched on 25 December 2021 on an Ariane 5 rocket from Kourou, French Guiana" ([Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). These two statements agree on the date and together provide the vehicle and location. The launch placed the observatory on a trajectory toward its operational orbit, which NASA describes as a solar orbit at the second Lagrange point, or L2, approximately 1.5 million kilometers from Earth ([NASA, 2026](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/)).

## Orbital Design: The Second Lagrange Point
JWST does not orbit Earth in the conventional low-Earth-orbit sense described by many space telescopes. NASA states that it "orbits the Sun 1.5 million kilometers ... away from the Earth at ... the second Lagrange point or L2" ([NASA, 2026](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/)). This orbital placement is a primary design feature. The distance of 1.5 million kilometers from Earth is explicitly reported by NASA ([NASA, 2026](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/)). The location is identified as the second Lagrange point, or L2 ([NASA, 2026](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/)). The observatory therefore orbits the Sun at L2 rather than orbiting Earth directly ([NASA, 2026](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/)). This design choice is part of the overall architecture that supports the telescope's infrared observations ([NASA, 2026](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/)).

The orbital information also clarifies the scale of the mission. A distance of 1.5 million kilometers places the observatory far from Earth, and the source describes this as its solar orbital position at L2 ([NASA, 2026](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/)). The same source states that the telescope uses its segmented, gold-coated mirror to observe in infrared from this location ([NASA, 2026](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/)). The orbit and the mirror are thus presented together as elements of the observatory's design ([NASA, 2026](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/)). No other orbital location is mentioned in the supplied NASA source ([NASA, 2026](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/)). Wikipedia's supplied excerpt focuses on launch and mirror details but does not contradict the L2 orbit described by NASA ([Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope); [NASA, 2026](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/)).

## Optical Design and Primary Mirror

Wikipedia states that "Webb's primary mirror is a 6.5 m (21 ft)-diameter gold-coated beryllium reflector with a collecting area of 25.4 m2" ([Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). NASA describes the mirror more generally as "segmented, gold-coated" ([NASA, 2026](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/)). Taken together, the sources indicate that the primary mirror is a 6.5-meter-diameter, segmented, gold-coated beryllium reflector with a collecting area of 25.4 square meters ([Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope); [NASA, 2026](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/)). The diameter is given as 6.5 meters, or 21 feet ([Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). The collecting area is given as 25.4 m2 ([Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). The material is beryllium, and the reflective surface is gold-coated ([Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). The mirror is also segmented, according to NASA ([NASA, 2026](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/)).

## Wavelength Coverage and Observational Purpose
The purpose of JWST, as presented in the supplied sources, is to observe the universe in infrared light. NASA states that the telescope uses its mirror "to observe in infrared" ([NASA, 2026](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/)). Wikipedia provides a more precise statement: "Webb observes a lower frequency range, from long-wavelength visible light (red) through mid-infrared (0.6-28.5 um)" ([Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). This means the observatory is designed to detect radiation across a wavelength interval of 0.6 to 28.5 micrometers, beginning in the red end of the visible spectrum and extending into the mid-infrared ([Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). The source describes this as a lower frequency range than higher-frequency visible light ([Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). The stated observational purpose is therefore infrared astronomy, with some coverage of long-wavelength visible red light at the shortest wavelengths ([Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope); [NASA, 2026](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/)).

## Science Instrument Suite
JWST's main instruments are identified by Wikipedia as NIRCam, NIRSpec, MIRI, and FGS/NIRISS. The source states: "Its four science instruments are NIRCam, NIRSpec, MIRI, and FGS/NIRISS" ([Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). These four instruments constitute the science payload described in the supplied information ([Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). In standard JWST nomenclature, NIRCam is the Near-Infrared Camera, NIRSpec is the Near-Infrared Spectrograph, MIRI is the Mid-Infrared Instrument, and FGS/NIRISS is the Fine Guidance Sensor/Near-Infrared Imager and Slitless Spectrograph ([Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). The instrument suite therefore includes near-infrared and mid-infrared capabilities, which aligns with the telescope's stated wavelength range of 0.6-28.5 um ([Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)).

## Integrated Design and Purpose
The design of JWST can be understood as an integrated system. The telescope orbits the Sun at L2, approximately 1.5 million kilometers from Earth ([NASA, 2026](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/)). It uses a segmented, gold-coated mirror to observe in infrared ([NASA, 2026](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/)). The primary mirror is 6.5 meters in diameter and has a collecting area of 25.4 m2 ([Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). The observatory covers wavelengths from 0.6 um to 28.5 um, from red visible light through mid-infrared ([Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). Its four science instruments are NIRCam, NIRSpec, MIRI, and FGS/NIRISS ([Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). These elements together define an infrared space observatory rather than a general visible-light telescope ([NASA, 2026](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/); [Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)).

The purpose of JWST, as reported, is to perform infrared observations from space. NASA states that it observes in infrared ([NASA, 2026](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/)). Wikipedia specifies the wavelength coverage and identifies the four science instruments ([Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). The launch on 25 December 2021 on an Ariane 5 from Kourou, French Guiana, placed the observatory into its mission ([Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). The launch date is also recorded by NASA as Dec. 25th 2021 ([NASA, 2026](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/)). The overall mission design therefore links a large segmented gold-coated mirror, a solar orbit at L2, and a four-instrument science suite to the goal of observing the universe in infrared light ([NASA, 2026](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/); [Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)).

## Conclusion
The James Webb Space Telescope is a large infrared space observatory. It was launched on 25 December 2021 on an Ariane 5 rocket from Kourou, French Guiana ([Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). NASA records the launch date as Dec. 25th 2021 ([NASA, 2026](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/)). It orbits the Sun 1.5 million kilometers from Earth at the second Lagrange point, or L2 ([NASA, 2026](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/)). Its primary mirror is a 6.5-meter-diameter gold-coated beryllium reflector with a collecting area of 25.4 m2 ([Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). NASA describes the mirror as segmented and gold-coated ([NASA, 2026](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/)). The telescope observes a lower frequency range from long-wavelength visible red light through mid-infrared, covering 0.6-28.5 um ([Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). Its four science instruments are NIRCam, NIRSpec, MIRI, and FGS/NIRISS ([Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). Based on these reported specifications, JWST is designed and purposed for infrared astronomy, with a large mirror, a distant solar orbit, and a dedicated instrument suite ([NASA, 2026](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/); [Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)).
