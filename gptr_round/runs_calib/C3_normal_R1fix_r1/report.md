# The James Webb Space Telescope: Design, Instruments, and Purpose

## Introduction

The James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) is a space-based astronomical observatory designed to observe the universe in infrared light. According to NASA, Webb launched on December 25, 2021, and orbits the Sun 1.5 million kilometers away from Earth at the second Lagrange point, or L2, using a segmented, gold-coated mirror to observe in infrared ([NASA, 2026](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/)). Wikipedia corroborates the launch date, reporting that Webb was launched on 25 December 2021 on an Ariane 5 rocket from Kourou, French Guiana ([Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). This report describes the telescope’s design, its four main science instruments, and its purpose. It draws primarily on NASA’s mission page, a public-domain source, and secondarily on the Wikipedia article, a CC BY-SA 4.0 source, prioritizing the NASA source for mission-level facts and using Wikipedia for technical specifications ([NASA, 2026](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/); [Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). The central finding is that JWST is engineered around infrared observation: its orbit, mirror, wavelength coverage, and instrument suite all support the collection and analysis of infrared light from astronomical sources ([NASA, 2026](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/); [Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)).

The primary mirror is one of the most important elements of JWST’s design. Wikipedia reports that Webb’s primary mirror is a 6.5 m (21 ft)-diameter gold-coated beryllium reflector with a collecting area of 25.4 m² ([Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). NASA describes the mirror as segmented and gold-coated ([NASA, 2026](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/)). Combining these sources, the mirror is a large, segmented, gold-coated beryllium reflector with a 6.5 m diameter and a 25.4 m² collecting area ([NASA, 2026](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/); [Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). The diameter of 6.5 m is equivalent to 21 ft, as Wikipedia notes ([Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). The collecting area of 25.4 m² is a quantitative measure of the mirror’s light-gathering capability ([Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). The segmented design, reported by NASA, means the mirror is not a single continuous piece ([NASA, 2026](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/)). The gold coating, reported by both sources, is a design feature of the reflecting surface ([NASA, 2026](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/); [Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). The use of beryllium as the mirror material is specified by Wikipedia ([Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). ## Science Instruments

### The Four Science Instruments

Wikipedia identifies four science instruments on JWST: NIRCam, NIRSpec, MIRI, and FGS/NIRISS ([Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). These instruments are the observatory’s main scientific payload ([Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). The source lists them by acronym, and their presence indicates that the telescope is equipped for multiple types of infrared observation ([Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). In standard mission nomenclature, NIRCam is the Near-Infrared Camera, NIRSpec is the Near-Infrared Spectrograph, MIRI is the Mid-Infrared Instrument, and FGS/NIRISS is the Fine Guidance Sensor/Near-Infrared Imager and Slitless Spectrograph ([Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). These expanded names clarify the intended functions of the instruments and align with the telescope’s near- and mid-infrared wavelength coverage ([Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)). The combination of near-infrared and mid-infrared instruments matches the 0.6–28.5 µm range reported for JWST ([Wikipedia, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope)).
