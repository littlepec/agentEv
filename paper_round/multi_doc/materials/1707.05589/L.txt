Third-party research note: On the State of the Art of Evaluation in Neural Language Models

Key questions addressed: Penn Treebank word-level test perplexity LSTM language model | state of the art evaluation neural language models Penn Treebank | single model perplexity Penn Treebank without dynamic evaluation or cache | comparison of LSTM and RHN on Penn Treebank perplexity | Penn Treebank perplexity baselines neural language models

The paper reports a best word-level test perplexity of 58.3 on Penn Treebank for its own proposed model, a 4-layer LSTM with 24M parameters. The authors state that at 24M, all depths obtain very similar results, reaching 58.3 at depth 4. This result is reported for a single model, without dynamic evaluation, cache/pointer, ensemble, or fine-tuning. The paper explicitly refrains from including techniques that are known to push perplexities even lower, because its aim is strictly to do better model comparisons for the architectures under study.

The paper lists baseline test perplexities on Penn Treebank: Medium LSTM 82.7, Large LSTM 78.4, VD LSTM (Press & Wolf) 73.2, VD LSTM (Inan et al., 9M) 73.9, VD LSTM (Inan et al., 28M) 69.0, VD RHN 65.4, NAS (25M) 64.0, NAS (54M) 62.4, and AWD-LSTM 57.3. The paper's own best model at 58.3 outperforms all listed baselines except AWD-LSTM at 57.3, which is a parallel work and slightly better.

The paper does not report Penn Treebank results for its own model with dynamic evaluation, cache/pointer, ensemble, or fine-tuning. All reported results are well below the previous state of the art for models without dynamic evaluation or caching. The comparison therefore places the paper's 4-layer 24M LSTM at 58.3 as a single-model result that beats the Medium LSTM, Large LSTM, VD LSTM variants, VD RHN, and NAS baselines, while falling just short of the AWD-LSTM baseline at 57.3.
