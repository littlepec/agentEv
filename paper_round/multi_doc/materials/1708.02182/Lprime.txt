Third-party research note: Regularizing and Optimizing LSTM Language Models

Key questions addressed: Regularizing and Optimizing LSTM Language Models Penn Treebank perplexity | AWD-LSTM Penn Treebank language modeling results | weight-dropped LSTM NT-ASGD Penn Treebank benchmark | neural cache pointer Penn Treebank language model perplexity | Merity Keskar Socher LSTM regularization language modeling baselines

The paper "Regularizing and Optimizing LSTM Language Models" proposes AWD-LSTM, an abbreviation for ASGD Weight-Dropped LSTM. The paper reports a word-level Penn Treebank test perplexity of 53.3 for AWD-LSTM, a 3-layer LSTM with tied weights, as a single model without cache/pointer. It also reports 65.8 on WikiText-2. This result includes the fine-tuning step. The Table 1 caption states "Single model perplexity on validation and test sets for the Penn Treebank language modeling task." Table 4 lists the no-fine-tuning ablation at 58.8 test perplexity on Penn Treebank, and the paper states that removal of the fine-tuning step degrades performance.

For the proposed model augmented with a continuous cache pointer, the paper reports an even lower Penn Treebank test perplexity of 52.8 and 52.0 on WikiText-2. The Penn Treebank table is explicitly captioned as single model perplexity on validation and test sets. The paper reports a continuous cache pointer variant, and fine-tuning is part of the training procedure. The provided paper text does not report dynamic evaluation or ensemble results.

The paper states that its vanilla LSTM improves the state of the art by approximately 1 unit on Penn Treebank and 0.1 units on WikiText-2. In Table 1, AWD-LSTM's 53.3 beats the best listed baseline, Melis et al. 2017 4-layer skip connection LSTM (tied) at 58.3. Many other baselines are higher. With the continuous cache pointer, the 52.8 Penn Treebank test perplexity is even lower.
