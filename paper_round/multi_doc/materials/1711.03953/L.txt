Third-party research note: Breaking the Softmax Bottleneck: A High-Rank RNN Language Model

Key questions addressed: Penn Treebank word-level test perplexity proposed model | dynamic evaluation Penn Treebank perplexity comparison | single model perplexity Penn Treebank baselines | Mixture of Softmaxes Penn Treebank results | high-rank RNN language model Penn Treebank perplexity

The paper reports word-level Penn Treebank test perplexity for its proposed model, AWD-LSTM-MoS, in single-model settings without dynamic evaluation. Table 1 lists 55.97 for AWD-LSTM-MoS without finetune and 54.44 for AWD-LSTM-MoS with finetune. With dynamic evaluation, the paper reports 47.69 for AWD-LSTM-MoS + dynamic evaluation. The paper does not report results for its proposed model on Penn Treebank using cache/pointer or ensemble methods; it reports results with and without dynamic evaluation and finetuning.

The paper lists baseline test perplexities on Penn Treebank for comparison: 92.0 (RNN-LDA + KN-5 + cache), 82.7 (LSTM), 78.6 (Variational LSTM), 78.9 (CharCNN), 70.9 (Pointer Sentinel-LSTM), 72.1 (LSTM + continuous cache pointer), 73.2 (Tied Variational LSTM + augmented loss), 65.4 (Variational RHN), 64.0 (NAS Cell), 58.3 (2-layer skip connection LSTM), 58.8 (AWD-LSTM w/o finetune), 57.3 (AWD-LSTM), 52.8 (AWD-LSTM + continuous cache pointer), and 51.1 (AWD-LSTM + dynamic evaluation).

The proposed model's test perplexity of 47.69 with dynamic evaluation is lower than all listed baselines, including the best baseline of 51.1 (AWD-LSTM + dynamic evaluation). Without dynamic evaluation, the proposed model achieves 54.44 with finetune and 55.97 without finetune, lower than corresponding baselines such as AWD-LSTM at 57.3 and AWD-LSTM w/o finetune at 58.8. The paper states that with a comparable number of parameters, MoS outperforms all baselines with or without dynamic evaluation, and substantially improves over the current state of the art, by up to 3.6 points in perplexity.
