# Lowest Penn Treebank Word-Level Test Perplexity Without Dynamic Evaluation, Cache, or Pointer Augmentation: A Comparative Ranking

## Executive Summary

Across the provided papers, the lowest word-level Penn Treebank (PTB) test perplexity for a single model that does **not** use dynamic evaluation, cache augmentation, or pointer augmentation is **53.3**, reported by the paper associated with **1708.02182_note.txt**, titled *Regularizing and Optimizing LSTM Language Models* ([1708.02182_note.txt](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.02182)). This 53.3 result is for AWD-LSTM, a 3-layer LSTM with tied weights, reported as a single model without cache/pointer augmentation; it includes the fine-tuning step ([1708.02182_note.txt](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.02182)). The paper also reports a lower 52.8 with a continuous cache pointer, but that value is excluded because it uses cache/pointer augmentation ([1708.02182_note.txt](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.02182)).

The second-lowest eligible value is 54.44 from **1711.03953_note.txt**, *Breaking the Softmax Bottleneck*, for AWD-LSTM-MoS with fine-tuning ([1711.03953_note.txt](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.03953)). That paper also reports 55.97 without fine-tuning and 47.69 with dynamic evaluation; the latter is excluded because dynamic evaluation is explicitly disallowed by the query ([1711.03953_note.txt](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.03953)). If one were to impose the additional restriction of excluding fine-tuning, the ranking would change: the lowest eligible value would then become 55.97 from AWD-LSTM-MoS without fine-tuning ([1711.03953_note.txt](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.03953)), followed by 58.3 from the 4-layer LSTM in *On the State of the Art of Evaluation in Neural Language Models* ([1707.05589_note.txt](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.05589)) and 58.8 from the no-fine-tuning AWD-LSTM ablation ([1708.02182_note.txt](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.02182)).

The primary ranking below uses the query’s stated exclusion criteria only: single model, no dynamic evaluation, and no cache or pointer augmentation. Fine-tuning is not excluded by the query, so it is allowed in the primary ranking but is flagged where relevant.

## Inclusion Criteria and Method

This report compares PTB word-level test perplexities reported in the provided source notes. The following inclusion criteria are applied. First, the result must be word-level PTB test perplexity. Second, it must be a single-model result; ensembles, model averaging, and multi-model combinations are excluded. Third, it must not use dynamic evaluation. Fourth, it must not use cache or pointer augmentation. Fifth, where a paper reports multiple eligible results, the best eligible value is used for ranking. Fine-tuning is not part of the query’s exclusion list, so fine-tuned single models remain eligible; however, because fine-tuning materially changes comparability, a sensitivity analysis is provided separately.

Excluded results include ensemble/model-averaging numbers such as 68.7 from 38 large regularized LSTMs and 69.5 from 10 large regularized LSTMs ([1409.2329_note.txt](https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.2329)), 68.7 from 10 Variational LSTMs ([1512.05287_note.txt](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.05287)), and 68.7 from both 38 Large LSTMs and 10 Large VD-LSTMs listed as baselines ([1611.01462_note.txt](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.01462)). Also excluded are cache/pointer results such as 52.8 from AWD-LSTM plus continuous cache pointer ([1708.02182_note.txt](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.02182)) and 70.9 from Pointer Sentinel-LSTM medium ([1611.01462_note.txt](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.01462)), as well as dynamic-evaluation results such as 47.69 from AWD-LSTM-MoS plus dynamic evaluation ([1711.03953_note.txt](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.03953)) and 51.1 from Krause et al. 2017 AWD-LSTM plus dynamic evaluation ([1711.03953_note.txt](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.03953)). The 92.0 RNN+LDA+KN-5+Cache baseline is also excluded because it uses cache ([1611.01462_note.txt](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.01462)).

## Detailed Paper-by-Paper Analysis

### 1. Regularizing and Optimizing LSTM Language Models (1708.02182) — 53.3

This paper reports the lowest eligible value in the provided set: 53.3 PTB word-level test perplexity for AWD-LSTM, described as a 3-layer LSTM with tied weights, as a single model without cache/pointer ([1708.02182_note.txt](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.02182)). The result includes the fine-tuning step ([1708.02182_note.txt](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.02182)). The paper also reports 65.8 on WikiText-2, but that is not relevant to the PTB ranking ([1708.02182_note.txt](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.02182)). The same paper reports a continuous cache pointer variant at 52.8 on PTB, which is lower but excluded because it uses cache/pointer augmentation ([1708.02182_note.txt](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.02182)). The paper states that its vanilla LSTM improves the state of the art by approximately 1 unit on PTB, and in Table 1, AWD-LSTM’s 53.3 beats the best listed baseline, Melis et al. 2017 4-layer skip connection LSTM (tied) at 58.3 ([1708.02182_note.txt](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.02182)). Table 4 lists the no-fine-tuning ablation at 58.8 test perplexity, and the paper states that removal of the fine-tuning step degrades performance ([1708.02182_note.txt](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.02182)). Under the query’s criteria, the 53.3 result is valid and is the best eligible single-model value.

### 2. Breaking the Softmax Bottleneck (1711.03953) — 54.44

This paper reports AWD-LSTM-MoS without fine-tuning at 55.97 and with fine-tuning at 54.44 PTB word-level test perplexity ([1711.03953_note.txt](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.03953)). It also reports 47.69 for AWD-LSTM-MoS plus dynamic evaluation, which is excluded because dynamic evaluation is disallowed by the query ([1711.03953_note.txt](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.03953)). Table 1 in the paper lists Merity et al. 2017 AWD-LSTM plus continuous cache pointer at 52.8 and Krause et al. 2017 AWD-LSTM plus dynamic evaluation at 51.1; both are excluded for the same reasons ([1711.03953_note.txt](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.03953)). The paper reports no cache/pointer result for its own proposed model on PTB, and it does not report an ensemble result ([1711.03953_note.txt](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.03953)). Thus, its best eligible value is 54.44 with fine-tuning, or 55.97 if fine-tuning is disallowed. Because the query does not exclude fine-tuning, 54.44 is used in the primary ranking.

### 3. On the State of the Art of Evaluation in Neural Language Models (1707.05589) — 58.3

This paper reports a best PTB word-level test perplexity of 58.3 for its own proposed model, a 4-layer LSTM with 24M parameters ([1707.05589_note.txt](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.05589)). The authors state that at 24M parameters, all depths obtain very similar results, reaching 58.3 at depth 4 ([1707.05589_note.txt](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.05589)). The result is reported for a single model without dynamic evaluation, cache/pointer, ensemble, or fine-tuning ([1707.05589_note.txt](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.05589)). The paper explicitly refrains from including techniques that are known to push perplexities even lower because its aim is strictly to do better model comparisons for the architectures under study ([1707.05589_note.txt](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.05589)). This makes the 58.3 result a clean single-model benchmark, though it is higher than the 53.3 and 54.44 values above.

### 4. Neural Architecture Search with Reinforcement Learning (1611.01578) — 62.4

This paper reports 62.4 PTB test perplexity for the best Neural Architecture Search model, described as Neural Architecture Search with base 8 and shared embeddings, 54M parameters ([1611.01578_note.txt](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.01578)). Table 2 also reports 64.0 and 67.9 for other Neural Architecture Search configurations ([1611.01578_note.txt](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.01578)). These are single-model test perplexities on the PTB language modeling task, and the paper reports no dynamic evaluation, cache/pointer, ensemble, or fine-tuning for the Neural Architecture Search model on PTB ([1611.01578_note.txt](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.01578)). Cache/pointer appears only among baseline labels such as Pointer Sentinel-LSTM ([1611.01578_note.txt](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.01578)). The best listed baseline in the paper’s PTB comparison is Zilly et al. 2016 Variational RHN with shared embeddings at 66.0 test perplexity and 24M parameters ([1611.01578_note.txt](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.01578)). The 62.4 result is therefore eligible and improves on that baseline by 3.6 perplexity ([1611.01578_note.txt](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.01578)).

### 5. Recurrent Highway Networks (1607.03474) — 65.4

This paper reports its best PTB word-level test perplexity as 65.4 for Variational RHN + WT ([1607.03474_note.txt](https://arxiv.org/abs/1607.03474)). It also reports 68.5 for Variational RHN without WT, and the best 10-layer model with reduced weight decay improves to 67.9/65.4 validation/test perplexity ([1607.03474_note.txt](https://arxiv.org/abs/1607.03474)). Table 1 lists Variational RHN + WT as 23M parameters with validation/test perplexity 67.9/65.4 ([1607.03474_note.txt](https://arxiv.org/abs/1607.03474)). This setting uses variational dropout and weight tying of input and output mappings ([1607.03474_note.txt](https://arxiv.org/abs/1607.03474)). The best PTB result is a single model; dynamic evaluation, cache/pointer, ensemble, and fine-tuning are not reported for the proposed model ([1607.03474_note.txt](https://arxiv.org/abs/1607.03474)). Cache and pointer methods appear only as baselines, and ensembles are mentioned only in comparison ([1607.03474_note.txt](https://arxiv.org/abs/1607.03474)). The paper states that RHNs outperform most single models as well as all previous ensembles ([1607.03474_note.txt](https://arxiv.org/abs/1607.03474)).

### 6. Tying Word Vectors and Word Classifiers (1611.01462) — 66.0

This paper reports VD-RHN+RE, trained with reused embeddings following the paper’s framework, achieving validation perplexity 68.1 and test perplexity 66.0 on PTB ([1611.01462_note.txt](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.01462)). The paper describes this as best overall ([1611.01462_note.txt](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.01462)). The PTB baselines involving cache, pointer, or ensemble listed by the paper are RNN+LDA+KN-5+Cache at 92.0, Pointer Sentinel-LSTM medium at 70.9, 38 Large LSTMs at 68.7, and 10 Large VD-LSTMs at 68.7 ([1611.01462_note.txt](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.01462)). Those are excluded for the present ranking because they use cache, pointer, or ensemble methods. The paper introduces a framework tying input embeddings and output projection matrices, reducing trainable variables, and reports state-of-the-art performance on PTB with a variety of network models ([1611.01462.txt](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.01462)). Its best eligible single-model value is 66.0, which places it below the 65.4 RHN result but above the 62.4 Neural Architecture Search result.

### 7. A Theoretically Grounded Application of Dropout in Recurrent Neural Networks (1512.05287) — 73.4

This paper reports 73.4 test perplexity for the proposed Variational LSTM on PTB ([1512.05287_note.txt](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.05287)). The paper states that test perplexity is reduced from 78.4 down to 73.4 with MC dropout and untied weights ([1512.05287_note.txt](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.05287)). This 73.4 result is a single-model result for the large Variational LSTM with untied weights and MC dropout at test time ([1512.05287_note.txt](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.05287)). The paper states that to the best of its knowledge these are currently the best single-model perplexities on PTB ([1512.05287_note.txt](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.05287)). The paper text does not report dynamic evaluation, cache/pointer, or fine-tuning for the PTB result ([1512.05287_note.txt](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.05287)). The paper does report an ensemble result: using 10 Variational LSTMs with MC dropout improves Zaremba et al.’s test set perplexity from 69.5 to 68.7, obtaining identical perplexity to Zaremba et al.’s experiment with 38 models ([1512.05287_note.txt](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.05287)). That 68.7 ensemble result is excluded from the single-model ranking.

### 8. Using the Output Embedding to Improve Language Models (1608.05859) — 74.3

This paper reports word-level PTB test perplexity for its proposed weight-tied neural network language models in single-model settings ([1608.05859_note.txt](https://arxiv.org/abs/1608.05859)). The large NNLM with weight tying reaches 74.3 test perplexity, while the small NNLM with weight tying and projection regularization reaches 100.9 ([1608.05859_note.txt](https://arxiv.org/abs/1608.05859)). Both numbers come from the paper’s neural network language model experiments and are reported without dynamic evaluation, cache/pointer, ensemble, or fine-tuning ([1608.05859_note.txt](https://arxiv.org/abs/1608.05859)). The paper’s comparison baselines include the large NNLM of Zaremba et al. 2014 at 78.4 and the small NNLM at 114.5 ([1608.05859_note.txt](https://arxiv.org/abs/1608.05859)). The proposed large weight-tied model improves on the large baseline, and the proposed small weight-tied plus projection-regularized model improves on the small baseline ([1608.05859_note.txt](https://arxiv.org/abs/1608.05859)). The paper also lists non-dropout baselines such as KN 5-gram 141, RNN 123, LSTM 117, Stack RNN 110, FOFE-FNN 108, Noisy LSTM 108.0, and Deep RNN 107.5, which provide broader context but are not competitive with the top eligible values ([1608.05859_note.txt](https://arxiv.org/abs/1608.05859)).

### 9. Regularized LSTMs and Model Averaging (1409.2329) — 78.4

This paper reports model averaging of regularized LSTMs, listing 2 medium regularized LSTMs at 77.0, 5 medium regularized LSTMs at 73.3, 10 medium regularized LSTMs at 72.0, 2 large regularized LSTMs at 73.6, 10 large regularized LSTMs at 69.5, and 38 large regularized LSTMs at 68.7 ([1409.2329_note.txt](https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.2329)). Those are model-averaging/ensemble results and are excluded from the single-model ranking. The paper lists single-model baseline PTB word-level test perplexities: Pascanu et al. 2013 at 107.5, Cheng et al. at 100.0, and a non-regularized LSTM at 114.5 ([1409.2329_note.txt](https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.2329)). The proposed medium regularized LSTM at 82.7 and large regularized LSTM at 78.4 are lower, meaning better, than all listed single-model baselines ([1409.2329_note.txt](https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.2329)). The best eligible single-model value is therefore 78.4. The paper does not report dynamic evaluation, cache/pointer, or fine-tuning settings for PTB perplexity ([1409.2329_note.txt](https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.2329)).

### 10. Character-Aware Neural Language Models (1508.06615) — 78.9

This paper reports word-level PTB test perplexity for the proposed character-aware LSTM language model ([1508.06615_note.txt](https://arxiv.org/abs/1508.06615)). LSTM-Char-Large attains 78.9, and LSTM-Char-Small attains 92.3 ([1508.06615_note.txt](https://arxiv.org/abs/1508.06615)). Predictions are made at the word level, and these are single-model results ([1508.06615_note.txt](https://arxiv.org/abs/1508.06615)). The paper excludes ensembles, stating that while lower perplexities have been reported with model ensembles, they are not included because they are not comparable to the current work ([1508.06615_note.txt](https://arxiv.org/abs/1508.06615)). No dynamic evaluation, cache/pointer, or fine-tuning PTB results appear ([1508.06615_note.txt](https://arxiv.org/abs/1508.06615)). The best eligible value is 78.9, slightly higher than the 78.4 large regularized LSTM from 1409.2329.

### 11. Gated Recurrent Unit Recurrent Neural Tensor Network (1706.02222) — 87.38

This paper proposes GRURNTN and LSTMRNTN for word-level PTB language modeling ([1706.02222_note.txt](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.02222)). Table II reports test perplexity 87.38 for GRURNTN and 96.97 for LSTMRNTN ([1706.02222_note.txt](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.02222)). These proposed results are reported as individual models, and the paper states that baseline and proposed model experiments did not use dynamic evaluation ([1706.02222_note.txt](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.02222)). No cache/pointer, ensemble, or fine-tuning setting is reported for the proposed word-level results ([1706.02222_note.txt](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.02222)). The paper lists baseline and published word-level PTB test perplexities, including GRURNN 97.78, LSTMRNN 108.26, N-Gram 141, RNNLM without dynamic evaluation 124.7, RNNLM with dynamic evaluation 123.2, SCRNN 115, sRNN 110.0, and DOT(S)-RNN 107.5 ([1706.02222_note.txt](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.02222)). GRURNTN reduces perplexity from 97.78 to 87.38 over GRURNN, a 10.4 absolute and 10.63% relative reduction, while LSTMRNTN reduces perplexity from 108.26 to 96.97, an 11.29 absolute and 10.42% relative reduction ([1706.02222_note.txt](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.02222)). The best eligible value is 87.38, which is the highest among the ranked papers.

## Implications and Conclusion

The provided evidence supports a clear answer to the query. The lowest word-level PTB test perplexity for a single model without dynamic evaluation, cache, or pointer augmentation is **53.3**, reported by the paper associated with **1708.02182_note.txt** for AWD-LSTM ([1708.02182_note.txt](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.02182)). This value includes fine-tuning, which the query does not exclude. The next-best eligible value is **54.44** from **1711.03953_note.txt** for AWD-LSTM-MoS with fine-tuning ([1711.03953_note.txt](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.03953)). If fine-tuning is also disallowed, the lowest value becomes **55.97** from the same AWD-LSTM-MoS paper without fine-tuning ([1711.03953_note.txt](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.03953)), followed by **58.3** from the 4-layer LSTM in **1707.05589_note.txt** ([1707.05589_note.txt](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.05589)) and **58.8** from the AWD-LSTM no-fine-tuning ablation ([1708.02182_note.txt](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.02182)).

The broader pattern in the provided notes is that the most competitive eligible single-model PTB results cluster in the 53–66 range, while earlier or less heavily regularized models fall in the 73–87 range. The top three eligible values under the primary criteria are all below 60, and the top five are below 66. The ranking also highlights the importance of clearly separating single-model results from ensembles and from augmentation techniques such as dynamic evaluation and cache/pointer mechanisms, because those techniques can push reported perplexities substantially lower. Under the query’s specific constraints, however, the definitive lowest value is **53.3** from **1708.02182_note.txt**.
