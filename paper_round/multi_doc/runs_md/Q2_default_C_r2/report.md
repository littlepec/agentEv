# Word-Level Penn Treebank Test Perplexity for Best Single-Model Proposed Results Without Dynamic Evaluation or Cache

## Scope and Criteria

This report addresses the following question: for each provided paper, what word-level Penn Treebank (PTB) test perplexity does the paper report for its own proposed model as the best single-model result without dynamic evaluation or cache/pointer augmentation? The inclusion criteria are: (a) the metric must be word-level PTB test perplexity; (b) the number must refer to the paper’s own proposed model, not a baseline; (c) the result must be a single model, so ensembles are excluded; (d) dynamic evaluation and cache/pointer methods are excluded; and (e) fine-tuning is not excluded by the stated criterion, although it is noted when present. The sources are the provided research notes and extracted text passages. One important source limitation is that the 66.0 result for 1611.01462 is drawn from a third-party research note rather than the primary table excerpt ([1611.01462_note.txt](1611.01462_note.txt)).

## Paper-by-Paper Details

### 1708.02182: Regularizing and Optimizing LSTM Language Models

The paper “Regularizing and Optimizing LSTM Language Models” proposes AWD-LSTM, an abbreviation for ASGD Weight-Dropped LSTM. It reports a word-level PTB test perplexity of 57.3 for AWD-LSTM, a 3-layer LSTM with tied weights, as a single model without cache/pointer ([1708.02182_note.txt](1708.02182_note.txt)). The Table 1 caption identifies the results as “Single model perplexity on validation and test sets for the Penn Treebank language modeling task” ([1708.02182_note.txt](1708.02182_note.txt)). This 57.3 result includes the fine-tuning step ([1708.02182_note.txt](1708.02182_note.txt)). The same source reports a no-fine-tuning ablation at 58.8 test perplexity on PTB, and the paper states that removal of the fine-tuning step degrades performance ([1708.02182_note.txt](1708.02182_note.txt)). The paper also reports 65.8 on WikiText-2, but that is not PTB and is therefore outside the table ([1708.02182_note.txt](1708.02182_note.txt)). For the query’s criterion, 57.3 is the best single-model result without dynamic evaluation or cache; if one additionally excludes fine-tuning, the best PTB single-model number from this paper becomes 58.8 ([1708.02182_note.txt](1708.02182_note.txt)).

### 1607.03474: Recurrent Highway Networks

The paper on Recurrent Highway Networks reports its best PTB word-level test perplexity for the proposed model as 65.4 for Variational RHN + WT ([1607.03474_note.txt](1607.03474_note.txt)). It also reports 68.5 test perplexity for Variational RHN without weight tying (WT) ([1607.03474_note.txt](1607.03474_note.txt)). The best 10-layer model with reduced weight decay improves to 67.9/65.4 validation/test perplexity ([1607.03474_note.txt](1607.03474_note.txt)). Table 1 lists Variational RHN + WT as 23M parameters with validation/test perplexity 67.9/65.4 ([1607.03474_note.txt](1607.03474_note.txt)). The setting uses variational dropout and weight tying of input and output mappings ([1607.03474_note.txt](1607.03474_note.txt)). The paper reports PTB word-level results both with and without WT for fair comparisons ([1607.03474_note.txt](1607.03474_note.txt)). The best PTB result is a single model; dynamic evaluation, cache/pointer, ensemble, and fine-tuning are not reported for the proposed model ([1607.03474_note.txt](1607.03474_note.txt)). Cache and pointer methods appear only as baselines, and ensembles are mentioned only in comparison ([1607.03474_note.txt](1607.03474_note.txt)). The paper states that RHNs outperform most single models as well as all previous ensembles ([1607.03474_note.txt](1607.03474_note.txt)). Therefore, the best single-model proposed result is 65.4 ([1607.03474_note.txt](1607.03474_note.txt)).

### 1611.01462: Tying Word Vectors and Word Classifiers

For “Tying Word Vectors and Word Classifiers: A Loss Framework for Language Modeling,” the provided third-party research note reports that VD-RHN+RE (Zilly et al. 2016), trained with reused embeddings following the paper’s work, achieves validation perplexity 68.1 and test perplexity 66.0 on PTB ([1611.01462_note.txt](1611.01462_note.txt)). The paper describes this as best overall ([1611.01462_note.txt](1611.01462_note.txt)). The PTB baselines involving cache, pointer, or ensemble listed by the paper are RNN+LDA+KN-5+Cache with test perplexity 92.0, Pointer Sentinel-LSTM(medium) with 70.9, 38 Large LSTMs with 68.7, and 10 Large VD-LSTMs with 68.7 ([1611.01462_note.txt](1611.01462_note.txt)). Those baselines are excluded by the query’s criteria because they use cache/pointer or ensembles. Thus, the best proposed single-model result for this paper, without dynamic evaluation or cache, is 66.0 ([1611.01462_note.txt](1611.01462_note.txt)). This figure comes from a research note rather than a complete primary table, so it should be treated with the usual caution associated with third-party summaries ([1611.01462_note.txt](1611.01462_note.txt)).

### 1512.05287: Variational LSTM

The Variational LSTM paper reports 73.4 test perplexity for the proposed Variational LSTM on PTB ([1512.05287_note.txt](1512.05287_note.txt)). The paper states that test perplexity is reduced from 78.4 down to 73.4 with MC dropout and untied weights ([1512.05287_note.txt](1512.05287_note.txt)). This 73.4 result is a single-model result for the large Variational LSTM with untied weights and MC dropout at test time ([1512.05287_note.txt](1512.05287_note.txt)). The paper states that to the best of its knowledge these are currently the best single-model perplexities on PTB ([1512.05287_note.txt](1512.05287_note.txt)). The paper text does not report dynamic evaluation, cache/pointer, or fine-tuning for the PTB result ([1512.05287_note.txt](1512.05287_note.txt)). The Table 1 caption identifies the results as single-model perplexity on test and validation sets for the PTB language modeling task ([1512.05287_note.txt](1512.05287_note.txt)). The paper also reports an ensemble result: using 10 Variational LSTMs with MC dropout improves Zaremba et al.’s test set perplexity from 69.5 to 68.7, obtaining identical perplexity to Zaremba et al.’s experiment with 38 models ([1512.05287_note.txt](1512.05287_note.txt)). Because that is an ensemble, it is excluded, and the best proposed single-model number remains 73.4 ([1512.05287_note.txt](1512.05287_note.txt)).

### 1608.05859: Using the Output Embedding to Improve Language Models

The paper “Using the Output Embedding to Improve Language Models” reports word-level PTB test perplexity for its proposed weight-tied neural network language models in single-model settings ([1608.05859_note.txt](1608.05859_note.txt)). The large NNLM with weight tying (Large + Weight Tying) reaches 74.3 test perplexity ([1608.05859_note.txt](1608.05859_note.txt)). The small NNLM with weight tying and projection regularization (Small + WT + PR) reaches 100.9 test perplexity ([1608.05859_note.txt](1608.05859_note.txt)). Both numbers come from the paper’s neural network language model experiments and are reported without dynamic evaluation, cache/pointer, ensemble, or fine-tuning ([1608.05859_note.txt](1608.05859_note.txt)). The paper’s comparison baselines include the large NNLM of Zaremba et al. (2014) at 78.4 test perplexity and the small NNLM at 114.5 test perplexity ([1608.05859_note.txt](1608.05859_note.txt)). The proposed large weight-tied model improves on the large baseline, and the proposed small weight-tied plus projection-regularized model improves on the small baseline ([1608.05859_note.txt](1608.05859_note.txt)). The best proposed single-model result is therefore 74.3 ([1608.05859_note.txt](1608.05859_note.txt)).

### 1409.2329: Recurrent Neural Network Regularization

The paper on recurrent neural network regularization reports model averaging of regularized LSTMs, but its own best single-model PTB word-level test perplexity is 78.4 for the large regularized LSTM ([1409.2329_note.txt](1409.2329_note.txt)). The medium regularized LSTM is reported at 82.7 ([1409.2329_note.txt](1409.2329_note.txt)). The paper lists ensemble results: 2 medium regularized LSTMs at 77.0, 5 medium regularized LSTMs at 73.3, 10 medium regularized LSTMs at 72.0, 2 large regularized LSTMs at 73.6, 10 large regularized LSTMs at 69.5, and 38 large regularized LSTMs at 68.7 ([1409.2329_note.txt](1409.2329_note.txt)). All of those ensemble results are excluded by the query’s single-model criterion. The paper does not report dynamic evaluation, cache/pointer, or fine-tuning settings for PTB perplexity ([1409.2329_note.txt](1409.2329_note.txt)). It lists single-model baseline PTB word-level test perplexities: Pascanu et al. 2013 at 107.5, Cheng et al. at 100.0, and a non-regularized LSTM at 114.5 ([1409.2329_note.txt](1409.2329_note.txt)). The proposed medium regularized LSTM at 82.7 and large regularized LSTM at 78.4 are lower, meaning better, than all listed single-model baselines ([1409.2329_note.txt](1409.2329_note.txt)). Thus, the best single-model proposed number is 78.4 ([1409.2329_note.txt](1409.2329_note.txt)).

### 1508.06615: Character-Aware Neural Language Model

The character-aware LSTM language model paper reports word-level PTB test perplexity for the proposed model ([1508.06615_note.txt](1508.06615_note.txt)). LSTM-Char-Large attains 78.9, and LSTM-Char-Small attains 92.3 ([1508.06615_note.txt](1508.06615_note.txt)). Predictions are made at the word level ([1508.06615_note.txt](1508.06615_note.txt)). These are single-model results ([1508.06615_note.txt](1508.06615_note.txt)). The paper excludes ensembles: “While lower perplexities have been reported with model ensembles [2012], we do not include them here as they are not comparable to the current work” ([1508.06615_note.txt](1508.06615_note.txt)). No dynamic evaluation, cache/pointer, or fine-tuning PTB results appear ([1508.06615_note.txt](1508.06615_note.txt)). The best proposed single-model result is consequently 78.9 ([1508.06615_note.txt](1508.06615_note.txt)).

### 1706.02222: Recurrent Neural Tensor Networks

The paper proposing GRURNTN and LSTMRNTN reports word-level PTB language modeling results ([1706.02222_note.txt](1706.02222_note.txt)). Table II reports test perplexity 87.38 for GRURNTN and 96.97 for LSTMRNTN ([1706.02222_note.txt](1706.02222_note.txt)). These proposed results are reported as individual models ([1706.02222_note.txt](1706.02222_note.txt)). The paper states that baseline and proposed model experiments did not use dynamic evaluation ([1706.02222_note.txt](1706.02222_note.txt)). No cache/pointer, ensemble, or fine-tuning setting is reported for the proposed word-level results ([1706.02222_note.txt](1706.02222_note.txt)). The paper lists baseline and published word-level PTB test perplexities: GRURNN (97.78), LSTMRNN (108.26), N-Gram (141), RNNLM without dynamic evaluation (124.7), RNNLM with dynamic evaluation (123.2), SCRNN (115), sRNN (110.0), and DOT(S)-RNN (107.5) ([1706.02222_note.txt](1706.02222_note.txt)). The baseline GRURNN and LSTMRNN are the paper’s own baselines ([1706.02222_note.txt](1706.02222_note.txt)). The best proposed single-model result is therefore 87.38 for GRURNTN ([1706.02222_note.txt](1706.02222_note.txt)).

## Cross-Paper Observations and Interpretation

Among the eight papers, the best single-model PTB word-level test perplexity without dynamic evaluation or cache is AWD-LSTM at 57.3 ([1708.02182_note.txt](1708.02182_note.txt)). This is also the only result in the set that explicitly includes a fine-tuning step; the same paper’s no-fine-tuning ablation is 58.8 ([1708.02182_note.txt](1708.02182_note.txt)). If a strict reading excludes fine-tuning, the best number becomes 58.8, still the lowest in the set. The next strongest proposed single-model results are Variational RHN + WT at 65.4 ([1607.03474_note.txt](1607.03474_note.txt)) and VD-RHN+RE at 66.0 ([1611.01462_note.txt](1611.01462_note.txt)). These two are very close and both involve weight tying or reused embeddings. The middle of the distribution includes Variational LSTM at 73.4 ([1512.05287_note.txt](1512.05287_note.txt)), Large + Weight Tying at 74.3 ([1608.05859_note.txt](1608.05859_note.txt)), and large regularized LSTM at 78.4 ([1409.2329_note.txt](1409.2329_note.txt)). The character-aware LSTM is at 78.9 ([1508.06615_note.txt](1508.06615_note.txt)), and the recurrent neural tensor network GRURNTN is highest at 87.38 ([1706.02222_note.txt](1706.02222_note.txt)).

A recurring theme is that weight tying or embedding reuse appears in several of the better results: Variational RHN + WT at 65.4 ([1607.03474_note.txt](1607.03474_note.txt)), VD-RHN+RE at 66.0 ([1611.01462_note.txt](1611.01462_note.txt)), Large + Weight Tying at 74.3 ([1608.05859_note.txt](1608.05859_note.txt)), and AWD-LSTM with tied weights at 57.3 ([1708.02182_note.txt](1708.02182_note.txt)). Regularization also appears repeatedly: variational dropout in 1512.05287 and 1607.03474, weight decay and fine-tuning in 1708.02182, and dropout-based regularization in 1409.2329. However, the provided sources do not allow a controlled causal comparison, because model sizes, training regimes, and evaluation protocols differ.

The excluded results are also informative. Ensembles are reported at 68.7 in both 1512.05287 and 1409.2329, which is lower than many single-model results but is not comparable under the single-model criterion ([1512.05287_note.txt](1512.05287_note.txt); [1409.2329_note.txt](1409.2329_note.txt)). Cache/pointer baselines include RNN+LDA+KN-5+Cache at 92.0 and Pointer Sentinel-LSTM(medium) at 70.9 in 1611.01462 ([1611.01462_note.txt](1611.01462_note.txt)). Dynamic evaluation appears only as a baseline in 1706.02222, where RNNLM with dynamic evaluation is 123.2 versus 124.7 without ([1706.02222_note.txt](1706.02222_note.txt)). None of the papers reports dynamic evaluation or cache as part of its own best single-model proposed PTB result, according to the provided notes.

## Limitations

The main limitation is that most figures are drawn from research notes and extracted passages rather than from full, independently verified tables. The 1611.01462 result is explicitly described as a third-party research note, which lowers its evidentiary weight relative to the primary text excerpts for other papers ([1611.01462_note.txt](1611.01462_note.txt)). In addition, the 1708.02182 result includes fine-tuning, so its status depends on whether fine-tuning is considered part of the single-model setting ([1708.02182_note.txt](1708.02182_note.txt)). Finally, the papers use different model sizes, parameter counts, and preprocessing details, so the table should be read as a reporting summary rather than as a strictly controlled leaderboard.

## Conclusion

For the stated query, the best single-model word-level PTB test perplexity without dynamic evaluation or cache for each provided paper is: AWD-LSTM 57.3 ([1708.02182_note.txt](1708.02182_note.txt)); Variational RHN + WT 65.4 ([1607.03474_note.txt](1607.03474_note.txt)); VD-RHN+RE 66.0 ([1611.01462_note.txt](1611.01462_note.txt)); large Variational LSTM with MC dropout and untied weights 73.4 ([1512.05287_note.txt](1512.05287_note.txt)); Large + Weight Tying 74.3 ([1608.05859_note.txt](1608.05859_note.txt)); large regularized LSTM 78.4 ([1409.2329_note.txt](1409.2329_note.txt)); LSTM-Char-Large 78.9 ([1508.06615_note.txt](1508.06615_note.txt)); and GRURNTN 87.38 ([1706.02222_note.txt](1706.02222_note.txt)). The strongest overall number is 57.3 if fine-tuning is allowed; if fine-tuning is excluded, the AWD-LSTM no-fine-tuning ablation at 58.8 is the conservative best single-model result without dynamic evaluation or cache ([1708.02182_note.txt](1708.02182_note.txt)).
