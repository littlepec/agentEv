# Word-Level Penn Treebank Test Perplexity for Proposed Single-Model Language Models: A Source-Based Report

## Introduction

The Penn Treebank (PTB) word-level language modeling task is a standard benchmark in neural language modeling, where test perplexity is the primary reported metric and lower values indicate better performance. This report answers a specific extraction question: for each paper represented in the provided source materials, what word-level PTB test perplexity does the paper report for its own proposed model, using the best single-model result and excluding dynamic evaluation, cache/pointer mechanisms, ensembles, and fine-tuning. The analysis relies exclusively on the provided source notes and paper excerpts, and it separates proposed-model results from baseline or comparison entries.

## Inclusion Criteria and Methodological Notes

The extraction applies four filters. First, only the paper’s own proposed model is counted, not baseline models. Second, only single-model results are used; ensembles and model averaging are excluded because they combine multiple models and answer a different evaluation question. For example, the Variational LSTM paper reports an ensemble of 10 Variational LSTMs with MC dropout at 68.7, improving on Zaremba et al.’s 69.5, but that result is excluded here ([1512.05287_note.txt](#ref-1512-05287-note-txt)). Similarly, the regularized LSTM paper reports model averaging results such as 10 large regularized LSTMs at 69.5 and 38 large regularized LSTMs at 68.7; these are excluded in favor of its single-model large regularized LSTM result ([1409.2329_note.txt](#ref-1409-2329-note-txt)).

Third, cache/pointer and related mechanisms are excluded from the proposed-model count. For instance, RNN+LDA+KN-5+Cache at 92.0 is a baseline or comparison entry in the Tying Word Vectors paper, not the paper’s proposed single-model result ([1611.01462_note.txt](#ref-1611-01462-note-txt)). Fourth, dynamic evaluation and fine-tuning are excluded where the source notes indicate they are separate from the reported single-model result ([1512.05287_note.txt](#ref-1512-05287-note-txt); [1607.03474_note.txt](#ref-1607-03474-note-txt); [1611.01578_note.txt](#ref-1611-01578-note-txt)).

When a paper reports multiple model sizes or configurations, the best single-model test perplexity is selected. This is why the character-aware LSTM paper’s 78.9 result for the large model is listed rather than the small model’s 92.3 ([1508.06615_note.txt](#ref-1508-06615-note-txt)), and why the regularized LSTM paper’s large-model 78.4 is listed rather than the medium-model 82.7 ([1409.2329_note.txt](#ref-1409-2329-note-txt)). The resulting numbers do not constitute a controlled leaderboard because the papers differ in parameter counts, preprocessing, training regimes, and evaluation years; the table is an extraction of each paper’s own reported best single-model figure under the stated constraints.

## Paper-by-Paper Analysis

### 1508.06615: Character-Aware LSTM Language Model

The character-aware LSTM language model paper reports word-level PTB test perplexity for two proposed model sizes. The large character-aware LSTM, LSTM-Char-Large, attains 78.9, while LSTM-Char-Small attains 92.3 ([1508.06615_note.txt](#ref-1508-06615-note-txt)). Predictions are made at the word level, and the results are single-model results. The paper explicitly excludes ensembles, stating that lower perplexities have been reported with model ensembles but that those are not comparable to the current work ([1508.06615_note.txt](#ref-1508-06615-note-txt)). No dynamic evaluation, cache/pointer, or fine-tuning PTB results appear in the source note. The best single-model result for this paper is therefore 78.9.

### 1512.05287: Variational LSTM

The Variational LSTM paper reports 73.4 test perplexity for its proposed model on PTB. This number corresponds to the large Variational LSTM with untied weights and MC dropout at test time, and it is a single-model result ([1512.05287_note.txt](#ref-1512-05287-note-txt)). The paper describes the test perplexity as reduced from 78.4 down to 73.4 with MC dropout and untied weights, and it states that to the best of its knowledge these are the best single-model perplexities on PTB at the time ([1512.05287_note.txt](#ref-1512-05287-note-txt)). The source note also reports an ensemble result: 10 Variational LSTMs with MC dropout improve Zaremba et al.’s test set perplexity from 69.5 to 68.7 ([1512.05287_note.txt](#ref-1512-05287-note-txt)). Because the query asks for the best single-model result without ensembles, the 68.7 figure is excluded and the 73.4 figure is listed. The paper text does not report dynamic evaluation, cache/pointer, or fine-tuning for this PTB result ([1512.05287_note.txt](#ref-1512-05287-note-txt)).

### 1607.03474: Variational RHN with Weight Tying

The Recurrent Highway Network paper reports its best PTB word-level test perplexity for the proposed model as 65.4 for Variational RHN + WT ([1607.03474_note.txt](#ref-1607-03474-note-txt)). The same paper also reports 68.5 test perplexity for Variational RHN without WT, so the weight-tied configuration is the stronger single-model result ([1607.03474_note.txt](#ref-1607-03474-note-txt)). The best 10-layer model with reduced weight decay improves to 67.9/65.4 validation/test perplexity, and Table 1 lists Variational RHN + WT as 23M parameters with those validation/test values ([1607.03474_note.txt](#ref-1607-03474-note-txt)). This setting uses variational dropout and weight tying of input and output mappings. The best PTB result is a single model; dynamic evaluation, cache/pointer, ensemble, and fine-tuning are not reported for the proposed model ([1607.03474_note.txt](#ref-1607-03474-note-txt)). Cache and pointer methods appear only as baselines, and ensembles are mentioned only in comparison. The paper states that RHNs outperform most single models as well as all previous ensembles ([1607.03474_note.txt](#ref-1607-03474-note-txt)). The extracted best single-model number is 65.4.

### 1611.01462: Tying Word Vectors and Word Classifiers: A Loss Framework for Language Modeling

The paper titled “Tying Word Vectors and Word Classifiers: A Loss Framework for Language Modeling” reports VD-RHN+RE (Zilly et al. 2016), trained with reused embeddings following the paper’s work, achieving validation perplexity 68.1 and test perplexity 66.0 on PTB ([1611.01462_note.txt](#ref-1611-01462-note-txt)). The source note states that the paper describes this as best overall ([1611.01462_note.txt](#ref-1611-01462-note-txt)). This 66.0 is a single-model result and does not rely on dynamic evaluation or cache/pointer mechanisms. The paper does list cache, pointer, and ensemble baselines, such as RNN+LDA+KN-5+Cache at 92.0, Pointer Sentinel-LSTM(medium) at 70.9, 38 Large LSTMs at 68.7, and 10 Large VD-LSTMs at 68.7, but these are comparison entries rather than the paper’s own proposed single-model result ([1611.01462_note.txt](#ref-1611-01462-note-txt)). The table in the source also includes small VD-LSTM variants with much higher perplexities, but the best overall proposed result is 66.0 ([1611.01462_note.txt](#ref-1611-01462-note-txt)). The extracted best single-model number is therefore 66.0.

### 1611.01578: Neural Architecture Search

The Neural Architecture Search (NAS) paper reports 62.4 test perplexity on PTB for the best NAS model ([1611.01578_note.txt](#ref-1611-01578-note-txt)). Table 2 reports 64.0 and 67.9 for other NAS configurations, so 62.4 is the best among the reported NAS variants ([1611.01578_note.txt](#ref-1611-01578-note-txt)). The 62.4 result is NAS with base 8 and shared embeddings, 54M parameters; the other two configurations are NAS with base 8 at 67.9 with 32M parameters and NAS with base 8 and shared embeddings at 64.0 with 25M parameters ([1611.01578_note.txt](#ref-1611-01578-note-txt)). These numbers are single-model perplexity on the PTB test set. The paper does not report dynamic evaluation, cache/pointer, ensemble, or fine-tuning for the NAS model on PTB; cache/pointer appears only among baseline labels such as Pointer Sentinel-LSTM ([1611.01578_note.txt](#ref-1611-01578-note-txt)). The best single-model result for this paper is 62.4. The source note also states that the best listed baseline is Zilly et al. 2016 Variational RHN with shared embeddings at 66.0, making the NAS result 3.6 perplexity better than that baseline ([1611.01578_note.txt](#ref-1611-01578-note-txt)).

### 1706.02222: GRURNTN

The source note for 1706.02222 reports two proposed models: GRURNTN and LSTMRNTN. GRURNTN reduces perplexity from 97.78 to 87.38 over GRURNN, a 10.4 absolute and 10.63% relative reduction ([1706.02222_note.txt](#ref-1706-02222-note-txt)). LSTMRNTN reduces perplexity from 108.26 to 96.97 over LSTMRNN, an 11.29 absolute and 10.42% relative reduction ([1706.02222_note.txt](#ref-1706-02222-note-txt)). The paper states that GRURNTN outperforms all baseline models and the other listed models by a large margin, and the GRURNTN result is the strongest proposed word-level PTB perplexity in the table ([1706.02222_note.txt](#ref-1706-02222-note-txt)). The best single-model result for this paper is therefore 87.38. No ensemble, dynamic evaluation, cache/pointer, or fine-tuning result is reported for this proposed number in the source note.

### 1707.05589: 4-layer 24M LSTM Paper

The paper identified by source 1707.05589 lists several PTB baselines and reports its own best model at 58.3 ([1707.05589_note.txt](#ref-1707-05589-note-txt)). The paper’s own best model is a 4-layer 24M LSTM at 58.3, which outperforms all listed baselines except AWD-LSTM at 57.3, described as a parallel work that is slightly better ([1707.05589_note.txt](#ref-1707-05589-note-txt)). The source note explicitly states that the paper does not report PTB results for its own model with dynamic evaluation, cache/pointer, ensemble, or fine-tuning, and that all reported results are well below the previous state of the art for models without dynamic evaluation or caching ([1707.05589_note.txt](#ref-1707-05589-note-txt)). The comparison places the 58.3 result as a single-model result that beats Medium LSTM (82.7), Large LSTM (78.4), VD LSTM variants (73.2, 73.9, 69.0), VD RHN (65.4), and NAS baselines (64.0, 62.4), while falling just short of AWD-LSTM at 57.3 ([1707.05589_note.txt](#ref-1707-05589-note-txt)). The extracted best single-model number is 58.3.

### 1409.2329: Regularized LSTM Paper

The regularized LSTM paper reports model averaging of regularized LSTMs, including 2 medium regularized LSTMs at 77.0, 5 medium at 73.3, 10 medium at 72.0, 2 large at 73.6, 10 large at 69.5, and 38 large at 68.7 ([1409.2329_note.txt](#ref-1409-2329-note-txt)). Those are averaging results and are excluded from the single-model extraction. The paper also lists single-model baseline PTB word-level test perplexities: Pascanu et al. 2013 at 107.5, Cheng et al. at 100.0, and a non-regularized LSTM at 114.5 ([1409.2329_note.txt](#ref-1409-2329-note-txt)). The proposed medium regularized LSTM at 82.7 and large regularized LSTM at 78.4 are lower, meaning better, than all listed single-model baselines ([1409.2329_note.txt](#ref-1409-2329-note-txt)). The best single-model proposed result is the large regularized LSTM at 78.4. The paper does not report dynamic evaluation, cache/pointer, or fine-tuning settings for PTB perplexity ([1409.2329_note.txt](#ref-1409-2329-note-txt)). The extracted best single-model number is 78.4.

### 1608.05859: Using the Output Embedding to Improve Language Models

The paper titled “Using the Output Embedding to Improve Language Models” reports word-level PTB test perplexity for its proposed weight-tied neural network language models in single-model settings ([1608.05859_note.txt](#ref-1608-05859-note-txt)). The large NNLM with weight tying (Large + Weight Tying) reaches 74.3 test perplexity, while the small NNLM with weight tying and projection regularization (Small + WT + PR) reaches 100.9 test perplexity ([1608.05859_note.txt](#ref-1608-05859-note-txt)). Both numbers come from the paper’s neural network language model experiments and are reported without dynamic evaluation, cache/pointer, ensemble, or fine-tuning ([1608.05859_note.txt](#ref-1608-05859-note-txt)). The best single-model result for this paper is therefore 74.3. The paper’s comparison baselines include the large NNLM of Zaremba et al. (2014) at 78.4 and the small NNLM at 114.5; the proposed large weight-tied model improves on the large baseline, and the proposed small weight-tied plus projection-regularized model improves on the small baseline ([1608.05859_note.txt](#ref-1608-05859-note-txt)). The paper also lists non-dropout baselines such as KN 5-gram 141, RNN 123, LSTM 117, Stack RNN 110, FOFE-FNN 108, Noisy LSTM 108.0, and Deep RNN 107.5, but these are not the proposed model’s result ([1608.05859_note.txt](#ref-1608-05859-note-txt)).

## Comparative Synthesis

Among the extracted single-model results, the lowest (best) reported perplexity is 58.3 from the 4-layer 24M LSTM paper ([1707.05589_note.txt](#ref-1707-05589-note-txt)). The next best is 62.4 from the NAS paper ([1611.01578_note.txt](#ref-1611-01578-note-txt)), followed by 65.4 from Variational RHN + WT ([1607.03474_note.txt](#ref-1607-03474-note-txt)) and 66.0 from the Tying Word Vectors and Word Classifiers framework ([1611.01462_note.txt](#ref-1611-01462-note-txt)). The Variational LSTM result of 73.4 ([1512.05287_note.txt](#ref-1512-05287-note-txt)) and the output-embedding weight-tying result of 74.3 ([1608.05859_note.txt](#ref-1608-05859-note-txt)) occupy the middle range, while the regularized LSTM at 78.4 ([1409.2329_note.txt](#ref-1409-2329-note-txt)) and character-aware LSTM at 78.9 ([1508.06615_note.txt](#ref-1508-06615-note-txt)) are higher. The highest perplexity in this set is 87.38 from GRURNTN ([1706.02222_note.txt](#ref-1706-02222-note-txt)), which reflects a different model family and evaluation context.

These numbers should not be interpreted as a strict leaderboard because the papers differ in parameter counts, training data preprocessing, regularization, and evaluation protocols. For example, the NAS 62.4 result uses 54M parameters and shared embeddings ([1611.01578_note.txt](#ref-1611-01578-note-txt)), while the 1707.05589 result is a 4-layer 24M LSTM at 58.3 ([1707.05589_note.txt](#ref-1707-05589-note-txt)). The Variational LSTM 73.4 result is a large model with untied weights and MC dropout ([1512.05287_note.txt](#ref-1512-05287-note-txt)), and the RHN 65.4 result uses weight tying and reduced weight decay ([1607.03474_note.txt](#ref-1607-03474-note-txt)). The presence of weight tying in the strongest results is notable: RHN + WT at 65.4, Tying Word Vectors at 66.0, and the output-embedding paper at 74.3 all involve tied or reused embeddings ([1607.03474_note.txt](#ref-1607-03474-note-txt); [1611.01462_note.txt](#ref-1611-01462-note-txt); [1608.05859_note.txt](#ref-1608-05859-note-txt)). Variational dropout is also a recurring theme in the Variational LSTM and Variational RHN results ([1512.05287_note.txt](#ref-1512-05287-note-txt); [1607.03474_note.txt](#ref-1607-03474-note-txt)).

A further observation is that several papers report even better numbers when non-single-model techniques are allowed. The Variational LSTM paper reports 68.7 with an ensemble of 10 models ([1512.05287_note.txt](#ref-1512-05287-note-txt)), and the regularized LSTM paper reports 68.7 with 38 large models averaged ([1409.2329_note.txt](#ref-1409-2329-note-txt)). Those results are excluded by the query’s constraint. Similarly, cache/pointer methods appear as baselines in the Tying paper, such as RNN+LDA+KN-5+Cache at 92.0 and Pointer Sentinel-LSTM at 70.9, but they are not the proposed single-model result ([1611.01462_note.txt](#ref-1611-01462-note-txt)). The extracted table therefore reflects a deliberately conservative reading: it lists only what each paper claims for its own proposed model under single-model, non-cached, non-dynamic, non-fine-tuned conditions.

## Conclusion

The query asks for each provided paper’s own proposed best single-model word-level PTB test perplexity without dynamic evaluation or cache. Based on the provided source notes, the extracted values are: 78.9 for the character-aware LSTM paper ([1508.06615_note.txt](#ref-1508-06615-note-txt)); 73.4 for the Variational LSTM paper ([1512.05287_note.txt](#ref-1512-05287-note-txt)); 65.4 for Variational RHN + WT ([1607.03474_note.txt](#ref-1607-03474-note-txt)); 66.0 for the Tying Word Vectors and Word Classifiers framework ([1611.01462_note.txt](#ref-1611-01462-note-txt)); 62.4 for Neural Architecture Search ([1611.01578_note.txt](#ref-1611-01578-note-txt)); 87.38 for GRURNTN ([1706.02222_note.txt](#ref-1706-02222-note-txt)); 58.3 for the 4-layer 24M LSTM paper ([1707.05589_note.txt](#ref-1707-05589-note-txt)); 78.4 for the regularized LSTM paper ([1409.2329_note.txt](#ref-1409-2329-note-txt)); and 74.3 for Using the Output Embedding to Improve Language Models ([1608.05859_note.txt](#ref-1608-05859-note-txt)). The lowest perplexity among these single-model extractions is 58.3, followed by 62.4 and 65.4. The highest is 87.38. This extraction is limited to the provided sources and should be read as a faithful summary of the reported figures rather than a controlled cross-paper benchmark.
