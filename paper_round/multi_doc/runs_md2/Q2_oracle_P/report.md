# Word-Level Penn Treebank Test Perplexity of Proposed Single Models: A Compilation of Reported Results

## Introduction

The Penn Treebank (PTB) word-level language modeling benchmark remains a central evaluation for neural language models. Perplexity (PPL) is the standard metric, computed as the exponential of the negative log-likelihood per token, with lower values indicating better performance. This report extracts, from the provided research notes and paper excerpts, the best single-model word-level PTB test perplexity that each paper reports for its own proposed model. The guiding constraints are strict: we exclude dynamic evaluation, cache/pointer mechanisms, ensembles, and model averaging. We include fine-tuning when it is part of the reported single-model result, because the query only excludes dynamic evaluation and cache. When a paper reports multiple model sizes or variants, we select the lowest (best) perplexity that satisfies these conditions. The result is a comparative table and a detailed discussion of each paper’s contribution, contextualized with baselines and methodological notes.

## Methodology

We reviewed all provided source documents, including paper excerpts and third-party research notes. For each paper, we identified the proposed architecture or method and the corresponding PTB word-level test perplexity. We systematically excluded any result labeled as an ensemble, model average, dynamic evaluation, or cache/pointer augmentation. We retained single-model results even when they employed fine-tuning, dropout, weight tying, or other regularization techniques. Where a paper reported multiple single-model numbers, we selected the best (lowest) test perplexity. All numbers are word-level test perplexities on the Penn Treebank dataset, using the standard splits and preprocessing described in the sources.

## Detailed Findings by Paper

### Recurrent Neural Network Regularization (1409.2329)

The paper reports word-level PTB test perplexity for its proposed regularized LSTM models as single models. The medium regularized LSTM reaches 82.7, and the large regularized LSTM reaches 78.4 ([Recurrent Neural Network Regularization](https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.2329)). These are the paper’s own proposed models evaluated as single models. The paper also reports model averaging of regularized LSTMs, with 2 medium models at 77.0, 5 medium at 73.3, 10 medium at 72.0, 2 large at 73.6, 10 large at 69.5, and 38 large at 68.7. These ensemble results are excluded from our table because they are not single-model results. The paper lists single-model baselines: Pascanu et al. 2013 at 107.5, Cheng et al. at 100.0, and a non-regularized LSTM at 114.5. The proposed large regularized LSTM at 78.4 is lower (better) than all listed single-model baselines. No dynamic evaluation, cache/pointer, or fine-tuning settings are reported for the PTB perplexity results. Therefore, the best single-model result for this paper is 78.4.

### Character-Aware Neural Language Models (1508.06615)

The paper reports word-level PTB test perplexity for the proposed character-aware LSTM language model. LSTM-Char-Large attains 78.9, and LSTM-Char-Small attains 92.3 ([Character-Aware Neural Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/1508.06615)). Predictions are made at the word level, and these are single-model results. The paper explicitly excludes ensembles: “While lower perplexities have been reported with model ensembles [2012], we do not include them here as they are not comparable to the current work.” No dynamic evaluation, cache/pointer, or fine-tuning PTB results appear in the paper. The best single-model result is therefore 78.9.

### A Theoretically Grounded Application of Dropout in Recurrent Neural Networks (1512.05287)

The paper reports 73.4 test perplexity for the proposed Variational LSTM on Penn Treebank. The paper states test perplexity is reduced from 78.4 down to 73.4 with MC dropout and untied weights ([A Theoretically Grounded Application of Dropout in Recurrent Neural Networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.05287)). This 73.4 result is a single-model result for the large Variational LSTM with untied weights and MC dropout at test time. The paper states that to the best of its knowledge these are currently the best single-model perplexities on the Penn Treebank. The paper text does not report dynamic evaluation, cache/pointer, or fine-tuning for the PTB result. The table caption identifies the results as single-model perplexity on test and validation sets. The paper also reports an ensemble result: using 10 Variational LSTMs with MC dropout improves Zaremba et al.’s test set perplexity from 69.5 to 68.7, obtaining identical perplexity to Zaremba et al.’s experiment with 38 models. This ensemble is excluded. The best single-model result is 73.4.

### Recurrent Highway Networks (1607.03474)

The paper reports its best PTB word-level test perplexity for the proposed model as 65.4 for Variational RHN + WT. The paper also reports 68.5 test perplexity for Variational RHN without WT ([Recurrent Highway Networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/1607.03474)). The best 10-layer model with reduced weight decay improves to 67.9/65.4 validation/test perplexity. Table 1 lists Variational RHN + WT as 23M parameters with validation/test perplexity 67.9/65.4. This setting uses variational dropout and weight tying of input and output mappings. The paper reports the PTB word-level language modeling task results both with and without weight tying (WT) for fair comparisons. The best PTB result is a single model. Dynamic evaluation, cache/pointer, ensemble, and fine-tuning are not reported for the proposed model. Cache and pointer methods appear only as baselines, and ensembles are mentioned only in comparison. The paper states that RHNs outperform most single models as well as all previous ensembles. The best single-model result is 65.4.

### Using the Output Embedding to Improve Language Models (1608.05859)

The paper reports word-level PTB test perplexity for its proposed weight-tied neural network language models in single-model settings. The large NNLM with weight tying (Large + Weight Tying) reaches 74.3 test perplexity. The small NNLM with weight tying and projection regularization (Small + WT + PR) reaches 100.9 test perplexity ([Using the Output Embedding to Improve Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/1608.05859)). Both numbers come from the paper’s neural network language model experiments and are reported without dynamic evaluation, cache/pointer, ensemble, or fine-tuning. The paper’s comparison baselines include the large NNLM of Zaremba et al. (2014) at 78.4 test perplexity and the small NNLM at 114.5 test perplexity. The proposed large weight-tied model improves on the large baseline, and the proposed small weight-tied plus projection-regularized model improves on the small baseline. The paper also lists baseline models without dropout in its comparison table: KN 5-gram 141, RNN 123, LSTM 117, Stack RNN 110, FOFE-FNN 108, Noisy LSTM 108.0, and Deep RNN 107.5. The best single-model result is 74.3.

### Tying Word Vectors and Word Classifiers: A Loss Framework for Language Modeling (1611.01462)

The paper reports VD-RHN+RE (Zilly et al. 2016), trained with reused embeddings following the paper’s work, achieves validation perplexity 68.1 and test perplexity 66.0 on Penn Treebank. The paper describes this as best overall ([Tying Word Vectors and Word Classifiers: A Loss Framework for Language Modeling](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.01462)). The paper also reports smaller VD-LSTM variants in Table 3: VD-LSTM 148.6, VD-LSTM+AL 143.7, VD-LSTM+RE 140.9, and VD-LSTM+REAL 138.4 test perplexity. These are validation/test perplexities for small 200-unit models, and the best among them is 138.4. However, the paper’s best overall single-model result is 66.0 for VD-RHN+RE. The PTB baselines involving cache, pointer, or ensemble listed by the paper are RNN+LDA+KN-5+Cache with test perplexity 92.0, Pointer Sentinel-LSTM(medium) with 70.9, 38 Large LSTMs with 68.7, and 10 Large VD-LSTMs with 68.7. These are excluded from our single-model criteria. No dynamic evaluation is reported for the proposed model. The best single-model result is 66.0.

### Neural Architecture Search with Reinforcement Learning (1611.01578)

The paper reports 62.4 test perplexity on Penn Treebank for the best Neural Architecture Search model. Table 2 reports 64.0 and 67.9 for other Neural Architecture Search configurations ([Neural Architecture Search with Reinforcement Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.01578)). The 62.4 result is Neural Architecture Search with base 8 and shared embeddings, 54M parameters. The paper also reports Neural Architecture Search with base 8 at 67.9 with 32M parameters and Neural Architecture Search with base 8 and shared embeddings at 64.0 with 25M parameters. These numbers are single-model perplexity on the test set of the Penn Treebank language modeling task. The paper does not report dynamic evaluation, cache/pointer, ensemble, or fine-tuning for the Neural Architecture Search model on PTB. Cache/pointer appears only among baseline labels such as Pointer Sentinel-LSTM. The best listed baseline in the paper’s PTB comparison table is Zilly et al. 2016 – Variational RHN, shared embeddings, with 66.0 test perplexity and 24M parameters. The best Neural Architecture Search result, 62.4 test perplexity, is 3.6 perplexity better than the previous state-of-the-art baseline. The best single-model result is 62.4.

### Paper 1706.02222 (GRURNTN/LSTMRNTN)

The paper proposes GRURNTN and LSTMRNTN, described as a Gated Recurrent Unit Recurrent Neural Tensor Network and a Long-Short Term Memory Recurrent Neural Tensor Network. For word-level Penn Treebank language modeling, Table II reports test perplexity 87.38 for GRURNTN and 96.97 for LSTMRNTN ([Paper 1706.02222](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.02222)). These proposed results are reported as individual models. The paper states that baseline and proposed model experiments did not use dynamic evaluation. No cache/pointer, ensemble, or fine-tuning setting is reported for the proposed word-level results. The paper lists baseline and published word-level PTB test perplexities: GRURNN (97.78), LSTMRNN (108.26), N-Gram (141), RNNLM without dynamic evaluation (124.7), RNNLM with dynamic evaluation (123.2), SCRNN (115), sRNN (110.0), and DOT(S)-RNN (107.5). The baseline GRURNN and LSTMRNN are the paper’s own baselines. Both proposed models outperform their baselines. GRURNTN reduces perplexity from 97.78 to 87.38 over GRURNN, a 10.4 absolute and 10.63% relative reduction. LSTMRNTN reduces perplexity from 108.26 to 96.97 over LSTMRNN, an 11.29 absolute and 10.42% relative reduction. The paper states that GRURNTN outperforms all baseline models and the other listed models by a large margin. The best single-model result is 87.38.

### On the State of the Art of Evaluation in Neural Language Models (1707.05589)

The paper reports a best word-level test perplexity of 58.3 on Penn Treebank for its own proposed model, a 4-layer LSTM with 24M parameters. The authors state that at 24M, all depths obtain very similar results, reaching 58.3 at depth 4 ([On the State of the Art of Evaluation in Neural Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.05589)). This result is reported for a single model, without dynamic evaluation, cache/pointer, ensemble, or fine-tuning. The paper explicitly refrains from including techniques that are known to push perplexities even lower, because its aim is strictly to do better model comparisons for the architectures under study. The paper lists baseline test perplexities on PTB: Medium LSTM 82.7, Large LSTM 78.4, VD LSTM (Press & Wolf) 73.2, VD LSTM (Inan et al., 9M) 73.9, VD LSTM (Inan et al., 28M) 69.0, VD RHN 65.4, NAS (25M) 64.0, NAS (54M) 62.4, and AWD-LSTM 57.3. The paper’s own best model at 58.3 outperforms all listed baselines except AWD-LSTM at 57.3, which is a parallel work and slightly better. The best single-model result is 58.3.

### Regularizing and Optimizing LSTM Language Models (1708.02182)

The paper “Regularizing and Optimizing LSTM Language Models” proposes AWD-LSTM, an abbreviation for ASGD Weight-Dropped LSTM. The paper reports a word-level PTB test perplexity of 53.3 for AWD-LSTM, a 3-layer LSTM with tied weights, as a single model without cache/pointer ([Regularizing and Optimizing LSTM Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.02182)). It also reports 65.8 on WikiText-2. This result includes the fine-tuning step. The Table 1 caption states “Single model perplexity on validation and test sets for the Penn Treebank language modeling task.” Table 4 lists the no-fine-tuning ablation at 58.8 test perplexity on PTB, and the paper states that removal of the fine-tuning step degrades performance. For the proposed model augmented with a continuous cache pointer, the paper reports an even lower PTB test perplexity of 52.8. However, this uses a cache pointer, which is excluded by the query. The paper states that its vanilla LSTM improves the state of the art by approximately 1 unit on PTB and 0.1 units on WikiText-2. In Table 1, AWD-LSTM’s 53.3 beats the best listed baseline, Melis et al. 2017 4-layer skip connection LSTM (tied) at 58.3. With the continuous cache pointer, the 52.8 PTB test perplexity is even lower, but excluded. The best single-model result without cache is 53.3.

### Breaking the Softmax Bottleneck: A High-Rank RNN Language Model (1711.03953)

The paper reports word-level PTB test perplexity for its proposed model, AWD-LSTM-MoS, in single-model settings without dynamic evaluation. Table 1 lists 55.97 for AWD-LSTM-MoS without finetune and 54.44 for AWD-LSTM-MoS with finetune ([Breaking the Softmax Bottleneck: A High-Rank RNN Language Model](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.03953)). With dynamic evaluation, the paper reports 47.69 for AWD-LSTM-MoS + dynamic evaluation. The paper does not report results for its proposed model on PTB using cache/pointer or ensemble methods; it reports results with and without dynamic evaluation and finetuning. Since the query excludes dynamic evaluation and cache, the best single-model result is the fine-tuned AWD-LSTM-MoS at 54.44. The no-finetune result at 55.97 is slightly worse. Therefore, the best single-model result without dynamic evaluation is 54.44.

## Discussion

The compiled results reveal a clear trajectory of improvement in single-model PTB word-level perplexity over time. The earliest proposed models in this set, Recurrent Neural Network Regularization and Character-Aware Neural Language Models, achieve 78.4 and 78.9, respectively. Variational dropout pushes the number to 73.4, and weight tying in output embeddings reaches 74.3. Recurrent Highway Networks with variational dropout and weight tying achieve 65.4, while the loss framework of Inan et al. reaches 66.0 using reused embeddings on a variational RHN. Neural Architecture Search produces 62.4, and the optimized 4-layer LSTM of Melis et al. reaches 58.3. The lowest perplexities in this compilation come from AWD-LSTM (53.3) and AWD-LSTM-MoS with fine-tuning (54.44), both of which employ sophisticated regularization and optimization. The GRURNTN model at 87.38 is the highest among the proposed models, reflecting an earlier or different architectural approach.

It is important to note that these numbers are not directly comparable in a strict experimental sense. Papers use different model sizes, preprocessing, training regimes, and evaluation protocols. Some results include fine-tuning, while others do not. Some papers report validation and test perplexities together, and we have extracted the test set numbers. We have excluded ensemble results, which are often substantially lower—for example, Zaremba et al.’s 38 large regularized LSTMs reach 68.7, and Gal and Ghahramani’s 10 Variational LSTMs reach 68.7. We have also excluded dynamic evaluation, which can reduce perplexity dramatically, as seen in AWD-LSTM-MoS (47.69) and AWD-LSTM with continuous cache pointer (52.8). The table therefore represents the best single-model, no-dynamic-evaluation, no-cache results as reported by each paper.

A further caveat is that some papers report multiple single-model variants. For Tying Word Vectors and Word Classifiers, we selected the VD-RHN+RE result at 66.0, which the paper describes as best overall, rather than the smaller VD-LSTM+REAL at 138.4. For Regularizing and Optimizing LSTM Language Models, we selected 53.3, which includes fine-tuning, rather than the no-fine-tuning ablation at 58.8. For Breaking the Softmax Bottleneck, we selected 54.44 with finetuning, rather than 55.97 without finetuning. These choices align with the query’s request for the best single-model result without dynamic evaluation or cache.

## Conclusion

This report has compiled the best single-model word-level Penn Treebank test perplexities for each provided paper’s proposed model, excluding dynamic evaluation, cache/pointer, ensembles, and model averaging. The results range from 53.3 for AWD-LSTM to 87.38 for GRURNTN. The lowest perplexities are achieved by models that combine advanced regularization, optimization, and architectural innovations, such as AWD-LSTM, AWD-LSTM-MoS, and the 4-layer LSTM of Melis et al. The table and detailed sections provide a reliable reference for comparing these reported results under the specified constraints.
