# Word-Level Penn Treebank Test Perplexity for Proposed Single Models: A Structured Review of Provided Papers

## Introduction

The Penn Treebank (PTB) word-level language modeling benchmark has served as a central evaluation task for neural language models for more than a decade. Reported PTB test perplexities, however, are not directly comparable unless one accounts for evaluation protocol, model size, regularization, weight tying, fine-tuning, ensembling, dynamic evaluation, and cache or pointer mechanisms. The present report addresses a specific extraction task: for each provided paper, identify the word-level PTB test perplexity that the paper reports for its own proposed model using the best single-model result without dynamic evaluation or cache. This criterion excludes model averaging, ensembles, continuous cache pointers, and dynamic evaluation. It does not exclude fine-tuning, weight tying, variational dropout, or other single-model regularization techniques unless those techniques are themselves cache-like or dynamic. The report is based exclusively on the supplied source notes and paper excerpts, and all figures are cited to those sources.

## Selection Criteria and Method

The inclusion criteria were applied as follows. First, only results for the paper’s own proposed model were considered. Baseline results from other papers were excluded unless the paper explicitly trained or adapted a model as part of its own proposed framework. Second, only single-model results were included. Results labeled as ensembles, model averaging, or multi-model combinations were excluded. Third, results using dynamic evaluation were excluded. Fourth, results using cache or pointer mechanisms were excluded. Fifth, fine-tuning was allowed because the query does not exclude it; where a paper reports both fine-tuned and non-fine-tuned results, the best single-model result without dynamic evaluation or cache is reported, with the non-fine-tuned number noted where relevant. Applying these criteria yields the table below.

## Detailed Findings by Paper

### Recurrent Neural Network Regularization (1409.2329)

The paper reports two single-model regularized LSTM results: a medium regularized LSTM at 82.7 and a large regularized LSTM at 78.4 ([Source: 1409.2329_note.txt](1409.2329_note.txt)). The large regularized LSTM is therefore the best single-model result without dynamic evaluation or cache. The paper also reports model averaging results of 77.0 for 2 medium regularized LSTMs, 73.3 for 5 medium regularized LSTMs, 72.0 for 10 medium regularized LSTMs, 73.6 for 2 large regularized LSTMs, 69.5 for 10 large regularized LSTMs, and 68.7 for 38 large regularized LSTMs ([Source: 1409.2329_note.txt](1409.2329_note.txt)). These averaged results are lower, but they are excluded by the single-model criterion. The paper lists single-model baselines of Pascanu et al. 2013 at 107.5, Cheng et al. at 100.0, and a non-regularized LSTM at 114.5; the proposed large regularized LSTM at 78.4 is lower, and therefore better, than all listed single-model baselines ([Source: 1409.2329_note.txt](1409.2329_note.txt)).

### Character-Aware Neural Language Models (1508.06615)

The proposed character-aware LSTM language model reports LSTM-Char-Large at 78.9 and LSTM-Char-Small at 92.3, with predictions made at the word level ([Source: 1508.06615_note.txt](1508.06615_note.txt)). These are single-model results. The paper explicitly excludes ensembles, stating that while lower perplexities have been reported with model ensembles, they are not included because they are not comparable to the current work ([Source: 1508.06615_note.txt](1508.06615_note.txt)). The paper also does not report dynamic evaluation, cache/pointer, or fine-tuning PTB results ([Source: 1508.06615_note.txt](1508.06615_note.txt)). The best single-model result under the query’s criteria is therefore 78.9.

### Variational Dropout and the Local Reparameterization Trick (1512.05287)

The paper reports 73.4 test perplexity for the proposed Variational LSTM on Penn Treebank ([Source: 1512.05287_note.txt](1512.05287_note.txt)). The paper states that test perplexity is reduced from 78.4 down to 73.4 with MC dropout and untied weights, and this 73.4 result is a single-model result for the large Variational LSTM with untied weights and MC dropout at test time ([Source: 1512.05287_note.txt](1512.05287_note.txt)). The paper also reports an ensemble result: using 10 Variational LSTMs with MC dropout improves Zaremba et al.’s test set perplexity from 69.5 to 68.7 ([Source: 1512.05287_note.txt](1512.05287_note.txt)). That ensemble result is excluded. The paper’s table excerpt also shows the large Variational LSTM test result at 73.4 ± 0.0 ([Source: 1512.05287.txt](1512.05287.txt)). The best single-model result under the query’s criteria is therefore 73.4.

### Recurrent Highway Networks (1607.03474)

The paper reports its best PTB word-level test perplexity for the proposed model as 65.4 for Variational RHN + WT ([Source: 1607.03474_note.txt](1607.03474_note.txt)). It also reports 68.5 test perplexity for Variational RHN without WT ([Source: 1607.03474_note.txt](1607.03474_note.txt)). The best 10-layer model with reduced weight decay improves to 67.9 validation and 65.4 test perplexity ([Source: 1607.03474_note.txt](1607.03474_note.txt)). Table 1 lists Variational RHN + WT as 23M parameters with validation/test perplexity 67.9/65.4 ([Source: 1607.03474_note.txt](1607.03474_note.txt)). This setting uses variational dropout and weight tying of input and output mappings. The paper reports no dynamic evaluation, cache/pointer, ensemble, or fine-tuning for the proposed model; cache and pointer methods appear only as baselines, and ensembles are mentioned only in comparison ([Source: 1607.03474_note.txt](1607.03474_note.txt)). The best single-model result without dynamic evaluation or cache is therefore 65.4.

### Using the Output Embedding to Improve Language Models (1608.05859)

The paper reports word-level PTB test perplexity for its proposed weight-tied neural network language models in single-model settings ([Source: 1608.05859_note.txt](1608.05859_note.txt)). The large NNLM with weight tying reaches 74.3 test perplexity, while the small NNLM with weight tying and projection regularization reaches 100.9 ([Source: 1608.05859_note.txt](1608.05859_note.txt)). Both numbers come from the paper’s neural network language model experiments and are reported without dynamic evaluation, cache/pointer, ensemble, or fine-tuning ([Source: 1608.05859_note.txt](1608.05859_note.txt)). The paper also lists baseline models without dropout, including KN 5-gram at 141, RNN at 123, LSTM at 117, Stack RNN at 110, FOFE-FNN at 108, Noisy LSTM at 108.0, and Deep RNN at 107.5 ([Source: 1608.05859_note.txt](1608.05859_note.txt)). The proposed large weight-tied model improves on the large NNLM baseline of 78.4, while the proposed small weight-tied plus projection-regularized model improves on the small NNLM baseline of 114.5 ([Source: 1608.05859_note.txt](1608.05859_note.txt)). The best single-model result under the query’s criteria is 74.3.

### Tying Word Vectors and Word Classifiers: A Loss Framework for Language Modeling (1611.01462)

The paper reports VD-RHN+RE, a variational RHN trained with reused embeddings following the paper’s framework, achieving validation perplexity 68.1 and test perplexity 66.0 on Penn Treebank ([Source: 1611.01462_note.txt](1611.01462_note.txt)). The paper describes this as best overall ([Source: 1611.01462_note.txt](1611.01462_note.txt)). The Penn Treebank baselines involving cache, pointer, or ensemble listed by the paper are RNN+LDA+KN-5+Cache at 92.0, Pointer Sentinel-LSTM (medium) at 70.9, 38 Large LSTMs at 68.7, and 10 Large VD-LSTMs at 68.7 ([Source: 1611.01462_note.txt](1611.01462_note.txt)). Those are not the paper’s own proposed single-model result and are excluded. The paper also includes smaller VD-LSTM variants in its table, such as VD-LSTM at 148.0 test, VD-LSTM+AL at 142.5 test, VD-LSTM+RE at 141.9 test, and VD-LSTM+REAL at 140.6 test ([Source: 1611.01462.txt](1611.01462.txt)). These are far higher perplexities than the best reported framework result. A later comparison table lists Inan et al. 2016 Variational LSTM (tied) + augmented loss at 73.2 test for 24M parameters and 68.5 test for 51M parameters ([Source: 1708.02182.txt](1708.02182.txt)). If one restricts attention strictly to the paper’s LSTM variants, the best number would be 68.5; however, the paper’s own note identifies 66.0 as the best overall result from its framework. Under the query’s criteria, the most defensible extraction is 66.0, with the caveat that this result uses an RHN backbone rather than a pure LSTM.

### Neural Architecture Search with Reinforcement Learning (1611.01578)

The paper reports 62.4 test perplexity on Penn Treebank for the best Neural Architecture Search model ([Source: 1611.01578_note.txt](1611.01578_note.txt)). Table 2 also reports 64.0 and 67.9 for other Neural Architecture Search configurations ([Source: 1611.01578_note.txt](1611.01578_note.txt)). The 62.4 result is Neural Architecture Search with base 8 and shared embeddings, 54M parameters ([Source: 1611.01578_note.txt](1611.01578_note.txt)). The paper reports no dynamic evaluation, cache/pointer, ensemble, or fine-tuning for the Neural Architecture Search model on Penn Treebank; cache/pointer appears only among baseline labels such as Pointer Sentinel-LSTM ([Source: 1611.01578_note.txt](1611.01578_note.txt)). The best listed baseline in the paper’s comparison table is Zilly et al. 2016 Variational RHN with shared embeddings at 66.0 test perplexity and 24M parameters ([Source: 1611.01578_note.txt](1611.01578_note.txt)). The 62.4 result is 3.6 perplexity better than that baseline ([Source: 1611.01578_note.txt](1611.01578_note.txt)). The best single-model result under the query’s criteria is therefore 62.4.

### Gated Recurrent Neural Tensor Network (1706.02222)

The paper proposes GRURNTN and LSTMRNTN, described as a Gated Recurrent Unit Recurrent Neural Tensor Network and a Long-Short Term Memory Recurrent Neural Tensor Network ([Source: 1706.02222_note.txt](1706.02222_note.txt)). For word-level Penn Treebank language modeling, Table II reports test perplexity 87.38 for GRURNTN and 96.97 for LSTMRNTN ([Source: 1706.02222_note.txt](1706.02222_note.txt)). These proposed results are reported as individual models, and the paper states that baseline and proposed model experiments did not use dynamic evaluation ([Source: 1706.02222_note.txt](1706.02222_note.txt)). No cache/pointer, ensemble, or fine-tuning setting is reported for the proposed word-level results ([Source: 1706.02222_note.txt](1706.02222_note.txt)). The paper lists baseline and published word-level PTB test perplexities including GRURNN at 97.78, LSTMRNN at 108.26, N-Gram at 141, RNNLM without dynamic evaluation at 124.7, RNNLM with dynamic evaluation at 123.2, SCRNN at 115, sRNN at 110.0, and DOT(S)-RNN at 107.5 ([Source: 1706.02222_note.txt](1706.02222_note.txt)). The best proposed single-model result under the query’s criteria is 87.38 for GRURNTN.

### On the State of the Art of Evaluation in Neural Language Models (1707.05589)

The paper reports a best word-level test perplexity of 58.3 on Penn Treebank for its own proposed model, a 4-layer LSTM with 24M parameters ([Source: 1707.05589_note.txt](1707.05589_note.txt)). The authors state that at 24M parameters, all depths obtain very similar results, reaching 58.3 at depth 4 ([Source: 1707.05589_note.txt](1707.05589_note.txt)). This result is reported for a single model without dynamic evaluation, cache/pointer, ensemble, or fine-tuning ([Source: 1707.05589_note.txt](1707.05589_note.txt)). The paper explicitly refrains from including techniques that are known to push perplexities even lower because its aim is strictly to do better model comparisons for the architectures under study ([Source: 1707.05589_note.txt](1707.05589_note.txt)). The best single-model result under the query’s criteria is therefore 58.3.

### Regularizing and Optimizing LSTM Language Models (1708.02182)

The paper proposes AWD-LSTM, an abbreviation for ASGD Weight-Dropped LSTM ([Source: 1708.02182_note.txt](1708.02182_note.txt)). The paper reports a word-level Penn Treebank test perplexity of 57.3 for AWD-LSTM, a 3-layer LSTM with tied weights, as a single model without cache/pointer ([Source: 1708.02182.txt](1708.02182.txt)). The abstract states that the paper achieves state-of-the-art word-level perplexities of 57.3 on Penn Treebank and 65.8 on WikiText-2 ([Source: 1708.02182.txt](1708.02182.txt)). Table 1 is captioned as single-model perplexity on validation and test sets for the Penn Treebank language modeling task, and it lists AWD-LSTM at 60.0 validation and 57.3 test ([Source: 1708.02182.txt](1708.02182.txt)). The paper also reports a continuous cache pointer variant that reaches 52.8 on Penn Treebank, but that result uses a cache mechanism and is excluded ([Source: 1708.02182_note.txt](1708.02182_note.txt)). One third-party note states that the paper reports 53.3 for AWD-LSTM, but the paper’s abstract and Table 1 both report 57.3; the 53.3 figure appears to be an error or a conflation with another result ([Source: 1708.02182_note.txt](1708.02182_note.txt); [Source: 1708.02182.txt](1708.02182.txt)). Table 4 lists the no-fine-tuning ablation at 58.8 test perplexity on Penn Treebank, and the paper states that removal of the fine-tuning step degrades performance ([Source: 1708.02182_note.txt](1708.02182_note.txt)). Because the query does not exclude fine-tuning, the best single-model result without dynamic evaluation or cache is 57.3.

### Breaking the Softmax Bottleneck: A High-Rank RNN Language Model (1711.03953)

The paper reports word-level PTB test perplexity for its proposed model, AWD-LSTM-MoS, in single-model settings without dynamic evaluation ([Source: 1711.03953_note.txt](1711.03953_note.txt)). Table 1 lists 55.97 for AWD-LSTM-MoS without fine-tuning and 54.44 for AWD-LSTM-MoS with fine-tuning ([Source: 1711.03953_note.txt](1711.03953_note.txt)). With dynamic evaluation, the paper reports 47.69 for AWD-LSTM-MoS + dynamic evaluation ([Source: 1711.03953_note.txt](1711.03953_note.txt)). The paper does not report results for its proposed model on Penn Treebank using cache/pointer or ensemble methods ([Source: 1711.03953_note.txt](1711.03953_note.txt)). Because dynamic evaluation is excluded but fine-tuning is not excluded by the query, the best single-model result under the stated criteria is 54.44. If fine-tuning were also excluded, the relevant number would be 55.97.

## Comparative Analysis

The extracted single-model PTB test perplexities range from 54.44 to 87.38 across the provided papers. Ranked from best to worst under the query’s criteria, the results are: AWD-LSTM-MoS with fine-tuning at 54.44 ([Source: 1711.03953_note.txt](1711.03953_note.txt)); AWD-LSTM at 57.3 ([Source: 1708.02182.txt](1708.02182.txt)); the 4-layer LSTM from Melis et al. at 58.3 ([Source: 1707.05589_note.txt](1707.05589_note.txt)); NAS with base 8 and shared embeddings at 62.4 ([Source: 1611.01578_note.txt](1611.01578_note.txt)); Variational RHN + WT at 65.4 ([Source: 1607.03474_note.txt](1607.03474_note.txt)); VD-RHN+RE at 66.0 ([Source: 1611.01462_note.txt](1611.01462_note.txt)); the large Variational LSTM at 73.4 ([Source: 1512.05287_note.txt](1512.05287_note.txt)); the large NNLM with weight tying at 74.3 ([Source: 1608.05859_note.txt](1608.05859_note.txt)); the large regularized LSTM at 78.4 ([Source: 1409.2329_note.txt](1409.2329_note.txt)); LSTM-Char-Large at 78.9 ([Source: 1508.06615_note.txt](1508.06615_note.txt)); and GRURNTN at 87.38 ([Source: 1706.02222_note.txt](1706.02222_note.txt)).

This ranking should not be interpreted as a strict historical progression, because the papers differ in model size, vocabulary, preprocessing, regularization, training duration, and whether fine-tuning or weight tying is used. For example, the 57.3 result for AWD-LSTM includes fine-tuning, and the paper’s no-fine-tuning ablation is 58.8 ([Source: 1708.02182_note.txt](1708.02182_note.txt)). Similarly, the 54.44 result for AWD-LSTM-MoS includes fine-tuning, while the non-fine-tuned result is 55.97 ([Source: 1711.03953_note.txt](1711.03953_note.txt)). The 66.0 result from the tying framework uses an RHN backbone rather than a pure LSTM, which further complicates direct architectural comparison ([Source: 1611.01462_note.txt](1611.01462_note.txt)). The 62.4 NAS result uses a searched architecture with shared embeddings and 54M parameters, whereas the 65.4 RHN result uses 23M parameters and weight tying ([Source: 1611.01578_note.txt](1611.01578_note.txt); [Source: 1607.03474_note.txt](1607.03474_note.txt)). These differences mean that the table is an extraction of reported numbers under a consistent exclusion rule, not a controlled benchmark comparison.

The most notable discrepancy in the provided information concerns the AWD-LSTM result. One note states that the paper reports 53.3 for AWD-LSTM as a single model without cache/pointer ([Source: 1708.02182_note.txt](1708.02182_note.txt)). However, the paper’s abstract states 57.3 on Penn Treebank, and Table 1 lists AWD-LSTM at 57.3 test perplexity ([Source: 1708.02182.txt](1708.02182.txt)). The 52.8 figure belongs to the continuous cache pointer variant, and 53.9 is the corresponding validation perplexity ([Source: 1708.02182.txt](1708.02182.txt)). The 53.3 note therefore appears to be a typographical or extraction error. For the present report, 57.3 is used as the paper’s own proposed single-model result without dynamic evaluation or cache.

Another important caveat concerns the 1611.01462 paper. The note identifies VD-RHN+RE at 66.0 as the paper’s best overall result, but this model uses a recurrent highway network backbone trained with the paper’s reused-embeddings framework ([Source: 1611.01462_note.txt](1611.01462_note.txt)). If one restricts the extraction to the paper’s LSTM-based proposed models, the best reported number is 68.5 for a 51M Variational LSTM (tied) + augmented loss, as listed in a later comparison table ([Source: 1708.02182.txt](1708.02182.txt)). The table above uses 66.0 because the query asks for the paper’s own proposed model and the note explicitly describes 66.0 as the paper’s best overall result.

## Conclusion

Applying the query’s criteria—own proposed model, single model, no dynamic evaluation, no cache/pointer—produces the following extracted PTB word-level test perplexities: 78.4 for the large regularized LSTM ([Source: 1409.2329_note.txt](1409.2329_note.txt)); 78.9 for LSTM-Char-Large ([Source: 1508.06615_note.txt](1508.06615_note.txt)); 73.4 for the large Variational LSTM with untied weights and MC dropout ([Source: 1512.05287_note.txt](1512.05287_note.txt)); 65.4 for Variational RHN + WT ([Source: 1607.03474_note.txt](1607.03474_note.txt)); 74.3 for the large NNLM with weight tying ([Source: 1608.05859_note.txt](1608.05859_note.txt)); 66.0 for VD-RHN+RE as the tying framework’s best overall result ([Source: 1611.01462_note.txt](1611.01462_note.txt)); 62.4 for NAS with base 8 and shared embeddings ([Source: 1611.01578_note.txt](1611.01578_note.txt)); 87.38 for GRURNTN ([Source: 1706.02222_note.txt](1706.02222_note.txt)); 58.3 for the 4-layer LSTM at 24M parameters ([Source: 1707.05589_note.txt](1707.05589_note.txt)); 57.3 for AWD-LSTM ([Source: 1708.02182.txt](1708.02182.txt)); and 54.44 for AWD-LSTM-MoS with fine-tuning ([Source: 1711.03953_note.txt](1711.03953_note.txt)). The lowest, and therefore best, single-model result under the stated criteria among the provided papers is 54.44 from AWD-LSTM-MoS with fine-tuning. The highest, and therefore worst, is 87.38 from GRURNTN. These numbers should be read with the caveats noted above, particularly regarding fine-tuning, weight tying, backbone architecture, and the discrepancy in the AWD-LSTM note.
