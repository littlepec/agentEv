# Hand-Crafted Templates in Question Generation from a Knowledge Base: A Detailed Analysis

## Introduction

Question generation from a knowledge base (KB) is the task of generating questions related to the domain of the input KB ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). A proposed system for this task generates fluent and natural questions from a KB while significantly reducing human effort by leveraging massive web resources ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). The only human labor in this work is the construction of question templates ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). This report addresses the query: "How many hand-crafted templates did they have to make?" To answer this, we examine the reported figures across two evaluations: one on 500 randomly selected triples from Freebase, and one on an in-house power tool domain KB. We also identify and resolve a discrepancy between the primary source paper and a third-party research note.

## The Role of Hand-Crafted Templates

The system's workflow begins with a small set of question templates that are hand-crafted based on the predicates in the KB ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). Each template is associated with a predicate and consists of a transcription of the predicate in the KB (e.g., performsActivity ⇒ how to) and placeholders for the subject (#X#) and the object (#Y#) ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). A seed question set is then generated by applying the templates on the KB ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). For example, taking an in-house KB of the power tool domain, the template "how to use #X#" is first constructed for the predicate "performsActivity" ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). The seed question "how to use jigsaw" is then generated by applying the template on the triple ⟨jigsaw, performsActivity, CurveCut⟩ ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). After seed generation, more questions are retrieved by iteratively forming already obtained questions as search queries into a standard search engine, before finally questions are selected by estimating their fluency and domain relevance ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). The paper states that the only human labor in this work is question template construction ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). Therefore, the number of templates directly measures the human effort required.

## Template Counts for the Freebase Evaluation

In the first evaluation, the system was compared on 500 randomly selected triples from Freebase ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). For these 500 triples, the authors hand-crafted 106 templates ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). The triples shared only 53 distinct predicates, so the authors made 2 templates for each predicate on average ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). Applying the templates to the triples generated 991 seed questions ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). The experiment also retrieved 1529 more questions from Google ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). To evaluate fluency, a 4-gram language model was trained on Gigaword with Kneser-Ney smoothing, and the top 500 questions were selected to compare with prior results ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). Three native English speakers evaluated fluency and naturalness on a 4-point scheme ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). Table 1 summarizes these figures.

### Table 1: Freebase Evaluation Template and Question Counts

| Metric | Count | Source |
|--------|-------|--------|
| Randomly selected triples | 500 | document_1.txt |
| Distinct predicates | 53 | document_1.txt |
| Hand-crafted templates | 106 | document_1.txt |
| Average templates per predicate | 2 | document_1.txt |
| Seed questions generated | 991 | document_1.txt |

The third-party research note confirms these figures: "For the Freebase evaluation, the work uses 500 randomly selected triples. The paper reports 106 hand-crafted templates for those 500 Freebase triples. The triples share only 53 distinct predicates, so the authors made 2 templates for each predicate on average. Applying the templates to the triples generated 991 seed questions. The experiment also retrieved 1529 more questions from Google" ([document_2.txt](document_2.txt)). Thus, for the Freebase evaluation, the answer is unambiguously 106 hand-crafted templates.

## Template Counts for the In-House Power Tool Domain

The second evaluation used an in-house KB in the power tool domain ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). This KB contains 67 distinct predicates, 293 distinct subjects, and 279 distinct objects ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). For the 67 predicates, the primary source states that the authors "hand-craft 163 templates" ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). The system generated 12,228 seed questions from which 20,000 more questions were expanded with Google ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). The paper provides examples of expanded questions such as "how to change circular saw blade" and "how to cut a groove in wood without a router" ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). The same language model as in the first experiment was used, along with a skip-gram model on Wikipedia for evaluating domain relevance ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)).

However, the third-party research note reports a different number. It states: "For the in-house power tool domain knowledge base, the paper reports 67 predicates. For the 67 predicates, the authors hand-crafted 103 templates. Together with the 106 Freebase templates, the authors wrote 209 templates in total. The paper reports this total in the template-construction experiment" ([document_2.txt](document_2.txt)) [document_1.txt states: 163 templates for the power tool domain]. This creates a discrepancy: 163 versus 103 for the power tool domain. Table 2 compares the two sources.

### Table 2: In-House Power Tool Domain Template Counts Across Sources

| Source | Predicates | Hand-crafted templates | Total with Freebase |
|--------|------------|------------------------|---------------------|
| document_1.txt (primary) | 67 | 163 | 269 (106 + 163) |
| document_2.txt (third-party note) | 67 | 103 | 209 (106 + 103) |

The third-party note explicitly claims that the paper reports a total of 209 templates ([document_2.txt](document_2.txt)); document_1.txt does not state a total, reporting 106 for Freebase and 163 for the power tool domain separately. Yet the primary source does not state a total; it presents 106 for Freebase and 163 for the power tool domain separately ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). This suggests that the third-party note may contain a transcription error or may be based on a different version of the paper. As an analyst, I must weigh the reliability of sources. The primary source, document_1.txt, contains the actual paper text, including the specific sentence "For the 67 predicates, we hand-craft 163 templates" ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). The third-party note, document_2.txt, is a research note that summarizes the paper but provides an inconsistent figure. Therefore, the more reliable figure for the power tool domain is 163 templates, and the total across both evaluations is 269 templates ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)).

## Total Number of Hand-Crafted Templates

The query asks: "How many hand-crafted templates did they have to make?" This can be interpreted as the total number of templates constructed across all evaluations. Based on the primary source, the total is 269 templates: 106 for the Freebase evaluation and 163 for the power tool domain ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). If one relies on the third-party note, the total would be 209 templates ([document_2.txt](document_2.txt)). However, because the primary source is more authoritative and internally consistent with its own reported predicate counts and template descriptions, the best answer is 269. It is important to note that the Freebase experiment alone required 106 templates, which is likely the number the query seeks if it refers to the first evaluation. The paper's abstract and introduction emphasize the Freebase evaluation when comparing to prior work ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). The abstract states: "Evaluated by human graders on 500 random-selected triples from Freebase, questions generated by our system are judged to be more fluent than those of ? ) by human graders" ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). The human labor in that evaluation was the 106 templates.

## Why the Template Count is Relatively Low

The paper argues that the system does not require a large number of templates because: (1) the iterative question expansion can produce a large number of questions even with a relatively small number of seed questions, as seen in the experiments, and (2) multiple entities in the KB share the same predicates ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). For example, in the Freebase evaluation, 106 templates covered 500 triples and 53 predicates, generating 991 seed questions and retrieving 1529 more ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). In the power tool domain, 163 templates (or 103 according to the third-party note) covered 67 predicates, generating 12,228 seed questions and 20,000 expanded questions ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). This demonstrates the leverage of templates: a modest manual investment enables a large-scale question generation process. The only human labor is template construction, making the template count a direct measure of human effort ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). Another advantage is that the system can easily generate updated questions as the web is self-updating consistently ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)).

The question expansion method, described in Algorithm 1, initializes the expanded question set E as the seed question set ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). In each iteration, an already-obtained question is expanded from the web, and the retrieved questions are added to E if E does not contain them ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). The maximum number of iterations is limited by I_max ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). This iterative process allows a small seed set to grow into a much larger question pool, further justifying the low template count.

## Methodological Considerations and Source Reliability

When reconciling conflicting numbers, it is essential to prioritize the primary source. document_1.txt is the original paper text, providing detailed experimental sections. document_2.txt is a third-party research note that summarizes the paper and explicitly cites document_1.txt. The note states: "These figures show the manual template-building effort scales with predicate coverage in each evaluation. Source: document_1.txt" ([document_2.txt](document_2.txt)). Despite citing the primary source, the note reports 103 templates for the power tool domain, which contradicts the primary source's 163. This could be a typographical error (e.g., transposing 1 and 0, or misreading 163 as 103). Given that the primary source is the ultimate authority, I conclude that the correct count for the power tool domain is 163. Therefore, the total number of hand-crafted templates is 269. If the query is limited to the Freebase evaluation, the answer is 106.

## Conclusion

The number of hand-crafted templates depends on the evaluation. For the Freebase evaluation on 500 triples, the authors made 106 templates ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). For the in-house power tool domain with 67 predicates, the primary source reports 163 templates ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)), while a third-party note reports 103 templates ([document_2.txt](document_2.txt)). The most reliable total, based on the primary source, is 269 templates (106 + 163) ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). If the third-party note is used, the total is 209 templates (106 + 103). Given the principle of prioritizing primary sources, the answer to "How many hand-crafted templates did they have to make?" is 106 for the Freebase experiment alone, or 269 across both evaluations. This conclusion is grounded in the provided data and acknowledges the discrepancy transparently. The relatively low template count, despite covering hundreds of triples and dozens of predicates, underscores the system's efficiency in reducing human effort through web-based iterative expansion and selection.
