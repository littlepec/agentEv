# How Many Hand-Crafted Templates Were Required? An Evidence-Based Assessment of Template Construction in *Question Generation from a Knowledge Base with Web Exploration*

## Executive Summary

The question of how many hand-crafted question templates were required by the system described in *Question Generation from a Knowledge Base with Web Exploration* has a precise, though partly conflicting, answer depending on which figure in the available sources is treated as authoritative. The primary source document reports **106 hand-crafted templates** for the Freebase evaluation, which covered 500 randomly selected triples sharing only 53 distinct predicates ([Song & Zhao, n.d.-a](document_1.txt)). The same primary source reports **163 hand-crafted templates** for the in-house power tool domain knowledge base, which contained 67 distinct predicates ([Song & Zhao, n.d.-a](document_1.txt)). A third-party research note, however, reports the power tool figure as **103 templates** and derives an aggregate total of **209 templates** across both evaluations ([Third-Party Research Note, n.d.-b](document_2.txt)).

Consequently, the answer to the query depends on the source selected. If the primary paper text is accepted, the two reported template sets sum to **269 templates** (106 + 163). If the third-party note is accepted, the total is **209 templates** (106 + 103). This report presents both figures, examines the methodological logic behind each, and explains why the discrepancy matters for interpreting the system's claim of substantially reduced human effort.

## Background: Why Template Construction Is the Central Human Cost

The system under discussion answers a specific problem: generating fluent, natural, and domain-relevant questions from a knowledge base (KB) rather than from raw text ([Song & Zhao, n.d.-a](document_1.txt)). A KB is described as a directed graph in which nodes are entities (such as "jigsaw" and "CurveCut") and edges are relations (such as "performsActivity"), or equivalently, as a list of triples in the format ⟨subject, predicate, object⟩ ([Song & Zhao, n.d.-a](document_1.txt)). Because function words and morphological forms for entities are abstracted away when a KB is created, generating natural-language questions from triples is not straightforward ([Song & Zhao, n.d.-a](document_1.txt)).

Prior work addressed this challenge by treating question generation as a machine translation problem and training a neural machine translation (NMT) system on approximately 10,000 human-generated ⟨triple, question⟩ pairs, which required a large amount of human effort ([Song & Zhao, n.d.-a](document_1.txt)). The system examined here deliberately avoids that expense. It leverages massive web resources instead, and the paper states explicitly that "the only human labor in this work is the question template construction" ([Song & Zhao, n.d.-a](document_1.txt)).

This statement is the reason the template count is such a consequential number. If template construction is the sole manual component, then the number of templates is, in effect, the total human annotation burden of the entire system. Every figure about system efficiency, scalability, and reduced human effort ultimately rests on how many templates a human had to write by hand.

## The Predicate-Anchored Template Design

The system's template construction is tightly coupled to the predicate structure of the knowledge base rather than to the number of triples. Given an input KB, a small set of question templates is first hand-crafted based on the predicates in that KB ([Song & Zhao, n.d.-a](document_1.txt)). Each template consists of a transcription of the predicate in the KB — for example, "performsActivity ⇒ how to" — together with placeholders for the subject (#X#) and the object (#Y#) ([Song & Zhao, n.d.-a](document_1.txt)). A concrete illustration is provided from the power tool domain: the template "how to use #X#" is first constructed for the predicate "performsActivity," and the seed question "how to use jigsaw" is then generated by applying that template to the triple ⟨jigsaw, performsActivity, CurveCut⟩ ([Song & Zhao, n.d.-a](document_1.txt)).

Three design consequences follow directly from this predicate anchoring:

1. **Template creation follows the predicates present in the KB**, so the manual effort scales with predicate coverage rather than with the number of triples ([Third-Party Research Note, n.d.-b](document_2.txt)).
2. **Multiple entities in the KB share the same predicates**, so one template can serve many triples ([Song & Zhao, n.d.-a](document_1.txt)).
3. **The iterative question expansion can produce a large number of questions even with a relatively small number of seed questions**, as the experiments reportedly demonstrate ([Song & Zhao, n.d.-a](document_1.txt)).

Shown in Figure 1, the system contains the sub-modules of question template construction, seed question generation, question expansion, and selection ([Song & Zhao, n.d.-a](document_1.txt)). The template construction is therefore the first and only manual stage in a pipeline that is otherwise automated.

## Reported Template Counts by Evaluation Setting

### Freebase Evaluation

For the Freebase evaluation, the system was compared against a previous state-of-the-art method on 500 randomly selected triples from Freebase, a domain-general KB ([Song & Zhao, n.d.-a](document_1.txt)). The primary source states: "For the 500 triples, we hand-crafted 106 templates, as these triples share only 53 distinct predicates (we made 2 templates for each predicate on average)" ([Song & Zhao, n.d.-a](document_1.txt)). The third-party note corroborates this figure exactly: "The paper reports 106 hand-crafted templates for those 500 Freebase triples. The triples share only 53 distinct predicates, so the authors made 2 templates for each predicate on average" ([Third-Party Research Note, n.d.-b](document_2.txt)).

The arithmetic is internally consistent: 53 distinct predicates multiplied by an average of 2 templates per predicate yields approximately 106 templates. Applying those templates to the triples generated **991 seed questions**, and a further **1,529 questions** were retrieved from Google ([Third-Party Research Note, n.d.-b](document_2.txt); [Song & Zhao, n.d.-a](document_1.txt)). This means the 106 templates were amplified into 991 seed questions before web expansion even began — a ratio of roughly 9.35 seed questions per template.

### In-House Power Tool Domain Knowledge Base

The final experiment was conducted on an in-house KB in the power tool domain, which contains **67 distinct predicates, 293 distinct subjects, and 279 distinct objects** ([Song & Zhao, n.d.-a](document_1.txt)). For these 67 predicates, the primary source states that the authors "hand-craft **163** templates" ([Song & Zhao, n.d.-a](document_1.txt)).

The third-party research note, however, reports a different number for the same evaluation: "For the in-house power tool domain knowledge base, the paper reports 67 predicates. For the 67 predicates, the authors hand-crafted **103** templates" ([Third-Party Research Note, n.d.-b](document_2.txt)). The note further claims that "together with the 106 Freebase templates, the authors wrote **209 templates in total**," asserting that "the paper reports this total in the template-construction experiment" ([Third-Party Research Note, n.d.-b](document_2.txt)).

This assertion of an aggregate total is not supported by the primary source. The primary document, as reproduced in the provided information, contains no statement of a 209-template grand total. The 209 figure is an arithmetic construction of the third-party note (106 + 103 = 209), not a reported result from the paper itself.

The table makes the nature of the conflict clear. The Freebase figure is stable across both sources at 106. The divergence is confined entirely to the power tool domain, and it propagates into every aggregate derived from it. The implied templates-per-predicate ratio also shifts: with 163 templates for 67 predicates the ratio is approximately 2.43, whereas with 103 templates it is approximately 1.54. Notably, the primary source's own Freebase discussion justifies its template count by comparison with the two-templates-per-predicate average, which suggests the authors were conscious of this ratio as a design norm.

## Why the Template Set Is Intentionally Compact

The paper offers two explicit reasons why the system does not require a large number of templates ([Song & Zhao, n.d.-a](document_1.txt)):

- **Iterative expansion multiplies output from small seed sets.** The iterative question expansion can produce a large number of questions even from a relatively small number of seed questions, as the experiments reportedly show ([Song & Zhao, n.d.-a](document_1.txt)).
- **Predicates are shared across entities.** Multiple entities in the KB share the same predicates, so a single template covers many triples ([Song & Zhao, n.d.-a](document_1.txt)).

A further stated advantage is that the system can easily generate updated questions because the web is consistently self-updating ([Song & Zhao, n.d.-a](document_1.txt)). This means the template set does not need to encode time-sensitive content; currency is supplied by the search engine rather than by manual template revision.

The third-party note frames the same design principle in terms of coverage economics: "This design ties template creation to predicates, keeping the template set compact relative to the triple set" ([Third-Party Research Note, n.d.-b](document_2.txt)). It also observes that "the domain-specific KB has its own predicate set and required its own template set," and that "these figures show the manual template-building effort scales with predicate coverage in each evaluation" ([Third-Party Research Note, n.d.-b](document_2.txt)).

## The Scale of Amplification: From Templates to Questions

To appreciate what a few hundred templates accomplish, it is useful to trace the expansion arithmetic in both experiments.

In the Freebase setting, 106 templates applied to 500 triples produced 991 seed questions, from which 1,529 additional questions were retrieved from Google ([Third-Party Research Note, n.d.-b](document_2.txt)). To evaluate fluency, a 4-gram language model was trained on Gigaword (LDC2011T07) with Kneser-Ney smoothing, and the top 500 questions by averaged language model score were selected for comparison against the baseline ([Song & Zhao, n.d.-a](document_1.txt)).

In the power tool setting, the primary source reports that **12,228 seed questions** were generated and from those, **20,000 more questions** were expanded with Google ([Song & Zhao, n.d.-a](document_1.txt)). Even under the conservative third-party template count of 103, that implies well over 100 expanded questions per template before any filtering. Under the primary source's count of 163 templates, the ratio is approximately 75 seed questions per template and roughly 198 total questions per template.

Either way, the central economic argument of the paper holds: the manual burden is concentrated in a small, predicate-indexed template set, while the volume of generated output is supplied by automated expansion and filtering.

## Quality and Performance Context for the Template Investment

The template investment is justified in the paper by measured outcomes. On the Freebase evaluation, three native English speakers evaluated fluency, grammaticality, and naturalness on a 4-point scheme where 4 is best ([Song & Zhao, n.d.-a](document_1.txt)). The reported averages were as follows:

| System | Grammaticality | Naturalness |
|---|---|---|
| Baseline (Serban et al., 2016) | 3.36 | 3.14 |
| Ours | 3.53 | 3.31 |

*Source: ([Song & Zhao, n.d.-a](document_1.txt)).*

The paper notes that naturalness scores are lower than grammaticality scores for both methods because naturalness is a stricter metric — a natural question should also be grammatical ([Song & Zhao, n.d.-a](document_1.txt)). It also reports that questions from the baseline were either ungrammatical (such as "who was someone who was involved in the leukemia?") or unnatural (such as "what's one of the mountain where can you found in argentina in netflix?") ([Song & Zhao, n.d.-a](document_1.txt)).

Separately, the domain-relevance evaluation method was tested on the web snippet dataset, which contains 10,060 training and 2,280 test snippets in 8 classes, with each snippet averaging 18 words ([Song & Zhao, n.d.-a](document_1.txt)). The reported precisions were 82.18 for Phan et al. (2008), 85.31 for Chen et al. (2011), 85.48 for Ma et al. (2015), and **85.65 for the proposed method** ([Song & Zhao, n.d.-a](document_1.txt)). The paper attributes the improvement to word embeddings capturing similarity between distinct words (such as "finance" and "economy"), which traditional methods struggle to do ([Song & Zhao, n.d.-a](document_1.txt)).

These results matter for the template-count question because they indicate the manual template effort was not merely small but also sufficient: the templates, combined with automated expansion and filtering, yielded questions rated more grammatical and more natural than a system trained on 10,000 human-generated pairs ([Song & Zhao, n.d.-a](document_1.txt)).

## Assessment and Opinion

Based on the evidence available, the most defensible answer to the query is that the work required **106 hand-crafted templates for the Freebase evaluation and either 103 or 163 templates for the in-house power tool domain**, for a total of **209 or 269 templates** depending on which source is credited. My assessment, based strictly on source reliability, is that the figure of **163 for the power tool domain**, and therefore **269 in aggregate**, deserves greater weight, for three reasons.

First, document_1 is the primary source — the paper's own text — whereas document_2 is explicitly labeled a "third-party research note" ([Third-Party Research Note, n.d.-b](document_2.txt)). Second, document_2's aggregate of 209 depends on a claim that "the paper reports this total in the template-construction experiment," but no such total appears in the primary text provided; the number is the note's own arithmetic ([Third-Party Research Note, n.d.-b](document_2.txt)). Third, the primary source's Freebase discussion reveals an explicit design norm of approximately two templates per predicate, and 163 templates across 67 predicates (~2.43 per predicate) is more consistent with that stated norm than 103 templates (~1.54 per predicate) would be.

That said, this conclusion should be held with appropriate caution. The provided primary document is visibly corrupted by optical character recognition and mathematical-formatting artifacts, including the substitution of "?" for author names throughout the text and mangled numerical expressions ([Song & Zhao, n.d.-a](document_1.txt)). Under such conditions, a digit transcription error is plausible, and it is conceivable that 103 is the correct figure and that the "163" in document_1 is itself the corrupted form. Without access to the clean published version of the paper, the discrepancy cannot be definitively resolved. An impartial reading therefore requires reporting both figures rather than silently selecting one.

## Limitations of the Evidence

Several limitations bear on the confidence of this assessment:

- **Source corruption.** The primary document contains extensive OCR artifacts, including "1eukemia" for "leukemia," "temp1ates" for "templates," "Miko1ov" for "Mikolov," and displaced table and equation fragments ([Song & Zhao, n.d.-a](document_1.txt)).
- **Unresolved numeric conflict.** The 103 versus 163 divergence in the power tool domain is not reconciled anywhere in the provided materials.
- **Missing publication metadata.** Neither provided document carries a title field, and the primary document shows no publication date, although its reference list includes works through 2016, indicating a publication date no earlier than that year ([Song & Zhao, n.d.-a](document_1.txt)).
- **Baseline attribution gaps.** The primary source repeatedly renders the compared baseline as "? )", which the surrounding text identifies as Serban et al. (2016) ([Song & Zhao, n.d.-a](document_1.txt)).

## Conclusion

Directly answering the query: the authors hand-crafted **106 templates** for the 500-triple Freebase evaluation and either **103 or 163 templates** for the 67-predicate in-house power tool knowledge base, producing aggregate totals of **209 or 269 hand-crafted templates** respectively. The Freebase figure of 106 is corroborated by both available sources and can be treated as reliable. The power tool figure is contested between the primary paper text (163) and the third-party note (103). Because the primary text is the higher-quality source and its power tool count aligns better with the approximately two-templates-per-predicate design norm stated elsewhere in the same work, I judge 163 — and thus a total of 269 templates — to be the more probable figure, while acknowledging that OCR corruption prevents full certainty. What is not in dispute is the significance of the number: because the paper states that template construction is the only human labor in the system, these few hundred manually written templates represent the entire manual cost of a pipeline that generated 991 seed questions plus 1,529 web-retrieved questions in the Freebase evaluation and 12,228 seed questions plus 20,000 expanded questions in the power tool evaluation ([Song & Zhao, n.d.-a](document_1.txt); [Third-Party Research Note, n.d.-b](document_2.txt)).
