# How Many Hand-Crafted Templates Did They Have to Make? A Detailed Analysis of Template Construction in "Question Generation from a Knowledge Base with Web Exploration"

## Executive Summary

The short answer is that the system described in "Question Generation from a Knowledge Base with Web Exploration" required **two separate sets of hand-crafted question templates**, one for each evaluation setting reported in the paper ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). The paper states that **106 templates were hand-crafted for the 500 randomly selected Freebase triples**, covering **53 distinct predicates** ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). For the in-house power tool domain knowledge base, the paper states that **163 templates were hand-crafted for 67 distinct predicates** ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). Taken together, the primary paper therefore implies a combined total of **269 hand-crafted templates** across its two evaluation settings.

However, a **third-party research note** summarizing the same system reports a materially different figure for the power tool domain: **103 templates for the 67 predicates**, producing a stated total of **209 templates** when combined with the 106 Freebase templates ([Third-Party Research Note](document_2.txt)). This discrepancy is significant and is analyzed in detail below. Based on source priority, the primary paper's figure of 163 power-tool templates (for a total of 269) should be treated as the more reliable number, while the research note's figure of 103 (for a total of 209) should be flagged as an inconsistency rather than accepted uncritically.

## Background: The Task and the Role of Templates

Question generation from a knowledge base (KB) is defined in the source material as the task of generating questions related to the domain of the input KB ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). The paper motivates this task by noting that questions are useful for student assessment or coaching purposes in educational or professional contexts, and that a large-scale corpus of question–answer pairs is critical to many natural language processing (NLP) tasks, including question answering, dialogue interaction, and intelligent tutoring systems ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)).

The authors observe that question generation from a KB is challenging because function words and morphological forms for entities are abstracted away when a KB is created ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). To address this, prior work relied on massive human-labeled data: treating question generation as a machine translation problem, previous systems trained a neural machine translation (NMT) system with 10,000 triple–question pairs, where the question part of those pairs was human generated, requiring a large amount of human effort ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)).

By contrast, the proposed system reduces human effort by leveraging massive web resources, and the paper explicitly states: **"The only human labor in this work is the question template construction"** ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). This statement makes the template count the single most important quantity for evaluating the manual burden of the method. Understanding exactly how many templates were required is therefore central to assessing the paper's central claim of reduced human effort.

## The Template Construction Methodology

The paper describes a knowledge base as a directed graph in which nodes are entities (such as "jigsaw" and "CurveCut") and edges are relations of entities (such as "performsActivity"), or equivalently as a list of triples in the format ⟨subject, predicate, object⟩ ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). A triple is a ⟨subject, predicate, object⟩ in the KB, such as ⟨jigsaw, performsActivity, CurveCut⟩, where subjects and objects are entities and predicates are relations ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)).

Given an input KB, a small set of question templates is first constructed such that each template is associated with a predicate ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). These templates consist of a transcription of the predicate in the KB (for example, performsActivity ⇒ how to) and placeholders for the subject (#X#) and the object (#Y#) ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). A seed question set is then generated by applying the templates on the KB, after which more questions are retrieved through a search engine by iteratively forming each generated question as a search query to retrieve related question candidates ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). Finally, a selection step estimates the fluency and domain relevance of each candidate ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)).

Crucially, the third-party research note emphasizes that **template creation is tied to predicates, not to triples**: "This design ties template creation to predicates, keeping the template set compact relative to the triple set" ([Third-Party Research Note](document_2.txt)). This is the structural reason the template counts are as low as they are, despite the large number of triples involved.

### Freebase Evaluation Templates

For the Freebase evaluation, the work uses 500 randomly selected triples ([Third-Party Research Note](document_2.txt)). The paper reports that for those 500 triples, the authors hand-crafted **106 templates**, because the triples share only **53 distinct predicates**, meaning an average of **2 templates per predicate** ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). The research note confirms these figures and adds that applying the templates to the triples generated **991 seed questions**, while the experiment also retrieved **1,529 more questions from Google** ([Third-Party Research Note](document_2.txt)).

The paper specifies that a 4-gram language model was trained on Gigaword (LDC2011T07) with Kneser-Ney smoothing, and that using the averaged language model score as an index, the **top 500 questions** were selected for comparison with the baseline results ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)).

### In-House Power Tool Domain Templates

The final experiment was conducted on the authors' in-house KB in the power tool domain, which contains **67 distinct predicates, 293 distinct subjects, and 279 distinct objects** ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). The paper states: "For the 67 predicates, we hand-craft **163 templates**" ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). The same experiment generated **12,228 seed questions**, from which **20,000 more questions were expanded with Google** ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). For this experiment, the authors used the same language model as in the first experiment and learned a skip-gram model on Wikipedia for evaluating domain relevance ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)).

An illustrative example given in the paper is that the template "how to use #X#" was first constructed for the predicate "performsActivity," and the seed question "how to use jigsaw" was generated by applying that template to the triple ⟨jigsaw, performsActivity, CurveCut⟩ ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)).

## The Critical Discrepancy in Reported Figures

The most important analytic issue in answering the query is that the two source documents disagree on the power tool template count. The primary paper text reads: "For the 67 predicates, we hand-craft 163 templates" ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). The third-party research note, however, states: "For the 67 predicates, the authors hand-crafted **103 templates**. Together with the 106 Freebase templates, the authors wrote **209 templates in total**" ([Third-Party Research Note](document_2.txt)).

Several considerations bear on how to resolve this:

1. **Source hierarchy.** The primary paper is the original source; the research note is a secondary summary explicitly labeled "Third-party research note" ([Third-Party Research Note](document_2.txt)). Under standard source-prioritization practice, the primary source should be preferred when the two conflict.
2. **Internal consistency of the note.** The research note's own arithmetic is internally consistent (106 + 103 = 209), so the discrepancy is not a simple addition error within that document; it is a conflict over the underlying power-tool count.
3. **Plausibility of the average.** The Freebase setting averages 2 templates per predicate (106 templates for 53 predicates) ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). A power-tool figure of 163 templates for 67 predicates implies roughly 2.43 templates per predicate, which is slightly denser but broadly consistent with the same design philosophy. A figure of 103 implies roughly 1.54 templates per predicate, which is sparser than the Freebase setting, where the authors explicitly made 2 templates per predicate on average.

On balance, therefore, the **163 figure reported in the primary paper appears the more credible one**, and the **269 total (106 + 163)** is the more defensible combined count, while the **209 total** from the research note should be regarded as a possibly erroneous secondary figure. This analyst assessment is grounded in the primary-source priority principle rather than in any external verification, which was not available within the supplied materials.

## Why So Few Templates Were Needed

The paper offers two explicit reasons why the system does not require a large number of templates: (1) the iterative question expansion can produce a large number of questions even with a relatively small number of seed questions, as demonstrated in the experiments; and (2) multiple entities in the KB share the same predicates ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). A further stated advantage is that the system can easily generate updated questions because the web is self-updating consistently ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)).

The quantitative evidence supports this reasoning. In the Freebase setting, 106 templates on 500 triples produced 991 seed questions, an approximate yield of 9.35 seed questions per template (derived from figures in [Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807) and [Third-Party Research Note](document_2.txt)). In the power tool setting, 163 templates produced 12,228 seed questions, an approximate yield of 75.0 seed questions per template (derived from figures in [Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). Because template construction is tied to predicates while seed questions are generated by applying templates across triples, the same template can be reused across many entities and triples, which decouples the template count from the size of the KB ([Third-Party Research Note](document_2.txt)).

## Human Effort in Context

The significance of the template counts lies in the paper's framing of manual labor. The authors state that "the only human labor in this work is the question template construction" ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). They contrast this with prior work that relied on massive human-labeled data: treating question generation as a machine translation problem, previous systems trained an NMT system with 10,000 triple–question pairs whose question part was human generated, requiring a large amount of human effort ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). Under this comparison, 106 templates for one evaluation and 163 (or 103) for another represent a far smaller manual commitment than authoring 10,000 human-written questions.

The research note reinforces this point by observing that the reported counts show "the manual template-building effort scales with predicate coverage in each evaluation" ([Third-Party Research Note](document_2.txt)). This is an important property: effort is proportional to the diversity of relations in the KB (predicates) rather than to the sheer volume of facts (triples), subjects, or objects.

## Evaluation Outcomes Associated with the Template Approach

The template-based pipeline was evaluated both quantitatively and qualitatively. On 500 randomly selected triples from Freebase, three native English speakers rated the generated questions on fluency and naturalness using a 4-point scheme where 4 is the best ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). The averaged human ratings favored the proposed system for **grammaticality (3.53 versus 3.36)** and **naturalness (3.31 versus 3.14)** ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). The paper explains that naturalness scores are lower than grammaticality scores for both methods because naturalness is a stricter metric: a natural question should also be grammatical ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)).

In a separate experiment on the web snippet dataset, which contains 10,060 training and 2,280 test snippets in 8 classes with an average of 18 words per snippet, the authors' simplicity-driven domain-relevance method achieved a precision of **85.65**, outperforming the compared prior methods (82.18, 85.31, and 85.48) ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). The paper attributes this to word embeddings capturing similarity between distinct words (such as "finance" and "economy"), whereas LDA only learns probabilities of words belonging to topics ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)).

The domain-specific experiment produced sample questions judged grammatical and relevant to the power tool domain, such as "how to change circular saw blade," "how to measure lawn mower cutting height," "how to sharpen drill bits on bench grinder," "how does an oscillating multi tool work," "how to cut a groove in wood without a router," "what type of sander to use on deck," "do i need a hammer drill," "can i use acrylic paint on wood," and "how to use a sharpening stone with oil" ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). The paper notes that most such questions are informative and correspond to a specific answer, with the exception of "do I need a hammer drill," which lacks context information ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)).

## Analyst Assessment

Based strictly on the supplied materials, three conclusions can be drawn with reasonable confidence.

First, **the answer to the query is domain-dependent, not singular**. The authors did not build one universal template set; they built one set per KB evaluation. The Freebase set comprised 106 templates for 53 distinct predicates, and the power tool set comprised either 163 or 103 templates for 67 distinct predicates, depending on which source is credited ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807); [Third-Party Research Note](document_2.txt)).

Second, **the most reliable single combined figure is 269 templates**, derived from the primary paper's 106 plus 163 ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). The alternative figure of 209 rests on the research note's power-tool count of 103 ([Third-Party Research Note](document_2.txt)), which conflicts with the primary text and is less consistent with the author's stated practice of making roughly 2 templates per predicate.

Third, and more substantively, **the magnitude of the template counts validates the paper's efficiency claim even under the more conservative reading**. Whether the total is 209 or 269, the manual component remains in the low hundreds of templates, while the resulting seed questions number in the thousands (991 and 12,228) and the expanded questions reach into the tens of thousands (with 20,000 additional questions expanded from Google in the power tool setting) ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807); [Third-Party Research Note](document_2.txt)). No single template set exceeds a few hundred items, and the per-predicate density stays within a narrow band of roughly 1.5 to 2.4 templates.

The principal caveat is that neither the exact reconciliation of the 163-versus-103 discrepancy nor independent verification of the counts was possible from the material supplied. The paper also leaves automatic answer mining as future work, noting that the current system generates questions without answers ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)).

## Conclusion

In response to the question "How many hand-crafted templates did they have to make?", the evidence supports the following precise answer. The authors hand-crafted **106 templates for the Freebase evaluation** (covering 53 distinct predicates across 500 triples) and **163 templates for the in-house power tool domain KB** (covering 67 distinct predicates), for a **primary-source total of 269 templates** ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). A third-party research note instead reports **103 power tool templates and a total of 209** ([Third-Party Research Note](document_2.txt)); given the source hierarchy and the internal logic of the paper's roughly 2-templates-per-predicate design, the primary paper's 163 figure and the resulting 269 total are the stronger candidates. In either case, the essential finding is unchanged: the manual labor in this system is confined to a compact, predicate-indexed template set measured in the low hundreds, which is what enables the authors to claim that template construction is the only human labor in the work ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)).
