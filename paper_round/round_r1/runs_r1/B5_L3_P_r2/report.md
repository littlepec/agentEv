# How Much Improvement Do the Introduced Models Achieve Compared to Previous Models?

## Introduction

The Gated Recurrent Neural Tensor Network paper introduces two recurrent neural network architectures: the Long-Short Term Memory Recurrent Neural Tensor Network (LSTMRNTN) and the Gated Recurrent Unit Recurrent Neural Tensor Network (GRURNTN) ([Tjandra et al., n.d.](document_1.txt)). These models are designed to combine two ideas: gating mechanisms, which help recurrent networks learn long-term dependencies, and tensor products, which permit more expressive second-degree interactions between current inputs and previous hidden states ([Tjandra et al., n.d.](document_1.txt)). The paper compares these proposed models against two direct baselines: the standard Gated Recurrent Unit RNN (GRURNN) and the standard Long-Short Term Memory RNN (LSTMRNN) ([Tjandra et al., n.d.](document_1.txt); [Third-party research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)). Evaluation is conducted on word-level and character-level language modeling using the PennTreeBank corpus ([Tjandra et al., n.d.](document_1.txt)).

The core question is: how much improvement do the introduced models achieve compared to the previous models? The answer depends on the task and metric. On character-level language modeling, GRURNTN reduces bits-per-character (BPC) from 1.39 to 1.33, an absolute reduction of 0.06 and a relative reduction of 4.32% over GRURNN; LSTMRNTN reduces BPC from 1.37 to 1.34, an absolute reduction of 0.03 and a relative reduction of 2.22% over LSTMRNN ([Tjandra et al., n.d.](document_1.txt); [Third-party research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)). On word-level language modeling, the primary paper reports that GRURNTN reduces perplexity (PPL) from 97.78 to 87.38, an absolute reduction of 10.4 and a relative reduction of 10.63% over GRURNN, while LSTMRNTN reduces PPL from 108.26 to 96.97, an absolute reduction of 11.29 and a relative reduction of 10.42% over LSTMRNN ([Tjandra et al., n.d.](document_1.txt)). A secondary research note reports a different GRURNTN word-level result, stating a reduction from 97.78 to 92.98, or 4.8 absolute and 4.91% relative PPL improvement ([Third-party research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)). Because the primary paper is the more reliable source and its own table and conclusion report 87.38 and 10.63%, this report treats the primary figures as authoritative while noting the discrepancy.

## Background on the Proposed and Previous Models

The paper positions GRURNTN and LSTMRNTN as extensions of GRURNN and LSTMRNN ([Tjandra et al., n.d.](document_1.txt)). Standard RNNs rely on linear projection, addition, and a nonlinear activation function to model the relationship between the current input and the previous hidden state ([Tjandra et al., n.d.](document_1.txt)). The paper argues that this transition is shallow because no intermediate hidden layers exist for projecting hidden states, and that adding tensor products allows richer, second-degree polynomial interactions compared with first-degree dot-product interactions ([Tjandra et al., n.d.](document_1.txt)). In GRURNTN, the tensor product is applied inside the candidate hidden layer equation, and in LSTMRNTN, it is applied inside the cell equation ([Tjandra et al., n.d.](document_1.txt)). The gating units continue to control information flow, which is intended to preserve the long-term dependency benefits of LSTM and GRU models ([Tjandra et al., n.d.](document_1.txt)).

The paper also notes prior work using tensor products, such as the Recursive Neural Tensor Network (RecNTN) and multiplicative RNNs, but argues that those models either lack gating mechanisms or fail to fully utilize tensor weights because of vanishing or exploding gradients ([Tjandra et al., n.d.](document_1.txt)). The proposed architecture is therefore presented as a combination that captures both advantages in a single RNN: gating for long-term memory and tensor products for more expressive input-hidden interactions ([Tjandra et al., n.d.](document_1.txt)).

## Experimental Setup and Metrics

The experiments use the PennTreeBank corpus, a standard benchmark for statistical language modeling ([Tjandra et al., n.d.](document_1.txt)). The paper reports two tasks. For word-level language modeling, the model predicts the next word probability given previous words and the current word, and performance is measured by perplexity (PPL), where lower values are better ([Tjandra et al., n.d.](document_1.txt)). For character-level language modeling, the model predicts the next character probability, and performance is measured by bits-per-character (BPC), where lower values are better ([Tjandra et al., n.d.](document_1.txt)). The paper uses the same embedding dimensions for words and characters across models and constrains the baseline GRURNN and LSTMRNN to have similar numbers of parameters as their proposed tensor counterparts for fair comparison ([Tjandra et al., n.d.](document_1.txt)).

For word-level modeling, the proposed GRURNTN and LSTMRNTN use 256 hidden units, while GRURNN uses 860 and LSTMRNN uses 740; all models use 128-dimensional word embeddings ([Tjandra et al., n.d.](document_1.txt)). For character-level modeling, the proposed models again use 256 hidden units, while GRURNN uses 820 and LSTMRNN uses 600, according to the provided text, with 32-dimensional character embeddings ([Tjandra et al., n.d.](document_1.txt)). The total parameter counts are about 12 million for GRURNN and GRURNTN and about 13 million for LSTMRNN and LSTMRNTN in the word-level task, and about 2.2 million for GRURNN and GRURNTN and about 2.6 million for LSTMRNN and LSTMRNTN in the character-level task ([Tjandra et al., n.d.](document_1.txt)). This parameter-matching design is important because it makes the reported improvements less likely to be explained simply by adding more parameters.

## Character-Level Language Modeling Improvements

The direct character-level comparison is consistent across the primary paper and the third-party note. Table 1 summarizes the reported improvements.

| Task | Baseline model | Proposed model | Baseline BPC | Proposed BPC | Absolute reduction | Relative reduction |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Character-level BPC | GRURNN | GRURNTN | 1.39 | 1.33 | 0.06 | 4.32% |
| Character-level BPC | LSTMRNN | LSTMRNTN | 1.37 | 1.34 | 0.03 | 2.22% |

Source: ([Tjandra et al., n.d.](document_1.txt); [Third-party research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)).

The result is that GRURNTN improves over GRURNN by 0.06 BPC, or 4.32% relative, and LSTMRNTN improves over LSTMRNN by 0.03 BPC, or 2.22% relative ([Tjandra et al., n.d.](document_1.txt); [Third-party research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)). The paper also reports that both proposed models produced lower BPC than their baselines from the first epoch to the last epoch, and that GRURNTN slightly outperformed LSTMRNTN overall on this task ([Tjandra et al., n.d.](document_1.txt)). In training dynamics, GRURNN made faster progress than LSTMRNN, but LSTMRNN eventually converged to a better BPC; GRURNTN made faster and quicker progress than LSTMRNTN and converged to a similar BPC in the final epoch ([Tjandra et al., n.d.](document_1.txt)). Nevertheless, the key finding is that the tensor-product gating models achieved lower BPC throughout training than the corresponding baselines ([Tjandra et al., n.d.](document_1.txt)).

The character-level improvements are modest in absolute BPC terms, but they are not trivial in language modeling. A reduction of 0.06 BPC is a 4.32% relative reduction in the average number of bits needed to encode each character, which indicates a meaningful improvement in predictive probability mass assigned to the correct next character ([Tjandra et al., n.d.](document_1.txt)). At the same time, the LSTMRNTN gain of 0.03 BPC is smaller, suggesting that the tensor product benefited the GRU-based architecture more strongly on this task ([Tjandra et al., n.d.](document_1.txt)).

## Word-Level Language Modeling Improvements

The word-level results show larger relative gains, but there is a source discrepancy for GRURNTN. According to the primary paper, GRURNTN reduces test PPL from 97.78 to 87.38, which is 10.4 absolute and 10.63% relative PPL reduction over GRURNN, while LSTMRNTN reduces test PPL from 108.26 to 96.97, which is 11.29 absolute and 10.42% relative PPL reduction over LSTMRNN ([Tjandra et al., n.d.](document_1.txt)). Table 2 presents the primary paper's results.

| Task | Baseline model | Proposed model | Baseline PPL | Proposed PPL | Absolute reduction | Relative reduction |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Word-level PPL | GRURNN | GRURNTN | 97.78 | 87.38 | 10.4 | 10.63% |
| Word-level PPL | LSTMRNN | LSTMRNTN | 108.26 | 96.97 | 11.29 | 10.42% |

Source: ([Tjandra et al., n.d.](document_1.txt)).

A third-party research note reports a different GRURNTN word-level figure: it states that GRURNTN reduced test PPL from 97.78 to 92.98, which is 4.8 absolute and 4.91% relative PPL reduction over GRURNN ([Third-party research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)). That note agrees with the primary paper on LSTMRNTN, reporting a reduction from 108.26 to 96.97, or 11.29 absolute and 10.42% relative PPL improvement ([Third-party research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)). However, the primary paper's own text and table report 87.38 for GRURNTN and describe a 10.4 absolute and 10.63% relative reduction ([Tjandra et al., n.d.](document_1.txt)). Because the primary paper is the original source and provides the experimental table, the 87.38 / 10.63% figure should be given greater evidentiary weight. The secondary note's 92.98 figure may be a transcription error or a different aggregation that is not supported by the primary text.

Using the primary paper's figures, the word-level improvements are substantial. GRURNTN lowers PPL by 10.4 points, and LSTMRNTN lowers PPL by 11.29 points ([Tjandra et al., n.d.](document_1.txt)). In relative terms, both reductions are near 10.5%, with GRURNTN slightly higher at 10.63% and LSTMRNTN slightly lower at 10.42% ([Tjandra et al., n.d.](document_1.txt)). The paper states that GRURNTN was the best model in the word-level task, with consistently lower PPL than the other models, and that it outperformed all baselines as well as the other proposed model by a large margin ([Tjandra et al., n.d.](document_1.txt)). LSTMRNTN improved over LSTMRNN and its performance closely resembled the baseline GRURNN, but it did not surpass GRURNTN ([Tjandra et al., n.d.](document_1.txt)).

## Comparison with Published Results and Baselines

The paper's word-level table places the proposed models alongside several published baselines. The reported test PPLs include N-Gram at 141, RNNLM without dynamic evaluation at 124.7, RNNLM with dynamic evaluation at 123.2, SCRNN at 115, sRNN at 110.0, DOT(S)-RNN at 107.5, GRURNN baseline at 97.78, LSTMRNN baseline at 108.26, GRURNTN proposed at 87.38, and LSTMRNTN proposed at 96.97 ([Tjandra et al., n.d.](document_1.txt)). This context shows that GRURNTN achieved the lowest PPL among the models listed in that table, and LSTMRNTN also improved substantially over its direct LSTM baseline and over several earlier published models ([Tjandra et al., n.d.](document_1.txt)). The paper concludes that both proposed models outperformed their baseline models with a similar number of parameters in character-level and word-level language modeling tasks ([Tjandra et al., n.d.](document_1.txt); [Third-party research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)).

The character-level table in the provided text is more difficult to parse because of extraction issues, but the direct baseline comparisons are clear: GRURNTN improves over GRURNN and LSTMRNTN improves over LSTMRNN ([Tjandra et al., n.d.](document_1.txt)). The paper's conclusion states that GRURNTN obtained 0.06 absolute and 4.32% relative BPC reduction over GRURNN, and LSTMRNTN obtained 0.03 absolute and 2.22% relative BPC reduction over LSTMRNN in character-level language modeling ([Tjandra et al., n.d.](document_1.txt)). For word-level language modeling, the paper's conclusion repeats the primary figures: GRURNTN obtained 10.4 absolute and 10.63% relative PPL reduction over GRURNN, and LSTMRNTN obtained 11.29 absolute and 10.42% relative PPL reduction over LSTMRNN ([Tjandra et al., n.d.](document_1.txt)). This internal consistency strengthens confidence in the primary word-level GRURNTN figure despite the conflicting secondary note.

## Interpretation of the Magnitude of Improvement

The improvements can be interpreted by architecture family and task. On character-level modeling, the GRU-based tensor model, GRURNTN, shows a larger relative improvement (4.32%) than the LSTM-based tensor model, LSTMRNTN (2.22%) ([Tjandra et al., n.d.](document_1.txt); [Third-party research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)). On word-level modeling, the LSTM-based tensor model has the larger absolute improvement (11.29 PPL) but a slightly smaller relative improvement (10.42%) than GRURNTN (10.4 absolute, 10.63% relative) according to the primary source ([Tjandra et al., n.d.](document_1.txt)). Overall, GRURNTN appears to be the stronger proposed model across the two tasks: it slightly outperforms LSTMRNTN on character-level BPC and is the best model on word-level PPL in the primary paper ([Tjandra et al., n.d.](document_1.txt)). LSTMRNTN still provides meaningful gains over its own LSTM baseline, especially in absolute word-level PPL reduction ([Tjandra et al., n.d.](document_1.txt)).

The paper attributes these gains to the tensor product operation, which increases model expressiveness through second-degree polynomial interactions compared with first-degree interactions in standard dot-product-plus-addition RNNs ([Tjandra et al., n.d.](document_1.txt)). The gating mechanism is retained to handle long-term dependencies and mitigate vanishing or exploding gradients ([Tjandra et al., n.d.](document_1.txt)). In other words, the improvement is not merely from adding depth or more hidden units; the baselines were constrained to similar parameter counts, and the proposed models used fewer hidden units while still achieving lower BPC and PPL ([Tjandra et al., n.d.](document_1.txt)). This supports the paper's claim that combining gating and tensor products yields a more effective recurrent architecture for language modeling ([Tjandra et al., n.d.](document_1.txt)).

## Limitations and Source Reliability

Several limitations should temper the interpretation. First, the provided text does not report statistical significance tests, confidence intervals, or multiple random seeds, so the improvements should be treated as point estimates from the reported PennTreeBank experiments ([Tjandra et al., n.d.](document_1.txt)). Second, there is a discrepancy for the GRURNTN word-level PPL: the primary paper reports 87.38 and a 10.63% relative reduction, while the third-party note reports 92.98 and a 4.91% relative reduction ([Tjandra et al., n.d.](document_1.txt); [Third-party research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)). This report prioritizes the primary paper because it contains the original experimental table and conclusion, and because the secondary note's GRURNTN figure is inconsistent with the primary text ([Tjandra et al., n.d.](document_1.txt)). Third, the experiments cover only PennTreeBank word-level and character-level language modeling; the paper states that future work will apply the models to other temporal and sequential tasks such as speech recognition and video recognition ([Tjandra et al., n.d.](document_1.txt)). Therefore, the improvements demonstrated here are task-specific and benchmark-specific.

## Conclusion

Based on the primary paper, the introduced models achieve clear but task-dependent improvements over the previous GRURNN and LSTMRNN baselines. On character-level language modeling, GRURNTN reduces BPC by 0.06 absolute and 4.32% relative over GRURNN, while LSTMRNTN reduces BPC by 0.03 absolute and 2.22% relative over LSTMRNN ([Tjandra et al., n.d.](document_1.txt); [Third-party research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)). On word-level language modeling, the primary paper reports that GRURNTN reduces PPL by 10.4 absolute and 10.63% relative over GRURNN, and LSTMRNTN reduces PPL by 11.29 absolute and 10.42% relative over LSTMRNN ([Tjandra et al., n.d.](document_1.txt)). A secondary note reports a smaller GRURNTN word-level improvement of 4.8 absolute and 4.91% relative, but this conflicts with the primary paper's table and conclusion ([Third-party research note, n.d.](document_2.txt); [Tjandra et al., n.d.](document_1.txt)). Taken together, the evidence indicates that combining gating mechanisms with tensor products yields measurable language-modeling gains over standard GRU and LSTM baselines, with GRURNTN generally emerging as the strongest proposed model and LSTMRNTN providing a substantial absolute improvement for the LSTM family ([Tjandra et al., n.d.](document_1.txt)).
