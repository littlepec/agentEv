Content: Third-party research note: Convolutional Neural Networks and a Transfer Learning Strategy to Classify Parkinson's Disease from Speech in Three Different Languages

Key questions addressed: Parkinson disease speech datasets cross-lingual transfer learning | convolutional neural networks Parkinson speech time-frequency representations dataset | speech recordings Parkinson patients healthy controls multiple languages | transfer learning speech classification Parkinson disease languages | clinical speech corpus Parkinson disease speech tasks

The study uses speech recordings of patients in three different languages: Spanish, German, and Czech. The Spanish data come from the PC-GITA corpus. PC-GITA contains utterances from 50 PD patients and 50 HC, Colombian Spanish native speakers. The Spanish dataset speaker composition is therefore 50 PD patients and 50 healthy control speakers, Colombian Spanish native speakers. This corpus provides the Spanish portion of the cross-lingual speech data.

The German data consist of speech recordings of 44 PD patients and 44 HC speakers. The German dataset description reports this composition. The Czech dataset contains a total of 100 native Czech speakers, comprising 50 PD patients and 50 healthy controls. These datasets supply speech recordings of Parkinson’s disease patients and healthy controls across multiple languages. The speaker counts for the German and Czech data are 44 PD patients with 44 HC speakers and 50 PD patients with 50 HC, respectively.

The patients in the three datasets were evaluated by a neurologist expert according to the third section of the movement disorder society, unified Parkinson’s disease rating scale (MDS-UPDRS-III). The Spanish, German, and Czech recordings form the dataset base for the convolutional neural network and transfer learning strategy. The study’s data include named corpora, language-specific speaker groups, and clinical scale evaluations. The PC-GITA corpus, German speech recordings, and Czech speech recordings support Parkinson’s disease classification from speech in three languages. Source: document_1.txt

Title: 
Content: Third-party research note: Convolutional Neural Networks and a Transfer Learning Strategy to Classify Parkinson's Disease from Speech in Three Different Languages

Key questions addressed: Parkinson disease speech datasets cross-lingual transfer learning | convolutional neural networks Parkinson speech time-frequency representations dataset | speech recordings Parkinson patients healthy controls multiple languages | transfer learning speech classification Parkinson disease languages | clinical speech corpus Parkinson disease speech tasks

The study uses speech recordings of patients in three different languages: Spanish, German, and Czech. The Spanish data come from the PC-GITA corpus. PC-GITA contains utterances from 50 PD patients and 50 HC, Colombian Spanish native speakers. The Spanish dataset speaker composition is therefore 50 PD patients and 50 healthy control speakers, Colombian Spanish native speakers. This corpus provides the Spanish portion of the cross-lingual speech data.

The German data consist of speech recordings of 44 PD patients and 44 HC speakers. The German dataset description reports this composition. The Czech dataset contains a total of 100 native Czech speakers, comprising 50 PD patients and 50 healthy controls. These datasets supply speech recordings of Parkinson’s disease patients and healthy controls across multiple languages. The speaker counts for the German and Czech data are 44 PD patients with 44 HC speakers and 50 PD patients with 50 HC, respectively.

The patients in the three datasets were evaluated by a neurologist expert according to the third section of the movement disorder society, unified Parkinson’s disease rating scale (MDS-UPDRS-III). The Spanish, German, and Czech recordings form the dataset base for the convolutional neural network and transfer learning strategy. The study’s data include named corpora, language-specific speaker groups, and clinical scale evaluations. The PC-GITA corpus, German speech recordings, and Czech speech recordings support Parkinson’s disease classification from speech in three languages. Source: document_1.txt

Content: Third-party research note: Convolutional Neural Networks and a Transfer Learning Strategy to Classify Parkinson's Disease from Speech in Three Different Languages

Key questions addressed: Parkinson disease speech datasets cross-lingual transfer learning | convolutional neural networks Parkinson speech time-frequency representations dataset | speech recordings Parkinson patients healthy controls multiple languages | transfer learning speech classification Parkinson disease languages | clinical speech corpus Parkinson disease speech tasks

The study uses speech recordings of patients in three different languages: Spanish, German, and Czech. The Spanish data come from the PC-GITA corpus. PC-GITA contains utterances from 50 PD patients and 50 HC, Colombian Spanish native speakers. The Spanish dataset speaker composition is therefore 50 PD patients and 50 healthy control speakers, Colombian Spanish native speakers. This corpus provides the Spanish portion of the cross-lingual speech data.

The German data consist of speech recordings of 44 PD patients and 44 HC speakers. The German dataset description reports this composition. The Czech dataset contains a total of 100 native Czech speakers, comprising 50 PD patients and 50 healthy controls. These datasets supply speech recordings of Parkinson’s disease patients and healthy controls across multiple languages. The speaker counts for the German and Czech data are 44 PD patients with 44 HC speakers and 50 PD patients with 50 HC, respectively.

The patients in the three datasets were evaluated by a neurologist expert according to the third section of the movement disorder society, unified Parkinson’s disease rating scale (MDS-UPDRS-III). The Spanish, German, and Czech recordings form the dataset base for the convolutional neural network and transfer learning strategy. The study’s data include named corpora, language-specific speaker groups, and clinical scale evaluations. Content: Third-party research note: Convolutional Neural Networks and a Transfer Learning Strategy to Classify Parkinson's Disease from Speech in Three Different Languages

Key questions addressed: Parkinson disease speech datasets cross-lingual transfer learning | convolutional neural networks Parkinson speech time-frequency representations dataset | speech recordings Parkinson patients healthy controls multiple languages | transfer learning speech classification Parkinson disease languages | clinical speech corpus Parkinson disease speech tasks

The study uses speech recordings of patients in three different languages: Spanish, German, and Czech. The Spanish data come from the PC-GITA corpus. PC-GITA contains utterances from 50 PD patients and 50 HC, Colombian Spanish native speakers. The Spanish dataset speaker composition is therefore 50 PD patients and 50 healthy control speakers, Colombian Spanish native speakers. This corpus provides the Spanish portion of the cross-lingual speech data.

The German data consist of speech recordings of 44 PD patients and 44 HC speakers. The German dataset description reports this composition. The Czech dataset contains a total of 100 native Czech speakers, comprising 50 PD patients and 50 healthy controls. These datasets supply speech recordings of Parkinson’s disease patients and healthy controls across multiple languages. The speaker counts for the German and Czech data are 44 PD patients with 44 HC speakers and 50 PD patients with 50 HC, respectively.

The patients in the three datasets were evaluated by a neurologist expert according to the third section of the movement disorder society, unified Parkinson’s disease rating scale (MDS-UPDRS-III). The Spanish, German, and Czech recordings form the dataset base for the convolutional neural network and transfer learning strategy. The study’s data include named corpora, language-specific speaker groups, and clinical scale evaluations. The PC-GITA corpus, German speech recordings, and Czech speech recordings support Parkinson’s disease classification from speech in three languages.
