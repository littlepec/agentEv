# How Many Hand-Crafted Templates Were Required? A Detailed Report on Template Construction in *Question Generation from a Knowledge Base with Web Exploration*

## Executive Summary

The primary source for this report, Song and Zhao’s paper *Question Generation from a Knowledge Base with Web Exploration*, reports two distinct template-construction efforts across two evaluations. For the Freebase evaluation, the authors hand-crafted **106 templates** to cover 500 randomly selected triples that shared only 53 distinct predicates ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). For the in-house power tool domain evaluation, the same paper states that the authors hand-crafted **163 templates** for 67 distinct predicates ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). Therefore, if the question asks how many hand-crafted templates were made across both reported evaluations, the total is **269** (106 + 163). If the question is confined to the Freebase experiment, the answer is **106**. A third-party research note included in the provided materials contradicts the primary source regarding the power tool templates, claiming that the 163 templates were generated automatically rather than hand-crafted ([Third-party research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)). Because the original paper is the more reliable source, this report treats the 163 power tool templates as hand-crafted while explicitly noting the discrepancy.

## Background and Context

Question generation from a knowledge base (KB) is the task of producing natural-language questions that are related to the domain of the input KB ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). The challenge is that KBs store entities and relations in structured triples, such as ⟨subject, predicate, object⟩, but they abstract away function words and morphological forms that are necessary for fluent questions ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). Previous work, such as the neural machine translation approach by Serban et al. (2016), relied on 10,000 human-generated ⟨triple, question⟩ pairs, which required extensive human effort and did not guarantee grammaticality or naturalness ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)).

Song and Zhao (2016) proposed an alternative system that significantly reduces human effort by leveraging massive web resources. Their pipeline consists of four sub-modules: question template construction, seed question generation, question expansion through a search engine, and question selection based on fluency and domain relevance ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). The only human labor in this system is the construction of question templates ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). Each template is associated with a predicate in the KB and includes a transcription of the predicate plus placeholders for the subject (#X#) and the object (#Y#) ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). For example, the predicate “performsActivity” can be transcribed into the template “how to use #X#,” which then generates the seed question “how to use jigsaw” from the triple ⟨jigsaw, performsActivity, CurveCut⟩ ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)).

## Direct Answer: Reported Hand-Crafted Template Counts

### Freebase Evaluation

In the first experiment, the authors compared their end-to-end system with the previous state-of-the-art method on 500 randomly selected triples from Freebase, a domain-general KB ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). For these 500 triples, they hand-crafted **106 templates** because the triples shared only 53 distinct predicates ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). On average, this corresponds to two templates per predicate ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807); [Third-party research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)). Applying these templates to the triples generated 991 seed questions, and an additional 1,529 questions were retrieved from Google ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). The third-party research note confirms these figures, stating that the work uses 500 randomly selected triples, 106 hand-crafted templates, 53 distinct predicates, 991 seed questions, and 1,529 retrieved questions ([Third-party research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)).

### Power Tool Domain Evaluation

In the third experiment, the authors evaluated their system on an in-house KB in the power tool domain ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). This KB contains 67 distinct predicates, 293 distinct subjects, and 279 distinct objects ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). For the 67 predicates, the paper states: “we hand-craft 163 templates” ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). This yields an average of approximately 2.43 templates per predicate. From these templates, the system generated 12,228 seed questions, and 20,000 more questions were expanded with Google ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). The third-party note also reports 67 predicates and 163 templates but asserts that the 163 templates were “generated automatically from the predicate names rather than hand-crafted” ([Third-party research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)). This claim conflicts with the primary source.

### Total Hand-Crafted Templates Across Both Evaluations

If one aggregates the hand-crafted template counts from the two evaluations described in the primary source, the total is **269 templates**: 106 for Freebase and 163 for the power tool domain ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). It is important to note that these template sets were constructed for different KBs and different predicate sets; they are not necessarily interchangeable. The Freebase templates cover 53 predicates, while the power tool templates cover 67 predicates ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807); [Third-party research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)). Thus, the hand-crafted template effort scales with the number of distinct predicates in each KB rather than with the total number of triples.

## Reconciling Conflicting Claims About the Power Tool Templates

The primary source explicitly states that the authors “hand-craft 163 templates” for the 67 predicates in the power tool domain ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). The third-party research note, however, states that “the 163 templates were generated automatically from the predicate names rather than hand-crafted” ([Third-party research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)). This is a direct contradiction. In evaluating source reliability, the original paper should be prioritized over a third-party summary because the original authors are reporting their own methodology. The third-party note may have misinterpreted the paper, or it may have introduced an error. Consequently, this report takes the position that the 163 power tool templates were hand-crafted, as stated in the primary source. The third-party note’s broader point—that template counts differ between Freebase and the power tool domain because each KB has its own predicate set—remains valid and is consistent with the primary source ([Third-party research note, n.d.](document_2.txt); [Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)).

It is also worth noting that the paper’s abstract and introduction describe the template construction as “hand-crafted” for the general system, and the only human labor is described as “question template construction” ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). This framing further supports the interpretation that templates are manually created in both evaluations. If the power tool templates had been generated automatically, the authors would likely have highlighted this as a difference in methodology, but they do not.

## Implications of the Template Counts

### Efficiency and Scalability

The reported template counts demonstrate a key efficiency advantage of the proposed system. For the Freebase evaluation, 106 hand-crafted templates were sufficient to generate 991 seed questions from 500 triples, which were then expanded to include 1,529 additional questions from the web ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). For the power tool domain, 163 hand-crafted templates generated 12,228 seed questions, from which 20,000 more questions were expanded ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). In both cases, the number of hand-crafted templates is modest compared with the volume of questions produced. This supports the authors’ argument that a small template set can be scaled up through web exploration ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)).

### Human Effort Reduction

The primary paper emphasizes that the only human labor in the system is template construction ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). By contrast, the previous state-of-the-art neural machine translation approach required 10,000 human-generated question-answer pairs ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). The hand-crafted template counts of 106 and 163 are orders of magnitude smaller than 10,000, which illustrates the substantial reduction in human effort. The third-party note also stresses that the template-building effort scales with predicate coverage in each evaluation, rather than with the number of triples ([Third-party research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)). This design choice keeps the manual component compact.

### Quality Outcomes

The template-based approach did not sacrifice quality. Human graders evaluated questions generated by the system and the baseline on a 4-point scheme. The authors’ system achieved higher grammaticality (3.53 vs. 3.36) and naturalness (3.31 vs. 3.14) ratings than the baseline ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). In the power tool domain, the expanded questions were described as mostly grammatical, domain-relevant, and informative, with examples such as “how to change circular saw blade” and “how to cut a groove in wood without a router” ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). These outcomes suggest that the relatively small number of hand-crafted templates did not limit the system’s ability to produce fluent and natural questions.

## Methodological Notes and Limitations

Several methodological details are relevant to interpreting the template counts. First, the Freebase experiment used 500 randomly selected triples, but the templates were constructed based on the 53 distinct predicates present in that sample ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). The count of 106 templates is therefore tied to that particular sample and may not generalize to the entire Freebase KB. Second, the power tool KB contained 67 distinct predicates, 293 distinct subjects, and 279 distinct objects, and the authors hand-crafted 163 templates for those predicates ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). The exact distribution of templates per predicate is not fully specified, but the average is approximately 2.43. Third, the third-party note’s claim about automatic template generation introduces uncertainty, but the primary source’s language is sufficiently clear to resolve the discrepancy in favor of hand-crafting ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807); [Third-party research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)).

Additionally, the paper notes that the questions collected from the web search engine may not be fluent or domain-relevant, especially as the iteration goes on ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). The system therefore applies a skip-gram model for domain relevance and a language model for fluency, filtering out questions below thresholds ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). This selection step is important because it ensures that the expanded question set remains useful despite the lack of human curation.

## Conclusion

The question “How many hand-crafted templates did they have to make?” has two distinct answers depending on the evaluation. For the Freebase experiment, the authors hand-crafted **106 templates** for 500 triples with 53 distinct predicates ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). For the in-house power tool domain experiment, the primary source states that they hand-crafted **163 templates** for 67 distinct predicates ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). Aggregating across both evaluations yields a total of **269 hand-crafted templates**. A third-party note disputes the hand-crafted nature of the 163 power tool templates, but the original paper is the more authoritative source on its own methodology ([Third-party research note, n.d.](document_2.txt); [Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). The relatively small number of templates—compared with 10,000 human-generated pairs in prior work—demonstrates the efficiency of the proposed web-exploration approach. The templates are predicate-centric, compact, and sufficient to seed a pipeline that generates thousands of questions through iterative web search and selection.
