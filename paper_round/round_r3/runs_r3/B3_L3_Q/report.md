# Hand-Crafted Template Effort in *Question Generation from a Knowledge Base with Web Exploration*

## Direct Answer to the Query

The system described in *Question Generation from a Knowledge Base with Web Exploration* required **106 hand-crafted question templates** for its main Freebase evaluation and **163 hand-crafted templates** for its in-house power tool domain knowledge base (KB), for a reported total of **269 hand-crafted templates across the two evaluation settings** ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt)). The figure most frequently cited in connection with the paper's headline Freebase benchmark is **106**, a number that the authors explicitly tie to the 53 distinct predicates shared by the 500 randomly selected triples used in that experiment ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt)). A third-party research note on the same work reproduces the 106 and 163 counts but additionally claims that the 163 power tool templates were generated automatically from predicate names rather than hand-crafted, a characterization that conflicts with the primary paper's own wording and should be treated with caution ([Third-party research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)).

The remainder of this report documents how those numbers arise, how the templates function inside the pipeline, why the template set could remain so compact relative to the KB, and how the template counts relate to the system's reported evaluation results.

## Background: The Task and the System

Question generation from a knowledge base (KB) is defined in the paper as the task of generating questions related to the domain of the input KB ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt)). The authors motivate the task by noting that questions are useful for student assessment or coaching in educational and professional contexts, and that large-scale question–answer corpora are critical to question answering, dialogue interaction, and intelligent tutoring systems ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt)). The specific challenge they identify is that function words and morphological forms for entities are abstracted away when a KB is created, so a system cannot simply copy natural-language surface forms out of the graph ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt)). Previous work such as Seyler et al. (2015) and Serban et al. (2016) addressed this by relying on massive human-labeled data, training a neural machine translation (NMT) system on 10,000 human-generated triple–question pairs ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt)). The present work instead proposes to reduce human effort by leveraging massive web resources ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt)).

### Knowledge Base Structure

A KB is described as a directed graph whose nodes are entities (such as "jigsaw" and "CurveCut") and whose edges are relations of entities (such as "performsActivity"), or equivalently as a list of triples in the format ⟨subject, predicate, object⟩ ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt)). An example triple is ⟨jigsaw, performsActivity, CurveCut⟩, where subjects and objects are entities and predicates are relations ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt)).

### System Modules

The system contains four sub-modules: question template construction, seed question generation, question expansion, and selection ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt)). Given an input KB, a small set of question templates is first constructed so that each template is associated with a predicate; a seed question set is then generated by applying the template set to the input KB; finally, more questions are generated from related questions iteratively retrieved from a search engine using already-obtained questions as queries ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt)).

## The Template Mechanism

Templates consist of a transcription of the predicate in the KB (for example, `performsActivity ⇒ how to`) together with placeholders for the subject (`#X#`) and the object (`#Y#`) ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt)). The paper gives a concrete illustration from the in-house power tool KB: the template "how to use #X#" is first constructed for the predicate "performsActivity", and the seed question "how to use jigsaw" is generated by applying that template to the triple ⟨jigsaw, performsActivity, CurveCut⟩ ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt)). Because templates are keyed to predicates rather than to individual entities or triples, a single template can serve many triples that share the same predicate ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt)).

## Hand-Crafted Template Counts by Evaluation Setting

### Freebase Evaluation: 106 Templates

For the Freebase evaluation, the authors compared their system against the previous state of the art on 500 randomly selected triples from Freebase ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt)). For those 500 triples they report: "we hand-crafted 106 templates, as these triples share only 53 distinct predicates (we made 2 templates for each predicate on average)" ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt)). The arithmetic is transparent: 53 distinct predicates × approximately 2 templates per predicate = 106 templates. Applying those templates to the triples generated **991 seed questions**, and a further **1,529 questions were retrieved from Google** ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt)). Using an averaged language model score as an index, the top 500 questions were selected for comparison with the prior system's results ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt)).

### In-House Power Tool Domain: 163 Templates

The final experiment was run on the authors' in-house KB in the power tool domain, which contains **67 distinct predicates, 293 distinct subjects, and 279 distinct objects** ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt)). Regarding template construction, the paper states: "For the 67 predicates, we hand-craft 163 templates" ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt)). From these templates the system generated **12,228 seed questions**, from which **20,000 more questions were expanded with Google** ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt)). The average number of templates per predicate in this setting is therefore roughly 2.43 (163 ÷ 67), a slightly denser coverage than the Freebase setting's average of 2.0 ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt)).

It also confirms the power tool figures of 67 predicates and 163 templates ([Third-party research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)).

## Source Discrepancy Regarding the 163 Power Tool Templates

One point of divergence exists between the primary paper and the third-party note. The primary paper states plainly that for the 67 power tool predicates the authors "hand-craft 163 templates" ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt)). The third-party note, by contrast, asserts that "the 163 templates were generated automatically from the predicate names rather than hand-crafted" ([Third-party research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)). The note also rephrases the paper's statement about human labor as "the only human labor in this work is the Freebase question template construction," whereas the primary paper says, without restricting the claim to Freebase, that "the only human labor in this work is the question template construction" ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt); [Third-party research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)).

My assessment, based on the evidence provided, is that the primary paper's wording should be preferred. The paper's own general statement that template construction is the sole human labor, and its explicit use of the verb "hand-craft" for both the Freebase and power tool template sets, indicate that all 163 power tool templates were manual artifacts ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt)). The third-party note's automatic-generation claim appears to be an unsupported inference rather than a reported fact, and it is not corroborated anywhere in the primary text. Consequently, the defensible answer to the query "How many hand-crafted templates did they have to make?" is **106 for the Freebase benchmark**, **163 for the power tool domain**, and **269 in total across the two reported evaluations**, with the caveat that a third-party source disputes the hand-crafted status of the second set.

## Why the Hand-Crafted Template Burden Stays Small

The authors argue that their system does not require a large number of templates for two reasons: (1) the iterative question expansion can produce a large number of questions even from a relatively small number of seed questions, as demonstrated in the experiments; and (2) multiple entities in the KB share the same predicates ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt)). The Freebase setting illustrates this directly: 500 triples collapsed onto just 53 distinct predicates, so a 106-template inventory (two per predicate) was sufficient to cover the entire evaluation set ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt)). The power tool setting shows the same economy at larger scale: 67 predicates and 293 subjects plus 279 objects were covered by 163 templates, which then yielded 12,228 seed questions before any web expansion ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt)).

The authors also highlight a secondary advantage: because the web is self-updating, the system can easily generate updated questions ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt)). This contrasts with approaches that depend on a fixed corpus of human-labeled training pairs, which cannot refresh themselves without new annotation effort ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt)).

## Downstream Results Enabled by the Templates

### Human Ratings

Three native English speakers evaluated the fluency and naturalness of both systems' outputs on a 4-point scale where 4 is best, judging whether people would ask the questions in reality ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt)). The averaged human ratings are shown below ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt)):

| System | Grammaticality | Naturalness |
|---|---|---|
| Prior work (Serban et al., 2016) | 3.36 | 3.14 |
| Ours | **3.53** | **3.31** |

The paper notes that naturalness scores are lower than grammaticality scores for both methods because naturalness is a stricter metric: a natural question should also be grammatical ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt)). The authors attribute their advantage to the fact that their questions resemble what people actually ask on the web, whereas the prior system produced ungrammatical or unnatural items such as "who was someone who was involved in the leukemia ?" and "what's one of the mountain where can you found in argentina in netflix ?" ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt)).

### Domain Relevance and Selection

To filter web-retrieved candidates, the system uses a skip-gram model for domain relevance and a language model for fluency ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt)). Domain relevance is defined as Rel(q) = cos(v(q), v(D_in)), where v(·) is the document embedding formed by averaging word embeddings within the document, and D_in is the seed question set treated as in-domain data ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt)). Fluency uses the averaged language model score AvgLM(q) = Lm(q)/Len(q), where Lm(·) is the general-domain language model score (log probability) and Len(·) is the word count ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt)). Thresholds t_rel and t_flu are applied to filter out low-scoring questions ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt)). For fluency evaluation, a 4-gram language model was trained on Gigaword (LDC2011T07) with Kneser-Ney smoothing ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt)).

On the web snippet dataset — 10,060 training and 2,280 test snippets in 8 classes, averaging 18 words each — the authors' domain-relevance method achieved a precision of **85.65**, compared with 82.18, 85.31, and 85.48 for the three prior methods ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt)). The authors suggest this is because word embeddings capture similarity between distinct words such as "finance" and "economy," which traditional topic models handle poorly ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt)).

## Discussion and Assessment

Three observations follow from the reported figures. First, the template inventory is genuinely small relative to the KB coverage it enables: 106 templates for 500 Freebase triples and 163 templates for 67 power tool predicates is a modest and bounded annotation task, especially when compared with the 10,000 human-generated question–answer pairs used by the prior NMT approach ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt)). Second, the template count scales with predicate diversity rather than with triple count, which is the key design decision that keeps manual effort low ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt); [Third-party research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)). Third, the exact total depends on which evaluation setting is being discussed: a reader interested only in the Freebase benchmark should cite 106, while a reader describing the full paper should cite 106 plus 163.

The third-party note's claim that the power tool templates were automatically generated is the one element I would not adopt without further confirmation ([Third-party research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)). The primary source uses the verb "hand-craft" and asserts that template construction is the sole human labor in the work, so treating 163 as a hand-crafted figure is the more faithful reading of the available evidence ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt)).

## Conclusion

The answer to the query is that the system required **106 hand-crafted templates for the Freebase evaluation** and **163 hand-crafted templates for the in-house power tool domain**, totaling **269 across the two reported settings** ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt)). The Freebase figure derives from 500 randomly selected triples sharing only 53 distinct predicates at roughly two templates per predicate, which produced 991 seed questions and 1,529 web-retrieved questions ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt)). The power tool figure derives from 67 distinct predicates and 163 templates, which produced 12,228 seed questions and 20,000 expanded questions ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt)). These modest template inventories, combined with iterative web exploration and fluency/domain-relevance filtering, yielded questions that human graders rated higher than the prior state of the art on both grammaticality (3.53 vs. 3.36) and naturalness (3.31 vs. 3.14) ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt)). The paper leaves automatic answer mining as future work, since the current system generates questions without answers ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt)).
