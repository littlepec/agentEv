# Hand-Crafted Template Counts in Question Generation from a Knowledge Base with Web Exploration

## Executive Summary

The query asks how many hand-crafted templates the authors had to make. Based on the provided sources, the unambiguous hand-crafted template count is **106** for the Freebase evaluation. The primary document states that for 500 randomly selected Freebase triples, the authors hand-crafted 106 templates because those triples shared only 53 distinct predicates, resulting in an average of 2 templates per predicate ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt)). A third-party research note confirms that the only human labor in the work is the Freebase question template construction ([Third-party research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)). The same primary document also reports 163 templates for an in-house power tool knowledge base with 67 predicates ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt)), but the third-party note states that those 163 templates were generated automatically from predicate names rather than hand-crafted ([Third-party research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)). Therefore, the confirmed hand-crafted template count is **106**. If one interprets the primary document literally and counts the power tool templates as hand-crafted as well, the total would be **269**, but the evidence for that total is internally inconsistent.

## Introduction

Question generation from a knowledge base (KB) is the task of generating questions related to the domain of an input KB ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt)). The paper by Song and Zhao proposes a system that reduces human effort by combining a small number of hand-crafted templates with massive web resources ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt)). The system first generates a seed question set by applying templates to KB triples, then iteratively retrieves more questions from a search engine using already-obtained questions as queries, and finally selects questions by estimating fluency and domain relevance ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt)). The only human labor reported in the paper is question template construction ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt)). Consequently, the number of hand-crafted templates is a central measure of the system's human effort.

The provided information includes two sources: the primary paper text, labeled document_1.txt, and a third-party research note, labeled document_2.txt. The primary text contains the experimental details and template counts. The third-party note provides an interpretive summary and addresses key questions about hand-crafted templates, predicates, and template construction. This report synthesizes both sources to answer the query accurately and to explain the discrepancy regarding the power tool domain.

## Core Answer: 106 Hand-Crafted Templates for Freebase

The most direct answer to the query is that the authors hand-crafted **106 templates** for the Freebase evaluation. The primary document states: "For the 500 triples, we hand-crafted 106 templates, as these triples share only 53 distinct predicates (we made 2 templates for each predicate on average)" ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt)). This statement ties the template count directly to the predicate set rather than the number of triples. Because 500 triples shared only 53 distinct predicates, the authors did not need a unique template for every triple. Instead, they created an average of two templates per predicate, yielding 106 hand-crafted templates ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt)). The third-party note reiterates this design: "The paper reports 106 hand-crafted templates for those 500 Freebase triples. The triples share only 53 distinct predicates, so the authors made 2 templates for each predicate on average" ([Third-party research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)). It further states that this design "ties template creation to predicates, keeping the template set compact relative to the triple set" ([Third-party research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)).

Applying those 106 templates to the triples generated 991 seed questions ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt)). The experiment also retrieved 1,529 more questions from Google ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt)). Thus, the 106 hand-crafted templates served as the seed for a much larger candidate question set, demonstrating that a relatively small manual investment can be amplified through web exploration. The third-party note emphasizes that "the only human labor in this work is the Freebase question template construction" ([Third-party research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)). This statement reinforces that the 106 templates are the primary manual component.

## Evidence from the Freebase Evaluation

The Freebase evaluation is described as the first experiment and compares the end-to-end system with a previous state-of-the-art method on 500 randomly selected triples ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt)). The authors obtained baseline results from an external source ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt)). The template count of 106 is not an arbitrary number; it follows from the predicate distribution. With 53 distinct predicates, making two templates per predicate on average produces 106 templates ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt)). This approach means the template set scales with predicate coverage rather than triple count. The third-party note confirms that "template creation follows the predicates present in the KB" ([Third-party research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)). If the KB had contained more distinct predicates, more templates would have been needed; if it had contained many triples but few predicates, the template set could remain compact.

The human evaluation further supports the effectiveness of this manual template effort. Three native English speakers rated fluency and naturalness on a 4-point scale, where 4 is best ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt)). The system's questions received an average grammaticality score of 3.53 and a naturalness score of 3.31, compared with 3.36 and 3.14 for the baseline ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt)). The naturalness score was lower than the grammaticality score for both methods because naturalness is a stricter metric; a natural question should also be grammatical ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt)). The paper also notes that the baseline generated ungrammatical, unnatural, or confusing questions, such as "who was someone who was involved in the leukemia?" and "what's one of the mountain where can you found in argentina in netflix?" ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt)). In contrast, the template-and-web-expansion approach produced questions that people usually ask on the web ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt)).

## Power Tool Domain: 163 Templates, but Hand-Crafted Status Is Disputed

The primary document also reports a domain-specific evaluation on an in-house KB in the power tool domain. That KB contains 67 distinct predicates, 293 distinct subjects, and 279 distinct objects ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt)). For those 67 predicates, the primary document states: "we hand-craft 163 templates" ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt)). The system generated 12,228 seed questions from which 20,000 more questions were expanded with Google ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt)). Table 4 in the paper shows examples such as "how to change circular saw blade," "how to measure lawn mower cutting height," and "how to cut a groove in wood without a router" ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt)). Most of these expanded questions were grammatical, relevant to the power tool domain, informative, and corresponded to specific answers ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt)).

However, the third-party research note directly contradicts the hand-crafted characterization for the power tool domain. It states: "For the in-house power tool domain knowledge base, the paper reports 67 predicates. For the 67 predicates, the 163 templates were generated automatically from the predicate names rather than hand-crafted" ([Third-party research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)). The note explains that this count differs from the Freebase template count because the domain-specific KB has its own predicate set and required its own template set ([Third-party research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)). It further asserts that these figures show template-building effort scales with predicate coverage in each evaluation ([Third-party research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)). If the third-party note is correct, then the 163 power tool templates were not part of the hand-crafted human labor. Instead, they were automatically derived from predicate names, leaving the 106 Freebase templates as the only hand-crafted templates.

## Resolving the Discrepancy

The discrepancy between the two sources is important because the query asks specifically about hand-crafted templates. The primary document contains two explicit statements about hand-crafting: 106 templates for Freebase and 163 templates for the power tool domain ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt)). If both are accepted literally, the authors hand-crafted 269 templates in total (106 + 163). However, the third-party note states that only the Freebase template construction was human labor and that the power tool templates were generated automatically from predicate names ([Third-party research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)). This third-party interpretation is internally consistent with the primary document's own claim that "the only human labor in this work is the question template construction" ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt)). That statement does not specify Freebase, but the third-party note does: it says the only human labor is the Freebase question template construction ([Third-party research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)). If the power tool templates were also hand-crafted, then the paper's claim that template construction is the only human labor would still hold, but the third-party note's clarification would be wrong. Conversely, if the power tool templates were automatic, then the primary document's phrase "we hand-craft 163 templates" may be an overstatement, an OCR artifact, or a shorthand for "we produced 163 templates" without emphasizing the automatic method.

From an evidence-weighting perspective, the original paper is the primary source and is generally more authoritative for its own experimental details. The third-party note is a secondary source but is specifically structured to answer key questions about hand-crafted templates, predicates, and template construction ([Third-party research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)). It directly addresses the query and draws a clear distinction: 106 hand-crafted templates for Freebase, 163 automatic templates for power tool ([Third-party research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)). The note also states that "the only human labor in this work is the Freebase question template construction" ([Third-party research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)). This is a strong, unambiguous claim. Given that the query asks for hand-crafted templates, and given the third-party note's explicit correction, the most defensible answer is **106 hand-crafted templates**.

Nevertheless, a fully transparent report should acknowledge the alternative. If one counts every template that the primary document describes with the phrase "hand-craft," the total is 269. But that total relies on a literal reading that conflicts with the third-party note and with the paper's emphasis on minimizing human effort. The paper's abstract states that a "small number of hand-crafted templates" are applied to the input KB ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt)). The Freebase experiment uses 106 templates for 500 triples, which is small relative to the triple count. The power tool experiment uses 163 templates for 67 predicates, which is a larger absolute number but still modest relative to the 12,228 seed questions it produces. If those 163 were automatic, the human effort remains solely the 106 Freebase templates. If those 163 were hand-crafted, the human effort doubles and more than doubles in raw count. The third-party note's interpretation therefore has practical significance: it changes the reported human cost of the system.

## Implications for Human Effort and System Design

The hand-crafted template count is not merely a numerical detail; it reflects the system's design philosophy. The authors argue that their system does not require a large number of templates because iterative question expansion can produce a large number of questions even with a relatively small seed set, and because multiple entities in the KB share the same predicates ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt)). Another advantage is that the system can easily generate updated questions as the web is self-updating ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt)). These design choices explain why 106 hand-crafted templates can support 500 triples and 991 seed questions, which are then expanded to 1,529 additional web-retrieved questions ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt)). The template set is tied to predicates, so the manual effort scales with predicate diversity, not with the number of entities or triples.

The domain relevance filtering also supports efficiency. The system uses a skip-gram model and a language model to evaluate domain relevance and fluency, respectively ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt)). Domain relevance is computed as the cosine similarity between the question embedding and the in-domain seed question set ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt)). Fluency is computed as the averaged language model score ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt)). Thresholds for relevance and fluency filter out low-scoring questions ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt)). On a web snippet dataset with 10,060 training and 2,280 test snippets in 8 classes, the method achieved 85.65% precision, outperforming previous methods at 85.48%, 85.31%, and 82.18% ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt)). This validation indicates that the selection step effectively complements the template-based generation, allowing a small hand-crafted template set to yield high-quality questions.

## Conclusion

The query asks how many hand-crafted templates the authors had to make. The clearest and most defensible answer is **106**. The primary document states that 106 templates were hand-crafted for 500 Freebase triples because those triples shared 53 distinct predicates, yielding an average of 2 templates per predicate ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt)). The third-party note confirms this count and identifies it as the only human labor in the work ([Third-party research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)). The primary document also reports 163 templates for an in-house power tool KB with 67 predicates ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt)), but the third-party note states that those 163 templates were generated automatically from predicate names rather than hand-crafted ([Third-party research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)). Therefore, the confirmed hand-crafted template count is 106. If one counts both the Freebase and power tool templates as hand-crafted based on the primary document's wording, the total would be 269, but that reading conflicts with the third-party note and with the paper's stated goal of minimizing human labor. The most reliable conclusion is that the authors hand-crafted 106 templates for the Freebase evaluation, and the power tool templates should not be counted as hand-crafted unless further evidence resolves the discrepancy.
