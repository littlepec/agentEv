# Hand-Crafted Templates in Knowledge-Base Question Generation: Reported Counts, Context, and Analytical Assessment

## Answering the Query Directly

The question of how many hand-crafted templates the authors "had to make" has two distinct and equally valid answers, because the reported system was evaluated on two separate knowledge bases, each with its own predicate inventory and therefore its own manual template-building burden. For the **Freebase evaluation, the authors hand-crafted 106 templates** to cover 500 randomly selected triples ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). For the **in-house power tool domain knowledge base, the authors hand-crafted 163 templates** to cover 67 distinct predicates ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt); [document_2.txt](document_2.txt)). Taken together, the reported manual template-construction effort across both experiments amounts to **269 templates**.

The remainder of this report situates those two figures within the system's architecture, explains why the counts differ, quantifies the downstream question yield that each template set produced, and assesses what the numbers reveal about the scalability of the template-plus-web-exploration approach.

## Background: The System That Required the Templates

The reported work addresses question generation from a knowledge base (KB), defined as the task of generating questions related to the domain of an input KB ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). The authors frame the problem as challenging precisely because function words and morphological forms for entities are abstracted away when a KB is created ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). Prior approaches, including the work identified as Seyler et al. (2015) and Serban et al. (2016), relied on massive human-labeled data; one cited approach trains a neural machine translation (NMT) system on 10,000 triple–question pairs, where the question half of each pair is human generated ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). The authors explicitly position their contribution as a significant reduction in that human effort by leveraging massive web resources ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt); [document_2.txt](document_2.txt)).

Within this design, the only human labor in the entire work is question template construction ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt); [document_2.txt](document_2.txt)). This is a critical framing point: the 106 and 163 figures are not incidental implementation details but the entire manual cost of the system as reported.

The pipeline is described as comprising four sub-modules: question template construction, seed question generation, question expansion, and question selection ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). Given an input KB, a small set of question templates is first constructed such that each template is associated with a predicate; a seed question set is generated by applying the template set to the KB; and finally more questions are retrieved iteratively from a search engine using already-obtained questions as search queries ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). Templates consist of a transcription of the predicate in the KB — for example, *performsActivity* ⇒ *how to* — plus placeholders for the subject (#X#) and the object (#Y#) ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). A concrete illustration is provided: the template "how to use #X#" is constructed for the predicate "performsActivity," and the seed question "how to use jigsaw" is generated by applying that template to the triple ⟨jigsaw, performsActivity, CurveCut⟩ ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)).

## The Freebase Evaluation: 106 Hand-Crafted Templates

The first experiment compares the system's output against a prior system on 500 randomly selected triples from Freebase ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). For these 500 triples, the authors hand-crafted **106 templates**, justified by the fact that the triples share only **53 distinct predicates** — meaning the authors made **2 templates for each predicate on average** ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt); [document_2.txt](document_2.txt)). A third-party research note independently confirms these same figures, stating that the triples share only 53 distinct predicates and that two templates per predicate were made on average, and emphasizing that this design "ties template creation to predicates, keeping the template set compact relative to the triple set" ([document_2.txt](document_2.txt)).

### Table 1: Freebase Evaluation — Template and Question Counts

| Metric | Reported value |
|---|---|
| Randomly selected Freebase triples | 500 |
| Distinct predicates among those triples | 53 |
| Hand-crafted templates | 106 |
| Average templates per predicate | 2 |
| Seed questions generated by applying templates | 991 |
| Additional questions retrieved from Google | 1,529 |
| Questions selected for comparison (top-ranked by LM) | 500 |
| Human graders | 3 native English speakers |
| Rating scheme | 4-point scale (4 = best) |

*Sources: [document_1.txt](document_1.txt); [document_2.txt](document_2.txt).*

The yield from the 106 templates is notable. Applying them to the 500 triples generated **991 seed questions**, and **1,529 more questions were retrieved from Google** ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt); [document_2.txt](document_2.txt)). In other words, roughly two seed questions were produced per triple (991 ÷ 500 ≈ 1.98), which is consistent with the average of two templates per predicate. To evaluate fluency, the authors trained a 4-gram language model on Gigaword (LDC2011T07) with Kneser–Ney smoothing, and used the averaged language model score as the index for selecting the top 500 questions ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)).

## The Domain-Specific Evaluation: 163 Hand-Crafted Templates

The second, domain-specific experiment uses an in-house KB in the power tool domain, containing **67 distinct predicates, 293 distinct subjects, and 279 distinct objects** ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). For the 67 predicates, the authors hand-crafted **163 templates** ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt); [document_2.txt](document_2.txt)). This yields an average of approximately **2.43 templates per predicate**, higher than the flat 2-per-predicate average in the Freebase setting.

The third-party research note explicitly addresses why the two counts differ: the domain-specific KB "has its own predicate set and required its own template set," and the two figures "show the manual template-building effort scales with predicate coverage in each evaluation" ([document_2.txt](document_2.txt)). This is a defensible reading of the design, and it means the relevant unit of manual cost is not the number of triples but the number of distinct predicates.

### Table 2: Power Tool Domain KB — Template and Question Counts

| Metric | Reported value |
|---|---|
| Distinct predicates | 67 |
| Distinct subjects | 293 |
| Distinct objects | 279 |
| Hand-crafted templates | 163 |
| Average templates per predicate | ≈2.43 |
| Seed questions generated | 12,228 |
| Additional questions expanded with Google | 20,000 |
| Domain-relevance model | Skip-gram model trained on Wikipedia dumps |

*Sources: [document_1.txt](document_1.txt); [document_2.txt](document_2.txt).*

The scaling effect is dramatic in absolute terms: 163 templates produced **12,228 seed questions**, from which **20,000 more questions were expanded using Google** ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). For evaluating domain relevance, the authors used the same language model as in the first experiment and additionally learned a skip-gram model on Wikipedia dumps ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)).

## Why So Few Templates? The Authors' Stated Rationale

The authors explicitly state that the system "does not require a large number of templates" for two reasons: (1) the iterative question expansion can produce a large number of questions even with a relatively small number of seed questions, and (2) multiple entities in the KB share the same predicates ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). Both claims are empirically supported by the reported counts. On the first claim, 106 Freebase templates yielded 991 seed questions and 1,529 retrieved questions; 163 power-tool templates yielded 12,228 seed questions and 20,000 expanded questions. On the second claim, the 500 Freebase triples collapsed onto just 53 predicates, meaning that on average each predicate was instantiated by roughly 9.4 distinct triples (500 ÷ 53 ≈ 9.4). A single template therefore services multiple triples, amortizing the manual cost.

A secondary advantage claimed is that the system can easily generate updated questions because "web is self-updating consistently" ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)) — an argument that further reduces the long-run marginal cost of each template.

## The Expansion Mechanism That Multiplies Template Value

The expansion procedure is formalized in Algorithm 1: the expanded question set *E* is initialized as the seed question set; in each iteration, an already-obtained question is expanded from the web, and retrieved questions are added to *E* if *E* does not already contain them; the maximum number of iterations is bounded by *I_max* ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). This bounded iterative retrieval is what converts a modest template investment into tens of thousands of candidate questions. Examples of expanded power-tool questions include "how to change circular saw blade," "how to measure lawn mower cutting height," "how to sharpen drill bits on bench grinder," "how does an oscillating multi tool work," and the more complex "how to cut a groove in wood without a router" ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). The authors note that most expanded questions are grammatical and relevant to the power tool domain and correspond to a specific answer, with the exception of context-poor items such as "do i need a hammer drill" ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)).

## Outcomes: Did the 106 and 163 Templates Produce Good Questions?

Human evaluation for the Freebase experiment used three native English speakers rating fluency and naturalness on a 4-point scheme where 4 is best ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). The system's questions received a grammaticality score of **3.53** and a naturalness score of **3.31**, compared with **3.36** and **3.14** for the comparison system ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). The authors report that their questions were judged significantly better on grammaticality and naturalness ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)), and they attribute this to the fact that their questions are "what people usually ask on the web," whereas the comparison system produced ungrammatical, unnatural, or confusing outputs ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)).

On the web snippet dataset for domain classification — 10,060 training and 2,280 test snippets across 8 classes, averaging 18 words per snippet — the authors' domain-relevance method achieved a precision of **85.65**, compared with **82.18**, **85.31**, and **85.48** for three prior results ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)).

## Assessment and Opinion

Based on the reported evidence, the most defensible conclusion is that the template counts are not a weakness of the design but the point of it. The authors traded 10,000 human-written triple–question pairs of the NMT-style approach for 106 and 163 predicate-level templates ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)), while still producing 991 and 12,228 seed questions respectively. The predicate-level amortization is the decisive mechanism: because the 500 Freebase triples shared only 53 predicates, roughly 9.4 triples were served per template on average, and because 67 power-tool predicates generated 163 templates, the domain KB required more templates per predicate — about 2.43 rather than 2 — to capture predicate-specific phrasing variety.

At the same time, the counts should be read with appropriate caution. The reported comparison system in the Freebase experiment is rendered as an anonymized placeholder ("?") throughout the provided excerpts, so the identity of the baseline in the rating tables cannot be confirmed from the supplied information alone, even though the reference list names Seyler et al. (2015) and Serban et al. (2016) ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). The template counts themselves, however, are stated unambiguously and are corroborated by an independent research note ([document_2.txt](document_2.txt)).

## Conclusion

The direct answer to the query is twofold: **106 hand-crafted templates** for the Freebase evaluation covering 500 triples and 53 distinct predicates, and **163 hand-crafted templates** for the in-house power tool domain KB covering 67 predicates — a combined total of **269 templates** ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt); [document_2.txt](document_2.txt)). These templates are the sole manual component of the system and generated 991 seed questions plus 1,529 web-retrieved questions in the Freebase setting, and 12,228 seed questions plus 20,000 expanded questions in the power tool setting ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). The evidence supports the authors' claim that template counts scale with predicate coverage rather than with triple volume, and that iterative web expansion can multiply a small, hand-crafted template set into a large, fluent, and domain-relevant question corpus.
