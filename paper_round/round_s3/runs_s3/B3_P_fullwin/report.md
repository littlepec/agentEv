# Hand-Crafted Template Counts in “Question Generation from a Knowledge Base with Web Exploration”

## Executive Summary

The question of how many hand-crafted templates the authors had to make does not have a single, unambiguous answer across the provided sources. According to the primary source—the original paper by Song and Zhao (2016)—the authors created **106 hand-crafted templates** for the Freebase evaluation and **163 hand-crafted templates** for the in-house power tool domain evaluation ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). If these two experimental setups are considered together, the total number of hand-crafted templates is **269** (106 + 163). However, a third-party research note reports a different figure for the power tool domain: it states that the authors hand-crafted **103 templates** for the 67 predicates in that domain and that the combined total across both evaluations was **209** ([Third-party research note](document_2.txt)). Because the primary source is the original peer-reviewed paper and the third-party note appears to contain a transcription or reporting error, the most reliable answer is **106 templates for Freebase and 163 templates for the power tool domain, for a total of 269 templates across the two evaluations** ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)).

This report examines the template-construction process, the specific counts reported in each evaluation, the discrepancy between sources, and the implications for human effort and system scalability. All substantive claims are grounded in the two provided documents, with priority given to the original paper as the more authoritative source.

## Introduction

Question generation from a knowledge base (KB) is the task of generating natural language questions related to the domain of an input KB ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). The paper by Song and Zhao (2016) proposes a system that generates fluent and natural questions from a KB while significantly reducing human effort by leveraging massive web resources. The system first generates a seed question set by applying a small number of hand-crafted templates to the input KB. It then retrieves additional questions by iteratively using already-obtained questions as search queries in a standard search engine. Finally, it selects questions by estimating their fluency and domain relevance ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)).

A central design feature of this system is that the **only human labor** involved is the construction of question templates ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). Therefore, knowing how many templates were hand-crafted is essential for understanding the system’s manual effort, scalability, and practical applicability. The original paper reports template counts for two separate evaluations: one on Freebase, a domain-general KB, and one on an in-house KB in the power tool domain ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). A third-party research note also addresses these counts but presents a conflicting figure for the power tool domain ([Third-party research note](document_2.txt)). This report resolves that discrepancy by favoring the primary source.

## Template Construction Methodology

In the proposed system, a small set of question templates is first constructed such that each template is associated with a predicate in the knowledge base ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). Each template consists of a transcription of the predicate in the KB—for example, the predicate “performsActivity” becomes “how to”—and placeholders for the subject (#X#) and the object (#Y#) ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). As a concrete example, the template “how to use #X#” is constructed for the predicate “performsActivity.” Applying this template to the triple ⟨jigsaw, performsActivity, CurveCut⟩ produces the seed question “how to use jigsaw” ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)).

A seed question set is then generated by applying the templates on the KB. The seed question set is further expanded through a search engine (e.g., Google, Bing) by iteratively forming each generated question as a search query to retrieve more related question candidates ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). Finally, a selection step filters candidates by estimating fluency and domain relevance ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). The paper emphasizes that the system does not require a large number of templates because the iterative question expansion can produce many questions even with a relatively small number of seed questions, and because multiple entities in the KB share the same predicates ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). The association between templates and predicates means that template creation follows the predicates present in the KB ([Third-party research note](document_2.txt)).

## Freebase Evaluation Template Count

For the Freebase evaluation, the authors randomly selected 500 triples from Freebase ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). These 500 triples shared only 53 distinct predicates. To cover these predicates, the authors hand-crafted **106 templates**, which corresponds to an average of 2 templates per predicate ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). Applying these templates to the triples generated **991 seed questions**. The experiment then retrieved **1,529 more questions** from Google ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). This design ties template creation to predicates, keeping the template set compact relative to the triple set ([Third-party research note](document_2.txt)).

The third-party research note corroborates the Freebase figure: it states that the paper reports 106 hand-crafted templates for those 500 Freebase triples, and that the triples share only 53 distinct predicates, so the authors made 2 templates for each predicate on average ([Third-party research note](document_2.txt)). It also confirms that applying the templates generated 991 seed questions and that 1,529 more questions were retrieved from Google ([Third-party research note](document_2.txt)). Thus, for the Freebase evaluation, there is no disagreement between the primary source and the third-party note: the number of hand-crafted templates is **106** ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807); [Third-party research note](document_2.txt)).

## Power Tool Domain Evaluation Template Count

The second evaluation uses an in-house KB in the power tool domain. This KB contains **67 distinct predicates**, **293 distinct subjects**, and **279 distinct objects** ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). For the 67 predicates, the original paper states that the authors hand-crafted **163 templates** ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). Using the same language model as in the first experiment and a skip-gram model trained on Wikipedia for domain relevance, the system generated **12,228 seed questions**, from which **20,000 more questions** were expanded with Google ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)).

However, the third-party research note reports a different number. It states: “For the in-house power tool domain knowledge base, the paper reports 67 predicates. For the 67 predicates, the authors hand-crafted **103 templates**. Together with the 106 Freebase templates, the authors wrote **209 templates in total**” ([Third-party research note](document_2.txt)). This directly conflicts with the primary source’s figure of 163 templates for the power tool domain. The third-party note also claims that the paper reports this total in the template-construction experiment, but the original paper does not state a combined total of 209 anywhere ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807); [Third-party research note](document_2.txt)).

Because the original paper is the primary source and the third-party note is a secondary summary, the **163 templates** figure from the original paper should be treated as authoritative ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). The discrepancy is likely due to a transcription error in the third-party note, such as misreading “163” as “103” or conflating the power tool count with another number. The third-party note itself does not provide a rationale for the 103 figure, and its arithmetic (106 + 103 = 209) reveals that the total is derived from the disputed power tool count rather than from an independent source ([Third-party research note](document_2.txt)). Therefore, the most reliable conclusion is that the authors hand-crafted **163 templates** for the power tool domain ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)).

## Discrepancy Analysis and Source Reliability

The following table summarizes the reported template counts from both sources.

| Evaluation | Primary Source (Song & Zhao, 2016) | Third-Party Research Note |
|------------|-------------------------------------|----------------------------|
| Freebase (500 triples, 53 distinct predicates) | 106 templates | 106 templates |
| Power tool domain (67 predicates) | 163 templates | 103 templates |
| Combined total across both evaluations | 269 templates (computed: 106 + 163) | 209 templates (reported: 106 + 103) |
| Source type | Original arXiv paper | Secondary summary note |
| Reliability | High (primary source) | Lower (secondary, contains inconsistency) |

*Note.* The combined total of 269 is not explicitly stated in the original paper; it is computed by adding the two reported counts ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). The third-party note explicitly states 209 as the total but relies on the disputed 103 figure ([Third-party research note](document_2.txt)).

The primary source is the original paper by Song and Zhao (2016), available as arXiv preprint 1610.03807. It provides detailed methodology, experimental setups, and exact numbers. The third-party research note is a secondary summary that addresses key questions about the paper but introduces a conflicting figure. In cases of conflict, the primary source should be preferred because it is the original record of the research and is less likely to contain transcription errors ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). Moreover, the third-party note’s claim that the paper reports a total of 209 templates is not supported by the original text, which does not mention a combined total at all ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807); [Third-party research note](document_2.txt)). This further undermines the reliability of the 103/209 figures.

## Implications for Human Effort and Scalability

Understanding the template counts is important because template construction is the only human labor in the system ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). The Freebase evaluation required 106 templates for 53 predicates, or about 2 templates per predicate ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). The power tool evaluation required 163 templates for 67 predicates, or approximately 2.43 templates per predicate ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). These figures show that the manual template-building effort scales with predicate coverage in each evaluation ([Third-party research note](document_2.txt)). Even so, the system remains relatively efficient because a small number of templates can generate a large number of seed questions: 106 templates yielded 991 seed questions in Freebase, and 163 templates yielded 12,228 seed questions in the power tool domain ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)).

The paper also notes that the system does not require a large number of templates because iterative question expansion can produce many questions from a relatively small number of seed questions, and because multiple entities in the KB share the same predicates ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). Another advantage is that the system can easily generate updated questions as the web is self-updating ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). These design choices help justify the manual effort of crafting 269 templates across both evaluations. If the third-party note’s lower figure of 209 were correct, the manual effort would appear even smaller, but the weight of evidence from the primary source supports the higher total of 269 ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)).

## Conclusion

Based on the primary source, the authors hand-crafted **106 templates** for the Freebase evaluation and **163 templates** for the in-house power tool domain evaluation ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). Across both evaluations, the total number of hand-crafted templates is therefore **269** (106 + 163). A third-party research note reports 103 templates for the power tool domain and a combined total of 209, but this conflicts with the original paper and appears to be a secondary-source error ([Third-party research note](document_2.txt)). For any inquiry into the manual template-construction effort of this system, the most defensible answer is **106 templates for Freebase and 163 for the power tool domain, totaling 269 templates across the two reported experiments** ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)).
