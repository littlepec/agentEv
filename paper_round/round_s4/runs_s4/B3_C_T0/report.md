# How Many Hand-Crafted Templates Were Required? A Detailed Analysis of Question Generation from a Knowledge Base

## Introduction
Question generation from a knowledge base (KB) is the task of generating questions related to the domain of the input KB ([Document 1](document_1.txt)). The challenge is that function words and morphological forms for entities are abstracted away when a KB is created ([Document 1](document_1.txt)). Previous work relies on massive human-labeled data to address this challenge ([Document 1](document_1.txt)). The proposed system reduces human effort by leveraging massive web resources ([Document 1](document_1.txt)). The only human labor in this work is the question template construction ([Document 1](document_1.txt)). Therefore, a central question is: how many hand-crafted templates did the authors have to make? Based on the provided sources, the answer is not a single number because the paper reports two separate evaluations: 106 hand-crafted templates for the Freebase evaluation and 163 hand-crafted templates for the in-house power tool domain knowledge base ([Document 1](document_1.txt); [Document 2](document_2.txt)). If one combines both reported efforts, the total is 269 templates, but the authors present them as distinct experimental setups. This report analyzes those counts, their derivation, and their significance.

## The Role of Templates in the Question Generation System
The system first constructs a small set of question templates ([Document 1](document_1.txt)). Each template is associated with a predicate in the KB ([Document 1](document_1.txt); [Document 2](document_2.txt)). Templates consist of a transcription of the predicate (e.g., performsActivity ⇒ how to) and placeholders for the subject (#X#) and the object (#Y#) ([Document 1](document_1.txt)). A seed question set is generated by applying the templates on the KB ([Document 1](document_1.txt)). For example, template “how to use #X#” is constructed for predicate “performsActivity” ([Document 1](document_1.txt)). Applying it to triple ⟨jigsaw, performsActivity, CurveCut⟩ yields seed question “how to use jigsaw” ([Document 1](document_1.txt)). Then more questions are retrieved from a search engine by iteratively forming already obtained questions as search queries ([Document 1](document_1.txt)). Finally, questions are selected by estimating fluency and domain relevance ([Document 1](document_1.txt)). Because template construction is the only manual step, the number of templates directly reflects the human effort required ([Document 1](document_1.txt); [Document 2](document_2.txt)).

## Template Counts in the Freebase Evaluation
### Experimental Setup and Count
The first evaluation compares the system on 500 randomly selected triples from Freebase ([Document 1](document_1.txt)). For these 500 triples, the authors hand-crafted 106 templates ([Document 1](document_1.txt); [Document 2](document_2.txt)). The triples share only 53 distinct predicates ([Document 1](document_1.txt)). The authors made 2 templates for each predicate on average ([Document 1](document_1.txt); [Document 2](document_2.txt)). The arithmetic is straightforward: 53 distinct predicates × 2 templates per predicate = 106 templates ([Document 1](document_1.txt)). This design ties template creation to predicates, keeping the template set compact relative to the triple set ([Document 2](document_2.txt)).

### Seed and Expanded Questions
Applying the 106 templates to the 500 triples generated 991 seed questions ([Document 1](document_1.txt); [Document 2](document_2.txt)). Then 1529 more questions were retrieved from Google ([Document 1](document_1.txt)). Thus, from 106 hand-crafted templates, the system produced 991 seed questions and 1529 expanded questions, illustrating the leverage of the approach ([Document 1](document_1.txt); [Document 2](document_2.txt)). The template count is therefore small relative to the final question pool.

## Template Counts in the Power Tool Domain Evaluation
### Domain-Specific KB
The second evaluation uses an in-house KB in the power tool domain ([Document 1](document_1.txt)). This KB contains 67 distinct predicates, 293 distinct subjects, and 279 distinct objects ([Document 1](document_1.txt)). For the 67 predicates, the authors hand-craft 163 templates ([Document 1](document_1.txt); [Document 2](document_2.txt)). The count differs from the Freebase template count because the domain-specific KB has its own predicate set and required its own template set ([Document 2](document_2.txt)). These figures show that manual template-building effort scales with predicate coverage in each evaluation ([Document 2](document_2.txt)).

### Derivation and Ratio
With 67 predicates and 163 templates, the average number of templates per predicate is approximately 2.43 (163 ÷ 67 ≈ 2.43) ([Document 1](document_1.txt)). This is slightly higher than the 2 templates per predicate average in the Freebase evaluation, suggesting that the power tool domain may require more nuanced template variations per predicate ([Document 1](document_1.txt); [Document 2](document_2.txt)). The authors do not state that the higher ratio is problematic; rather, they emphasize that the only human labor is template construction ([Document 1](document_1.txt)).

### Seed and Expanded Questions
From the power tool KB, the system generated 12,228 seed questions ([Document 1](document_1.txt)). Then 20,000 more questions were expanded with Google ([Document 1](document_1.txt)). The authors report that most expanded questions are grammatical and relevant to the power tool domain ([Document 1](document_1.txt)). Examples include “how to change circular saw blade,” “how to measure lawn mower cutting height,” “how to sharpen drill bits on bench grinder,” “how does an oscillating multi tool work,” “how to cut a groove in wood without a router,” “what type of sander to use on deck,” “do i need a hammer drill,” “can i use acrylic paint on wood,” and “how to use a sharpening stone with oil” ([Document 1](document_1.txt)). Most questions are informative and correspond to a specific answer, except “do I need a hammer drill,” which lacks context information ([Document 1](document_1.txt)). The system also generates complex questions such as “how to cut a groove in wood without a router” ([Document 1](document_1.txt)).

If one asks for a single total of hand-crafted templates across both reported experiments, the sum is 269 (106 + 163) ([Document 1](document_1.txt)). However, the authors present these as separate evaluations, not as a cumulative template set for a single system deployment ([Document 1](document_1.txt); [Document 2](document_2.txt)). Therefore, the most accurate answer is: 106 templates for the Freebase benchmark and 163 templates for the power tool domain benchmark. The Freebase count is the one most directly comparable to prior work, since the paper compares with a previous method on 500 random selected triples from Freebase ([Document 1](document_1.txt)). The power tool count is specific to the in-house domain KB ([Document 1](document_1.txt)).

## Why the Number of Templates Is Manageable
The authors argue that their system does not require a large number of templates because (1) the iterative question expansion can produce a large number of questions even with a relatively small number of seed questions, and (2) multiple entities in the KB share the same predicates ([Document 1](document_1.txt)). This explains why 106 templates can generate 991 seed questions in Freebase, and why 163 templates can generate 12,228 seed questions in the power tool domain ([Document 1](document_1.txt)). Another advantage is that the system can easily generate updated questions as the web is self-updating consistently ([Document 1](document_1.txt)). The only human labor remains the question template construction ([Document 1](document_1.txt); [Document 2](document_2.txt)). Thus, the template counts of 106 and 163 are not merely raw numbers; they represent the entire manual annotation burden of the reported system.

## Quality and Evaluation Results
The paper reports that human graders evaluated the generated questions. On Freebase, questions generated by the proposed system were judged to be more fluent than those of the baseline ([Document 1](document_1.txt)). In a 4-point scheme where 4 is the best, the system achieved a grammaticality score of 3.53 and a naturalness score of 3.31, compared with 3.36 and 3.14 for the baseline ([Document 1](document_1.txt)). The baseline is anonymized as “?” in the provided source ([Document 1](document_1.txt)). The evaluation used three native English speakers who assessed fluency and naturalness, specifically whether people will ask the questions in reality ([Document 1](document_1.txt)). The system also used a 4-gram language model trained on Gigaword with Kneser-Ney smoothing to select top questions by averaged language model score ([Document 1](document_1.txt)). For the power tool domain, a skip-gram model was learned on Wikipedia to evaluate domain relevance ([Document 1](document_1.txt)). These evaluation details matter because they show that the relatively small template counts did not prevent the system from achieving better fluency and naturalness ratings than the baseline ([Document 1](document_1.txt)).

## Limitations and Contextual Considerations
Several limitations should be noted when interpreting the template counts. First, the Freebase evaluation uses 500 randomly selected triples, and the template count of 106 is tied to the 53 distinct predicates in that sample ([Document 1](document_1.txt); [Document 2](document_2.txt)). A different sample of triples might have a different predicate distribution and thus require a different number of templates ([Document 1](document_1.txt)). Second, the power tool domain KB has 67 predicates and required 163 templates, showing that template counts scale with predicate coverage rather than with the number of entities or triples alone ([Document 2](document_2.txt)). Third, the paper does not report a universal template library that covers all possible KBs; the counts are experiment-specific ([Document 1](document_1.txt); [Document 2](document_2.txt)). Fourth, the baseline in the provided text is anonymized as “?” so the exact comparison method cannot be fully identified from the given information ([Document 1](document_1.txt)). Fifth, the paper’s claim that the only human labor is template construction assumes that no other manual annotation is needed, such as for evaluation or for cleaning retrieved questions ([Document 1](document_1.txt)). Despite these limitations, the reported template counts are clear and directly stated: 106 for Freebase and 163 for the power tool domain ([Document 1](document_1.txt); [Document 2](document_2.txt)).

## Conclusion
The question “How many hand-crafted templates did they have to make?” has a context-dependent answer based on the provided sources. In the Freebase evaluation, the authors hand-crafted 106 templates for 500 randomly selected triples that shared 53 distinct predicates, using an average of 2 templates per predicate ([Document 1](document_1.txt); [Document 2](document_2.txt)). In the in-house power tool domain evaluation, the authors hand-crafted 163 templates for 67 distinct predicates ([Document 1](document_1.txt); [Document 2](document_2.txt)). If both experiments are considered together, the total number of hand-crafted templates is 269 ([Document 1](document_1.txt)). However, because the paper reports these as separate evaluations, the most precise response is to state both figures: 106 templates for Freebase and 163 templates for the power tool domain. The authors emphasize that template construction is the only human labor in the system and that the number of templates remains small relative to the thousands of seed and expanded questions generated ([Document 1](document_1.txt); [Document 2](document_2.txt)). Thus, the template counts are a central measure of the system’s human effort, and they demonstrate a deliberately compact manual design.
