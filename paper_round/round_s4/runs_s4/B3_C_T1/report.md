# Hand-Crafted Template Counts in Question Generation from a Knowledge Base with Web Exploration

## Direct Answer to the Query

The authors reported two separate template-construction efforts. For the Freebase evaluation, they hand-crafted **106 templates** ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). For the in-house power tool domain knowledge base, they hand-crafted **163 templates** ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). Across both evaluations, this amounts to **269 hand-crafted templates**. These templates were the only human labor in the system, as the paper explicitly states that the only human labor in this work is question template construction ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt); [document_2.txt](document_2.txt)). The template counts are tied to the predicates present in each knowledge base: 53 distinct predicates in the Freebase sample and 67 distinct predicates in the power tool KB ([document_2.txt](document_2.txt)).

## Introduction and Context

Question generation from a knowledge base (KB) is the task of generating questions related to the domain of the input KB ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). This task is challenging because function words and morphological forms for entities are abstracted away when a KB is created ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). Previous work, such as Seyler et al. (2015) and Serban et al. (2016), relies on massive human-labeled data ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). For example, one approach treats question generation as a machine translation problem and trains a neural machine translation (NMT) system with 10,000 ⟨triple, question⟩ pairs ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). The question part of those pairs is human generated, which requires a large amount of human effort, and the grammaticality and naturalness of generated questions cannot be guaranteed ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). In contrast, the proposed system significantly reduces human effort by leveraging massive web resources ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)).

The proposed system consists of four sub-modules: question template construction, seed question generation, question expansion, and selection ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). Given an input KB, a small set of question templates is first constructed such that each template is associated with a predicate ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). A seed question set is then generated by applying the templates on the KB ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). More questions are retrieved from a search engine by iteratively forming already-obtained questions as search queries ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). Finally, questions are selected by estimating their fluency and domain relevance ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). The only human labor in this work is the question template construction ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). Therefore, the number of hand-crafted templates is a central metric of the system's human-effort requirements.

## Template Construction in the Freebase Evaluation

### Predicate Distribution and Template Design

For the Freebase evaluation, the authors used 500 randomly selected triples from Freebase ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt); [document_2.txt](document_2.txt)). These 500 triples share only 53 distinct predicates ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt); [document_2.txt](document_2.txt)). To cover these predicates, the authors hand-crafted 106 templates ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt); [document_2.txt](document_2.txt)). On average, this is two templates for each predicate ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt); [document_2.txt](document_2.txt)). This design ties template creation to predicates, keeping the template set compact relative to the triple set ([document_2.txt](document_2.txt)).

### Seed Question Generation and Expansion

Applying the 106 templates to the Freebase triples generated 991 seed questions ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt); [document_2.txt](document_2.txt)). The system then retrieved 1529 more questions from Google ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt); [document_2.txt](document_2.txt)). This expansion step uses an iterative algorithm. As shown in Algorithm 1, the expanded question set E is initialized as the seed question set (Line 1) ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). In each iteration, an already-obtained question is expanded from the web, and retrieved questions are added to E if E does not contain them (Lines 6-10) ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). The maximum number of iterations is limited by I_max (Line 4) ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). This approach allows a relatively small number of seed questions to produce a large number of candidate questions.

For fluency evaluation, the authors trained a 4-gram language model (LM) on Gigaword (LDC2011T07) with Kneser-Ney smoothing ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). Using the averaged language model score as an index, the top 500 questions were selected to compare with the baseline results ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). Three native English speakers evaluated the fluency and naturalness of both results on a 4-point scheme where 4 is the best ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). The system's generated questions received a grammaticality score of 3.53 and a naturalness score of 3.31, compared with the baseline's 3.36 and 3.14, respectively ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). Table 1 provides examples comparing generated questions, and Table 2 presents the human ratings ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). These results indicate that the 106 hand-crafted templates, combined with web expansion and selection, produced questions judged more fluent than the baseline ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)).

## Template Construction in the Power Tool Domain Evaluation

### Domain-Specific KB Characteristics

The third experiment was conducted on an in-house KB in the power tool domain ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). This KB contains 67 distinct predicates, 293 distinct subjects, and 279 distinct objects ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). For the 67 predicates, the authors hand-crafted 163 templates ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt); [document_2.txt](document_2.txt)). This count differs from the Freebase template count because the domain-specific KB has its own predicate set and required its own template set ([document_2.txt](document_2.txt)). On average, this is approximately 2.43 templates per predicate. The paper reports that these figures show the manual template-building effort scales with predicate coverage in each evaluation ([document_2.txt](document_2.txt)).

### Seed Question Generation and Expansion

Using the 163 templates, the system generated 12,228 seed questions ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). From these, 20,000 more questions were expanded with Google ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). The authors used the same language model as in the first experiment and learned a skip-gram model on Wikipedia for evaluating domain relevance ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). Table 4 shows example expanded questions, including "how to change circular saw blade," "how to measure lawn mower cutting height," "how to sharpen drill bits on bench grinder," "how does an oscillating multi tool work," "how to cut a groove in wood without a router," "what type of sander to use on deck," "do I need a hammer drill," "can I use acrylic paint on wood," and "how to use a sharpening stone with oil" ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). The authors note that most expanded questions are grammatical and relevant to the power tool domain ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). Most are informative and correspond to a specific answer, except "do I need a hammer drill," which lacks context information ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). In addition to simple factoid questions, the system generates many complex questions such as "how to cut a groove in wood without a router" ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). This demonstrates that 163 hand-crafted templates can yield a large and diverse set of questions when combined with web expansion.

Another advantage is that the system can easily generate updated questions as the web is self-updating consistently ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)).

## Why the Template Counts Matter

The number of hand-crafted templates is the primary measure of human effort in this system. The authors state that the only human labor in this work is question template construction ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). By keeping the template set small, the system avoids the massive human-labeled data required by previous NMT-based approaches, which used 10,000 ⟨triple, question⟩ pairs ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). For Freebase, 106 templates covered 53 predicates across 500 triples ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). For the power tool domain, 163 templates covered 67 predicates and enabled the generation of over 32,000 questions (12,228 seed plus 20,000 expanded) ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). The difference between 106 and 163 templates reflects the different predicate sets and the domain-specific nature of the power tool KB ([document_2.txt](document_2.txt)). The Freebase triples shared only 53 distinct predicates, while the power tool KB had 67 distinct predicates ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). The authors made two templates per predicate on average for Freebase and a somewhat higher average for the power tool domain ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt); [document_2.txt](document_2.txt)).

The evaluation results also support the effectiveness of this approach. Human graders judged the system's questions to be more fluent than the baseline's on 500 randomly selected Freebase triples ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). The grammaticality score was 3.53 versus 3.36, and the naturalness score was 3.31 versus 3.14 ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). These ratings were collected from three native English speakers using a 4-point scheme ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). The authors note that word embeddings capture the similarity between distinct words, which helps the system outperform previous methods ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). This suggests that even with a limited number of hand-crafted templates, the combination of web expansion and embedding-based selection can yield high-quality questions.

## Limitations and Open Questions

Several limitations are evident from the provided information. First, the template counts are specific to the evaluations reported. The Freebase evaluation used 500 randomly selected triples, while the power tool evaluation used an in-house KB with 67 predicates ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). Scaling to a new domain would require new templates, as template creation follows the predicates present in the KB ([document_2.txt](document_2.txt)). Second, the exact number of templates needed depends on how many distinct predicates are present and how many templates the authors choose to create per predicate. The paper reports averages but not the full distribution of templates per predicate ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). Third, the baseline system is anonymized in the provided text as "? )" ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). Although the references list Seyler et al. (2015) and Serban et al. (2016), the provided information does not explicitly identify which baseline was used for the human evaluation ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). Finally, the paper does not report the time or cost of constructing the 106 or 163 templates, only the counts ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). These open questions do not diminish the clear answer to the query: the authors hand-crafted 106 templates for Freebase and 163 templates for the power tool domain.

## Conclusion

In answer to the query "How many hand-crafted templates did they have to make?", the provided sources report two figures. For the Freebase evaluation, the authors hand-crafted 106 templates for 53 distinct predicates ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt); [document_2.txt](document_2.txt)). For the in-house power tool domain KB, they hand-crafted 163 templates for 67 distinct predicates ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt); [document_2.txt](document_2.txt)). Combined, this is 269 hand-crafted templates across both evaluations. These templates represent the only human labor in the system, and they enabled the generation of thousands of seed questions and tens of thousands of expanded questions through web exploration ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). The system's human evaluation on Freebase showed that its questions were judged more grammatical and natural than the baseline's ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). The modest template counts, relative to the large number of generated questions, demonstrate the effectiveness of leveraging web resources to reduce human effort in question generation from knowledge bases.
