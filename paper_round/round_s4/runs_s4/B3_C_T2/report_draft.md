# Hand-Crafted Templates in Knowledge Base Question Generation: A Detailed Report

## Introduction

Question generation from a knowledge base (KB) is the task of generating questions related to the domain of the input KB ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt)). The challenge is that function words and morphological forms for entities are abstracted away when a KB is created ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt)). Previous work relies on massive human-labeled data, such as 10,000 ⟨triple, question⟩ pairs ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt)). The proposed system reduces human effort by leveraging massive web resources ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt)). The only human labor in this work is the question template construction ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt)). Therefore, the number of hand-crafted templates is a central figure for understanding the system's manual cost.

The query asks: "How many hand-crafted templates did they have to make?" Based on the provided information, the paper reports two distinct template counts for two separate evaluations. For the Freebase evaluation on 500 randomly selected triples, the authors hand-crafted 106 templates ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt); [Third-party research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)). For the in-house power tool domain KB, the authors hand-crafted 163 templates ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt); [Third-party research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)). There is no single combined total reported across both experiments. Thus, the answer is 106 templates for the Freebase experiment and 163 templates for the domain-specific power tool experiment. This report details these figures, the context in which they were produced, and their implications.

## Background on the System and Template Construction

The proposed system contains sub-modules of question template construction, seed question generation, question expansion, and selection ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt)). Given an input KB, a small set of question templates is first constructed such that each template is associated with a predicate ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt)). These templates consist of a transcription of the predicate in the KB (e.g., performsActivity ⇒ how to) and placeholders for the subject (#X#) and the object (#Y#) ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt)). For example, template "how to use #X#" is constructed for predicate "performsActivity" ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt)). A seed question set is then generated by applying the templates on the KB ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt)). For instance, seed question "how to use jigsaw" is generated by applying the template on triple ⟨jigsaw, performsActivity, CurveCut⟩ ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt)).

The seed question set is further expanded through a search engine (e.g., Google, Bing), by iteratively forming each generated question as a search query to retrieve more related question candidates ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt)). Algorithm 1 describes the question expansion method: the expanded question set E is initialized as the seed question set, and in each iteration, an already-obtained question is expanded from the web and retrieved questions are added to E if E does not contain them ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt)). The maximum number of iterations is limited by I_max ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt)). Finally, a selection step is applied by estimating the fluency and domain relevance of each question candidate ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt)).

The only human labor in this work is the question template construction ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt); [Third-party research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)). The system does not require a large number of templates because: (1) the iterative question expansion can produce a large number of questions even with a relatively small number of seed questions, and (2) multiple entities in the KB share the same predicates ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt)). Another advantage is that the system can easily generate updated questions as the web is self-updating consistently ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt)). Thus, template construction is the manual bottleneck, and its size is directly tied to the predicates present in the KB.

## Template Counts in the Freebase Evaluation

### Experimental Setup and Template Count

The first evaluation compares the system with a baseline on 500 randomly selected triples from Freebase ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt)). For these 500 triples, the authors hand-crafted 106 templates ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt)). The triples share only 53 distinct predicates, so the authors made 2 templates for each predicate on average ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt); [Third-party research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)). This design ties template creation to predicates, keeping the template set compact relative to the triple set ([Third-party research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)). The paper reports 106 hand-crafted templates for those 500 Freebase triples ([Third-party research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)). The triples share only 53 distinct predicates, so the authors made 2 templates for each predicate on average ([Third-party research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)).

Applying the templates to the triples generated 991 seed questions ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt); [Third-party research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)). The experiment also retrieved 1529 more questions from Google ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt); [Third-party research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)). To evaluate the fluency of the candidate questions, the authors trained a 4-gram language model (LM) on gigaword (LDC2011T07) with Kneser Ney smoothing ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt)). Using the averaged language model score as index, the top 500 questions were selected to compare with the results from the baseline ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt)). Three native English speakers evaluated the fluency and the naturalness of both results based on a 4-point scheme where 4 is the best ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt)). The system achieved grammaticality 3.53 and naturalness 3.31, compared to the baseline's 3.36 and 3.14 ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt)).

### Interpretation of the 106 Figure

The 106 templates are the manual input required for the Freebase experiment. Because there are 53 distinct predicates and the authors made 2 templates per predicate on average, the arithmetic is consistent: 53 × 2 = 106 ([Third-party research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)). This count is explicitly reported as the number of hand-crafted templates for the Freebase evaluation ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt); [Third-party research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)). The templates are associated with predicates, not with individual triples, which explains why 500 triples require only 106 templates: many triples share the same predicate ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt)). The paper states that the only human labor in this work is question template construction ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt); [Third-party research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)). Therefore, for the Freebase evaluation, the answer to the query is 106 hand-crafted templates.

## Template Counts in the Domain-Specific Power Tool KB

### Experimental Setup and Template Count

The last experiment is on an in-house KB in the power tool domain ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt)). It contains 67 distinct predicates, 293 distinct subjects, and 279 distinct objects respectively ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt)). For the 67 predicates, the authors hand-craft 163 templates ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt); [Third-party research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)). This count differs from the Freebase template count because the domain-specific KB has its own predicate set and required its own template set ([Third-party research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)). The Freebase and power tool counts provide the reported template-construction figures for the system ([Third-party research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)). These figures show the manual template-building effort scales with predicate coverage in each evaluation ([Third-party research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)).

The authors generate 12,228 seed questions from which 20,000 more questions are expanded with Google ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt)). Here they use the same language model as in the first experiment and learn a skip-gram model on Wikipedia for evaluating domain relevance ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt)). Shown in Table 4 are some expanded questions from which we can see that most of them are grammatical and relevant to the power tool domain ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt)). In addition, most questions are informative and correspond to a specific answer, except the one "do I need a hammer drill" that lacks context information ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt)). Finally, in addition to simple factoid questions, the system generates many complex questions such as "how to cut a groove in wood without a router" ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt)). Examples include "how to change circular saw blade," "how to measure lawn mower cutting height," "how to sharpen drill bits on bench grinder," "how does an oscillating multi tool work," "what type of sander to use on deck," and "can I use acrylic paint on wood" ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt)).

### Interpretation of the 163 Figure

The 163 templates are the manual input required for the power tool domain KB. With 67 distinct predicates, this corresponds to an average of about 2.43 templates per predicate (163 ÷ 67 ≈ 2.43). This is slightly higher than the 2 templates per predicate average in the Freebase experiment, but the paper does not explicitly state the average for the power tool domain. The count is reported as 163 hand-crafted templates for the 67 predicates ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt); [Third-party research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)). The domain-specific KB required its own template set because it has its own predicate set ([Third-party research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)). Thus, for the power tool domain experiment, the answer to the query is 163 hand-crafted templates.

## Why the Template Counts Differ

The difference between 106 and 163 templates is explained by the different predicate sets in the two KBs ([Third-party research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)). Template creation follows the predicates present in the KB ([Third-party research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)). The Freebase evaluation used a subset of 500 triples that shared only 53 distinct predicates, so the authors made 2 templates per predicate on average, yielding 106 templates ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt); [Third-party research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)). The power tool KB had 67 distinct predicates, and the authors hand-crafted 163 templates for those predicates ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt); [Third-party research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)). The manual template-building effort therefore scales with predicate coverage in each evaluation ([Third-party research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)). There is no single number that applies to both experiments because they used different KBs and different predicate sets. If the query refers to the Freebase evaluation, the answer is 106. If the query refers to the domain-specific power tool KB, the answer is 163.

## Methodological Significance of the Template Counts

The template counts are significant because template construction is the only human labor in the system ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt); [Third-party research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)). By keeping the template set small relative to the number of triples and generated questions, the system reduces human effort dramatically compared to previous work that relies on 10,000 human-generated ⟨triple, question⟩ pairs ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt)). For example, in the Freebase evaluation, 106 templates generated 991 seed questions, which were expanded to 1,529 more questions, and finally 500 were selected for human evaluation ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt)). In the power tool domain, 163 templates generated 12,228 seed questions, which were expanded to 20,000 more questions ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt)). This demonstrates that a relatively small number of templates can produce a large number of questions through iterative web exploration and selection ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt)). The paper states that the system does not require a large number of templates because iterative question expansion can produce a large number of questions even with a relatively small number of seed questions, and multiple entities in the KB share the same predicates ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt)).

The template counts also show that the manual effort is tied to predicates rather than entities. In the power tool KB, there are 293 distinct subjects and 279 distinct objects, but only 67 predicates ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt)). The 163 templates are associated with those 67 predicates, not with the hundreds of subjects and objects ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt)). Similarly, in Freebase, 500 triples share only 53 predicates, so the 106 templates cover all those triples ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt); [Third-party research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)). This design is efficient because one template can be applied to many triples that share the same predicate ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt)).

## Limitations and Open Questions

The 106 and 163 figures are for separate evaluations ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt); [Third-party research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)). Therefore, one cannot simply add them to get a single "total" number of templates made, because the templates were created for different KBs and different predicate sets. The paper reports the Freebase evaluation with 106 templates and the power tool evaluation with 163 templates ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt); [Third-party research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)). If a single figure is required for the primary Freebase evaluation mentioned in the abstract, the answer is 106 ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt)). If the question refers to the domain-specific experiment, the answer is 163 ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt)).

Another limitation is that the provided information does not specify the exact number of templates per predicate in the power tool domain. The paper states 163 templates for 67 predicates, but does not explicitly give the average ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt)). The calculated average is approximately 2.43 templates per predicate, but this is not stated in the source. For the Freebase evaluation, the paper explicitly states 2 templates per predicate on average ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt); [Third-party research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)).

Also, the baseline method is referred to as "? )" in the provided excerpts, and its exact name is not given ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt)). The baseline relies on 10,000 human-labeled ⟨triple, question⟩ pairs, which highlights the human-effort reduction of the proposed system ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt)). The human evaluation for Freebase used three native English speakers and a 4-point scheme ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt)). The power tool evaluation used a language model and a skip-gram model on Wikipedia for domain relevance, but no human evaluation is reported in the provided excerpts ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt)).

## Conclusion

The question "How many hand-crafted templates did they have to make?" has two answers depending on which evaluation is referenced. For the Freebase evaluation on 500 randomly selected triples, the authors hand-crafted 106 templates ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt); [Third-party research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)). For the in-house power tool domain KB, the authors hand-crafted 163 templates ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt); [Third-party research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)). The Freebase experiment used 53 distinct predicates and 2 templates per predicate on average, yielding 106 templates ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt); [Third-party research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)). The power tool experiment used 67 distinct predicates, yielding 163 templates ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt); [Third-party research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)). Template construction is the only human labor in the system, and the counts demonstrate that the manual effort is kept compact relative to the triples and questions generated ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt); [Third-party research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)). Therefore, the most direct answer for the primary Freebase evaluation is 106 hand-crafted templates, while the domain-specific experiment required 163 hand-crafted templates ([Song & Zhao, n.d.](document_1.txt); [Third-party research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)).
