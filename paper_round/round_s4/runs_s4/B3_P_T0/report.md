# Hand-Crafted Template Requirements in Question Generation from a Knowledge Base with Web Exploration

## Introduction

The query asks how many hand-crafted templates the authors had to make in the question generation system described in the provided documents. This question is central to understanding the human effort involved in the proposed method, because the paper explicitly states that the only human labor in the work is question template construction ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). The answer is not a single number unless the scope of the question is clarified. The original paper reports two separate evaluations: one on Freebase and one on an in-house power tool domain knowledge base. For Freebase, the authors hand-crafted **106 templates** for 500 randomly selected triples ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). For the power tool domain, the original paper states that they hand-crafted **163 templates** for 67 predicates ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). Taken together, the total number of hand-crafted templates across both evaluations is **269**. A third-party research note, however, reports a different figure for the domain-specific knowledge base: **103 templates**, which would make the total **209** ([document_2.txt](document_2.txt)). This report examines both figures, explains the discrepancy, and provides a reasoned conclusion about the most reliable answer.

## The Function of Templates in the Proposed System

The proposed system generates fluent and natural questions from a knowledge base by leveraging massive web resources ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). The overall pipeline includes question template construction, seed question generation, question expansion, and question selection ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). Templates are associated with predicates in the knowledge base. For example, the predicate “performsActivity” can be transcribed into the question form “how to use #X#,” where #X# is a placeholder for the subject and #Y# is a placeholder for the object ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). A seed question set is then generated by applying these templates to the triples in the knowledge base ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). After that, each generated question is iteratively used as a search query in a standard search engine such as Google or Bing to retrieve more related question candidates ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). Finally, a selection step estimates the fluency and domain relevance of each candidate ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)).

The paper emphasizes that this design significantly reduces human effort because the iterative question expansion can produce a large number of questions even with a relatively small number of seed questions ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). In addition, multiple entities in the knowledge base share the same predicates, so the template set does not need to scale linearly with the number of triples ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). Another advantage is that the system can easily generate updated questions as the web is self-updating ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). Nevertheless, template construction remains the only manual component, making the count of hand-crafted templates a critical measure of the method’s human cost.

## Freebase Evaluation: 106 Hand-Crafted Templates

In the first experiment, the authors compare their system with a prior method on 500 randomly selected triples from Freebase ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). The paper reports that for these 500 triples, they hand-crafted **106 templates** ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). This number is explained by the fact that the triples share only 53 distinct predicates, so the authors made approximately two templates for each predicate on average ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). The third-party research note confirms this figure, stating that the paper reports 106 hand-crafted templates for the 500 Freebase triples ([document_2.txt](document_2.txt)). Applying the templates to the triples generated 991 seed questions, and the experiment also retrieved 1529 more questions from Google ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). The evaluation used three native English speakers who rated fluency and naturalness on a 4-point scale ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). The system’s questions were judged to be more fluent and natural than those of the comparison method, with grammaticality ratings of 3.53 versus 3.36 and naturalness ratings of 3.31 versus 3.14 ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)).

The Freebase template count is therefore well documented in both the original paper and the secondary note. The number **106** is the answer if the query is specifically about the Freebase evaluation.

## Power Tool Domain Evaluation: 163 or 103 Templates?

The second evaluation is on an in-house knowledge base in the power tool domain. This knowledge base contains 67 distinct predicates, 293 distinct subjects, and 279 distinct objects ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). The original paper states: “For the 67 predicates, we hand-craft 163 templates” ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). The same paper reports that the system generated 12,228 seed questions, from which 20,000 more questions were expanded with Google ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). It also provides examples of expanded questions such as “how to change circular saw blade,” “how to measure lawn mower cutting height,” and “how to cut a groove in wood without a router” ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). The paper notes that most expanded questions are grammatical, relevant to the power tool domain, and informative ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)).

However, the third-party research note gives a different figure. It states: “For the in-house power tool domain knowledge base, the paper reports 67 predicates. For the 67 predicates, the authors hand-crafted **103 templates**. Together with the 106 Freebase templates, the authors wrote **209 templates** in total” ([document_2.txt](document_2.txt)). This directly conflicts with the original paper’s statement of 163 templates. The secondary note also claims that “the paper reports this total in the template-construction experiment,” but it does not provide a direct quotation from the original paper to support the 103 or 209 figures ([document_2.txt](document_2.txt)).

## Comparing the Reported Figures

The table below summarizes the template counts reported by the two sources.

| Evaluation Set | Original Paper ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)) | Third-Party Note ([document_2.txt](document_2.txt)) |
|---|---:|---:|
| Freebase (500 triples, 53 predicates) | 106 | 106 |
| Power tool domain (67 predicates) | 163 | 103 |
| Total across both evaluations | 269 | 209 |

The discrepancy is confined to the power tool domain. The Freebase figure is consistent across both sources. The total therefore depends entirely on which power tool figure is accepted. If the original paper is correct, the total is 106 + 163 = **269**. If the third-party note is correct, the total is 106 + 103 = **209**.

## Reconciliation and Assessment

This report concludes that the original paper’s figure of **163 templates** for the power tool domain is more reliable, and therefore the total number of hand-crafted templates across both evaluations is **269**. Several factors support this conclusion. First, the original paper is the primary source and provides the figure directly in its experimental section ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). Second, the third-party note appears to contain an internal arithmetic consistency: 106 + 103 = 209, which suggests the note may have simply misread or transcribed 163 as 103 ([document_2.txt](document_2.txt)). Third, the original paper’s description of the power tool domain as having 67 predicates and requiring 163 templates implies an average of about 2.43 templates per predicate, which is broadly consistent with the Freebase average of 2 templates per predicate for 53 predicates ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). By contrast, 103 templates for 67 predicates would imply only about 1.54 templates per predicate, which is lower than the Freebase average and less consistent with the paper’s explanation that multiple entities share predicates and that templates are constructed per predicate ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)).

It is also worth noting that the third-party note explicitly states that the only human labor is question template construction and that template-building effort scales with predicate coverage ([document_2.txt](document_2.txt)). If the domain-specific knowledge base has 67 predicates, it is plausible that the authors would create more than one template per predicate to cover different question forms, just as they did for Freebase. The figure of 163 templates fits that pattern better than 103.

## Why the Template Count Matters

The number of hand-crafted templates is not merely a technical detail. It directly measures the amount of human effort required by the system. The paper states that the only human labor in this work is the question template construction ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). Because the system relies on web exploration to expand the seed question set, the authors do not need a large number of templates. The iterative question expansion can produce a large number of questions even with a relatively small number of seed questions ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). This is a key advantage over previous work that relies on massive human-labeled data, such as the NMT system trained with 10,000 ⟨triple, question⟩ pairs ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). In that prior work, the question part of the pairs was human-generated, requiring a large amount of human effort, and the grammaticality and naturalness of generated questions could not be guaranteed ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)).

By contrast, the proposed system requires only template construction. The templates consist of a transcription of the predicate in the knowledge base and placeholders for the subject and object ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). For example, “performsActivity” becomes “how to use #X#” ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). The seed question “how to use jigsaw” is generated by applying this template to the triple ⟨jigsaw, performsActivity, CurveCut⟩ ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). This design allows the system to generate questions for many entities without writing a separate template for each triple. The template count therefore scales with the number of distinct predicates rather than the number of triples, which is a significant efficiency gain.

## Direct Answer to the Query

If the question is intended to ask for the single most commonly cited figure for the Freebase experiment, the answer is **106**. If the question is intended to ask for the total human-constructed template set across the entire study, the most reliable answer is **269**. The third-party note’s total of 209 should be treated with caution because it relies on a disputed figure for the power tool domain.

## Conclusion

The proposed system for question generation from a knowledge base with web exploration uses hand-crafted templates as its only manual component. The original paper reports 106 templates for 500 Freebase triples and 163 templates for 67 power tool domain predicates, yielding a total of 269 hand-crafted templates across both evaluations. A secondary source reports 103 templates for the power tool domain and a total of 209, but this discrepancy is best resolved in favor of the primary source. Therefore, the most accurate answer to the query is: **106 templates for Freebase, 163 templates for the power tool domain, and 269 templates in total**. If a single number is required, the Freebase-specific answer is 106, while the whole-study answer is 269.
