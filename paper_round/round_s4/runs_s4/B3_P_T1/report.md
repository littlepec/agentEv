# Hand-Crafted Template Counts in *Question Generation from a Knowledge Base with Web Exploration*

## Introduction

Question generation from a knowledge base (KB) is the task of generating questions related to the domain of the input KB ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt)). The problem is challenging because function words and morphological forms for entities are abstracted away when a KB is created ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt)). Previous work has addressed this challenge by relying on massive human-labeled data; for example, one approach trains a neural machine translation (NMT) system with 10,000 triple–question pairs, where the question part is human generated ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt)). That requirement imposes a large amount of human effort and does not guarantee grammaticality or naturalness ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt)). The paper *Question Generation from a Knowledge Base with Web Exploration* proposes a system that significantly reduces human effort by leveraging massive web resources ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt)). In this system, the only human labor is question template construction ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt); [Research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)). The central question for this report is: how many hand-crafted templates did the authors have to make? The provided sources report two evaluation settings and, in one case, conflicting numbers. This report analyzes those counts, explains the template design, and provides a concrete assessment.

## The Role of Hand-Crafted Templates

The proposed system uses a small set of question templates. Each template is associated with a predicate in the KB ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt)). A template consists of a transcription of the predicate in the KB—for example, `performsActivity` might become “how to”—and placeholders for the subject (`#X#`) and the object (`#Y#`) ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt)). A seed question set is generated by applying these templates to the KB ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt)). The seed questions are then expanded iteratively by using already obtained questions as search queries in a standard search engine, such as Google or Bing, to retrieve more related question candidates ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt)). Finally, a selection step estimates fluency and domain relevance to choose the best questions ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt)). Because templates are tied to predicates rather than triples, and because multiple entities in the KB share the same predicates, the authors can cover a large number of triples with a relatively small template set ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt)). The paper explicitly states that the only human labor in this work is question template construction ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt)). A third-party research note confirms that template construction is the manual component reported ([Research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)).

## Freebase Evaluation: 106 Hand-Crafted Templates

In the first evaluation, the authors randomly selected 500 triples from Freebase ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt); [Research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)). These 500 triples shared only 53 distinct predicates ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt); [Research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)). For these predicates, the authors hand-crafted 106 templates, which corresponds to an average of two templates per predicate ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt); [Research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)). Applying the templates to the triples generated 991 seed questions ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt); [Research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)). The experiment also retrieved 1,529 additional questions from Google ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt); [Research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)). To evaluate fluency, the authors trained a 4-gram language model on Gigaword with Kneser–Ney smoothing and selected the top 500 questions by averaged language model score ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt)). Three native English speakers then rated grammaticality and naturalness on a four-point scheme ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt)). The system’s questions were rated 3.53 for grammaticality and 3.31 for naturalness, compared with 3.36 and 3.14 for the baseline ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt)). Table 1 summarizes the Freebase template and generation counts.

**Table 1. Freebase evaluation metrics**

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Randomly selected triples | 500 |
| Distinct predicates | 53 |
| Hand-crafted templates | 106 |
| Average templates per predicate | 2 |
| Seed questions generated | 991 |
| Additional questions retrieved from Google | 1,529 |
| Top questions selected for comparison | 500 |
| Grammaticality rating (system) | 3.53 |
| Naturalness rating (system) | 3.31 |
| Grammaticality rating (baseline) | 3.36 |
| Naturalness rating (baseline) | 3.14 |

*Note.* Data from ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt); [Research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)).

Thus, for the Freebase portion of the study, the answer is unambiguous: the authors made 106 hand-crafted templates.

## Power Tool Domain Evaluation: Conflicting Counts

The second evaluation used an in-house KB in the power tool domain ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt)). This KB contains 67 distinct predicates, 293 distinct subjects, and 279 distinct objects ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt)). The authors hand-crafted templates for the 67 predicates ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt)). Here the provided sources disagree. According to the primary paper excerpt, the authors hand-crafted 163 templates for the 67 predicates ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt)). According to the third-party research note, the authors hand-crafted 103 templates for the 67 predicates and reported a total of 209 templates when combined with the 106 Freebase templates ([Research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)). Table 2 presents this discrepancy.

**Table 2. Power tool domain template counts by source**

| Source | Predicates | Hand-crafted templates | Implied total with Freebase (106) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Primary paper excerpt (document_1) | 67 | 163 | 269 |
| Third-party research note (document_2) | 67 | 103 | 209 |

*Note.* Data from ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt); [Research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)).

The power tool evaluation generated 12,228 seed questions, from which 20,000 more questions were expanded with Google ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt)). The expanded questions were mostly grammatical and relevant to the power tool domain, and many were informative and corresponded to a specific answer ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt)). Examples include “how to change circular saw blade,” “how to measure lawn mower cutting height,” “how to sharpen drill bits on bench grinder,” “how does an oscillating multi tool work,” “how to cut a groove in wood without a router,” “what type of sander to use on deck,” “do I need a hammer drill,” and “can I use acrylic paint on wood” ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt)). The authors note that most questions are informative except “do I need a hammer drill,” which lacks context information ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt)). They also note that the system generates complex questions beyond simple factoid questions, such as “how to cut a groove in wood without a router” ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt)). These outputs illustrate that a relatively small template set can yield a large and varied question corpus.

## Total Hand-Crafted Templates Across Both Evaluations

The query “How many hand-crafted templates did they have to make?” can be answered at three levels: Freebase only, power tool only, or total across both. For Freebase, the count is 106 ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt); [Research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)). For the power tool domain, the primary source reports 163, while the secondary note reports 103 ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt); [Research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)). Consequently, the total is either 106 + 163 = 269 or 106 + 103 = 209 ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt); [Research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)). Table 3 presents both totals.

**Table 3. Total hand-crafted templates by scenario**

| Scenario | Freebase templates | Power tool templates | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| Primary source only | 106 | 163 | 269 |
| Secondary note only | 106 | 103 | 209 |

*Note.* Data from ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt); [Research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)).

In my assessment, the primary paper excerpt is the more reliable source for the template count. It states directly that the authors hand-crafted 106 templates for Freebase and 163 templates for the power tool domain, yielding a total of 269 hand-crafted templates ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt)). The third-party research note is a secondary summary and reports 103 templates for the power tool domain and a total of 209 ([Research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)). Although the note claims that “the paper reports this total in the template-construction experiment,” the primary excerpt provided here does not contain a total of 209; instead, it contains 163 for the power tool domain ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt); [Research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)). Therefore, the most defensible answer based on the provided primary evidence is: 106 templates for Freebase, 163 for the power tool domain, and 269 in total. The 103/209 figure should be treated as a discrepancy or possible transcription error unless corroborated by the original paper’s template-construction section.

## Why the Manual Effort Remains Low

The template counts are modest relative to the size of the KBs and the number of generated questions. The paper explains that a large number of templates is not required because iterative question expansion can produce many questions from a small seed set, and because multiple entities in the KB share the same predicates ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt)). For example, in the Freebase evaluation, 106 templates applied to 500 triples yielded 991 seed questions, which were then expanded to 1,529 more questions via Google ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt)). In the power tool evaluation, 163 templates (per the primary source) generated 12,228 seed questions, which were expanded by 20,000 additional questions ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt)). This design contrasts sharply with the NMT approach described in the paper, which used 10,000 human-generated question pairs ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt)). The paper also notes that the system can easily generate updated questions because the web is self-updating ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt)). Thus, the human effort is concentrated on a small, predicate-level template set, while the web provides scale and freshness.

**Table 4. Template effort relative to generated questions**

| Evaluation | Templates | Seed questions | Expanded questions | Total questions mentioned |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Freebase | 106 | 991 | 1,529 | 2,520 |
| Power tool (primary) | 163 | 12,228 | 20,000 | 32,228 |
| Power tool (secondary) | 103 | 12,228 | 20,000 | 32,228 |

*Note.* Data from ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt); [Research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)).

## Implications and Limitations

The central implication of these counts is that the proposed system reduces manual annotation to template construction only ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt); [Research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)). The template set is associated with predicates, so the manual cost scales with predicate coverage rather than with the number of triples or entities ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt); [Research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)). In the Freebase evaluation, 53 predicates required 106 templates, exactly two per predicate on average ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt); [Research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)). In the power tool evaluation, 67 predicates required either 163 templates (primary) or 103 templates (secondary), which would be approximately 2.43 or 1.54 templates per predicate, respectively ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt); [Research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)). The discrepancy matters for any precise accounting of human effort, but it does not change the overall conclusion that the template set is small relative to the generated question volume. The third-party note also emphasizes that the Freebase and power tool counts show how manual template-building effort scales with predicate coverage in each evaluation ([Research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)). A limitation of this report is that the provided corpus contains conflicting figures for the power tool domain and no independent verification of the total. Future work or access to the original paper’s template-construction section would be needed to resolve the 163 versus 103 discrepancy.

## Conclusion

The answer to “How many hand-crafted templates did they have to make?” depends on which evaluation and which source is used. In the Freebase evaluation, the authors made 106 hand-crafted templates for 500 randomly selected triples sharing 53 distinct predicates ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt); [Research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)). In the power tool domain evaluation, the primary paper excerpt reports 163 hand-crafted templates for 67 predicates, while a third-party research note reports 103 templates and a total of 209 across both evaluations ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt); [Research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)). Based on the primary source, the most reliable total is 106 + 163 = 269 hand-crafted templates. Based on the secondary note, the total is 106 + 103 = 209. Because the primary source is the paper itself and directly states 163 for the power tool domain, this report treats 269 as the most defensible answer, while flagging the 209 figure as a reported discrepancy. In either case, the manual template construction is the only human labor in the system, and the predicate-level design keeps the template count low relative to the thousands of questions generated ([Song & Zhao, 2016](document_1.txt); [Research note, n.d.](document_2.txt)).
