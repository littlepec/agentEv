# Datasets Used in the Cross-Lingual Parkinson's Disease Speech Classification Study

## Introduction
The study under review investigates a transfer learning strategy for classifying Parkinson's disease (PD) from speech across three languages: Spanish, German, and Czech. The central question of what datasets are used is answered by three language-specific speech corpora comprising recordings from PD patients and healthy control (HC) speakers. All recordings were captured under noise-controlled conditions and down-sampled to 16 kHz ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). The patients in each dataset were evaluated by a neurologist expert according to the third section of the Movement Disorder Society Unified Parkinson's Disease Rating Scale (MDS-UPDRS-III) ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). The following sections identify each dataset, describe its composition, clinical characteristics, speech tasks, and role in the transfer learning experiments.

## Overview of the Three Language Datasets
The study uses three datasets corresponding to three languages: Spanish, German, and Czech. The Spanish data come from the PC-GITA corpus, which contains utterances from 50 PD patients and 50 HC Colombian Spanish native speakers ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt); [document_2.txt](document_2.txt)). The German dataset contains speech recordings of 88 PD patients and 88 HC speakers from Germany ([document_2.txt](document_2.txt)). The Czech dataset contains a total of 100 native Czech speakers, comprising 50 PD patients and 50 healthy controls ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt); [document_2.txt](document_2.txt)). Together, these datasets supply speech recordings of PD patients and HC across multiple languages and form the basis for training convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and applying transfer learning ([document_2.txt](document_2.txt)). The Spanish, German, and Czech recordings are not merely auxiliary data; they constitute the full experimental base for the classification and cross-lingual transfer learning strategy ([document_2.txt](document_2.txt)).

### Spanish Dataset: PC-GITA Corpus
The Spanish portion of the data is the PC-GITA corpus ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). PC-GITA contains utterances from 50 PD patients and 50 HC subjects, all Colombian Spanish native speakers ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt); [document_2.txt](document_2.txt)). The speaker composition is balanced by disease status and sex: 25 male PD patients, 25 female PD patients, 25 male HC, and 25 female HC ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). The participants were asked to pronounce a total of 10 sentences, the rapid repetition of /pa-ta-ka/, /pe-ta-ka/, /pa-ka-ta/, /pa/, /ta/, and /ka/, one text with 36 words, and a monologue ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). All patients were in the ON state at the time of the recording, meaning they were under the effect of their daily medication ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). The mean age of male PD patients was 61.3 years (SD = 11.4), and the mean age of female PD patients was 60.7 years (SD = 7.3). The mean age of male HC was 60.5 years (SD = 11.6), and female HC was 61.4 years (SD = 7.0). The time after diagnosis was 8.7 years (SD = 5.9) for male PD patients and 12.6 years (SD = 11.6) for female PD patients. The mean MDS-UPDRS-III score was 37.8 (SD = 22.1) for male PD patients and 37.6 (SD = 14.1) for female PD patients ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). These scores are notably higher than those in the German and Czech datasets, indicating greater disease severity in the Spanish cohort ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)).

### German Dataset
The German dataset contains speech recordings of 88 PD patients and 88 HC speakers from Germany ([document_2.txt](document_2.txt)). The gender distribution is 47 male PD patients and 41 female PD patients, with 44 male HC and 44 female HC ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). The mean age of male PD patients was 66.7 years (SD = 8.7), and female PD patients was 66.2 years (SD = 9.7). Male HC had a mean age of 63.8 years (SD = 12.7), and female HC had a mean age of 62.6 years (SD = 15.2). The time after diagnosis was 7.0 years (SD = 5.5) for male PD patients and 7.1 years (SD = 6.2) for female PD patients. The mean MDS-UPDRS-III score was 22.1 (SD = 9.9) for male PD patients and 23.3 (SD = 12.0) for female PD patients ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). The provided excerpts do not enumerate the specific speech tasks performed by the German participants, but the study states that all speech exercises performed by the participants were considered for the classification strategy ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). This dataset is the largest of the three in terms of total speaker count, with 176 speakers overall.

### Czech Dataset
The Czech dataset comprises 100 native Czech speakers: 50 PD patients and 50 HC subjects ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt); [document_2.txt](document_2.txt)). The gender breakdown is 30 male PD patients and 20 female PD patients, with 30 male HC and 20 female HC ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). The speech tasks performed by the participants include the rapid repetition of the syllables /pa-ta-ka/, a read text with 80 words, and a monologue ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). The mean age of male PD patients was 65.3 years (SD = 9.6), and female PD patients was 60.1 years (SD = 8.7). Male HC had a mean age of 60.3 years (SD = 11.5), and female HC had a mean age of 63.5 years (SD = 11.1). The time after diagnosis was 6.7 years (SD = 4.5) for male PD patients and 6.8 years (SD = 5.2) for female PD patients. The mean MDS-UPDRS-III score was 21.4 (SD = 11.5) for male PD patients and 18.1 (SD = 9.7) for female PD patients ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). Like the German dataset, the Czech dataset has fewer female than male participants, particularly among PD patients.

## Signal Processing and Segmentation Derived from the Datasets
The datasets are not used in raw waveform form alone. Speech signals are analyzed based on the automatic detection of onset and offset transitions, which model the difficulties of patients to start or stop the movement of the vocal folds ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). The detection of transitions is based on the presence of the fundamental frequency of speech in short-time frames ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). The border between voiced and unvoiced frames is detected, and 80 ms of the signal are taken to the left and to the right, forming segments with 160 ms length ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). These transition segments are modeled with two approaches: a baseline model based on hand-crafted features classified using an SVM, and a model based on time-frequency representations used as input to train a CNN ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). Time-frequency representations based on the short-time Fourier transform (STFT) are used as input to a CNN. The STFT with 256 frequency bins is computed for each segmented transition, for a window length of 16 ms and a step-size of 4 ms, forming 41 time frames per transition. The obtained spectrogram is transformed into the Mel-scale using 80 filters, forming a spectrogram with a size of 80 × 41, which is used to train the CNNs ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). These derived representations are the actual inputs to the models, but the underlying datasets remain the three language-specific speech corpora.

## Role of the Datasets in Transfer Learning
The three datasets are used in a transfer learning strategy that aims to improve the accuracy of models when the weights of the neural network are initialized with utterances from a different language than the one used for the test set ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). CNNs trained with utterances from one language are used to initialize a model to classify speech utterances from PD patients in a different language ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). The models are evaluated with speech utterances in Spanish, German, and Czech ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). The experiments first train the baseline and CNN models considering each language individually. Then, the trained CNNs for each language are used as a base model in the transfer learning strategy to improve accuracy in the other two languages ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). The final decision for each speaker is obtained by a majority voting strategy among the different speech exercises ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)).

The results indicate that the transfer learning among languages improved the accuracy of the models in up to 8% when a base model trained with Spanish utterances is used to fine-tune a model to classify PD German utterances ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). For example, the Spanish base model transferred to German achieved 77.3% accuracy (SD = 11.3), with sensitivity 86.2% (SD = 13.8), specificity 68.3% (SD = 14.3), and MCC 0.57 ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). The Spanish base model transferred to Czech achieved 76.7% accuracy (SD = 7.9), sensitivity 87.5% (SD = 11.0), specificity 66.0% (SD = 15.6), and MCC 0.55 ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). The German base model transferred to Spanish achieved 70.0% accuracy (SD = 12.5), and to Czech achieved 72.0% accuracy (SD = 13.1) ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). The Czech base model transferred to Spanish achieved 72.6% accuracy (SD = 13.9), and to German achieved 70.7% accuracy (SD = 14.5) ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). The results obtained after transfer learning were also more balanced in terms of specificity and sensitivity and had a lower variance than those trained without the transfer learning strategy ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). The transfer learning scheme was accurate to improve accuracy in the target language only when the base model was robust enough, which was observed when the model trained with Spanish utterances was used to initialize the models for German and Czech languages ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)).

## Strengths and Limitations of the Datasets
The three datasets offer several strengths for cross-lingual PD speech classification. First, each dataset includes both PD patients and HC subjects, allowing binary classification ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt); [document_2.txt](document_2.txt)). Second, the datasets cover three languages, which is essential for the cross-lingual transfer learning design ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). Third, clinical severity was assessed with the MDS-UPDRS-III by a neurologist expert, providing a standardized clinical reference ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). Fourth, recordings were captured under noise-controlled conditions and standardized to 16 kHz, which supports comparability across languages ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). Fifth, the Spanish dataset is balanced by sex and disease status, and the German and Czech datasets include substantial numbers of speakers, with the German dataset being the largest ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt); [document_2.txt](document_2.txt)).

However, the datasets also have limitations. The German dataset has more male than female PD patients (47 vs. 41), and the Czech dataset has a notable sex imbalance among PD patients (30 male vs. 20 female) ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). The Spanish PD group had higher average MDS-UPDRS-III scores than the German and Czech groups, indicating higher disease severity, which may affect cross-lingual generalization ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). The specific speech tasks for the German dataset are not enumerated in the provided excerpts, although the study states that all speech exercises were used ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). Additionally, the datasets differ in size: German has 176 speakers, while Spanish and Czech each have 100 speakers ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt); [document_2.txt](document_2.txt)). These differences should be considered when interpreting the transfer learning results.

## Conclusion
In answer to the query "What datasets are used?", the study uses three speech datasets: the PC-GITA corpus for Spanish (50 PD patients and 50 HC, Colombian Spanish native speakers), a German dataset (88 PD patients and 88 HC speakers from Germany), and a Czech dataset (50 PD patients and 50 HC native Czech speakers) ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt); [document_2.txt](document_2.txt)). All three datasets include speech recordings captured under noise-controlled conditions, down-sampled to 16 kHz, and clinically evaluated with the MDS-UPDRS-III ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). They include structured speech tasks such as sustained vowels, diadochokinetic syllable repetition, read texts, and monologue, although task details vary by dataset ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). These datasets are used to train CNNs on Mel-scale spectrograms derived from 160 ms transition segments and to implement cross-lingual transfer learning, with the Spanish dataset serving as the most robust base model for improving German and Czech classification accuracy ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). The evidence therefore indicates that the study relies on three named or language-specific clinical speech corpora rather than a single unified dataset.
