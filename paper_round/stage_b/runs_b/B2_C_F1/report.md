# BLEU Score Differences Between the Proposed Insertion-Deletion Model and the Insertion-Only KERMIT Baseline

## Introduction and Framing of the Query

The query asks how much the BELU score differs between the proposed approach and the insertion-only method. In the provided documentation, the metric reported is BLEU, not BELU; therefore, this report interprets BELU as a typographical reference to BLEU. The proposed approach is the Insertion-Deletion Model, while the insertion-only comparison is the Insertion Model, also identified as KERMIT. Across the two tasks described in the supplied materials, the Insertion-Deletion Model outperforms the insertion-only model on BLEU, but the magnitude of the difference is not constant. On the alphabetic sequence shifting task, the reported difference is 21.34 BLEU points in favor of the Insertion-Deletion Model. On Caesar’s cipher, the reported difference is 2.02 BLEU points, described in the paper as approximately 2 BLEU points, again in favor of the Insertion-Deletion Model ([document_2.txt](document_2.txt); [document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). Thus, the direct answer to the query depends on which task is being referenced: the BLEU difference is either 21.34 points or 2.02 points, with no single pooled difference reported in the available sources.

The two sources are consistent for Caesar’s cipher: document_1.txt and document_2.txt both report 35.55 versus 37.57, and both characterize the gain as approximately 2 BLEU points ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt); [document_2.txt](document_2.txt)). Neither document includes publication dates, peer-review status, or external validation, so the analysis relies on the reported experimental figures rather than on an assessment of recency or venue. Nevertheless, because the documents are the only sources provided and they do not contradict one another, they form the complete evidence base for this report.

## Interpretation and Significance

The most defensible interpretation of the evidence is that the Insertion-Deletion Model is empirically superior to the insertion-only model on both reported tasks, but the size of the advantage is highly task-dependent. The alphabetic sequence shifting result shows a 21.34-point advantage, which is large on the BLEU scale, while the Caesar’s cipher result shows a 2.02-point advantage, which the paper itself characterizes as around 2 BLEU points ([document_2.txt](document_2.txt); [document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). A single overall BLEU difference is not reported, and one should not average the two values without accounting for task differences. If a simple arithmetic average were computed, it would be 11.68 BLEU points, but that figure is not reported by the sources and would mix two distinct tasks with different difficulty levels, data-generation procedures, and score ranges ([document_2.txt](document_2.txt); [document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). Therefore, the methodologically appropriate answer is to report both task-specific differences rather than to produce an unsupported aggregate.

A concrete opinion based on the provided information is that the proposed Insertion-Deletion Model demonstrates a consistent directional advantage over the insertion-only baseline across the two reported comparisons, but the claim of a general “2 BLEU point” improvement applies specifically to Caesar’s cipher, not to all tasks. The alphabetic sequence shifting result shows that the benefit can be much larger. Consequently, a reader should treat the 2.02-point Caesar’s cipher difference as a conservative lower-bound example within this evidence set, while the 21.34-point alphabetic difference shows the upper range of the observed benefit. The paper’s own wording supports this cautious reading: for Caesar’s cipher it says the deletion model “again increases” BLEU by around 2 BLEU points, implying a repeated but modest improvement in that setting ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)), whereas the alphabetic sequence shifting summary simply reports a much larger 21.34-point gain ([document_2.txt](document_2.txt)).
