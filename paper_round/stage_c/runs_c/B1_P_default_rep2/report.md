# How Big Is the AntiScam Dataset? Scale, Composition, and Annotation Coverage

## Introduction

The AntiScam dataset is a human–human anti-scam dialog corpus that was created to support research on non-collaborative dialog systems, specifically in connection with the paper *End-to-End Trainable Non-Collaborative Dialog System* ([Document 2](document_2.txt)). When researchers ask how "big" a dataset such as AntiScam is, the question is not answered by a single number. Dataset size can be measured through the total number of dialogs, the number of turns and utterances per conversation, the volume of annotated sentences, the number of annotators involved, and the number of participants who produced the data. The available documentation provides concrete figures for each of these dimensions. According to the source material, the AntiScam corpus comprises 320 human–human dialogs, with a manually annotated subset of 100 dialogs containing 3,044 sentences ([Document 2](document_2.txt)). This report examines those figures in depth, derives additional scale estimates from the reported averages, and situates the corpus's size within the context of its construction and annotation.

## Core Answer: The Total Size of the AntiScam Dataset

### Total Number of Dialogs

The most direct and frequently repeated measure of the AntiScam dataset's size is the total number of dialogs it contains: 320 human–human dialogs ([Document 2](document_2.txt)). This figure is described as the full size of the corpus and appears in the dataset description as well as in summary statements about the corpus ([Document 2](document_2.txt)). The corpus therefore consists of 320 distinct two-party conversations between a human playing the role of a potential scam target and a human playing the role of an attacker, a structure that supports the study of human elicitation strategies in adversarial dialogue.

The documentation also notes that the corpus serves as the dataset for non-collaborative dialog research in the associated paper ([Document 2](document_2.txt)). In that sense, the 320-dialog total is not merely a descriptive statistic; it represents the entire body of empirical evidence on which the paper's claims about non-collaborative dialog modeling rest ([Document 2](document_2.txt)).

### Collection Method and Domain

Understanding the size of the dataset also requires understanding how it was produced. AntiScam is described as a human–human anti-scam dialog corpus collected through a role-playing Amazon customer service scam scenario on Amazon Mechanical Turk ([Document 2](document_2.txt)). This means the 320 dialogs were generated by human participants recruited through a crowdsourcing platform and engaged in a scripted or semi-scripted adversarial exchange ([Document 2](document_2.txt)). The role-playing design is significant for interpreting dataset size: because each dialog involves two human participants, the 320-dialog corpus represents 640 participant roles in total, even though the documentation reports the participant-side outcome specifically as 172 out of 320 users ([Document 2](document_2.txt)).

### Average Conversation Length

Beyond raw dialog counts, the corpus's size is characterized by average conversation length: 12.45 turns ([Document 2](document_2.txt)). The documentation explicitly states that this average describes the full AntiScam corpus, meaning it is not limited to the annotated subset ([Document 2](document_2.txt)). In dialog research, the turn count is a central measure of conversational depth, because it indicates how many exchanges of utterances occur between the two speakers in a typical interaction. An average of 12.45 turns per dialog suggests relatively short, focused conversations rather than extended multi-hour exchanges, which is consistent with a role-playing scam scenario conducted in a crowdsourced task environment ([Document 2](document_2.txt)).

### Average Utterance Length

The corpus is further characterized by an average utterance length of 11.13 words ([Document 2](document_2.txt)). As with conversation length, this average describes the full AntiScam corpus ([Document 2](document_2.txt)). Average utterance length is an important indicator of the linguistic density of the dataset: an average of roughly eleven words per utterance indicates that individual contributions by speakers are concise, which aligns with the turn-based structure of a scam-role-play dialogue.

### Derived Scale Estimates

Although the documentation does not state the total number of turns or words in the corpus directly, the reported averages permit arithmetic estimates. Multiplying the 320 dialogs by the average conversation length of 12.45 turns yields approximately 3,984 turns across the full corpus. Multiplying that figure by the average utterance length of 11.13 words yields approximately 44,300 words (3,984 × 11.13 ≈ 44,342). These derived figures should be treated as illustrative approximations rather than as reported corpus statistics, because they assume that each turn corresponds to one utterance and that the averages apply uniformly across all dialogs. Nevertheless, they help convey the overall scale of the corpus: AntiScam is a moderately sized dialog dataset comprising thousands of conversational turns and tens of thousands of words, rather than a small qualitative sample.

## Annotation Subset Size and Structure

### Number of Annotated Dialogs and Sentences

A critical dimension of dataset size is annotation coverage. The documentation reports that a subset of 100 dialogs containing 3,044 sentences was manually annotated ([Document 2](document_2.txt)). This means that the fully annotated portion of AntiScam represents 100 of the 320 total dialogs, or approximately 31.25% of the corpus. The remaining dialogs are part of the total corpus but are not described as belonging to the manually annotated subset ([Document 2](document_2.txt)).

The 3,044 annotated sentences provide a finer-grained unit of measurement than dialogs or turns. Dividing 3,044 sentences by 100 annotated dialogs yields an average of approximately 30.44 sentences per annotated dialog. This indicates that the annotation schema operates at the sentence level and captures substantially more units than the turn-level average of 12.45 would suggest, implying that individual turns often contain multiple sentences.

### Number and Expertise of Annotators

The quality and reliability of the annotation are tied to who performed it. The documentation states that two expert annotators who have linguistic training performed the manual annotation ([Document 2](document_2.txt)). The involvement of two linguistically trained experts, rather than a large crowd of untrained workers, suggests that the 3,044-sentence annotation was produced with attention to linguistic detail and consistency ([Document 2](document_2.txt)). At the same time, the relatively small number of annotators means that inter-annotator agreement and adjudication procedures would be an important consideration for downstream users of the annotations, though the available documentation does not report agreement statistics.

### Relationship Between the Annotated Subset and the Full Corpus

The documentation is explicit that the average conversation length of 12.45 turns and the average utterance length of 11.13 words describe the full AntiScam corpus, not only the annotated subset ([Document 2](document_2.txt)). This distinction matters for interpreting dataset size: the descriptive statistics apply to all 320 dialogs, while the sentence-level annotation statistics apply only to the 100-dialog subset ([Document 2](document_2.txt)). Users of the dataset should therefore be careful not to assume that all 320 dialogs carry sentence-level annotations.

## Participant Outcomes and Dataset Composition

### Identification of the Attacker

The documentation reports that 172 out of 320 users successfully identified their partner as an attacker ([Document 2](document_2.txt)). This outcome is part of the AntiScam dataset description ([Document 2](document_2.txt)). Expressed as a proportion, 172 of 320 corresponds to 53.75%, meaning that slightly more than half of the participants recognized the adversarial nature of their interlocutor. Conversely, 148 of the 320 users, or 46.25%, did not successfully identify their partner as an attacker ([Document 2](document_2.txt)). These figures are relevant to dataset size because they describe the behavioral outcome distribution across the full participant pool associated with the 320-dialog corpus.

## Significance and Interpretation

### What the Size Figures Imply

The AntiScam dataset's size places it in the category of moderate-scale dialog corpora. With 320 dialogs, it is large enough to support model training and evaluation for non-collaborative dialog tasks, yet small enough that detailed, expert-driven annotation of a substantial subset remains feasible ([Document 2](document_2.txt)). The fact that 100 dialogs—nearly a third of the corpus—received sentence-level annotation by two linguistically trained experts suggests a deliberate trade-off between breadth and depth: the full 320 dialogs provide coverage, while the 100-dialog annotated subset provides granular linguistic labels across 3,044 sentences ([Document 2](document_2.txt)).

### The Non-Collaborative Dialog Context

The documentation frames AntiScam as the dataset underlying non-collaborative dialog research, specifically the study of human elicitation strategies ([Document 2](document_2.txt)). The size of the dataset is therefore not an arbitrary characteristic but a design choice tied to the research goal: the corpus must contain enough adversarial interactions for the paper to draw conclusions about how humans elicit information from one another, and the 320-dialog total, with its 12.45-turn average conversation length, is the empirical basis for those conclusions ([Document 2](document_2.txt)).

### Limitations of the Available Information

Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, the documentation does not report the total number of turns, utterances, or words in the corpus directly; the estimates presented in this report are derived from the reported averages and should be treated as approximations ([Document 2](document_2.txt)). Second, the documentation does not specify how many sentences or dialogs each expert annotator handled, nor does it report inter-annotator agreement ([Document 2](document_2.txt)). Third, while the participant outcome of 172 out of 320 is reported, the documentation does not break this down by dialog or by role ([Document 2](document_2.txt)). These gaps mean that the size profile of AntiScam, while well specified at the dialog and sentence levels, remains partially described at the turn, word, and annotation-process levels.

## Conclusion

The AntiScam dataset is a human–human anti-scam dialog corpus whose size is most clearly defined by its 320 human–human dialogs ([Document 2](document_2.txt)). Within that corpus, a manually annotated subset of 100 dialogs contains 3,044 sentences, annotated by two expert annotators with linguistic training ([Document 2](document_2.txt)). The full corpus is characterized by an average conversation length of 12.45 turns and an average utterance length of 11.13 words ([Document 2](document_2.txt)). Behaviorally, 172 out of 320 users successfully identified their partner as an attacker ([Document 2](document_2.txt)). Taken together, these figures describe a moderately sized, richly annotated corpus designed to support research on non-collaborative dialog and human elicitation strategies in a scam-role-play setting ([Document 2](document_2.txt)).
