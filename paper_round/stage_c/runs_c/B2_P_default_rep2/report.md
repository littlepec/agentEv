# BLEU Score Difference Between the Proposed Insertion-Deletion Model and the Insertion-Only KERMIT Method

## Executive Summary

The query asks how much the BLEU score differs between the proposed approach and the insertion-only method. In the supplied sources, the proposed approach is the **Insertion-Deletion Model**, and the insertion-only method is the **Insertion Model (KERMIT)** ([Document 2, n.d.](document_2.txt)). The reported differences are not a single uniform value. On the Caesar’s cipher task as labeled in Document 2, the Insertion-Deletion Model scores **91.49 BLEU** versus **70.15 BLEU** for KERMIT, a difference of **21.34 BLEU points** in favor of the proposed model ([Document 2, n.d.](document_2.txt)). On the shifted alphabetic sequence task as labeled in Document 2, the scores are **37.57 BLEU** versus **35.55 BLEU**, a difference of **2.02 BLEU points** ([Document 2, n.d.](document_2.txt)). Document 1 reports the same **37.57 versus 35.55** comparison but labels it as the Caesar’s cipher task and describes the increase as around **2 BLEU points** ([Document 1, n.d.](document_1.txt)). Therefore, the answer is: the proposed insertion-deletion model outperforms the insertion-only KERMIT model by **21.34 BLEU points** on the Caesar’s cipher task according to Document 2, and by **2.02 BLEU points** on the shifted alphabetic sequence task according to Document 2; Document 1 reports a **2.02 BLEU point** advantage for the Caesar’s cipher task. There is no reported case in which the insertion-only method outperforms the proposed approach.

Second, BLEU is a corpus-level n-gram overlap metric and may not fully capture the quality of character-level or sequence-to-sequence transformations, especially when deletions are involved ([Document 2, n.d.](document_2.txt)). Third, the task-labeling inconsistency between Document 1 and Document 2 means that the exact mapping between task names and score pairs cannot be fully resolved from the provided information ([Document 1, n.d.](document_1.txt); [Document 2, n.d.](document_2.txt)). Fourth, only two score pairs are reported, so the findings may not generalize to other tasks or datasets. Despite these limitations, the available evidence consistently favors the Insertion-Deletion Model over the insertion-only KERMIT model.
