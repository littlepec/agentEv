# BLEU Score Differences Between the Insertion-Deletion Transformer and the Insertion-Only Baseline

## Executive Summary

The proposed approach in the provided materials is the **Insertion-Deletion Transformer**, also referred to as the **Insertion-Deletion Model**, a transformer-based neural architecture and training method for sequence generation that combines an insertion phase with a deletion phase ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). The insertion-only baseline is the **Insertion Model (KERMIT)**, which extends the Insertion Transformer insertion-only framework and serves as the direct comparison point for the proposed model ([document_2.txt](document_2.txt)).

Across the two synthetic translation tasks reported in the source material, the BLEU score differences between the proposed Insertion-Deletion Model and the insertion-only baseline are **21.34 BLEU points** on one task and **2.02 BLEU points** on the other, both in favor of the proposed Insertion-Deletion Model ([document_2.txt](document_2.txt)). There is **no single overall BLEU difference** reported for the proposed approach versus the insertion-only method; instead, the paper reports separate per-task BLEU results. If one averages the two reported differences, the unweighted mean improvement is **11.68 BLEU points**, but this is a derived value, not a reported metric. The most accurate answer is therefore task-dependent: the proposed approach improves BLEU by **21.34 points** on one synthetic translation task and by **2.02 points** on the other, described in the paper as around 2 BLEU points ([document_2.txt](document_2.txt)).

A critical caveat must be noted: the provided documents are inconsistent in their task labels. **Document 2** associates the 21.34-point improvement with **Caesar’s cipher** and the 2.02-point improvement with the **shifted alphabetic sequence** task ([document_2.txt](document_2.txt)). **Document 1**, by contrast, labels the 91.49 versus 70.15 BLEU comparison as **Alphabetic Sequence Shifting** in Table 2, and labels the 37.57 versus 35.55 BLEU comparison as **Caesar’s Cipher** in Table 3 ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). The numerical differences remain 21.34 and 2.02 BLEU points, but which task corresponds to which difference is disputed across the two documents. This report will present both per-task differences, explicitly flag the labeling inconsistency, and avoid treating any single pooled number as an official paper result.

## Background: Insertion-Based and Insertion-Deletion Sequence Generation

Insertion-based non- or partially autoregressive models have attracted increased research attention because they are more flexible than their autoregressive counterparts ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). They can generate sequences in any order, benefit from parallel token generation, and learn complex orderings such as tree orderings. They may also be more applicable to tasks such as cloze question answering and text simplification, where the order of generation is not naturally left to right and the source sequence might not be fully observed ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). The Insertion Transformer, introduced by Stern et al. (2019), is a representative insertion-only framework, and KERMIT is the insertion-only baseline used for comparison in the present work ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt); [document_2.txt](document_2.txt)).

The proposed Insertion-Deletion Transformer extends that insertion-only framework to handle both insertions and deletions ([document_2.txt](document_2.txt)). It consists of two phases that are executed iteratively: an insertion phase and a deletion phase ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). The insertion phase parameterizes a distribution of insertions on the current output hypothesis, while the deletion phase parameterizes a distribution of deletions over the current output hypothesis ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). The training method is described as a principled and simple algorithm in which the deletion model obtains its signal directly on-policy from the insertion model output ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). Architecturally, the model can be implemented with a simple stack of two Transformer decoders, where the top deletion transformer layer receives its signal from the bottom insertion transformer ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)).

The motivation for adding a deletion phase is that in a conventional insertion-based model, if the model makes a mistake during generation, the mistake cannot be undone ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). By introducing deletion, the framework can undo mistakes made by the insertion model because the deletion model is trained on the on-policy errors of the insertion phase ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). This insertion-deletion framework also enables flexible sequence generation, parallel token generation, and text editing. It can efficiently handle tasks such as text simplification and style transfer by starting the decoding process from the original source sequence ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). The deletion phase therefore serves as a refinement mechanism that iteratively improves the output sequence through sequences of insertions and deletions ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)).

## Experimental Setup

The Insertion-Deletion Transformer is evaluated on synthetic translation datasets. The paper demonstrates the capabilities of the model through experiments on these datasets, showing how the addition of deletion improves BLEU score and how the insertion and deletion models interact ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). The reported experiments focus on two synthetic character-based translation tasks ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). The source material indicates that adversarial deletion training did not improve BLEU scores on these synthetic tasks; however, the adversarial training scheme can still be helpful when the deletion model does not receive a signal during training by sampling from the insertion model alone, i.e., when the insertion model does not make any errors ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)).

The training and evaluation details differ across the two tasks. For one task, the authors generate **1,000 examples for training** and evaluate on **100 held-out examples**; the models are trained for **200k steps** with a **batch size of 32**, and no model selection is performed ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). For the other task, the authors generate **100k examples to train on** and evaluate on **1,000 held-out examples**; the models are again trained for **200k steps** with a **batch size of 32**, and no model selection is performed ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). These setup details matter because they contextualize the magnitude of the BLEU improvements and suggest that the deletion mechanism may produce different gains depending on training-data scale and task characteristics, although the task-label inconsistency between the documents makes a definitive association difficult.

## Reported BLEU Scores and Differences

The source material reports two separate BLEU comparisons between the Insertion-Deletion Model and the Insertion Model (KERMIT). The first comparison involves BLEU scores of **91.49** for the Insertion-Deletion Model and **70.15** for the Insertion Model (KERMIT), yielding a difference of **21.34 BLEU points** in favor of the Insertion-Deletion Model ([document_2.txt](document_2.txt)). The second comparison involves BLEU scores of **37.57** for the Insertion-Deletion Model and **35.55** for the Insertion Model (KERMIT), yielding a difference of **2.02 BLEU points**, which the paper describes as around 2 BLEU points ([document_2.txt](document_2.txt)). In both cases, the Insertion-Deletion Model improves the BLEU score over the insertion-only baseline ([document_2.txt](document_2.txt)).

### Task A: The Larger Improvement

According to **Document 2**, the larger improvement occurs on the **Caesar’s cipher task**. On this task, the reported BLEU scores are 91.49 for the Insertion-Deletion Model and 70.15 for the Insertion Model (KERMIT), with a difference of 21.34 BLEU points in favor of the Insertion-Deletion Model ([document_2.txt](document_2.txt)). Document 2 presents these results in Table 2 and states that the Insertion-Deletion Model improves the BLEU score on this task ([document_2.txt](document_2.txt)). However, **Document 1** labels the 91.49 versus 70.15 comparison as **Alphabetic Sequence Shifting BLEU** in Table 2, describing it as BLEU scores for the sequence shifting task ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). Under Document 1’s labeling, this larger improvement is therefore associated with alphabetic sequence shifting, not Caesar’s cipher. The discrepancy is significant for interpretation but does not alter the arithmetic: the difference is **+21.34 BLEU points** for the proposed Insertion-Deletion Model over the insertion-only baseline.

### Task B: The Smaller Improvement

According to **Document 2**, the smaller improvement occurs on the **shifted alphabetic sequence task**. On this task, the reported BLEU scores are 37.57 for the Insertion-Deletion Model and 35.55 for the Insertion Model (KERMIT), with a difference of 2.02 BLEU points ([document_2.txt](document_2.txt)). Document 2 notes that the paper describes this as around 2 BLEU points and presents the results in Table 3 ([document_2.txt](document_2.txt)). However, **Document 1** labels the 37.57 versus 35.55 comparison as **Caesar’s Cipher BLEU** in Table 3, stating that the deletion model again increases the BLEU score over just the insertion model by around 2 BLEU points ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). Under Document 1’s labeling, this smaller improvement is therefore associated with Caesar’s cipher, not the shifted alphabetic sequence. Again, the arithmetic difference is consistent: the proposed Insertion-Deletion Model improves by **+2.02 BLEU points** over the insertion-only baseline.

\[
\frac{21.34 + 2.02}{2} = 11.68 \text{ BLEU points}
\]

This mean is **not reported in the paper** and should be treated as a derived summary rather than an official result. The range of improvements is **2.02 to 21.34 BLEU points**, and the sum of the two reported differences is **23.36 BLEU points**, again a derived value. The safest and most accurate answer to the question “How much is the BLEU score difference between the proposed approach and the insertion-only method?” is therefore that the difference is **task-dependent**: it is **+21.34 BLEU points on one synthetic translation task** and **+2.02 BLEU points on the other**, with no single overall BLEU difference reported.

## Interpretation and Caveats

Several interpretive points follow from the reported results. First, the direction of the effect is consistent: in both synthetic translation tasks, the Insertion-Deletion Model outperforms the Insertion Model (KERMIT) on BLEU ([document_2.txt](document_2.txt)). This supports the paper’s central claim that adding a deletion phase to an insertion-only framework can significantly increase BLEU score ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). Second, the magnitude of the improvement varies substantially across tasks, from approximately 2 BLEU points to more than 21 BLEU points ([document_2.txt](document_2.txt)). This suggests that the benefit of deletion is not uniform and may depend on the specific task, the complexity of the required edit operations, and the amount of training data.

Third, the task-label inconsistency between Document 1 and Document 2 is a material limitation of the provided evidence. Document 2 explicitly states that the paper reports separate BLEU results for two synthetic translation tasks, with 21.34 BLEU points on Caesar’s cipher and 2.02 BLEU points on the shifted alphabetic sequence task ([document_2.txt](document_2.txt)). Document 1’s tables, however, label the 91.49 versus 70.15 comparison as Alphabetic Sequence Shifting and the 37.57 versus 35.55 comparison as Caesar’s Cipher ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). Because the numerical differences are identical across both documents, the inconsistency affects only the mapping between task names and improvement magnitudes, not the computed BLEU differences themselves. Nevertheless, any claim about which task benefits more from the deletion mechanism must acknowledge this discrepancy.

Fourth, the training-data scale differs between the two reported tasks. Under Document 1’s labeling, the larger BLEU improvement (91.49 vs. 70.15) is associated with a setup using 1,000 training examples and 100 held-out examples, while the smaller BLEU improvement (37.57 vs. 35.55) is associated with a setup using 100k training examples and 1,000 held-out examples ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). This pattern could suggest that deletion provides a larger relative benefit when training data are scarce, but the task-label inconsistency means this interpretation should be treated as provisional. Moreover, the paper reports no model selection in either setup, which reduces the risk of overfitting to a validation set but also means the results reflect fixed training runs ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)).

Fifth, the paper does not report a single pooled BLEU difference across tasks. This is important because a reader might otherwise expect a headline overall improvement number. The results are presented per task in separate tables: Table 2 for one task and Table 3 for the other ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). Therefore, any single-number answer—whether 21.34, 2.02, 11.68, or 23.36—involves either selecting one task, averaging across tasks, or summing across tasks. The most transparent answer is to report both per-task differences and explain that no overall difference is provided.

## Conclusion

The BLEU score difference between the proposed Insertion-Deletion Transformer and the insertion-only Insertion Model (KERMIT) is **not a single uniform value** in the provided materials. The paper reports two separate synthetic translation tasks, and the proposed model improves BLEU by **21.34 points** on one task and **2.02 points** on the other, both in favor of the Insertion-Deletion Model ([document_2.txt](document_2.txt)). The larger improvement corresponds to a BLEU comparison of 91.49 versus 70.15, while the smaller improvement corresponds to a BLEU comparison of 37.57 versus 35.55 ([document_2.txt](document_2.txt)). The task labels are inconsistent across the two source documents: Document 2 attributes the 21.34-point gain to Caesar’s cipher and the 2.02-point gain to the shifted alphabetic sequence, whereas Document 1 attributes the 91.49 versus 70.15 comparison to Alphabetic Sequence Shifting and the 37.57 versus 35.55 comparison to Caesar’s Cipher ([document_2.txt](document_2.txt); [document_1.txt](document_1.txt)).

If forced to provide a concise answer: the proposed Insertion-Deletion Model outperforms the insertion-only baseline by **2.02 to 21.34 BLEU points** across the two reported synthetic translation tasks, with an unweighted mean of **11.68 BLEU points** if the two task-level differences are averaged. The paper itself reports no overall BLEU difference. The most defensible conclusion, based on the provided evidence, is that the addition of the deletion phase yields a consistent improvement in BLEU over the insertion-only framework, with the magnitude of that improvement being strongly task-dependent.
