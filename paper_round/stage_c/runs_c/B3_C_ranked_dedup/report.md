# Hand-Crafted Template Counts in Knowledge-Base Question Generation: A Detailed Report

## Direct Answer

The reported number of hand-crafted question templates is not a single universal figure. In the Freebase evaluation using 500 randomly selected triples, the authors hand-crafted **106 templates** ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt); [document_2.txt](document_2.txt)). In the in-house power tool domain knowledge base, which contains 67 predicates, the authors hand-crafted **163 templates** ([document_2.txt](document_2.txt)). Therefore, if the query refers specifically to the Freebase experiment, the answer is **106**. If the query refers to all reported evaluations in the work, the answer is **106 for Freebase and 163 for the power tool domain**. The difference is not an inconsistency; it reflects the fact that template creation is tied to predicates, and the two knowledge bases have different predicate sets and domain requirements ([document_2.txt](document_2.txt)).

Based on the provided information, the most defensible interpretation is that the authors did not have to make one fixed number of templates for the system as a whole. Instead, they made templates per evaluation: 106 for the 500 Freebase triples and 163 for the 67 power tool predicates ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt); [document_2.txt](document_2.txt)). This distinction is important because the paper explicitly states that the only human labor in the work is question template construction ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt); [document_2.txt](document_2.txt)).

## Background: Template Construction as the Manual Component

The proposed system generates questions from a knowledge base by first constructing a small set of question templates, then generating seed questions, then expanding those seed questions through web search, and finally selecting the best candidates based on fluency and domain relevance ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). The paper states that each template is associated with a predicate in the knowledge base, meaning that template creation follows the predicates present in the KB rather than the raw number of triples ([document_2.txt](document_2.txt)). This design choice directly explains why the template counts are reported per knowledge base and per predicate set.

The templates themselves consist of a transcription of the predicate in the KB and placeholders for the subject and object. For example, the predicate `performsActivity` can be transcribed as `how to`, with `#X#` and `#Y#` placeholders for entities ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). In the power tool domain, the template `how to use #X#` is constructed for the predicate `performsActivity`, and applying it to the triple `⟨jigsaw, performsActivity, CurveCut⟩` produces the seed question `how to use jigsaw` ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). This example shows how one predicate-level template can cover multiple entity pairs.

The paper emphasizes that the system does not require a large number of templates because iterative question expansion can produce many questions even from a relatively small number of seed questions, and because multiple entities in the KB share the same predicates ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). Another stated advantage is that the system can easily generate updated questions as the web self-updates consistently ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). These arguments are used to justify why the manual template burden remains small relative to the number of triples or generated questions.

## Freebase Evaluation: 106 Hand-Crafted Templates

In the Freebase evaluation, the authors used 500 randomly selected triples ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt); [document_2.txt](document_2.txt)). For those 500 triples, they hand-crafted 106 templates ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt); [document_2.txt](document_2.txt)). The triples shared only 53 distinct predicates, so the authors made approximately 2 templates for each predicate on average ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt); [document_2.txt](document_2.txt)). This means the 106 templates were not created one per triple; they were created at the predicate level, which kept the template set compact relative to the triple set ([document_2.txt](document_2.txt)).

Applying the 106 templates to the triples generated 991 seed questions ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt); [document_2.txt](document_2.txt)). The experiment also retrieved 1529 more questions from Google ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt); [document_2.txt](document_2.txt)). To evaluate fluency, the authors trained a 4-gram language model on Gigaword with Kneser-Ney smoothing, used the averaged language model score as an index, and selected the top 500 questions to compare with prior results ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). Three native English speakers evaluated fluency and naturalness on a 4-point scheme where 4 is the best ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). The paper reports that questions generated by the system were significantly better than the comparison on grammaticality and naturalness ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)).

The Freebase count of 106 is therefore the answer to the query when the context is the Freebase evaluation. It is also the figure most directly associated with the 500-triple benchmark. However, it should not be treated as the only template count in the work, because the power tool domain evaluation reports a different number.

## Power Tool Domain: 163 Hand-Crafted Templates

For the in-house power tool domain knowledge base, the paper reports 67 predicates ([document_2.txt](document_2.txt)). For those 67 predicates, the authors hand-crafted 163 templates ([document_2.txt](document_2.txt)). This count differs from the Freebase template count because the domain-specific KB has its own predicate set and required its own template set ([document_2.txt](document_2.txt)). The 163 templates are therefore not a replacement for the 106 Freebase templates; they are the reported template-construction figure for a different evaluation setting.

The power tool evaluation is also larger in terms of generated questions. The authors generated 12,228 seed questions, from which 20,000 more questions were expanded with Google ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). The paper states that most expanded questions were grammatical and relevant to the power tool domain, and that most were informative and corresponded to a specific answer, with the exception of the question `do I need a hammer drill`, which lacked context information ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). The system also generated complex questions such as `how to cut a groove in wood without a router` ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). Examples of expanded questions include `how to change circular saw blade`, `how to measure lawn mower cutting height`, `how to sharpen drill bits on bench grinder`, `how does an oscillating multi tool work`, `what type of sander to use on deck`, and `can I use acrylic paint on wood` ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)).

These figures show that the manual template-building effort scales with predicate coverage in each evaluation ([document_2.txt](document_2.txt)). The power tool KB has 67 predicates, more than the 53 distinct predicates in the Freebase sample, and the template count is correspondingly higher: 163 versus 106 ([document_2.txt](document_2.txt)).

## Why the Number of Templates Is Not a Single Figure

The central reason there is no single template count is that the work reports two different evaluations on two different knowledge bases. The Freebase evaluation used 500 randomly selected triples with 53 distinct predicates and required 106 templates ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt); [document_2.txt](document_2.txt)). The power tool evaluation used a domain-specific KB with 67 predicates and required 163 templates ([document_2.txt](document_2.txt)). The paper states that the Freebase and power tool counts provide the reported template-construction figures for the system, and that these figures show the manual template-building effort scales with predicate coverage in each evaluation ([document_2.txt](document_2.txt)).

This design is predicate-driven rather than triple-driven. Each template is associated with a predicate, so the relevant unit for manual effort is the predicate, not the triple ([document_2.txt](document_2.txt)). In Freebase, 53 distinct predicates led to 106 templates, an average of 2 templates per predicate ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt); [document_2.txt](document_2.txt)). In the power tool domain, 67 predicates led to 163 templates, an average of about 2.43 templates per predicate ([document_2.txt](document_2.txt)). The number of triples in the Freebase sample—500—is much larger than the template count, which illustrates the compactness of the template set relative to the triple set ([document_2.txt](document_2.txt)).

The paper also argues that the system does not require a large number of templates because iterative question expansion can produce a large number of questions even with a relatively small number of seed questions, and because multiple entities in the KB share the same predicates ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). This is consistent with the observed counts: 106 templates produced 991 seed questions in Freebase, and 163 templates produced 12,228 seed questions in the power tool domain ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). The manual effort is concentrated at the template level, while the automated expansion and selection steps handle the scale.

## Derived Ratios and Their Meaning

Several ratios help interpret the reported template counts. For Freebase, 106 templates cover 500 triples, yielding approximately 0.212 templates per triple, or about 4.72 triples per template ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). This ratio is not a direct measure of manual effort per triple because templates are predicate-level and can be reused across many triples. For the power tool domain, the number of triples is not reported in the provided sources, so a comparable triple-to-template ratio cannot be calculated ([document_2.txt](document_2.txt)).

For predicates, the Freebase evaluation reports 53 distinct predicates and 106 templates, exactly 2 templates per predicate on average ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt); [document_2.txt](document_2.txt)). The power tool evaluation reports 67 predicates and 163 templates, which is about 2.43 templates per predicate ([document_2.txt](document_2.txt)). This suggests that the authors did not use a rigid fixed number of templates per predicate; instead, they created templates as needed for the domain and predicate set.

For seed questions, the Freebase evaluation generated 991 seed questions from 106 templates, an average of about 9.35 seed questions per template ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). The power tool evaluation generated 12,228 seed questions from 163 templates, an average of about 75.02 seed questions per template ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). These averages imply that the power tool KB had more entities or triples per predicate on average, or that the templates were applied more broadly. The provided sources do not specify the exact number of triples in the power tool KB, so this interpretation remains an inference from the reported aggregates.

The contrast with prior work is also relevant. The paper notes that previous work treated question generation as a machine translation problem and trained an NMT system with 10,000 triple-question pairs, where the question part was human generated and required a large amount of human effort ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). In contrast, the proposed system reports only template construction as human labor, with 106 templates in one evaluation and 163 in another ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt); [document_2.txt](document_2.txt)). This is a significant reduction in manual annotation relative to 10,000 human-generated question pairs, although the two settings are not directly identical.

## Source Reliability and Limitations

The provided information comes from two documents. `document_1.txt` contains the primary experimental details, including the Freebase count of 106 templates, the 991 seed questions, the 1529 Google-retrieved questions, and the power tool count of 12,228 seed questions and 20,000 expanded questions ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). `document_2.txt` provides a summarizing and structuring note that explicitly reports the Freebase figures—500 triples, 106 templates, 53 distinct predicates, 991 seed questions, and 1529 additional questions—and the power tool figures—67 predicates and 163 templates ([document_2.txt](document_2.txt)). The two sources are consistent on the template counts.

The main limitations are that the provided information does not include the full original paper, does not report the number of triples in the power tool KB, and does not provide a single aggregate template count across both evaluations. The derived ratios in this report are therefore arithmetic calculations from the reported figures, not additional reported statistics. The counts also depend on the specific knowledge bases and predicate sets used, so they should not be generalized to all knowledge-base question-generation systems without further evidence. The paper’s own framing supports this caution: template creation is tied to predicates in the KB, so different KBs with different predicate sets will require different template sets ([document_2.txt](document_2.txt)).

## Conclusion

The query “How many hand-crafted templates did they have to make?” is best answered with context. In the Freebase evaluation on 500 randomly selected triples, the authors hand-crafted **106 templates** ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt); [document_2.txt](document_2.txt)). In the in-house power tool domain knowledge base with 67 predicates, they hand-crafted **163 templates** ([document_2.txt](document_2.txt)). The Freebase templates covered 53 distinct predicates at an average of 2 templates per predicate, while the power tool templates covered 67 predicates at an average of about 2.43 templates per predicate ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt); [document_2.txt](document_2.txt)). If the question is specifically about the Freebase experiment, the answer is **106**. If the question is about all reported evaluations, the answer is **106 and 163**. The absence of a single number is not an omission; it reflects the predicate-driven design of the system and the use of two different knowledge bases in the reported experiments ([document_2.txt](document_2.txt)).
