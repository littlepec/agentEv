# Hand-Crafted Template Counts in Song and Zhao's Question Generation System: A Detailed Report

## Executive Summary

The question of how many hand-crafted templates the authors had to make does not have a single, unambiguous answer across the supplied materials. According to the primary source—the arXiv paper "Question Generation from a Knowledge Base with Web Exploration" by Song and Zhao—the authors hand-crafted **106 templates for the Freebase evaluation** and a further **163 templates for the in-house power tool domain knowledge base**, for a combined total of **269 templates** ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). A third-party research note summarizing the same work agrees on the Freebase figure of 106 but reports only **103 templates** for the power tool domain, yielding a total of **209 templates** ([Third-party research note](document_2.txt)). Because the primary source is the authors' own account and is internally consistent with the rest of its experimental reporting, this report treats **269** as the most defensible aggregate figure, while explicitly documenting the conflicting 103-template claim and the resulting 209-template total as an important discrepancy that any downstream user of these figures should be aware of.

## Introduction: Why the Template Count Matters

Question generation from a knowledge base (KB) is the task of producing questions related to the domain of the input KB ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). The system proposed by Song and Zhao is designed to reduce human effort by combining a small number of hand-crafted templates with massive web resources. Specifically, a seed question set is first generated by applying a small number of hand-crafted templates to the input KB; more questions are then retrieved by iteratively forming already-obtained questions as search queries into a standard search engine; finally, questions are selected by estimating their fluency and domain relevance ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). The authors state plainly that "the only human labor in this work is the question template construction" ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). Consequently, the template count is the single most important measure of the manual annotation cost of the entire pipeline, which is why the query under consideration—how many templates had to be made—is substantively significant rather than merely a bookkeeping detail.

## The Freebase Evaluation: 106 Templates

In the first experiment, the system is compared against a previous state-of-the-art method on 500 randomly selected triples from Freebase, a domain-general KB ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). For those 500 triples, the authors hand-crafted **106 templates**, because the triples share only **53 distinct predicates**; on average, they made approximately two templates per predicate ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). This design decision—tying template creation to predicates rather than to individual triples—keeps the template set compact relative to the triple set ([Third-party research note](document_2.txt)).

### Template-to-Predicate Ratio

The relationship between 53 predicates and 106 templates is exactly two templates per predicate on average. The authors explain that multiple entities in the KB share the same predicates, which is one of the two reasons the system does not require a large number of templates; the other reason is that iterative question expansion can produce a large number of questions even from a relatively small number of seed questions ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). This predicate-driven design means that the manual effort scales with predicate coverage, not with the number of triples or entities ([Third-party research note](document_2.txt)).

### From Templates to Seed Questions

Applying the 106 templates to the 500 Freebase triples generated **991 seed questions**; the experiment then retrieved **1,529 more questions from Google** ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). To evaluate fluency, a 4-gram language model was trained on Gigaword (LDC2011T07) with Kneser-Ney smoothing, and the top 500 questions by averaged language model score were selected for comparison ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). Three native English speakers then rated grammaticality and naturalness on a 4-point scheme where 4 is best; the system's questions scored **3.53 on grammaticality and 3.31 on naturalness**, against **3.36 and 3.14** for the comparison method ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). These results are the empirical justification for the authors' claim that their questions are more grammatical and natural than those produced by the baseline, and they rest on a template base of only 106 items for the domain-general evaluation.

## The Power Tool Domain Knowledge Base: 163 Templates According to the Primary Source

The third experiment runs the end-to-end system on a highly specialized in-house KB in the power tool domain ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). This KB contains **67 distinct predicates, 293 distinct subjects, and 279 distinct objects** ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). For the 67 predicates, the primary source states that the authors hand-craft **163 templates** ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). That is roughly 2.43 templates per predicate—a higher ratio than the approximately two-per-predicate ratio observed for Freebase, which is plausible given that a specialized technical domain may require more phrasings to capture the nuances of activities, tools, and materials. From this template base, the system generated **12,228 seed questions**, from which **20,000 more questions were expanded with Google** ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). Sample expanded questions include "how to change circular saw blade," "how to measure lawn mower cutting height," "how to sharpen drill bits on bench grinder," and "how to cut a groove in wood without a router" ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). The domain-specific KB has its own predicate set and therefore required its own template set ([Third-party research note](document_2.txt)).

## Discrepancy Between the Primary Paper and the Third-Party Note

The third-party research note reports the power tool domain figure differently. It states that "for the 67 predicates, the authors hand-crafted 103 templates" and that "together with the 106 Freebase templates, the authors wrote 209 templates in total" ([Third-party research note](document_2.txt)). This conflicts with the primary source's figure of 163 templates for the same 67 predicates ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). Both figures cannot be simultaneously correct as descriptions of the same template set.

My assessment, based on the supplied information, is as follows. First, the primary source is the authors' own published account and is the more authoritative record; it also coheres with the surrounding experimental narrative, in which the Freebase experiment explicitly derives its 106 templates from 53 predicates at roughly two per predicate, and the power tool experiment derives its templates from 67 predicates using the same template-construction methodology ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). Second, the 103 figure in the third-party note is internally consistent only with itself: 106 plus 103 equals 209, so the note's arithmetic is sound, but its input figure appears to diverge from the primary source ([Third-party research note](document_2.txt)). Third, the divergence is confined to the domain-specific count; there is no disagreement about the 106 Freebase templates, the 53 distinct Freebase predicates, the 991 seed questions, or the 1,529 Google-retrieved questions ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807); [Third-party research note](document_2.txt)).

On the conservative assumption that both documents are describing the same research, the most defensible answer to the query is therefore **269 hand-crafted templates in total**: 106 for Freebase and 163 for the power tool domain ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). The alternative reading yields **209 templates**: 106 for Freebase and 103 for the power tool domain ([Third-party research note](document_2.txt)). A cautious analyst should report the primary-source figure of 269 while flagging the 103/209 alternative, because the discrepancy could reflect a transcription error in either direction, a difference in how a "template" was counted (for example, whether near-duplicate phrasings were merged), or a revision between drafts.

## What the Numbers Imply About Human Effort

The authors explicitly justify why they do not need a large number of templates: (1) the iterative question expansion can produce a large number of questions even with a relatively small number of seed questions, and (2) multiple entities in the KB share the same predicates ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). Another advantage the authors claim is that the system can easily generate updated questions because the web is self-updating ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). Against the alternative of training a neural machine translation system on 10,000 triple–question pairs whose questions were human-generated—requiring substantial human effort and offering no guarantee of grammaticality or naturalness ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807))—the template-based approach trades a bounded, auditable manual investment of a few hundred templates for a scalable retrieval and filtering process. Whether the count is 209 or 269, the template set is small relative to the 500 triples, 991 seed questions, 1,529 retrieved questions, 12,228 power-tool seed questions, and 20,000 expanded power-tool questions involved ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)).

The template counts are also the reported floor of human involvement: the paper states that the only human labor in this work is question template construction ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). The 106 Freebase templates required covering 53 predicates at roughly two phrasings each, a ratio the authors describe directly in the text ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). The power tool templates likewise follow the predicate set of that KB, so the manual template-building effort scales with predicate coverage in each evaluation ([Third-party research note](document_2.txt)).

## Methodological Context: How Templates Were Built and Used

Each template consists of a transcription of a predicate in the KB—for example, "performsActivity" maps to "how to"—plus placeholders for the subject (#X#) and the object (#Y#) ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). As a worked example, the template "how to use #X#" is constructed for the predicate "performsActivity," and applying it to the triple ⟨jigsaw, performsActivity, CurveCut⟩ produces the seed question "how to use jigsaw," which is then used as a Google query to retrieve related questions ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). Expansion proceeds iteratively: the expanded set is initialized with the seed set, and in each iteration an already-obtained question is expanded from the web, with retrieved questions added if not already present, up to a maximum number of iterations ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). Retrieved questions are filtered using a skip-gram model for domain relevance, computed as the cosine similarity between the averaged word embedding of the question and that of the seed set, and a language model for fluency, computed as the averaged language model score per word, with thresholds applied to filter out weak candidates ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)).

This pipeline explains why a few hundred templates suffice. Templates seed the search process; the search engine supplies breadth; and the relevance and fluency filters supply quality control. The domain-relevance method was independently validated on the web snippet dataset—10,060 training and 2,280 test snippets across 8 classes, averaging 18 words per snippet—where it achieved **85.65% precision**, outperforming previous methods at 82.18%, 85.31%, and 85.48% ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)).

## Limitations and Cautionary Notes

Three limitations should be kept in mind. First, the discrepancy between 163 and 103 templates for the power tool domain is unresolved in the supplied materials, and the aggregate total therefore fluctuates between 269 and 209 depending on the source ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807); [Third-party research note](document_2.txt)). Second, the Freebase result is based on a 500-triple sample with only 53 distinct predicates, so the 106-template figure should not be extrapolated to the entirety of Freebase without further evidence. Third, the system generates questions without answers, and automatic answer mining is left as future work ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)).

## Conclusion

The direct answer to the query is that Song and Zhao's system required two batches of hand-crafted templates. The Freebase evaluation used **106** templates for 500 randomly selected triples sharing 53 distinct predicates at approximately two templates per predicate, and the in-house power tool domain evaluation used **163** templates for 67 predicates, giving a primary-source total of **269** hand-crafted templates ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). A third-party research note reports the power tool figure as **103**, producing a total of **209** ([Third-party research note](document_2.txt)). Given that the primary source is the authors' own account and is consistent with the rest of its reported methodology, I regard **269** as the best-supported answer, with the 209 figure noted as a genuine inconsistency in the secondary summary. What is not in dispute is the design philosophy: template creation was deliberately tied to predicates rather than triples, keeping the manual component small, auditable, and the only human labor in the pipeline ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)).
