# Hand-Crafted Template Counts in Knowledge-Base Question Generation: A Detailed Report

## Executive Summary

The reported manual template construction effort in the described question-generation work is **106 hand-crafted templates for the Freebase evaluation**, **103 hand-crafted templates for the in-house power tool domain knowledge base**, and **209 hand-crafted templates in total** across both evaluations ([Document 2](document_2.txt)). The Freebase templates were created for 500 randomly selected triples that share only 53 distinct predicates, yielding an average of two templates per predicate ([Document 1](document_1.txt); [Document 2](document_2.txt)). The power tool templates were created for 67 predicates ([Document 2](document_2.txt)). Therefore, if the query asks for the overall hand-crafted template count reported by the work, the answer is **209**; if it asks per evaluation, the answers are **106** and **103** respectively.

## Introduction: The Template-Centric Approach to Question Generation

Question generation from a knowledge base (KB) is the task of generating questions related to the domain of the input KB ([Document 1](document_1.txt)). The proposed system aims to generate fluent and natural questions while significantly reducing human effort by leveraging massive web resources ([Document 1](document_1.txt)). It contains sub-modules for question template construction, seed question generation, question expansion, and selection ([Document 1](document_1.txt)). Given an input KB, a small set of question templates is first constructed such that each template is associated with a predicate ([Document 1](document_1.txt)). A seed question set is generated by applying the template set on the input KB, and then more questions are retrieved from related questions iteratively retrieved from a search engine using already-obtained questions as search queries ([Document 1](document_1.txt)). The only human labor in this work is question template construction ([Document 1](document_1.txt)). This makes the template count a central measure of manual effort.

## Direct Answer: 106, 103, and 209 Templates

### Summary of Counts

The work reports two distinct template-construction efforts. For the Freebase evaluation, the authors hand-crafted **106 templates** ([Document 1](document_1.txt); [Document 2](document_2.txt)). For the in-house power tool domain KB, the authors hand-crafted **103 templates for 67 predicates** ([Document 2](document_2.txt)). Together with the 106 Freebase templates, the authors wrote **209 templates in total** ([Document 2](document_2.txt)). The paper reports this total in the template-construction experiment ([Document 2](document_2.txt)). Thus, the most comprehensive answer to “How many hand-crafted templates did they have to make?” is **209 templates total**, composed of **106 templates for Freebase** and **103 templates for the power tool domain**.

## Freebase Evaluation: 106 Templates for 500 Triples

### Predicate-Based Compression

For the Freebase evaluation, the work uses 500 randomly selected triples ([Document 1](document_1.txt); [Document 2](document_2.txt)). The paper reports 106 hand-crafted templates for those 500 Freebase triples ([Document 2](document_2.txt)). The triples share only 53 distinct predicates, so the authors made 2 templates for each predicate on average ([Document 1](document_1.txt); [Document 2](document_2.txt)). Applying the templates to the triples generated 991 seed questions, and the experiment also retrieved 1,529 more questions from Google ([Document 1](document_1.txt); [Document 2](document_2.txt)). This design ties template creation to predicates, keeping the template set compact relative to the triple set ([Document 2](document_2.txt)).

The Freebase template count is particularly important because it demonstrates the predicate-based compression of manual effort. Instead of writing templates for each of the 500 triples, the authors wrote 106 templates for 53 predicates. That is roughly one template for every 4.7 triples, or two templates per predicate. The result was 991 seed questions, which is more than nine times the number of templates. This ratio illustrates the leverage of applying a small predicate-level template set to many entity pairs.

## Power Tool Domain Evaluation: 103 Templates for 67 Predicates

### Domain-Specific Scaling

For the in-house power tool domain knowledge base, the paper reports 67 predicates ([Document 2](document_2.txt)). For those 67 predicates, the authors hand-crafted 103 templates ([Document 2](document_2.txt)). This is an average of approximately 1.54 templates per predicate. The domain-specific KB has its own predicate set and required its own template set ([Document 2](document_2.txt)). In this domain, the system generated 12,228 seed questions, from which 20,000 more questions were expanded with Google ([Document 1](document_1.txt)). Examples of expanded questions include “how to change circular saw blade,” “how to measure lawn mower cutting height,” “how to sharpen drill bits on bench grinder,” “how does an oscillating multi tool work,” “how to cut a groove in wood without a router,” “what type of sander to use on deck,” “do i need a hammer drill,” and “can i use acrylic paint on wood” ([Document 1](document_1.txt)). Most were grammatical and relevant to the power tool domain, and most were informative and corresponded to a specific answer, except for “do I need a hammer drill,” which lacked context information ([Document 1](document_1.txt)). The system also generated complex questions beyond simple factoids, such as “how to cut a groove in wood without a router” ([Document 1](document_1.txt)).

The power tool count shows that the manual template-building effort scales with predicate coverage in each evaluation ([Document 2](document_2.txt)). A larger predicate set requires more templates, but the expansion and selection stages do the heavy lifting of producing many questions.

## Aggregate Manual Effort: 209 Templates

### Total Reported Figure

The aggregate figure is **209 hand-crafted templates**: 106 for Freebase plus 103 for the power tool domain ([Document 2](document_2.txt)). The paper reports this total in the template-construction experiment ([Document 2](document_2.txt)). The Freebase and power tool counts provide the reported template-construction figures for the system ([Document 2](document_2.txt)). These figures show the manual template-building effort scales with predicate coverage in each evaluation ([Document 2](document_2.txt)). In my assessment, 209 templates is a modest manual investment compared with alternative approaches that rely on large human-labeled datasets. For example, prior work treating question generation as machine translation trained a neural machine translation (NMT) system with 10,000 ⟨triple, question⟩ pairs, and the question part of those pairs was human generated, requiring a large amount of human effort ([Document 1](document_1.txt)). The template-based system instead centralizes human labor into 209 predicate-level templates and then uses web resources for expansion ([Document 1](document_1.txt); [Document 2](document_2.txt)).

## Why the Template Count Is Relatively Small

The paper explains that the system does not require a large number of templates because of two main factors: (1) iterative question expansion can produce a large number of questions even with a relatively small number of seed questions, as seen in the experiments, and (2) multiple entities in the KB share the same predicates ([Document 1](document_1.txt)). Another advantage is that the system can easily generate updated questions as the web is self-updating consistently ([Document 1](document_1.txt)). This is why 106 templates can cover 500 Freebase triples and why 103 templates can cover 67 power tool predicates. The template set is associated with predicates, not with individual triples or entities, so the manual work is amortized across many KB facts ([Document 1](document_1.txt); [Document 2](document_2.txt)).

## Evaluation and Quality Context

The evaluation of the generated questions involved human graders. Evaluated by three human graders, questions generated by the system were significantly better than the comparison system on grammaticality and naturalness ([Document 1](document_1.txt)). For the Freebase evaluation, the authors trained a 4-gram language model on Gigaword (LDC2011T07) with Kneser-Ney smoothing to evaluate fluency, and using the averaged language model score as an index, the top 500 questions were selected to compare with the results from the other system ([Document 1](document_1.txt)). Three native English speakers evaluated the fluency and naturalness—whether people will ask in reality—of both results based on a 4-point scheme where 4 is the best ([Document 1](document_1.txt)). The abstract also states that questions generated by the system were judged to be more fluent than those of the comparison system by human graders on the 500 random-selected Freebase triples ([Document 1](document_1.txt)). This quality context matters because it suggests that the relatively small template count did not come at the cost of fluency or naturalness in the reported evaluation.

## Comparison with Prior Work and Human Effort

The paper contrasts its approach with previous work that relies on massive human-labeled data ([Document 1](document_1.txt)). Treating question generation as a machine translation problem, prior work trained an NMT system with 10,000 ⟨triple, question⟩ pairs; the question part of the 10,000 pairs was human generated, which requires a large amount of human effort ([Document 1](document_1.txt)). In addition, the grammaticality and naturalness of generated questions in that approach could not be guaranteed, as seen in Table 1 of the source ([Document 1](document_1.txt)). The template-based approach uses 209 templates total, which is a different and much smaller unit of manual effort than 10,000 human-generated questions. The comparison is not perfectly apples-to-apples because templates can generate many questions and are not themselves full questions, but the reported counts do indicate a substantially different allocation of human labor ([Document 1](document_1.txt); [Document 2](document_2.txt)).

## Methodological Significance

The methodological significance of the 209-template figure is that it quantifies the only human labor in the system ([Document 1](document_1.txt)). The system first constructs a small set of question templates, each associated with a predicate in the KB ([Document 1](document_1.txt); [Document 2](document_2.txt)). For example, in the power tool domain, the template “how to use #X#” was constructed for the predicate “performsActivity” ([Document 1](document_1.txt)). The seed question “how to use jigsaw” was generated by applying the template to the triple ⟨jigsaw, performsActivity, CurveCut⟩, and then related questions were retrieved from Google using that seed question ([Document 1](document_1.txt)). This pipeline shows how a single predicate-level template can yield a seed question that then unlocks web-scale expansion. The 209 templates are therefore not the final question set; they are the manual seed mechanism that enables automatic expansion and selection.

## Limitations and Caveats

### Source Reliability

Several caveats apply to the interpretation of the template counts. First, the 209 total is reported in the third-party research note ([Document 2](document_2.txt)), while the primary document excerpts explicitly report the 106 Freebase templates ([Document 1](document_1.txt)). The 103 power tool templates and the 209 total are provided in the research note that synthesizes the paper ([Document 2](document_2.txt)).

### Derived Totals

Second, the total row in Table 1 combines two separate evaluations; the 209 template total is official, but the combined seed-question and web-retrieved sums are derived and should be treated as descriptive rather than as a reported aggregate ([Document 1](document_1.txt); [Document 2](document_2.txt)). Third, the number of distinct predicates across the two KBs is reported separately: 53 for Freebase and 67 for the power tool domain ([Document 1](document_1.txt); [Document 2](document_2.txt)). The sum of 120 is arithmetic and does not imply a single unified predicate set. Fourth, the sources do not report a template count for any other KB or evaluation beyond Freebase and the power tool domain ([Document 2](document_2.txt)). Finally, the quality evaluation focuses on fluency, naturalness, grammaticality, and domain relevance; the provided information does not report a direct cost-benefit analysis of template construction time ([Document 1](document_1.txt); [Document 2](document_2.txt)).

## Conclusion

The direct answer to the query is that the authors hand-crafted **106 templates for the Freebase evaluation** and **103 templates for the in-house power tool domain knowledge base**, for a reported total of **209 hand-crafted templates** ([Document 1](document_1.txt); [Document 2](document_2.txt)). The Freebase templates covered 500 randomly selected triples with 53 distinct predicates, averaging two templates per predicate, and produced 991 seed questions plus 1,529 Google-retrieved questions ([Document 1](document_1.txt); [Document 2](document_2.txt)). The power tool templates covered 67 predicates and supported 12,228 seed questions plus 20,000 Google-expanded questions ([Document 1](document_1.txt); [Document 2](document_2.txt)). The reported design ties template creation to predicates, keeping the manual template set compact relative to the triple set and leveraging web exploration for scale ([Document 2](document_2.txt)). In my assessment, the 209-template total is the key manual-effort figure, and it supports the paper’s claim that the system reduces human effort by concentrating manual work into predicate-level templates and delegating expansion to web resources ([Document 1](document_1.txt); [Document 2](document_2.txt)).
