# How Many Hand-Crafted Templates Did They Have to Make? A Detailed Report on Template Construction in Knowledge Base Question Generation

## Abstract
This report answers the query by analyzing the provided documents on a question generation system that uses knowledge base (KB) triples and web exploration. The central finding is that the authors hand-crafted a total of **209 question templates** across two evaluation settings: **106 templates** for the Freebase evaluation and **103 templates** for an in-house power tool domain knowledge base. The Freebase templates were built for 500 randomly selected triples that shared only 53 distinct predicates, resulting in an average of two templates per predicate. The power tool templates were built for 67 predicates. The paper states that template construction is the only human labor in the work. This report details the derivation of these figures, the predicate-driven design, and the implications for reducing human effort in question generation.

## Introduction
Question generation from a knowledge base (KB) is the task of generating questions related to the domain of the input KB ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). The paper proposes a system that reduces human effort by leveraging massive web resources ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). The system first generates a seed question set by applying a small number of hand-crafted templates on the input KB, then retrieves more questions by iteratively forming already obtained questions as search queries into a standard search engine, and finally selects questions by estimating fluency and domain relevance ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). The only human labor in this work is the question template construction ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). Therefore, the number of hand-crafted templates is a critical measure of the manual effort required.

The query asks: "How many hand-crafted templates did they have to make?" Based on the provided sources, the answer is not a single number for one experiment but a total across two evaluations. The paper reports 106 templates for the Freebase evaluation and 103 templates for the in-house power tool domain knowledge base, yielding a total of 209 templates ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt); [document_2.txt](document_2.txt)). This report explains the breakdown, the predicate-based design, and the supporting metrics.

## The Freebase Evaluation: 106 Hand-Crafted Templates
### Experimental Setup
For the Freebase evaluation, the work uses 500 randomly selected triples ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt); [document_2.txt](document_2.txt)). The paper reports 106 hand-crafted templates for those 500 Freebase triples ([document_2.txt](document_2.txt)). These triples share only 53 distinct predicates, so the authors made 2 templates for each predicate on average ([document_2.txt](document_2.txt)). The calculation is straightforward: 53 distinct predicates multiplied by an average of 2 templates per predicate equals 106 templates ([document_2.txt](document_2.txt)). The paper explicitly states: "For the 500 triples, we hand-crafted 106 templates, as these triples share only 53 distinct predicates (we made 2 templates for each predicate on average)" ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)).

### Predicate-Driven Template Design
The proposed system first constructs a small set of question templates ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). Each template is associated with a predicate in the knowledge base ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). This association means template creation follows the predicates present in the KB ([document_2.txt](document_2.txt)). The paper states that the only human labor in this work is question template construction ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). The template construction therefore is the manual component reported ([document_2.txt](document_2.txt)). Templates consist of a transcription of the predicate in the KB (e.g., performsActivity ⇒ how to) and placeholders for the subject (#X#) and the object (#Y#) ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). For example, for predicate "performsActivity", a template "how to use #X#" is constructed ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)).

### Why 106 Templates for 53 Predicates?
The design ties template creation to predicates, keeping the template set compact relative to the triple set ([document_2.txt](document_2.txt)). With 500 triples but only 53 distinct predicates, the authors avoided creating a template per triple. Instead, they created an average of two templates per predicate. This approach recognizes that multiple entities in the KB share the same predicates, so templates can be reused across many triples ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). The compactness is evident: if one template were made per triple, 500 templates would be needed; instead, only 106 were needed. This is a reduction of approximately 78.8% in template count relative to the triple count. The paper reports that applying the templates to the triples generated 991 seed questions ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt); [document_2.txt](document_2.txt)). The experiment also retrieved 1529 more questions from Google ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt); [document_2.txt](document_2.txt)). Thus, the 106 templates served as the seed for a much larger question set.

### Seed Question Generation and Web Expansion
The 991 seed questions are generated by applying the templates on the triples, and 1529 more questions are retrieved from Google ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt); [document_2.txt](document_2.txt)). The system then trains a 4-gram language model on gigaword (LDC2011T07) with Kneser Ney smoothing to evaluate fluency ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). Using the averaged language model score as index, the top 500 questions are selected to compare with the results from ? ) ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). Three native English speakers evaluate the fluency and naturalness on a 4-point scheme where 4 is the best ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). The human graders judged that questions generated by the system are significantly better than ? ) on grammaticality and naturalness ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). This evaluation confirms that the 106 templates were sufficient to produce competitive questions.

## The In-House Power Tool Domain: 103 Hand-Crafted Templates
### Domain-Specific KB and Predicate Set
For the in-house power tool domain knowledge base, the paper reports 67 predicates ([document_2.txt](document_2.txt)). For the 67 predicates, the authors hand-crafted 103 templates ([document_2.txt](document_2.txt)). This domain-specific KB has its own predicate set and required its own template set ([document_2.txt](document_2.txt)). The Freebase and power tool counts provide the reported template-construction figures for the system ([document_2.txt](document_2.txt)). These figures show the manual template-building effort scales with predicate coverage in each evaluation ([document_2.txt](document_2.txt)).

### Template Density in the Power Tool Domain
The power tool domain required 103 templates for 67 predicates, which is approximately 1.54 templates per predicate. This is lower than the Freebase average of 2.0 templates per predicate. The difference suggests that the number of templates per predicate is not fixed but depends on the domain and the need for surface variation. The paper does not provide a detailed breakdown of why 103 rather than 134 templates (which would be 2 per predicate) were chosen. However, the key point is that the manual effort was calibrated to the predicate set, not to the number of entities or triples. The paper does not report the number of triples or entities in the power tool KB, but it does report that the system generates 12,228 seed questions from which 20,000 more questions are expanded with Google ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). This indicates that a relatively small template set (103) can seed a very large question set.

### Example Template and Expansion
Taking the in-house KB of power tool domain as an example, template "how to use #X#" is first constructed for predicate "performsActivity" ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). The system then generates seed questions by applying the template on the KB. The expanded questions include examples such as "how to change circular saw blade", "how to measure lawn mower cutting height", "how to sharpen drill bits on bench grinder", "how does an oscillating multi tool work", "how to cut a groove in wood without a router", "what type of sander to use on deck", "do i need a hammer drill", and "can i use acrylic paint on wood" ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). The paper notes that most expanded questions are grammatical and relevant to the power tool domain, and many are informative and correspond to a specific answer, except the one "do I need a hammer drill" that lacks context information ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). In addition to simple factoid questions, the system generates many complex questions such as "how to cut a groove in wood without a router" ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). This demonstrates that the 103 power tool templates were effective for generating diverse questions.

## Human Labor and Design Implications
### Only Human Labor Is Template Construction
The paper states that the only human labor in this work is the question template construction ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). This is a significant claim because previous work relies on massive human-labeled data. For example, previous work trains a neural machine translation (NMT) system with 10,000 ⟨triple, question⟩ pairs, where the question part is human generated ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). That requires a large amount of human effort. In contrast, the proposed system requires only 209 templates across two domains. The system does not require a large number of templates because (1) the iterative question expansion can produce a large number of questions even with a relatively small number of seed questions, as seen in the experiments, and (2) multiple entities in the KB share the same predicates ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). Another advantage is that the system can easily generate updated questions as the web is self-updating consistently ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)).

### Scalability with Predicate Coverage
The manual template-building effort scales with predicate coverage in each evaluation ([document_2.txt](document_2.txt)). For Freebase, 53 predicates required 106 templates. For the power tool domain, 67 predicates required 103 templates. This means the effort is tied to the number of distinct predicates, not the number of triples or entities. In the Freebase case, 500 triples collapsed to 53 predicates, so the template construction effort was about one-fifth of what it would have been if one template per triple were used. In the power tool case, the number of triples is not reported, but the 67 predicates required 103 templates. This design keeps the template set compact relative to the triple set ([document_2.txt](document_2.txt)).

### Comparison with Prior Work
Prior work relies on massive human-labeled data ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). Treating question generation as a machine translation problem, previous work trains an NMT system with 10,000 ⟨triple, question⟩ pairs ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). The question part of the 10,000 pairs are human generated, which requires a large amount of human effort ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). In addition, the grammaticality and naturalness of generated questions cannot be guaranteed ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). In contrast, the proposed system uses 209 hand-crafted templates total and leverages web resources to expand and select questions. Evaluated by human graders on 500 random-selected triples from Freebase, questions generated by the system are judged to be more fluent than those of the baseline ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)). Three human graders evaluated the questions, and the system's questions were significantly better on grammaticality and naturalness ([document_1.txt](document_1.txt)).

## Conclusion
The answer to the query "How many hand-crafted templates did they have to make?" is **209 templates in total**. This total comprises **106 templates** for the Freebase evaluation and **103 templates** for the in-house power tool domain knowledge base. The Freebase templates were built for 500 randomly selected triples that shared only 53 distinct predicates, resulting in an average of two templates per predicate. The power tool templates were built for 67 predicates. The paper states that template construction is the only human labor in the work. By tying templates to predicates, the authors kept the manual effort compact and scalable. The 209 templates seeded 13,219 questions (991 from Freebase and 12,228 from power tool) and enabled the retrieval of 21,529 additional questions from Google (1,529 from Freebase and 20,000 from power tool). The system's questions were judged by human graders to be more fluent and natural than a baseline. The 209 figure is therefore the central answer to the query, and it represents a significant reduction in human effort compared to prior work that required 10,000 human-generated question-triple pairs.
