# Hand-Crafted Template Counts in Question Generation from a Knowledge Base with Web Exploration

## Introduction
The query asks how many hand-crafted templates the authors had to make in the work on question generation from a knowledge base (KB) with web exploration. Based on the provided documents, the answer is not a single number because the system was evaluated in two separate settings: a Freebase evaluation and an in-house power tool domain evaluation. The authors hand-crafted 106 templates for the Freebase evaluation and 103 templates for the power tool domain knowledge base, producing a reported total of 209 hand-crafted templates ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807); [Document_2](document_2.txt)). This report explains those counts, the predicate-based design that produced them, and why the total manual template effort remained compact relative to the knowledge bases and question sets involved.

## Direct Answer and Summary of Counts
The most direct answer is that the authors had to make 209 hand-crafted templates in total. That total combines 106 templates for the Freebase evaluation and 103 templates for the in-house power tool domain knowledge base ([Document_2](document_2.txt)). The Freebase templates were built for 500 randomly selected triples, which shared only 53 distinct predicates, so the authors made approximately two templates for each predicate ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807); [Document_2](document_2.txt)). The power tool templates were built for 67 predicates, yielding 103 templates for that domain-specific knowledge base ([Document_2](document_2.txt)). Together, the two evaluations involved 120 predicates if the predicate sets are summed, and 209 templates, which is an average of about 1.74 templates per predicate across the reported evaluations.

Table 1 summarizes the reported and derived counts.

| Evaluation setting | Knowledge base | Distinct predicates | Hand-crafted templates | Seed questions | Additional questions retrieved or expanded | Source |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Freebase evaluation | Freebase (500 random triples) | 53 | 106 | 991 | 1,529 from Google | ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807); [Document_2](document_2.txt)) |
| In-house domain evaluation | Power tool domain KB | 67 | 103 | 12,228 | 20,000 expanded with Google | ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807); [Document_2](document_2.txt)) |
| Total reported | Both | 120 (derived sum) | 209 | 13,219 (derived sum) | 21,529 (derived sum) | ([Document_2](document_2.txt)) |

The derived totals in Table 1 are simple sums of the reported figures: 53 + 67 = 120 predicates, 106 + 103 = 209 templates, 991 + 12,228 = 13,219 seed questions, and 1,529 + 20,000 = 21,529 additional questions. The total number of generated or retrieved questions across both evaluations is therefore 34,748 if seed and additional questions are combined, although the Freebase comparison itself used the top 500 questions selected by language model score ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). The important point for the present query is that the manual template construction produced 209 templates, not thousands or tens of thousands.

## Freebase Evaluation: 106 Hand-Crafted Templates
In the Freebase evaluation, the authors used 500 randomly selected triples and hand-crafted 106 templates for those triples ([Document_2](document_2.txt)). The paper explains that these triples share only 53 distinct predicates, so the authors made two templates for each predicate on average ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807); [Document_2](document_2.txt)). This is the first and most prominent template count in the work. The 106 templates were applied to the triples to generate 991 seed questions, and the experiment retrieved 1,529 more questions from Google ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807); [Document_2](document_2.txt)). Thus, 106 manual templates led to 991 seed questions and 1,529 additional web-retrieved questions, for a total of 2,520 candidate questions in the Freebase setting before the final selection step.

The Freebase evaluation also included a comparison with a neural machine translation approach. The prior approach trained an NMT system with 10,000 human-generated triple-question pairs ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). By contrast, the reported system's only human labor is question template construction, and the Freebase portion of that labor was 106 templates ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807); [Document_2](document_2.txt)). This contrast is central to the paper's claim that human effort is significantly reduced. The authors selected the top 500 questions using an averaged 4-gram language model score, trained on Gigaword (LDC2011T07) with Kneser-Ney smoothing, and then asked three native English speakers to evaluate fluency and naturalness on a 4-point scheme where 4 is best ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). The template count of 106 is therefore the manual input for the Freebase comparison, while the evaluation itself used a subset of the generated questions.

## In-House Power Tool Domain: 103 Hand-Crafted Templates
For the in-house power tool domain knowledge base, the paper reports 67 predicates and 103 hand-crafted templates ([Document_2](document_2.txt)). This domain-specific KB has its own predicate set and required its own template set ([Document_2](document_2.txt)). The authors report that together with the 106 Freebase templates, the power tool templates bring the total to 209 ([Document_2](document_2.txt)). In the power tool domain, the system generated 12,228 seed questions, from which 20,000 more questions were expanded with Google ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). The paper gives examples of expanded questions such as “how to change circular saw blade,” “how to measure lawn mower cutting height,” “how to sharpen drill bits on bench grinder,” “how does an oscillating multi tool work,” “how to cut a groove in wood without a router,” “what type of sander to use on deck,” “do i need a hammer drill,” and “can i use acrylic paint on wood” ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). These examples illustrate that a relatively small set of 103 templates can produce a large and domain-relevant question set when combined with iterative web expansion.

The power tool domain also provides a concrete example of how templates are tied to predicates. The template “how to use #X#” is first constructed for the predicate “performsActivity,” and the seed question “how to use jigsaw” is generated by applying the template to the triple ⟨jigsaw, performsActivity, CurveCut⟩ ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). This example shows the template-predicate association directly: one template for one predicate, instantiated with different entities from the KB. The power tool evaluation thus reinforces the predicate-level design that keeps the template count at 103 for 67 predicates, an average of about 1.54 templates per predicate. The paper also states that most expanded questions are grammatical and relevant to the power tool domain, and most are informative and correspond to a specific answer, except the one “do I need a hammer drill” that lacks context information ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). In addition to simple factoid questions, the system generates complex questions such as “how to cut a groove in wood without a router” ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)).

## Total Template Effort: 209 Hand-Crafted Templates
The total reported hand-crafted template effort is 209. This figure is explicitly reported as the sum of 106 Freebase templates and 103 power tool templates ([Document_2](document_2.txt)). The paper states that the only human labor in the work is question template construction ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807); [Document_2](document_2.txt)). Therefore, the 209 figure represents the entire manual template-building component reported for the system. The system contains sub-modules for question template construction, seed question generation, question expansion and selection ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). Only the first sub-module requires human template writing; the remaining steps are automated or web-driven.

The authors explain that the system does not require a large number of templates for two reasons: (1) iterative question expansion can produce a large number of questions even with a relatively small number of seed questions, as shown in the experiments, and (2) multiple entities in the KB share the same predicates ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). This explanation directly accounts for the compactness of the 209-template total. Because templates are associated with predicates rather than with individual triples or entities, the manual effort scales with predicate coverage. In the Freebase evaluation, 500 triples collapse to 53 distinct predicates, so 106 templates cover the predicate space at about two templates per predicate. In the power tool evaluation, 67 predicates require 103 templates. The combined 120 predicates and 209 templates give an overall average of about 1.74 templates per predicate. The paper also notes that the system can easily generate updated questions because the web is self-updating consistently ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)).

## Why the Template Count Remains Compact
The template count remains compact because of the architecture of the system. The paper describes a pipeline in which a small set of question templates is first constructed, each associated with a predicate in the knowledge base ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). The template set is then applied to the input KB to generate a seed question set, and more questions are generated from related questions iteratively retrieved from a search engine using already-obtained questions as search queries ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). The association between templates and predicates means that template creation follows the predicates present in the KB, not the number of triples or entities ([Document_2](document_2.txt)). This is why 106 templates can support 500 Freebase triples: those triples share only 53 distinct predicates ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807); [Document_2](document_2.txt)). It is also why 103 templates can support a 67-predicate power tool KB ([Document_2](document_2.txt)).

A second reason is that the system can easily generate updated questions because the web is self-updating consistently ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). Once the initial templates and seed questions exist, the expansion and selection modules can retrieve new or updated questions from the web. This reduces the need to hand-craft additional templates for every new question or every new entity. The manual effort is concentrated at the predicate level, while the automated web exploration handles breadth and updating. The authors also note that the current system only generates questions without answers, leaving automatic answer mining as future work ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). That limitation does not change the template count, but it clarifies the scope of the reported manual effort.

## Comparative Context: 209 Templates vs. 10,000 Human-Generated Pairs
The significance of the 209 hand-crafted templates becomes clearer when compared with the baseline approach discussed in the paper. The prior neural machine translation approach trains on 10,000 ⟨triple, question⟩ pairs, and the question part of those 10,000 pairs is human generated, requiring a large amount of human effort ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). In addition, the grammaticality and naturalness of generated questions from that approach cannot be guaranteed ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). The reported system instead relies on 209 templates and web exploration. The paper reports that questions generated by the system were evaluated by three human graders and judged significantly better than the baseline on grammaticality and naturalness in the Freebase comparison ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). The abstract also states that questions generated by the system were judged to be more fluent than those of the baseline ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)).

From an objective standpoint, the evidence supports the conclusion that 209 templates is a modest manual cost for the reported evaluations. The number is not zero, and the templates are indeed hand-crafted, so the system is not fully unsupervised. However, the template count is small relative to the 500 Freebase triples, the 120 predicates across both evaluations, the 13,219 seed questions, and the 21,529 additional questions retrieved or expanded. The design achieves its coverage by tying templates to predicates and by using iterative web expansion to multiply the seed set. The authors' own explanation—that iterative expansion and shared predicates reduce the need for many templates—is consistent with the reported counts ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). Therefore, the answer to the query is best stated as: 106 templates for Freebase, 103 templates for the power tool domain, and 209 templates in total.

## Implications and Limitations
Several implications follow from the reported template counts. First, the manual effort scales with predicate coverage in each evaluation ([Document_2](document_2.txt)). A new domain with more predicates would require more templates, but the increase is tied to predicate diversity rather than to the number of triples. Second, the Freebase and power tool evaluations show that different KBs require different template sets because they have different predicate sets ([Document_2](document_2.txt)). The 209 total is therefore not a universal template library but the sum of two domain-specific template-construction efforts. Third, the system's reliance on web exploration means that the quality and scope of generated questions depend on search engine results, not only on the templates. The paper's human evaluation and example questions suggest that this combination works well for the reported domains, but the template count alone does not guarantee domain relevance or fluency.

A limitation is that the current system generates questions without answers ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). The template construction is the only human labor reported, but answer mining remains future work. Another limitation is that the paper's reported template count is tied to the specific evaluations: 500 Freebase triples with 53 predicates and an in-house power tool KB with 67 predicates. These numbers should not be generalized to all possible knowledge bases without considering the number and diversity of predicates. Nevertheless, for the query at hand, the reported figures are clear and consistent across the provided documents: 106, 103, and 209.

## Conclusion
The authors had to make 106 hand-crafted templates for the Freebase evaluation, 103 hand-crafted templates for the in-house power tool domain knowledge base, and 209 hand-crafted templates in total ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807); [Document_2](document_2.txt)). The Freebase templates covered 500 randomly selected triples that shared only 53 distinct predicates, and the power tool templates covered 67 predicates ([Document_2](document_2.txt)). The total of 209 reflects the only human labor in the system, namely question template construction ([Song & Zhao, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03807)). Because templates are associated with predicates and because iterative web expansion can generate many questions from a small seed set, the manual template burden remained compact relative to the number of triples, predicates, and questions involved. The most accurate single-number answer to the query is 209 hand-crafted templates overall, with 106 for Freebase and 103 for the power tool domain.
